# Woodworking and joinery machinery — manufacturers, specification and Southern African dealers

**Summary.** Joinery machinery divides cleanly by shop scale: a bench/site kit, a five-to-eight machine small shop built around a sliding table saw, a semi-industrial shop that adds an automatic edgebander and a nesting CNC, and a full production plant with beam saws, throughfeed lines and software-driven batch-of-one manufacture. This file maps the manufacturers, states what to look at on a machine specification, sets out what a shop needs at each scale, and records the Southern African representation — which for a Namibian or South African shop is often the deciding factor.

## The manufacturers

### Italian groups — SCM and Biesse
**SCM Group** (Rimini) is the largest single woodworking machinery group in the world, selling under **SCM** (panel saws, edgebanders, CNC, sanders, throughfeed), plus the group brands **Morbidelli** (CNC machining centres), **Stefani** (edgebanding), **Gabbiani** (beam saws), **DMC** (wide-belt sanding), **CMS** (composites/stone), **Superfici** (finishing) and **Cursal** (crosscutting). The **minimax** and **SCM Formula** lines address small shops; the **Class/Plus** lines are industrial. Strength: complete plant supply from one source, including software.

**Biesse Group** (Pesaro) covers **Biesse Wood** (Rover CNC, Selco beam saws, Akron/Stream edgebanders, Viet sanding), plus **Intermac** (glass/stone) and **Diamut** (tooling). Historically stronger in CNC than in classical machines; excellent Rover nesting platform.

### Austrian/German family houses — Felder, Altendorf, Martin, Hammer
**Felder Group** (Hall in Tirol) sells a tiered family: **Hammer** (entry professional, combination machines, very good value), **Felder** (the mainstream professional line), **Format-4** (premium, digital control, sliding tables to 3.8 m), and **MAYER** (beam saws). Felder's combination machines — saw/shaper, planer/thicknesser — are the standard answer for a shop that is space-constrained. **[ZA] Felder Group South Africa** operates directly (Gauteng, telephone +27 12 643 0515), which is unusual and valuable: factory-backed service and parts rather than an agent.

**Altendorf** (Minden) invented the sliding table saw in 1906 and remains the reference for it — the **F45**, **WA8**, **F25** and the **HAND GUARD** camera-based blade-contact protection system. A shop that buys one machine for twenty years buys an Altendorf.

**MARTIN** (Otto Martin Maschinenbau, Ottobeuren) is the other precision German house — T60 spindle moulder, T75 saw, T45 planer. Extremely high build quality, digital axis control on everything, and priced accordingly. Martin and Altendorf sit at the top of the classical-machine market.

### Industrial systems — HOMAG
**HOMAG Group** (Dürr AG; Schopfloch) is the industrial end: throughfeed edgebanders (KAL/Ambition), CNC processing centres (CENTATEQ), beam saws (SAWTEQ), CNC drilling and dowel insertion (DRILLTEQ), and full factory automation with **tapio** and **woodCommander/woodWOP** software. HOMAG also owns **Weeke**, **Brandt**, **Bütfering** and **Holzma** (now folded into the SAWTEQ naming). If the requirement is 200+ carcasses a week with batch-size-one, this is the tier.

### Others worth knowing
- **Laguna Tools** (US) — bandsaws, CNC (Swift/SmartShop), dust collection; strong value in the small-shop and hobby-to-pro segment.
- **Lamello** (Switzerland) — invented the biscuit joint; now best known for the **Clamex P-14**, **Tenso** and **Invis Mx2** demountable and self-clamping connectors, and the **Zeta P2** machine that cuts their profile groove. Transformative for demountable and site-assembled joinery.
- **Festool** — not a machinery house but the reference for portable precision: TS/TSC tracksaws, Domino DF 500/700 loose-tenon jointers, OF routers, CT extractors, LR 32 system-hole jig, Conturo edgebander.
- **Mafell** — the other German portable specialist; the best portable beam saws, scarf-jointers and the DDF 40 Duo-Doweller. CAS battery-compatible.
- **Nanxing**, **KDT**, **Woodtec/Weinig**, **Casadei**, **Griggio**, **Casolin**, **Robland** — mid-market and value alternatives common in SADC.
- **Weinig / Michael Weinig** — solid-wood specialist: moulders, optimising crosscut lines, finger-jointing, Powermat/Rondamat. The right answer for a shop machining large volumes of solid timber.

## What to look at on a machine specification

**Sliding table saw**
- Carriage length and its **repeatability under load** (the outrigger, not the carriage, deflects).
- Blade projection and **tilt** (0–46°), scoring adjustment (motorised is worth it), rip fence capacity.
- **Digital positioning**: motorised rip fence and blade height/tilt saves minutes per setup and pays back in a busy shop.
- Motor power 4–7.5 kW; 3-phase almost always.
- Blade-contact safety: SawStop (US), Altendorf HAND GUARD, Felder PCS.

**Spindle moulder / shaper**
- Spindle diameter (30 mm minimum), interchangeable spindles, **tilt** (±45°), variable speed 1 500–10 000 rpm, sliding table, and a **4-roller variable-speed power feed** — treat the feed as part of the machine, not an accessory.

**Planer / thicknesser**
- Width 300–630 mm; **helical/spiral cutterblock** with indexable carbide inserts is the single best upgrade for interlocked-grain African hardwoods (kiaat, saligna, imbuia) and drops noise dramatically.
- Table flatness and the ease of the changeover on combination machines.

**Wide-belt sander**
- Belt width (630/1 100/1 350 mm), number of heads (roller for calibration, pad for finishing), belt grit sequencing, and thickness range.

**Edgebander**
- Number of units: pre-milling → gluing/pressing → end trim → flush trim → contour scraping → glue scraping → buffing. A "corner-rounding" unit and **PUR capability** are the two upgrades that most change output quality.
- Feed speed, minimum panel length, tape thickness range (0.4–3 mm typical small, to 12 mm on solid lipping machines).

**CNC router**
- **Nesting** (flat table, vacuum, sheet-in/parts-out) versus **point-to-point** (pods, for solid rails/stiles and doors). Most cabinet shops need nesting.
- Table zoning (so a vacuum leak in one area does not lose the whole sheet), tool changer capacity (8–16+), spindle power and HSK-63F interface, aggregates for horizontal drilling/sawing, and — critically — **the CAM software and its cabinet library**. The machine is a commodity; the software is where the productivity is.
- Dust extraction volume at the head; a nesting router generates more chip than everything else in the shop combined.

## What a shop needs at each scale

### Scale 1 — installer / site joiner
Tracksaw and rails, cordless platform, router, Domino or biscuit joiner, mitre saw, M-class extractor, clamps, straightedges, cross-line laser. Buys cut-to-size board and edged panels from a merchant. Capital: modest; the constraint is board processing, which is outsourced.

### Scale 2 — small shop (1–4 people, kitchens and fitted furniture)
- **Sliding table saw**, 2.6–3.2 m carriage, with scorer — the anchor machine.
- **Planer/thicknesser** 300–410 mm (combination acceptable), helical block.
- **Spindle moulder** with power feed.
- **Bandsaw** 400–500 mm wheel.
- **Manual or entry edgebander** (or a hand edgebander plus a trimmer, for low volume).
- **Drill press** and a **32 mm line-boring** capability (or an LR 32 jig).
- **Wide-belt or drum sander** — the first "luxury" that immediately pays for itself in finish quality.
- **Dust extraction** sized at 1.5–2× the largest machine, with ducted branches at ~20 m/s.
- Typical brands at this level: Hammer, Felder, minimax/SCM Formula, Robland, Casadei, Laguna, Nanxing.

### Scale 3 — semi-industrial (5–15 people)
Add: **automatic edgebander** with pre-milling and corner rounding; **nesting CNC router** with a 12+ tool changer; **vertical panel saw or beam saw** if nesting alone cannot keep up; **case clamp**; **spray booth**; cabinet design and nesting software (Cabinet Vision, Imos, Microvellum, or the OEM's own). Typical brands: SCM, Biesse, Felder/Format-4, Altendorf, Casadei.

### Scale 4 — production plant
Beam saw with automatic feed and labelling, throughfeed edgebander at 25–60 m/min, CNC drilling and dowel insertion, return conveyors, barcode-driven part tracking, ERP integration. HOMAG, SCM Class/Plus, Biesse. At this scale the machinery decision is a **systems integration** decision, and the software and the service contract matter more than the iron.

## Southern African representation

**[ZA] [NA]** Almost all machinery for Namibia is bought through South Africa; there is no meaningful Namibian machinery importer at industrial scale. Practical routes:

| Manufacturer | Southern African route |
|---|---|
| **Felder / Format-4 / Hammer / MAYER** | **Felder Group South Africa** — a direct factory subsidiary (Gauteng, +27 12 643 0515), with showroom, parts and service. The strongest single argument for the Felder family in this region. |
| **SCM / minimax** | Appointed SCM agents in Gauteng and the Western Cape; confirm current appointment via SCM Group's contacts page. |
| **Altendorf** | Appointed agent; parts direct from Minden. |
| **HOMAG** | HOMAG has an African sales and service structure; check the HOMAG locations page for current representation. |
| **Biesse** | Agent-represented in South Africa; Biesse also services parts of Africa from its Middle East operation. |
| **MARTIN** | Agent-represented; low volume, specialist buyers. |
| **Festool / Mafell / Lamello** | Distributed through professional tool merchants; Festool has an established South African distributor network. |
| **Laguna** | Distributed through hobby/pro tool retailers. |
| **Chinese brands (Nanxing, KDT, Woodmaster)** | Multiple SA importers; the value proposition is price, and the risk is parts lead time. |

> ⚠️ **[NA]** When importing machinery into Namibia via South Africa, both are SACU members so no customs duty applies, but **import VAT (15%) does**, and a 3-phase machine needs its supply confirmed: much of rural northern Namibia is single-phase, and a phase converter or a VFD is a design decision, not an afterthought. Confirm supply voltage (400 V / 50 Hz) and available kVA before ordering.

**Second-hand.** A well-maintained 15-year-old Altendorf, Martin or SCM will outperform a new low-cost machine, and the European used market (via Mascus, Machineseeker, Exapro) supplies southern Africa steadily. Check: spindle bearing condition, carriage wear, whether the guards and riving knife are present, and whether the control electronics are still supported. Budget for shipping, import VAT, rigging, and a full electrical re-certification.
