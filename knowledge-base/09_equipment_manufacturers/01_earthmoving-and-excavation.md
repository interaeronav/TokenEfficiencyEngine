# Earthmoving and excavation equipment — manufacturers, families and size classes

**Summary.** Earthmoving is the largest capital block on most civil projects and the one where the wrong size class costs the most. This file maps the major manufacturers active in southern Africa, the machine families, the size classes with indicative operating weights and outputs, and a selection framework that separates small residential work from civil and bulk earthworks. Bell Equipment is covered in the most detail because it is the South African OEM, its Africa-specification machines differ materially from European ones, and its published data was directly verified.

## Machine families and size classes

Weights below are **indicative class ranges** for planning, not model specifications. Read the actual figure off the current spec sheet before committing.

### Hydraulic excavators (tracked)

| Class | Operating weight | Typical net power | Bucket (SAE heaped) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Micro / mini | 0.8–3 t | 7–20 kW | 0.02–0.10 m³ | Indoor, trenching in gardens, footings on tight residential plots |
| Compact | 3–8 t | 20–45 kW | 0.10–0.30 m³ | Residential foundations, services trenching, landscaping |
| Midi | 8–14 t | 45–80 kW | 0.30–0.60 m³ | Small civils, house platforms, drainage |
| **13–16 t** | 13–16 t | 70–95 kW | 0.5–0.8 m³ | The versatile all-rounder; low-bed transportable on standard trailers |
| **20–22 t** | 19–24 t | 95–130 kW | 0.8–1.4 m³ | The civil-works default; Cat 320-class, Komatsu PC210-class, Volvo EC220-class |
| 30 t | 28–34 t | 150–200 kW | 1.4–2.0 m³ | Bulk earthworks, loading 25–30 t ADTs |
| 35–50 t | 34–50 t | 200–320 kW | 1.8–3.2 m³ | Quarry/mining, loading 40 t+ ADTs |
| 50 t+ | 50–800 t | 320 kW+ | 3 m³+ | Mining; outside general construction |

Model-number heuristics: **Cat** 3xx (320 ≈ 20 t, 336 ≈ 36 t); **Komatsu** PC-nnn (PC210 ≈ 21 t, PC360 ≈ 36 t); **Volvo** EC-nnn (EC220 ≈ 22 t); **Hitachi** ZX-nnn; **Develon** DX-nnn; **SANY** SY-nnn; **JCB** JS-nnn. These are approximations that broadly hold.

Undercarriage variants matter: **LC** (long carriage — more stability, standard for civils), **NLC/standard**, **LR** (long reach), **HD**, **ZTS/SR** (zero/short tail swing, essential against boundary walls), and **wheeled** excavators (common in Europe, rarer in SADC).

### Backhoe loaders

The default machine for small contractors in southern Africa: one operator, one machine, loads, digs, trenches and travels on the road at 40 km/h.

| Class | Operating weight | Engine | Dig depth | Loader capacity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compact (JCB 1CX class) | ~3 t | ~25–35 kW | ~2.5 m | ~0.4 m³ |
| Standard 2WD (3CX class) | 7.5–8.5 t | 55–75 kW | ~4.4–4.7 m | ~1.0–1.1 m³ |
| Sidehift / 4WD (4CX class) | 8.5–9.5 t | 70–85 kW | ~5.5–6.0 m | ~1.1–1.3 m³ |

**JCB 3CX/4CX**, **Cat 428/432**, **Case 580/590**, **New Holland B-series**, **Komatsu WB93** and **Terex/TLB** clones dominate. In South Africa "TLB" (tractor-loader-backhoe) is the standard term; hire rates are quoted per hour with operator.

### Wheel loaders

| Class | Operating weight | Bucket (SAE 2:1 heaped) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compact | 4–8 t | 0.6–1.2 m³ | Yards, stockpiles, landscaping |
| Small | 10–14 t | 1.5–2.3 m³ | Batch-plant feed, general site |
| Medium (Cat 950 / Volvo L120 / Komatsu WA380 class) | 17–22 t | 3.0–4.0 m³ | Bulk loading, quarry secondary |
| Large (Cat 966–980 / Volvo L150–L180) | 23–33 t | 4.0–6.5 m³ | Quarry primary loading, truck loading |
| Very large | 35 t+ | 7 m³+ | Mining |

Key selection numbers are **full turn tipping load** (stability) and **breakout force**, not bucket size — a bucket you cannot lift full is decorative.

### Dozers (crawler tractors)

| Class | Operating weight | Net power | Blade capacity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (D3/D4 class) | 8–13 t | 55–90 kW | 1.5–3.0 m³ |
| Medium (D6 class) | 18–24 t | 110–160 kW | 3.5–5.5 m³ |
| Large (D8 class) | 37–40 t | 230–260 kW | 8–11 m³ |
| Very large (D9/D10/D11) | 48–110 t | 300–650 kW | 13–45 m³ |

Blade types: **S (straight)** for fine grading, **SU (semi-universal)** general, **U (universal)** for high-volume loose material, **PAT (power-angle-tilt)** for versatile small machines. **LGP** variants trade traction for flotation on soft ground.

### Motor graders

The finishing machine for roads and platforms. Classes are by blade length and weight: 12-ft (3.7 m) blade at ~15–18 t for municipal work; 14-ft (4.3 m) at ~19–23 t for road construction — this is where **Cat 140**, **Komatsu GD655**, **Volvo G930** and **Bell G140/G160/G200** sit; 16-ft at 25 t+ for mine haul roads. Verified: Bell G140 **179 kW / 21 289 kg**, G160 **209 kW / 21 638 kg**, G200 **231 kW / 22 538 kg** — note the weight barely changes across the range while power rises 29%, which is deliberate: the grader is traction-limited, and extra power buys blade speed and gradeability, not down-pressure.

### Articulated dump trucks (ADTs)

The southern African bulk-earthworks workhorse: 6×6 articulated frame, all-wheel drive, oscillating hitch, designed for soft and broken haul roads where a rigid truck cannot go. Class sizes cluster at **18, 25, 30, 40, 45, 50 t** payload, with the Bell B60E at **55 t** as a 4×4 outlier. Matching rule of thumb: the loading excavator should fill the truck in **3–5 passes**; fewer wastes truck capacity, more wastes cycle time. A 30 t ADT (≈15–17 m³ SAE 2:1) pairs with a 30–35 t excavator carrying a 1.6–2.0 m³ bucket.

### Skid steers and compact track loaders

Operating weights 1.5–5 t, rated operating capacity (ROC) 350–1 600 kg, hydraulic flow 60–130 l/min (high-flow options to 150+). The value is the attachment ecosystem — trenchers, augers, planers, brooms, breakers, mulchers — on a universal quick-attach plate. Compact **track** loaders (CTLs) roughly double flotation and traction for ~20% more cost and much higher undercarriage running cost. **Bobcat**, **Cat**, **Case**, **New Holland**, **JCB** (side-entry) and **Wacker Neuson** are the main players.

## Selection guidance

### Small residential work (single house, boundary walls, services)

The binding constraints are **access**, **damage risk** and **transport cost**, not productivity.

- **Site access under 2.5 m wide** → 1.5–3 t mini excavator with retractable tracks, plus wheelbarrows or a 1 t site dumper.
- **Normal suburban plot** → a **TLB (backhoe loader)** is usually the single best value: it digs footings and trenches, loads spoil, backfills, and drives itself between jobs without a low-bed.
- **Working against an existing boundary wall** → specify a **zero/short-tail-swing** excavator; a conventional counterweight will hit the wall.
- **Ground bearing on a finished driveway or paving** → mini excavator on rubber tracks or rubber pads; a steel-tracked machine will destroy it.
- Hire, do not buy, below roughly **400–600 machine hours per year** — below that, hire plus operator is cheaper than ownership once you include finance, insurance, service, transport and idle time.

### Civil works, bulk earthworks and roads

The binding constraints are **cycle cost per cubic metre** and **fleet balance**.

- Size the **loading tool** to the haul unit (3–5 pass rule), then size the **haul fleet** to the loading tool's cycle time and the haul distance. An unbalanced fleet is the most common and most expensive plant error.
- **Haul distance** decides the haul unit: under ~150 m, a dozer or a loader shuttling is cheaper than trucks; 150 m–1.5 km favours ADTs; beyond that, and on good roads, rigid tippers win on cost per tonne-km.
- For **layerworks and road formation**, the grader plus a compaction fleet (see `04_compaction-roads-and-paving.md`) sets the pace, not the excavator.
- **Standardise on one or two brands** to halve your parts inventory and simplify operator training — a real saving on a remote site.
- **Check dealer response distance.** **[NA]** In northern Namibia, a machine whose nearest dealer branch is Windhoek or Walvis Bay carries a multi-day downtime tail; a brand with an Oshakati/Ondangwa presence may be worth paying more for.

### Attachments

Budget for the attachment properly: **hydraulic breakers** (matched to carrier weight and auxiliary flow), **compaction plates**, **augers**, **grapples**, **tilt-rotators** (transformative on excavator versatility but expensive and maintenance-heavy), **quick-hitches**. Every attachment adds mass ahead of the pivot and reduces every number in the lift chart.
