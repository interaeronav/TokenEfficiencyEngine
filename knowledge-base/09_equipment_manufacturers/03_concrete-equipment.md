# Concrete equipment — batching, placing, finishing and formwork

**Summary.** Concrete equipment divides into four chains: producing the mix (batch plants and mixers), moving it (truck mixers, pumps, placing booms, skips), consolidating and finishing it (vibrators, screeds, power floats), and containing it (formwork, falsework, shoring). This file maps the manufacturers in each, gives the sizing numbers that determine what a pour actually needs, and covers the southern African formwork suppliers who dominate the local hire market.

## Batching plants

**Manufacturers.** **Liebherr** (Mixing Technology division — mobile, containerised and stationary plants, twin-shaft and ring-pan mixers), **Schwing Stetter** (the largest global batch plant installed base), **Ammann** (concrete and asphalt plants), **Simem** (Italian), **Elkon** (Turkish; strong in mobile and compact plants and very well represented in Africa), **Constmach** (Turkish), **Sicoma** and **BHS-Sonthofen** (mixer specialists supplying other plant builders), **Fibo Intercon** (small mobile), **SANY** and **Zoomlion** (Chinese full plants).

**Plant types.**
- **Mobile/trailer-mounted** — folds onto a low-bed, sets up in 1–3 days. 20–60 m³/h. Ideal for remote sites and short projects. **[NA]** This is usually the right answer for a large project in northern Namibia, where the nearest ready-mix plant may be 200 km away.
- **Compact / containerised** — 30–90 m³/h, modular, quicker foundations than stationary.
- **Stationary** — 60–240 m³/h, permanent foundations, silo farms, aggregate bins with reclaim.

**Mixer types.** *Twin-shaft* (fastest homogenisation, best for high-strength and RCC, 30–60 s cycle), *planetary/ring-pan* (best for precast and coloured concrete), *drum/tilting* (cheap, gentle on aggregate, slower), *continuous pug-mill* (stabilised layerworks rather than structural concrete).

**Sizing.** Plant output must exceed the peak placing rate, not the average. A 400 m³ raft placed in 8 hours needs 50 m³/h *placed*; allow ~30% for stoppages and batch-to-batch losses → a 60–90 m³/h plant. Cement silo capacity should hold at least 1.5 days of peak use; **[NA]** on a long supply line, 3–5 days.

## Truck mixers and delivery

**Manufacturers of drums/superstructures.** **Schwing Stetter**, **Liebherr**, **CIFA**, **Putzmeister** (Intermix), **Karrena**, **SANY**, **Zoomlion**, plus local bodybuilders on **UD/Volvo/MAN/Mercedes/Isuzu** chassis in South Africa.

**Practical numbers.** Rated 6 m³ and 8 m³ are the SADC standard. A fully loaded 8 m³ mixer runs 30–32 t GVM and needs 3.5 m headroom, ~4.5 m turning clearance and a firm 250 kPa-plus haul route. **Discharge time from arrival to empty is 8–15 minutes** at a good chute position; plan the pour around that, not around drum size.

**Concrete life.** **[NA] [ZA]** In hot semi-arid conditions, the 90-minute or 300-revolution limit in SANS 878/EN 206 practice is the binding constraint: at 35 °C ambient, workable life may be under an hour. Retarders, chilled water or ice, and night pouring are the standard mitigations. Beyond ~45 minutes' haul, site batching usually wins.

## Concrete pumps

**Manufacturers.** **Putzmeister** (German; BSF truck-mounted booms, BSA stationary/trailer pumps, Thom-Katt, MX placing booms), **Schwing** (German; S-series booms, SP stationary), **CIFA** (Italian, part of Zoomlion; Carbotech carbon-fibre booms), **SANY** (the world's largest pump manufacturer by volume; acquired Putzmeister in 2012 but runs it as a separate brand), **Zoomlion**, **Everdigm**, **KCP**, **Junjin**, **Concord** and **Alliance** (smaller line pumps).

**Types.**
- **Trailer / line (stationary) pumps** — 20–60 m³/h, pushed through 100–125 mm steel pipeline and rubber hose. Cheapest way to get concrete into a basement or across a site. Pipeline friction is the constraint: allow roughly 1 bar per 10 m of horizontal pipe for a normal mix, and treat each 90° bend as ~5–10 m of pipe; vertical rise costs ~0.24 bar/m from the concrete column alone.
- **Truck-mounted boom pumps** — the boom length class (e.g. "42 m") is *vertical reach*; usable horizontal reach is typically 8–10 m less. Boom folds: R (roll/Z), RZ, Z, multi-Z — a Z-fold opens in tighter headroom.
- **Truck mixer pumps** — mixer and short boom on one chassis; good for small remote pours.
- **Separate placing booms** — fed by a stationary pump; mast-, frame-, slab- or crane-mounted, self-climbing on high-rise. Frees the crane for other work, which is the real economic argument.

**Pumpability.** A pumpable mix needs continuous grading, at least ~350 kg/m³ of fines passing 0.125 mm (cement + extender + fine sand), slump ≥100 mm or a proper SCC/plasticised design, and a maximum aggregate ≤ 1/3 of the smallest pipe diameter. Harsh, gap-graded or low-fines mixes will block, and a blocked line under 80 bar is a serious injury hazard.

> ⚠️ Pipeline blowouts and whipping delivery hoses cause fatal injuries. Never stand over a pipeline coupling under pressure, always fit the hose safety chain/whip-check, and depressurise before opening any clamp.

## Vibration, screeding and finishing

**Internal (poker) vibrators.** **Wacker Neuson**, **Enar**, **Dynapac**, **Weber MT**, **Husqvarna**, **Bosch**, **Multiquip**. Drives: high-frequency electric (with converter), petrol flexible-shaft, hydraulic, and increasingly battery. Head diameters 25–75 mm; frequency 9 000–12 000 vpm.

Technique that matters more than brand: insert **vertically**, at spacings of about **1.5× the radius of action**, penetrate **150 mm into the previous lift**, hold **5–15 seconds** until the surface glosses and large bubbles stop, and withdraw **slowly (about 80 mm/s)**. Never use a poker to move concrete laterally — that segregates it.

**External and form vibrators** for precast and thin sections; **vibrating screeds** (single or double beam, 2–6 m) for slabs on grade; **truss screeds** for wide pours; **laser screeds** (**Somero** S-series, plus Ligchine and Allen) for large industrial floors, achieving FF/FL flatness numbers that hand screeding cannot.

**Power floats and trowels.** **Wacker Neuson**, **Multiquip (Whiteman)**, **Allen Engineering**, **Husqvarna**, **Bartell**, **Dynapac**. Classes: **walk-behind (edger, 600–1 200 mm)**, **ride-on twin-rotor (1 800–2 400 mm)**. Sequence: float with pans as soon as the slab bears a footprint of ~3 mm; then progressively steepen blade pitch through successive trowelling passes. Ride-on machines are roughly 3–5× the productivity of walk-behinds above about 500 m² and are the practical minimum for a warehouse floor.

**Curing and joints.** Concrete saws (**Husqvarna**, **Norton Clipper**, **Stihl**) for early-entry (soft-cut, 1–4 h) and conventional wet cutting; curing compounds and blankets. **[NA]** In hot dry conditions with high evaporation, plastic shrinkage cracking is the dominant early failure — plan for wind breaks, evaporation retarders and immediate curing, not just for the saw.

## Formwork systems

**Global manufacturers.** **PERI** (German; MAXIMO/TRIO wall, SKYDECK/MULTIFLEX slab, ACS climbing, PERI UP scaffold), **Doka** (Austrian, Umdasch Group; Framax/Frami wall, Dokadek/Dokaflex slab, SKE climbing, Ringlock scaffold), **ULMA Construction** (Spanish; ORMA/NEVI wall, CC-4 climbing, RAPID/ALUPROP slab), **MEVA**, **NOE**, **Hünnebeck/BrandSafway**, **RMD Kwikform**, **Efco**, **Symons**.

**Southern African suppliers.** **[ZA]** **Form-Scaff** (part of **Waco Africa**) is the largest local formwork and scaffolding hire company — steel and aluminium panel wall forms, slab systems, props, H-frame and Kwikstage scaffold, with branches nationally. **PERI South Africa** and **ULMA Construction South Africa** both operate local subsidiaries with engineering and hire. **Uco/UCS**, **SGB-Cape** (BrandSafway) and regional hire yards fill out the market. **[NA]** Namibian projects typically hire through the SA subsidiaries or through Windhoek-based scaffold hire, with cross-border movement under SACU (no duty; import VAT applies).

**System selection.**
- **Framed panel wall formwork** — steel or aluminium frame, plywood face. Fast, crane-handled or hand-set, pressure ratings typically 60–80 kN/m². The default for walls, columns and lift shafts.
- **Girder/beam formwork (H20 timber beam + steel waler)** — bespoke geometry, curved walls, deep beams, very high pressures. Slower but fully engineerable.
- **Slab formwork** — *panel-and-prop* (Dokadek, SKYDECK) for speed and few loose parts; *beam-and-prop (Multiflex/Dokaflex)* for irregular slabs; *tables* for repetitive large floors, moved by crane; *drop-head systems* to strike the panels early and leave props in place.
- **Climbing formwork** — crane-lifted or self-climbing (hydraulic) for cores, piers and shafts.
- **Column forms** — steel/aluminium adjustable, or single-use cardboard tube for circular columns.

**Formwork pressure.** Governed by DIN 18218 / CIRIA 108 as a function of **rate of rise**, **concrete temperature**, **consistency class**, **admixture retardation** and **pour height**. Rules of thumb: a wall poured at 2 m/h at 20 °C with S3 concrete generates far less pressure than the same wall at 5 m/h at 10 °C. SCC must generally be designed for **full hydrostatic pressure** (ρgh ≈ 24 kPa per metre of pour height) unless the supplier's data proves otherwise.

> ⚠️ Formwork and falsework collapses are among the most lethal construction failures. Falsework must be designed, checked and inspected by a competent person before the pour, with a signed permit to pour. Do not vary the pour rate above the design assumption on the day.

## Shoring, props and falsework

- **Adjustable steel props** — Classes to EN 1065 (A–E); working loads vary with extension. A prop rated 36 kN closed may only carry 15–20 kN at full extension. Always check the extension-versus-load chart, and never exceed 1.5° out of plumb.
- **Aluminium props** — lighter, common in slab systems.
- **Shoring towers / frames** — Ringlock/Cuplock/Kwikstage systems assembled as load-bearing towers; capacity per standard is set by lift height and bracing, typically 20–60 kN per leg.
- **Heavy-duty shore legs** — PERI HD/ULMA T-60/Doka Staxo for deep bridge soffits.
- **Trench shoring** — hydraulic trench boxes, drag boxes, sheet piles, and slide-rail systems. **[ZA]** Construction Regulations require an excavation deeper than 1.5 m to be shored or benched under a competent person's design.
- **Back-propping** — after striking, upper slabs shed load to slabs below; the back-prop layout is a structural calculation, not a site decision.

## Precast plant equipment

For a precast yard: **battery moulds** and **tilting tables** (Elematic, Weckenmann, Vollert, Progress Group, Avermann), **hollowcore extruders and slipformers** (Elematic, Echo, Weiler, Nordimpianti), **carousel/circulation plants** with pallet circulation and shuttering robots, **long-line prestressing beds** with stressing jacks and de-tensioning gear, **steam or dry-heat curing chambers**, **overhead gantries** for de-moulding, **tilters and stacking clamps**, and an on-site batch plant with a planetary mixer. Mesh and cage welding lines come from **Progress/Schnell/EVG**. **[ZA]** Local precast producers such as Cape Concrete, Echo Prestress, Corestruc and Infraset operate this equipment class; a small yard can start with tilting tables, a planetary mixer and a 10 t gantry.
