# Power tools, cordless platforms and fixings

**Summary.** Two decisions dominate this category: which **battery platform** the business commits to (because the batteries, not the tools, are the lock-in), and how **anchors** are selected and designed (because a fixing failure is a structural failure). This file compares the professional cordless ecosystems, covers drilling and demolition, diamond tooling, anchors and their load data, direct fastening, and measuring/laser tools, with the manufacturer documentation routes for each.

**Choosing.** For a **general building contractor** the decision is usually Bosch Professional or Makita on cost-per-tool breadth, with Hilti reserved for anchoring, breaking and diamond work where the Fleet Management model removes downtime risk. For a **specialist mechanical/electrical trade**, Milwaukee's trade tools are hard to beat. For a **joinery shop**, Festool plus Mafell/CAS. **[NA] [ZA]** Also weigh service network: Hilti and Bosch have direct service; Makita and DeWALT run through appointed agents; getting a warranty repair from a remote site can take weeks, which is exactly the risk Hilti Fleet Management prices out.

> ⚠️ Do not mix chargers and third-party battery clones. Counterfeit 18 V packs are widespread in southern Africa and are the leading cause of tool fires. Buy batteries through the manufacturer's channel and check the authentication feature where one exists.

## Drilling and demolition

| Tool | Typical range | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Combi drill / driver | 13 mm chuck, 60–120 N·m | General fixing, wood, steel |
| Impact driver | 1/4" hex, 150–250 N·m | Screws and coach screws; never for torque-critical bolts |
| **SDS-plus rotary hammer** | 2–3 J, holes to ~30 mm | The general anchor-drilling tool |
| **SDS-max rotary hammer** | 6–12 J, holes 12–55 mm, core to 150 mm | Structural anchoring, larger holes |
| **Demolition/breaker hammer** | 25–70 J (hand); 1 000–2 500 J (excavator-mounted) | Chasing, breaking slabs, wall removal |
| Diamond core drill | rig- or hand-held, wet or dry | Services penetrations, 20–500 mm |
| Wall saw / wire saw | electric or hydraulic | Structural openings in thick concrete |

**Hollow drill bits with integrated dust removal** (Hilti TE-CD/TE-YD, Bosch SpeedClean) are worth specifying wherever chemical anchors are used: they clean the hole *while* drilling, which removes the largest single source of chemical anchor failure. Many approvals now grant higher loads for a hollow-bit installation than for a blow-brush-blow cleaned hole.

**Dust and health.** Concrete drilling generates **respirable crystalline silica (RCS)**, a confirmed carcinogen. On-tool extraction with an **M-class or H-class** dust extractor is the required control in the EU and increasingly in SADC client specifications. Wet cutting is the alternative but creates slurry management problems. **HAV** (hand-arm vibration) from breakers and hammers must be assessed to ISO 5349 — the manufacturer states the m/s² value for each tool, and it drives exposure-time limits.

## Diamond tooling

- **Segmented blades** for hard aggregate, **turbo** for speed and finish, **continuous rim (wet)** for tile and stone.
- **Bond hardness runs opposite to material hardness**: a *soft* bond for *hard* material (so segments erode and expose new diamond) and a *hard* bond for *abrasive/soft* material (so segments last). Getting this backwards is the commonest reason a "good" blade won't cut.
- **Laser-welded** segments for dry cutting; **sintered/brazed** for wet only.
- **Core bits**: wet drilling clears slurry and cools; dry coring needs vacuum extraction and periodic withdrawal.
- Suppliers: **Hilti**, **Husqvarna Construction** (with a South African operation), **Norton Clipper (Saint-Gobain)**, **Tyrolit**, **Bosch**, **Diamond Products**, **Disco/Diaquip** and local re-tippers.

## Anchors and fixings

### Types

| Anchor type | Principle | Best use | Cracked concrete? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Torque-controlled expansion (wedge/through-bolt)** | Friction from expansion clip | Fast structural fixing in sound concrete | Only if approved for cracked (check ETA option 1/7 or ACI seismic category) |
| **Displacement-controlled (drop-in)** | Internal cone set by a setting tool | Flush fixing, threaded rod, overhead services | Generally lower capacity; needs full setting |
| **Undercut anchor** | Mechanical keying into an undercut | Highest-security structural, nuclear/tunnel | Yes — designed for it |
| **Screw anchor (concrete screw)** | Thread cuts into concrete | Fast, removable, small edge distances, temporary works | Many approved for cracked concrete |
| **Chemical / bonded (capsule or injection)** | Adhesive bond between rod and concrete | Rebar post-installation, close edge distances, high loads, hollow substrates | Depends on the specific mortar's approval |
| **Cavity/hollow-wall** (toggle, sleeve, nylon) | Spreads behind the board | Plasterboard, hollow block | Non-structural |
| **Direct fastening (powder/gas nail)** | High-velocity driven pin | Steel-to-steel, light fixing to concrete, decking | Not for tensile-critical structural |

**Hilti HIT chemical range** is the reference family: **HIT-HY 200** (fast-cure hybrid, general structural), **HIT-RE 500** (pure epoxy, highest loads, long working time, rebar and deep embedments; slow cure), **HIT-HY 170** (hybrid mortar for masonry and hollow substrates, used with mesh sleeves), and dedicated masonry/anchoring capsules. Competing systems: **fischer FIS**, **Würth**, **Simpson Strong-Tie SET-3G/AT-XP**, **Powers/DeWalt Pure110+**, **Rawl/Apex**, **Ramset**.

### The four numbers that govern a chemical anchor

1. **Embedment depth h_ef** — bond capacity rises roughly linearly with embedment until concrete cone failure governs. Deeper is stronger, up to a point.
2. **Edge distance c** — near an edge, concrete edge breakout governs and capacity collapses. Each approval states a **c_min** (installation minimum) and **c_cr** (the distance beyond which no reduction applies, often ~1.5–2.0 × h_ef).
3. **Spacing s** — overlapping cones between adjacent anchors reduce group capacity; **s_cr** is typically ~2–3 × h_ef.
4. **Member thickness h_min** — a thin slab cannot develop a full cone and may split.

Plus modifiers: **concrete strength class**, **cracked vs uncracked** concrete, **long-term/seismic/fire** load cases, **substrate temperature** at installation and in service, **hole drilling method** (hammer, hollow-bit, diamond-cored — diamond-cored holes are smooth and generally reduce bond capacity unless the approval specifically covers them), and **hole cleaning**.

> ⚠️ Never take an anchor load value from a catalogue table without checking which of these it assumes. Catalogue "recommended loads" usually assume uncracked C20/25 concrete, full edge distance and spacing, and perfect installation. Real conditions frequently halve them.

### Design route

The correct route is not a catalogue lookup:
1. Establish the **design action** (N_Ed, V_Ed, and any moment) at the fixing, with the correct load combination.
2. Choose an anchor with a valid **approval** (ETA to EAD 330232/330499 etc., or ICC-ES ESR) covering the substrate and the cracked/uncracked condition.
3. Run the check to **EN 1992-4** or **ACI 318 Ch. 17** for **all** failure modes: steel failure, pull-out, concrete cone, splitting, blow-out, pry-out, and concrete edge failure in shear.
4. Use the manufacturer's software (**Hilti PROFIS Engineering**, fischer FIXPERIENCE) — it applies the approval's own parameters and produces a signable calculation report.
5. Specify the **installation**: bit diameter, embedment, cleaning method, torque, cure time, and whether special inspection is required.
6. **Prove it on site** where the substrate is uncertain (old concrete, unknown mix, masonry) with a **pull-out test** to a stated proof load.

**[ZA]** SANS 10160 loading with EN 1992-4 anchor design is common practice; check what the project's structural engineer has specified. **[NA]** Namibian practice follows SANS.

### Direct fastening
Powder-actuated (Hilti DX) and gas-actuated (Hilti GX, Spit Pulsa, Ramset TrakFast) tools drive hardened pins into steel or concrete. Uses: steel deck to beam, track/channel to slab, insulation and membrane fixing, cable and pipe support. Constraints: **base steel thickness** minimums, **edge distance** on steel and concrete, **operator licensing** in most jurisdictions, and a strict cartridge control regime. Gas tools are lower-noise, lower-power and better for interior fit-out; powder tools carry the heavy-duty capacity.

## Measuring and laser tools

| Tool | Typical accuracy | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Laser distance meter** (Leica DISTO, Bosch GLM) | **±1.0–1.5 mm** typical; ±1.0 mm on premium models | Room and site measurement, area/volume, indirect height by Pythagoras |
| **Cross-line laser** | ±0.2–0.3 mm/m | Interior setting out, tiling, partitions, cabinetry |
| **Multi-line / 360° laser** | ±0.2 mm/m | Full-room layout, ceiling grids |
| **Rotating laser** (see file 07) | ±1–1.5 mm at 30 m | Levels, formwork, drainage falls |
| **Detector/scanner** (Hilti PS, Bosch D-tect) | detects rebar, live cable, pipe | Mandatory before any core drill or chase |
| **Thermal imager** | ±2 °C typical | Insulation defects, damp, electrical faults |
| **Moisture meter** | pin or capacitance | Screed readiness before floor covering |

Leica's DISTO range and Bosch's GLM range dominate professional laser measuring; Leica Geosystems also supplies the survey-grade equipment in file 07, and the two product lines share technology.

> ⚠️ Never core drill or chase without a detection scan and a services drawing. Cutting a live conductor or a post-tensioning tendon is a life-safety event.
