# Compaction, roads and paving equipment

**Summary.** Roadworks equipment is a coupled system: the compaction fleet, not the excavator, sets the rate at which layerworks can be built, and the paver's speed is bounded by the asphalt plant's output and the truck cycle. This file maps the manufacturers, classifies rollers, light compaction, pavers, milling machines and crushing/screening plant, and then sets out the compaction specification concepts — Proctor density, layer thickness, pass counts and the modern intelligent-compaction measures — that determine whether the equipment achieves anything.

## Key facts

| Item | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Standard Proctor compactive effort | **~600 kJ/m³** | ASTM D698 / AASHTO T99 |
| Modified Proctor compactive effort | **~2 700 kJ/m³** | ASTM D1557 / AASHTO T180; ~4.5× standard |
| **[ZA]** SA reference | **TMH1 method A7** (mod AASHTO) for density; **COLTO/SANS 3001** for testing | layerworks specified as % of mod AASHTO |
| Typical layerworks compaction targets **[ZA]** | subgrade 90–93%, selected 93%, subbase 95%, base **98% of mod AASHTO** | check the project specification |
| Compacted layer thickness, vibratory roller | **150–300 mm** (typical 150 mm for base, up to 300 mm for fill with heavy rollers) | thicker layers need heavier machines and more passes |
| Compacted layer thickness, plate compactor | 100–200 mm cohesionless; less in clay | manufacturer states max effective depth |
| Typical pass count | **4–8 passes** to reach target | established by a **trial section**, not by rule |
| Optimum moisture window | usually **OMC −2% to +1%** | on the dry side in hot climates due to evaporation loss |
| Asphalt paver widths | 1.8 m (compact) to 16 m (highway, extended) | screed extension governs |
| Asphalt lay temperature | typically **140–160 °C** for hot-mix; compaction must finish above ~85–90 °C | cessation temperature governs roller sequencing |
| Cold milling depths | 0–35 cm depending on class | drum widths 0.3–4.4 m |

## Rollers by type

### Single-drum vibratory soil compactors
The main earthworks compactor. Classes by operating weight: **3–7 t** (compact, for trenches and small platforms), **10–14 t** (municipal roads, layerworks), **15–20 t** (the highway/civils standard), **20–26 t** (heavy fill, rockfill). Configurations:
- **Smooth drum** — granular soils, base course, all bound layers.
- **Padfoot / sheepsfoot** — cohesive soils. The feet knead the clay and compact from the bottom of the lift up; a padfoot roller cannot finish a surface, so a smooth pass follows.
- **Padfoot shell kits** convert a smooth drum, which is worth doing on a mixed-material job.

Key numbers: **static linear load (kg/cm of drum width)**, **centrifugal force (kN)**, **amplitude (mm)** and **frequency (Hz)**. High amplitude/low frequency drives energy deep for thick lifts; low amplitude/high frequency finishes near the surface without over-stressing it. Most machines offer two or more amplitude settings and this choice matters more than machine weight.

### Tandem (double drum) rollers
For asphalt and finishing. **1–4 t** (walk-behind and compact ride-on for footways and patching), **4–9 t** (municipal), **9–14 t** (highway breakdown and intermediate rolling). Water spray systems and mat-friendly features (edge cutters, chip spreaders, oscillation instead of vibration for bridge decks and thin overlays) distinguish models.

### Pneumatic tyred rollers (PTR)
Multi-wheel, ballastable, **8–28 t**. The kneading action seals and densifies asphalt surfaces and works well on marginal granular material. Tyre pressure is an adjustable specification variable. Essential on chip-seal and on many asphalt intermediate-rolling specifications.

### Trench and remote rollers
**0.6–1.5 t** articulated padfoot trench rollers (Wacker Neuson RTx, Bomag BMP, Ammann) — remote-controlled, walk the trench, and the only correct machine for compacting pipe bedding and backfill in a narrow trench.

### Combination and static rollers
Combi rollers (vibrating steel drum front, pneumatic rear) for small asphalt jobs; three-point static rollers survive in older SADC fleets for chip seal.

## Plate compactors and rammers

| Type | Mass | Centrifugal force | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forward plate** | 50–100 kg | 10–20 kN | Paving bedding sand, small backfill, block paving |
| **Reversible plate** | 150–800 kg | 25–100 kN | Granular backfill, trench, subbase; the workhorse |
| **Heavy reversible / hydraulic** | 400–800 kg | 60–100 kN | Deep granular lifts, up to ~600 mm in ideal material |
| **Rammer ("jumping jack")** | 55–85 kg | ~10–20 kN impact | **Cohesive soils** and confined trenches — the impact action suits clay where a plate does not |
| **Excavator-mounted plate** | — | 20–120 kN | Trench work without entering the trench, slope facing |

**Wacker Neuson** and **Weber MT** dominate quality light compaction; **Bomag**, **Ammann**, **Dynapac**, **Husqvarna**, **Multiquip (Mikasa)** and **Belle** are all present in SADC. A paving contractor needs at minimum a rammer (clay/trench), a reversible plate (subbase), and a light forward plate with a rubber mat (finished block paving).

## Asphalt pavers

**Manufacturers.** VÖGELE (SUPER 700/1300/1800/2100 families), Caterpillar (AP series), Bomag (CR/BF), Dynapac (F series), Ammann (AFT/AFW), Volvo (P series), plus Sumitomo, Sany and XCMG.

**Classes.**
- **Mini/compact pavers** (0.5–3 m) for footways, cycle tracks, patching.
- **Highway class** (2.5–6 m basic width, extendable to 9–13 m) — tracked or wheeled. Wheeled pavers move between jobs faster; tracked pavers give better traction and mat quality on soft bases.
- **Large/extending screeds** to 16 m for airfields and multi-lane paving.

**The screed is the machine.** Mat thickness, texture and pre-compaction (typically 85–92% of target density before the roller touches it) come from the screed's tamper bar and vibrators, its heating, its angle of attack and its floating equilibrium. A paver running at inconsistent speed produces a wavy mat that no roller can fix. **Material feed continuity** — from a material transfer vehicle (MTV) or a well-scheduled truck fleet — is the single biggest quality driver.

## Milling machines, recyclers and stabilisers

- **Cold planers / milling machines** (WIRTGEN W-series, Cat PM, Bomag BM). Classes: **small (0.35–1.3 m drum)** for utility cuts and patching; **large (1.5–4.4 m)** for full-lane rehabilitation. Cutting depth to ~35 cm on large machines. Drum tool spacing sets texture: standard (15 mm), fine milling (6–8 mm), micro-fine.
- **Cold recyclers / soil stabilisers** (WIRTGEN WR series, Bomag MPH, Cat RM). Mill the existing pavement and mix in cement, lime, bitumen emulsion or foamed bitumen in situ. **[ZA] [NA]** In-situ recycling with foamed bitumen or cement is heavily used on SADC rural roads because it avoids hauling material over long distances — an important cost driver where the nearest quarry is 100 km away.
- **Slipform pavers** (WIRTGEN SP series, GOMACO, Power Curbers) for concrete pavements, kerbs and channels, barriers.

## Crushing and screening

**Kleemann** (MOBICAT jaw, MOBIREX impact, MOBICONE cone, MOBISCREEN screens), **Metso** (Lokotrack, Nordberg), **Sandvik** (QJ/QI/QH/QE ranges), **Terex Finlay**, **Powerscreen**, **McCloskey**, **Astec**, **Rubble Master** (compact impactors) and **Keestrack**.

Selection logic: **jaw** for primary reduction of hard rock (feed to ~1 000 mm, reduction 4–6:1); **impact** for softer rock, concrete recycling and shaping (better cubicity, higher wear cost in abrasive rock); **cone** for secondary/tertiary in hard abrasive rock; **VSI** for sand manufacture and product shaping. Screens size the product — 2- or 3-deck inclined for high volume, horizontal for fine separation, scalper ahead of the crusher to bypass fines and protect the chamber. **[NA]** A mobile jaw + screen combination is often the right answer for a remote project making its own G5/G7 from local rock rather than importing crusher run.

## Compaction specification concepts

### Proctor density
A laboratory compaction test establishes the relationship between **moisture content** and **dry density** for a specific material. The peak of that curve gives **Maximum Dry Density (MDD)** and **Optimum Moisture Content (OMC)**.

- **Standard Proctor** applies about **600 kJ/m³**; **Modified Proctor** about **2 700 kJ/m³**. Modified is the reference for road layerworks because modern rollers deliver far more energy than 1930s equipment.
- Field density is expressed as **% of MDD**: "98% mod AASHTO" means the field dry density is 98% of the modified Proctor maximum for that material.
- **[ZA]** Densities are determined in the field by sand-replacement (SANS 3001-GR30 lineage), nuclear density gauge, or increasingly by non-nuclear devices; the specification names the method.

### Moisture control
Compaction only works near OMC. Too dry and the particles will not rearrange; too wet and pore water carries the load ("pumping"), producing a spongy layer that will never pass. **[NA] [ZA]** In hot semi-arid conditions the practical problem is loss of moisture: a layer at OMC when mixed can be 3% dry two hours later. Mitigations — water tanker following the grader, working shorter sections, compacting immediately behind spreading, and early-morning work in summer.

### Layer thickness and pass count
Compaction energy dissipates with depth. A 15 t single-drum roller effectively compacts about **200–300 mm loose (≈150–250 mm compacted)** in granular material; a 700 kg reversible plate about **300–400 mm loose** in clean stone but far less in silt.

The correct approach is a **trial section (test strip)**:
1. Build 30–50 m of the actual material at the intended loose thickness.
2. Roll in a fixed pattern and test density after each pass (2, 4, 6, 8).
3. Plot density versus passes — the curve flattens at the point of diminishing returns.
4. Fix the layer thickness, roller type, amplitude, speed and pass count from the trial, and record it as the approved method.

Over-rolling is a real failure mode: past the optimum, a vibratory roller can shear a granular base, break down soft aggregate, and *reduce* density.

### Roller operating variables
- **Speed** — 3–6 km/h for soil, 3–5 km/h for asphalt. Faster means fewer impacts per metre; impact spacing should stay under ~30–35 mm.
- **Amplitude/frequency** — high amplitude for thick lifts, low for thin lifts and asphalt; never use vibration on a thin lift over a rigid base (it will crush the aggregate and can damage structures).
- **Overlap** — 100–150 mm between passes.
- **Asphalt rolling zones** — breakdown (immediately behind the paver, at the highest safe temperature), intermediate (pneumatic, sealing), finish (static tandem, removing marks). All must finish before the mat cools below the cessation temperature.

### Intelligent compaction (IC / CCC)
Modern rollers (Bomag **Terrameter/Evib**, Hamm **HCQ/Compaction Meter**, Ammann **ACE**, Dynapac **DCA**, Cat **CCV**, Trimble/Topcon retrofit) measure drum response and report a stiffness proxy (E_vib in MN/m², CMV, ACE Force) with GPS mapping of coverage and pass count. Value: **100% coverage mapping** instead of spot tests, immediate identification of soft spots, and automatic amplitude adjustment. It supplements, not replaces, density testing — the specification still calls for % MDD.
