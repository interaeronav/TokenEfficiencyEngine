# Specifying glass and glazing

**Summary.** A glass specification that says "6.38 mm laminated safety glass" has specified almost nothing. This file sets out what a glass and glazing specification must actually state, gives a glazing schedule format that makes quotations comparable, lists the submittals and certificates to require, covers tolerances, warranties and lead times, and ends with a reusable specification template.

## Key facts — the eleven things that must be stated

Every glazing type in a schedule must state all of these. Omit one and the quotation is not comparable and the delivered product may be legal, cheap and wrong.

| # | Item | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Make-up, ply by ply** | 6 mm HS / 1.52 mm ionoplast / 6 mm HS |
| 2 | **Heat state of each ply** | annealed (ANN), heat-strengthened (HS), fully toughened (FT) |
| 3 | **Interlayer type and thickness** | standard PVB / acoustic PVB / ionoplast, 0.38 / 0.76 / 1.52 mm |
| 4 | **Coating, product name and surface number** | SolarVue Low E XHL Serene Green, coating on surface #2 |
| 5 | **Performance targets** | VLT ≥ 44%, SHGC ≤ 0.42, U ≤ 3.8 W/m²·K, exterior VLR ≤ 20% |
| 6 | **Edge treatment** | arrised / ground / polished / mitred; hole and notch schedule |
| 7 | **Safety compliance and marking** | safety glazing to SANS 1263-1, permanently marked **[ZA]** |
| 8 | **Heat soak testing** | all FT panes heat soaked to EN 14179-1, certificates required |
| 9 | **Frame / system** | named system and range, finish, thermal break, glazing method |
| 10 | **Tolerances** | to EN 572-8 / EN 12150-1 / EN ISO 12543-6 / EN 1279-1 as applicable |
| 11 | **Warranty** | period and scope, separately for glass, coating, IGU seal, sealant, finish |

## 1. Why the eleven items matter

**Make-up ply by ply.** "6.38 laminated" could be 3/0.38/3 in annealed glass — a domestic safety glass — or it could need to be 3 mm HS / 0.38 / 3 mm HS for thermal stress reasons. The nominal thickness alone tells the processor nothing about heat state.

**Heat state.** This is the single most consequential omission. Annealed, heat-strengthened and fully toughened glass have different strengths, different thermal capabilities, different break patterns, different post-breakage behaviour and different spontaneous-fracture risk. Specify each ply.

**Interlayer.** PVB and ionoplast are not interchangeable. A balustrade designed on ionoplast stiffness and delivered with standard PVB will fail its post-breakage check. State the type, the thickness, and, for structural laminates, require the interlayer's shear modulus data at the design temperature.

**Coating and surface number.** The same coating on surface #2 and on surface #3 gives different SHGC. Name the product and the surface.

**Performance targets as well as a named product.** Name the product *and* state the performance envelope it must meet. That way a proposed alternative can be judged. Without targets, "or equal approved" is meaningless.

**Edge treatment.** Structural, not cosmetic (see `01_glass-types-and-manufacture.md`). Also: all holes, notches and cut-outs must be scheduled, because they must be made before toughening.

**Safety compliance and marking.** **[ZA]** State the standard (SANS 1263-1), require the SABS mark, and require the installer's permanent marking. State the manifestation requirement separately.

**Heat soak testing.** HST is never included unless asked for. Specify EN 14179-1 and require certificates as a deliverable.

**Frame and system.** The glass and the frame are one product. Name the system, the range, the finish, the thermal break and whether glazing is dry, wet or structural.

**Tolerances.** Reference the product standard rather than inventing a number.

**Warranty.** Warranties differ by component and are often much shorter than clients assume.

## 2. Glazing schedule format

One row per glazing type; every pane on the drawings carries a type mark. This is the artefact that makes quotations comparable.

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| **Type mark** | G1, G2, G3 … |
| **Location** | "Office facade, north elevation, levels 1–5" |
| **Make-up** | full ply-by-ply description |
| **Nominal thickness** | mm, overall |
| **Heat state** | per ply |
| **Coating / product** | manufacturer, product name, surface number |
| **VLT %** | target and/or scheduled value |
| **SHGC** | target and/or scheduled value |
| **U-value W/m²·K** | target and/or scheduled value, stated as centre-of-glass or whole-window |
| **Rw dB** | where acoustic performance is required |
| **Edgework** | arrised / ground / polished / mitred |
| **Holes & cut-outs** | reference to a setting-out drawing |
| **Safety standard** | SANS 1263-1 / EN 12600 class |
| **Heat soak** | yes / no, to EN 14179-1 |
| **Max pane size** | mm × mm |
| **Frame system** | name and range |
| **Glazing method** | dry (gasket) / wet (sealant) / structural silicone |
| **Manifestation** | yes / no, and reference to the detail |
| **Quantity** | m² and pane count |
| **Notes** | e.g. "spare panes required — see clause" |

## 3. Submittals and certificates to require

Make these contractual deliverables, not requests:

1. **Shop drawings** — every pane dimensioned, every hole and notch set out, every junction detailed.
2. **Glass product data sheets** for the exact make-up, showing VLT, VLR internal and external, SHGC, SC, U-value and, where relevant, Rw and Tdw-ISO, with the calculation methodology stated (NFRC or EN).
3. **Safety glazing certificates** — SANS 1263-1 conformity and evidence of the SABS mark **[ZA]**; EN 12600 classification where an EN specification is used.
4. **Heat soak test certificates** to EN 14179-1 for every batch of fully toughened glass so specified.
5. **IGU conformity** to EN 1279-5, with the secondary sealant type stated.
6. **Structural sealant compatibility and adhesion report**, project-specific, from the sealant manufacturer, covering every material the sealant will contact. (See `04_glazing-installation-and-detailing.md` — this is where projects fail.)
7. **Structural calculations** for glass thickness to EN 16612 or the local equivalent, and for balustrades, floors, fins and point-fixed glass including the post-breakage case.
8. **Thermal stress analysis** for any absorbing or coated glass with partial shading.
9. **System test data** for the curtain wall or window system — air, water and structural performance to EN 13830 or the AAMA/ASTM equivalents.
10. **Samples** — a full-size sample of each glass type, viewed on site in daylight, and gasket, setting block and sealant samples.
11. **Mock-up** — for any facade of consequence, a visual and performance mock-up, hose-tested, approved before production release.
12. **Maintenance and cleaning manual**, including the cleaning products that must **not** be used on the coating.

## 4. Tolerances

Reference the standard rather than inventing a dimension:

| Product | Tolerance standard |
|---|---|
| Basic float, cut sizes | **EN 572-8** |
| Thermally toughened | **EN 12150-1** (including bow and roller wave) |
| Heat strengthened | **EN 1863-1** |
| Heat soaked toughened | **EN 14179-1** |
| Laminated glass, dimensions and appearance | **EN ISO 12543-2 / -3 / -6** |
| IGUs, tolerances and visual quality | **EN 1279-1** |
| Curtain walling | **EN 13830** |

For **visual quality**, specify the viewing conditions as well as the standard: distance, illumination and viewing angle. Most disputes about "defects" are actually disputes about how the glass was inspected.

**Set-out tolerances** matter as much as product tolerances. State the permitted deviation of the supporting structure that the facade must accommodate, and require the facade brackets to have three-axis adjustment covering it.

## 5. Warranties

Ask for these separately; they are not one warranty:

| Component | Typical scope | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Glass breakage** | usually excluded after delivery | spontaneous NiS fracture is normally excluded — which is precisely why HST is specified |
| **Coating** | peel, corrosion, visual defects | commonly 5–10 years; longer for pyrolytic than sputtered in exposed use |
| **IGU edge seal** | against seal failure and internal condensation | commonly 5–10 years; often voided by incorrect glazing, blocked drainage or incompatible setting blocks |
| **Laminate** | against delamination | commonly 5–10 years; usually voided by exposed edges in standing water |
| **Structural sealant** | adhesion and cohesion | manufacturer's project-specific warranty, contingent on the compatibility report and on approved application |
| **Frame finish** | powder coat or anodising | period varies sharply with exposure category — coastal Namibia will attract a shorter warranty than inland |
| **Installation workmanship** | leaks, movement, fixings | from the glazing subcontractor |

> ⚠️ **Read the exclusions, not the headline period.** Nearly every glass warranty excludes breakage, excludes glass installed contrary to the manufacturer's instructions, excludes edges exposed to standing water, and excludes damage from incompatible sealants. The warranty is only worth what the installation detail allows it to be worth.

## 6. Lead times

Indicative only; **not verified** — confirm per project with the named processor. Southern African context.

| Item | Indicative lead time |
|---|---|
| Clear float, cut from stock | days |
| Standard laminate from stock | days to 2 weeks |
| Toughened, standard sizes | 1–3 weeks |
| Toughened **+ heat soak tested** | add 1–2 weeks |
| Coated laminate, made to order | 4–8 weeks |
| IGUs | 3–6 weeks |
| Aluminium system, standard profile and finish | 4–8 weeks |
| Aluminium system, special colour or anodising | add 2–4 weeks |
| Springs → Windhoek delivery | add about a week |
| Windhoek → northern Namibia | add several days |
| Imported specialist glass (curved, oversize, electrochromic) | months |

**Programme rule:** the glass order date is driven backwards from the *toughening* date, which is driven backwards from the *shop drawing approval* date, which is driven backwards from the *structural setting-out* date. Glass is nearly always on the critical path and is nearly always ordered too late.

## 7. Reusable specification template

Adapt; do not use unedited. Bracketed items must be completed.

---

### SECTION — GLASS AND GLAZING

**1. General**

1.1 This section covers the supply and installation of glass, glazing and the associated framing systems shown on the drawings and listed in the Glazing Schedule.

1.2 **[ZA]** All work to comply with SANS 10400-N (Glazing) and SANS 10137 (Installation of glazing in buildings). **[NA]** Confirm the applicable local authority requirement before ordering; specify to the SANS suite as the default.

1.3 All safety glazing materials to comply with **SANS 1263-1**, to bear the SABS mark, and to be permanently marked by the installer.

1.4 All glazing to be obvious to anyone who approaches it. Manifestation to the details shown, on all clear glazing in circulation routes.

1.5 Glass thickness to be confirmed by calculation to **BS EN 16612:2019** for the design wind pressure at each facade zone, allowing for corner and parapet pressure increases. Scheduled thicknesses are minima.

**2. Products**

2.1 **Float glass** — soda-lime-silica float to EN 572-2, from [manufacturer], [tint], in the thicknesses scheduled.

2.2 **Heat-strengthened glass** — to **EN 1863-1**.

2.3 **Toughened glass** — to **EN 12150-1**. Where scheduled for heat soaking, additionally to **EN 14179-1**, at (280 ± 10) °C with a 2-hour dwell; certificates to be provided for every batch.

2.4 **Laminated glass** — to **EN ISO 12543-2** and **EN 14449**, with the interlayer type and thickness scheduled. Interlayers to be [PVB / acoustic PVB / ionoplast] from [manufacturer]. For structural laminates, submit interlayer shear modulus data at [design temperature] °C.

2.5 **Insulating glass units** — to **EN 1279-5**, with polyisobutylene primary seal and [silicone / polysulphide] secondary seal, [warm-edge / aluminium] spacer, [argon / air] filled, [16 / 18] mm cavity. Silicone secondary seal is mandatory where the unit edge is exposed to UV or the unit is structurally glazed.

2.6 **Coated glass** — [manufacturer and product], coating on surface [#]. Performance, centre of glass: VLT [≥ x%], SHGC [≤ y], U-value [≤ z W/m²·K], exterior visible reflectance [≤ w%]. Alternatives will be considered only where full comparative data to the same methodology is submitted.

2.7 **Edgework** — as scheduled. All exposed edges polished. All holes, notches and cut-outs to the approved setting-out drawings, executed before heat treatment.

**3. Framing**

3.1 Aluminium system: [manufacturer and range, e.g. Crealco Façade 60 pressure-plate]. Finish: [powder coat to (specification and colour) / anodised to (grade and thickness)].

3.2 Thermally broken profiles where scheduled; substitution of a non-broken profile is not permitted without recalculation of spans and deflections.

3.3 The system to have demonstrated air, water and structural performance to **EN 13830** at not less than [state classes], with test reports submitted.

3.4 Brackets to provide not less than ±[25] mm adjustment in all three axes.

**4. Installation**

4.1 Glazing method: [dry gasket / wet sealant / structural silicone] as scheduled.

4.2 Setting blocks of [material and hardness], at the quarter points of the sill unless the system manual directs otherwise, of a width not less than the full glass thickness including, for IGUs, both lites and the spacer. Location blocks and distance pieces as required.

4.3 The glazing rebate to be drained and ventilated to the exterior. Weep holes to be provided, kept clear, and demonstrated open at handover.

4.4 Structural silicone glazing: bite and glueline sized by calculation and submitted; dead load to be carried on setting blocks, not on the sealant, in accordance with **ASTM C1401**. A project-specific compatibility and adhesion report from the sealant manufacturer, covering every contact material, to be submitted and approved before any sealant is applied.

4.5 Sealants to be neutral-cure. Acetoxy-cure silicone is not permitted on coated glass, mirror, masonry or metal.

4.6 Movement joints: sealant applied over closed-cell backer rod at a width-to-depth ratio of not less than 2:1; three-sided adhesion is not permitted.

**5. Handling, storage and protection**

5.1 Glass to be stored on edge on A-frames, leaning, on resilient bearers, dry, ventilated and out of direct sunlight. Glass stored flat or wet will be rejected.

5.2 Lifting by rated vacuum equipment with a valid test certificate, an operable low-vacuum alarm, and pads suited to the pane. Vacuum to be verified before and monitored throughout each lift.

5.3 Installed glazing to be protected until handover. Protective films to be UV-stable and removable without residue; removal before [state period] after installation.

**6. Spares**

6.1 Supply [2]% (minimum [4] panes) of each coated or tinted glass type as spares from the original production batch, delivered, labelled and stored on site as clause 5.1. Colour matching of a later batch is not warranted by the manufacturer.

**7. Submittals**

Shop drawings; product data sheets for each scheduled type; SANS 1263-1 certificates; EN 14179-1 heat soak certificates; EN 1279-5 IGU conformity; structural sealant compatibility and adhesion report; glass thickness calculations to EN 16612; balustrade, floor and point-fixed glass calculations including the post-breakage case; thermal stress analysis; system test reports to EN 13830; full-size samples of each glass type; [visual and performance mock-up]; maintenance and cleaning manual.

**8. Warranties**

Coating [x] years; IGU edge seal [x] years; laminate against delamination [x] years; structural sealant [x] years; frame finish [x] years; installation workmanship [x] years. Warranties to be issued in the Employer's name and to state their exclusions in full.

## 8. Ten specification mistakes to avoid

1. Writing a nominal thickness without the heat state of each ply.
2. Naming a coating without the surface number.
3. Naming a product without a performance envelope, so no alternative can be judged.
4. Forgetting to specify heat soak testing on toughened glass that is overhead or frameless.
5. Specifying monolithic toughened glass in a free-standing balustrade.
6. Specifying an IGU with polysulphide secondary seal where the edge is exposed to UV or structurally glazed.
7. Assuming a processor's furnace or autoclave can take the pane size drawn.
8. Omitting the compatibility and adhesion report from the deliverables.
9. Omitting spares from a coated or tinted glass order.
10. Ordering glass after the shop drawings rather than driving the shop drawings from the glass lead time.

## Open questions

- **All lead times are indicative trade practice and are not sourced.** Confirm with the named processor for every project.
- **All warranty periods are left as bracketed placeholders** because no manufacturer warranty documents were obtained in this research pass. PFG publishes a warranty for its copper-free mirrors; it was not retrieved.
- **SANS 10137's dimensional requirements** (setting-block positions and sizes, edge clearance, edge cover) are not verified; clause 4.2 of the template reflects trade practice and must be reconciled with the standard.
- **Visual quality acceptance criteria** (permitted defect sizes and densities by viewing distance) are in the EN product standards but were not retrieved; do not write numbers into a specification without reading them.
- **[NA]** The Namibian regulatory reference in clause 1.2 is deliberately conditional because Namibia's adoption of SANS 10400-N is unverified — see `05_standards-and-safety.md`.
