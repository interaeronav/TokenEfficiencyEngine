# Glass suppliers and the southern African supply chain

**Summary.** The southern African architectural glass supply chain has one primary manufacturer of scale — **PFG Building Glass** at Springs, part of the **PG Group** — a distribution and processing layer, an aluminium extrusion and systems layer led by **Wispeco/Crealco**, and a fabricator/glazier layer that thins rapidly outside South Africa's metros. Namibia is served by six PG Glass branches and a modest local aluminium and glazing trade. This file maps that chain and flags clearly what could not be verified — which, for Namibia specifically, is a good deal.

## 1. PG Group — the spine of the chain

PG Group, established in Cape Town in **1897**, is the dominant vertically integrated glass business in southern Africa. Its divisions:

| Division | Role |
|---|---|
| **PFG Building Glass** | Float glass manufacture (approx. 260 000 t/yr), laminated glass, mirrors and sealant products. Springs, Gauteng. |
| **PG Building Glass** | Distribution of float, mirror and **PG SmartGlass®** products — described as southern Africa's leading distributor. |
| **Shatterprufe** | Automotive glass: Shatterprufe® windscreens (laminated) and **Armourplate®** toughened side and rear glass. **Automotive only — not a building glass brand.** Damaged windscreens are recycled by PFG. |
| **PG Aluminium** | Manufacture and installation of customised architectural aluminium windows and doors. |
| **PG Primador** | Aluminium door, window and shower door frames supplied with glass. |
| **PG Glass** | Auto and building glass replacement and installation; approximately **130 fitment centres**. |
| **PG Glass Africa** | Distribution, replacement and repair outside South Africa. |
| **Widney** | Sliding window design and manufacture. |
| **LLumar** | Window film distribution — solar control, safety and decorative. |

### PFG product ranges to know

- **ClearVue** — clear and green-tinted float glass, **1.8–12 mm**, stock sheet **3 660 × 2 440 mm**.
- **Intruderprufe** — clear laminated safety glass, the base laminated product.
- **ColourVue** — tinted laminates (Cool and Deep Cool tints, Serene Green, Arctic Snow, Shadowlite).
- **SolarVue** — embedded metallic-coated laminates, HL and XHL coating densities.
- **Solarshield** — high-performance reflective coated laminates, S10 / S20 / S30 densities.
- **Soundprufe** — acoustic laminates with standard and acoustic PVB.
- **Vanceva** — coloured PVB decorative laminates (14 layer colours, combinable).
- **E Range** — any of the above combined with a pyrolytic low-E coating, taking single-glazed U-value from 5.8 to **3.7 W/m²·K**.
- **Images** — mirror, including a copper-free range with published storage, handling and installation guidance and a warranty.

PFG maintains an NFRC-accredited spectrophotometer covering **300–2 400 nm** and has characterised over **130** of its products; its data appears on the **International Glazing Database (IGDB)** and the **South African Glass Data Base (SAGDB)**. That matters practically: it means you can get modelled, comparable, third-party-referenced performance data for a locally made product, which is unusual in this region.

## 2. Processors and glaziers — South Africa

Between the float plant and the building sits the processing layer: toughening, lamination, IGU assembly, edgework, drilling, ceramic frit, and mirror silvering. This layer is where lead times and size limits actually live.

**Capabilities to check on any project:**

| Capability | Why it constrains you |
|---|---|
| **Toughening furnace size** | sets the maximum toughened pane; usually well below the jumbo sheet |
| **Heat soak furnace** | if the processor has none, HST panes must be sub-contracted or imported |
| **Autoclave size** | sets the maximum laminate; larger than the toughening furnace in some plants, smaller in others |
| **IGU line and sealant type** | whether silicone secondary seal (needed for SSG and exposed edges) is available |
| **Edgework** | polished edges, mitres, notches, countersunk holes |
| **Bending / curving** | rare in the region; assume unavailable unless proven |
| **Ceramic frit / digital print** | for spandrels, solar screens, bird-safe patterns and anti-slip |

> ⚠️ **Never write a specification that assumes a processing capability you have not confirmed with a named plant.** In this region the binding constraint is almost always the furnace, the autoclave or the transport, not the glass.

**Named businesses in the brief that could not be verified in this research pass** — **Smart Glass** (as an independent processor, distinct from PG's **PG SmartGlass®** product brand) and **Independent Glass**. Both appear in trade discussion; neither had a reachable, verifiable web presence at the time of research. Verify directly before relying on either. Note the naming trap: **PG SmartGlass®** is a *product range* distributed by PG Building Glass, and is not the same thing as a company called Smart Glass.

### Consol — verified as packaging, not building glass

**Consol Glass is a container glass manufacturer** — bottles and jars for the food, beverage and pharmaceutical trade — **not** an architectural flat glass producer. It should not appear in an architectural glass specification. Its website was not reachable during this research pass (robots/DNS failure), so this is recorded as trade knowledge pending confirmation rather than as a citation. **Treat as verified-in-substance, uncited.**

## 3. Aluminium extrusion and systems houses

Almost all architectural glazing in southern Africa is carried in aluminium.

**Wispeco** (Alrode, Alberton, Gauteng) is the major local extruder: an aluminium casting facility and **nine extrusion presses**, plus finishing, with national distribution centres. It produces industrial extrusions, standard extrusions (angles, flats, tubes, bars) and architectural extrusions, both proprietary and custom.

**Crealco** is Wispeco's architectural systems brand and the reference system range for South African and Namibian fabricators. Its ranges are organised by market segment:

| Range | Market |
|---|---|
| **Home** | entry-level residential |
| **Mansion** | upmarket residential, lodges, estates; some commercial crossover |
| **Mall** | shopping centres, offices, hospitals |
| **Tower** | high-rise, hotels, airports |

Named systems in the published product matrix include **Swift** windows (28, 30.5, 34 and 38 mm), **Evolution**, **Skyline** (41 mm), **Serene** (52 mm tilt-and-turn), **Elite**, the **500** and **1000 Series**, **Vert 70** vertical sliding, **Clip 44** shopfront, **Icon** and **Palace** doors, **Vistafold** sliding-folding doors, **Cassette**, **NewYork**, **Horizon**, **Guardian**, **Tranquil**, **Dusk**, **Seal** shower doors, **Splash** bath enclosures, and **Façade 60** curtain wall in pressure-plate and flush-glaze configurations. The system depth in millimetres (28 / 30.5 / 34 / 38 / 41 / 52) is the number that governs which glass make-ups a given system can actually take — check it before scheduling an IGU or a thick laminate. Crealco also runs a fabricator training programme.

**Hulamin** is South Africa's major aluminium **rolled products** producer (sheet, plate, coil and foil) plus extrusions. Its relevance to glazing is as a substrate supplier for cladding, composite panels and flashings rather than as a window systems house. **Its current division structure and product range could not be verified** — its site was unreachable during this research pass.

**International systems houses** — **Schüco**, **AluK**, **Reynaers**, **Technal** — operate in South Africa through licensed local fabricators rather than through manufacturing plants. Availability is real but is mediated by whichever fabricator holds the licence, and lead times reflect imported profile and hardware. **No South African office or licensee list was verified for any of them in this research pass.**

**AAAMSA** — the Association of Architectural Aluminium Manufacturers of South Africa — is the industry body, founded in **July 1974** by eight companies, describing itself as a non-profit voluntary organisation covering fenestration, glass, glazing and thermal products. **SAGGA**, the South African Glass and Glazing Association, is the glazing-trade counterpart; **its website was not reachable during this research pass and its scope, membership and publications are unverified.**

## 4. Namibia

**[NA]** This is where the chain thins, and where the specifier's assumptions must change.

### What is verified

**PG Glass operates six branches in Namibia**, all offering both automotive and building glass:

| Town | Region | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Windhoek** | Khomas | the primary Namibian node |
| **Swakopmund** | Erongo | coastal |
| **Walvis Bay** | Erongo | coastal; the port of entry for imported glass |
| **Otjiwarongo** | Otjozondjupa | the north-central staging town |
| **Oshakati** | Oshana | **the northern node** |
| **Ongwediva** | Oshana | adjacent to Oshakati |

This matters enormously for a project in the north. **Oshakati and Ongwediva are the northernmost points in the region where a branded glass supplier with building-glass capability is verified to exist.** For Ohangwena, Kavango and the far north-east, Oshakati is the practical supply point.

PG Glass Africa's total footprint outside South Africa: Botswana 3 branches, eSwatini 1, Lesotho 1, Malawi 2, Mozambique 6, Namibia 6, Zambia 6.

### What this means for a Namibian project

1. **There is no float glass manufacture in Namibia.** Every square metre is imported, overwhelmingly from PFG at Springs, roughly 1 400 km by road to Windhoek and a further 700 km to Oshakati.
2. **Toughening, lamination and IGU assembly capacity in Namibia is unverified and should be assumed to be limited or absent.** Assume processed glass is imported as finished panes, not processed locally. This has three consequences: sizes are frozen very early; transport damage is a real and uninsured-in-practice risk; and **replacement of a broken pane is a multi-week event**.
3. **Order spares with the original batch.** Coated glass colour varies between coating campaigns. A replacement pane ordered two years later may not match. For any facade with a coated or tinted glass, order a small percentage of spare panes with the main order and store them properly (on edge, on an A-frame, dry, out of sun).
4. **Aluminium fabrication capacity exists locally**, principally in Windhoek and the coastal towns, with at least one fabricator in Oshakati. Most Namibian fabricators work Crealco or comparable South African systems, so system availability generally tracks South African availability.
5. **Cross-border logistics are SACU-internal.** Namibia and South Africa are both SACU members, so there is no customs duty on movement between them, but import VAT and clearing procedures apply. See `11_logistics_remote_areas` and `07_materials_and_suppliers/11_procurement-and-pricing.md` in this knowledge base.
6. **Walvis Bay is the port** for anything imported from outside SACU — relevant for specialist products (electrochromic, oversize laminates, imported systems) that PFG does not make.
7. **Coastal versus inland is a specification fork.** Walvis Bay, Swakopmund and Lüderitz are severe marine environments: specify marine-grade coatings, AISI 316 stainless minimum, and expect accelerated degradation of exposed fixings. Inland Namibia is very low-corrosivity by comparison.

## 5. Procurement practice

**Getting a usable quotation.** A glass quotation is only comparable if the enquiry states, for every pane: the make-up (each ply thickness, heat state, interlayer type and thickness), the coating and its surface number, the size and shape, the edgework, the safety-glass standard and marking, whether heat soak testing is required, the tolerance standard, and the delivery point. Anything omitted will be quoted at the cheapest interpretation. See `08_specifying-glass.md`.

**Indicative lead times** (trade practice, **not verified** — confirm per project):

| Item | Indicative lead time |
|---|---|
| Clear float from stock, cut to size | days |
| Standard laminate from stock | days to 1–2 weeks |
| Toughened, standard sizes | 1–3 weeks |
| Toughened + heat soak tested | add 1–2 weeks |
| Coated laminate, made to order | 4–8 weeks |
| IGUs | 3–6 weeks |
| Delivery Springs → Windhoek | add roughly a week for consolidation and transit |
| Delivery Windhoek → northern Namibia | add several days |
| Imported specialist glass | months |

**Trade structure.** Buying is normally through the glazier or the aluminium fabricator, who buys from the processor, who buys from PFG or PG Building Glass. Direct purchase from PFG is a volume proposition. For a single building the practical route is: appoint the glazing subcontractor early, make them name their processor, and verify the processor's furnace, autoclave and HST capability before the elevations are frozen.
