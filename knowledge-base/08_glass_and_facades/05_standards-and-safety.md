# Glass standards and safety requirements

**Summary.** This file sets out the standards that govern glass and glazing in southern Africa and their international equivalents, and states where safety glass is legally required. **It is the most heavily caveated file in this domain.** Several widely repeated SANS clause references circulate in secondary sources without a primary citation, and Namibia's adoption position for glazing has not been verified. Everything below distinguishes what was verified from a cited source from what is trade knowledge awaiting verification, and the file's status is `needs-verification` for that reason.

> ⚠️ **Do not use this file as the sole basis for a compliance submission.** Buy SANS 10400-N, SANS 613, SANS 10137 and SANS 1263 from the SABS webstore and read the clauses. This file tells you what to look for; it does not replace the standards.

## 1. The South African framework

**[ZA]** Building work in South Africa is governed by the **National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act (Act 103 of 1977)**, with the technical content in **SANS 10400**, split into lettered parts. **Part N is Glazing.**

SANS 10400-N does three things:

1. **Requires that glazing safely sustain wind actions**, cross-referring to SANS 10400-B for structural design and to SANS 613 for impact and wind loads.
2. **Requires that glazing prevent water penetration** and be securely and durably fixed.
3. **Requires that glazing be obvious to anyone who approaches it**, so that people do not walk into it — the manifestation requirement.

It recognises four categories of glazing product:

- monolithic annealed glass
- patterned annealed and wired glass
- laminated annealed safety glass
- toughened safety glass

Only the last two are safety glasses. **Patterned and wired glass are not safety glasses** notwithstanding common site belief to the contrary.

### Deemed-to-satisfy thickness rules

Published commentary on SANS 10400-N gives the following, for clear (annealed) glass in exterior doors and windows:

| Area glazed | Nominal thickness | Maximum sheet size quoted |
|---|---|---|
| ≤ 0.75 m² | 3 mm | 2.44 × 1.83 m |
| 0.75 – 1.5 m² | 4 mm | 3.7 × 2.44 m |
| 1.5 – 2.1 m² | 5 mm | 3.21 × 2.44 m |
| 2.1 – 3.2 m² | 6 mm | 3.21 × 2.25 m |

and for the safety glasses in exterior doors and windows:

| Glass type | Maximum pane area range | Thickness range |
|---|---|---|
| Laminated annealed safety glass | 2.9 – 5.7 m² | 6 – 12 mm |
| Toughened safety glass | 1.9 – 8.0 m² | 4 – 12 mm |

> ⚠️ These tables are transcribed from published commentary, **not** from the standard. They are also deemed-to-satisfy simplifications: they do not replace calculation on tall, exposed or unusually shaped facades, and they do not account for site wind speed, terrain category, height above ground or local pressure coefficients. Verify against SANS 10400-N and SANS 10160 for any project of consequence.

## 2. Where safety glass is required — critical locations

**[ZA]** Published commentary on SANS 10400-N identifies the following as locations where safety glass must be used:

1. **Doors and sidelights** forming part of any entrance, **up to 2 100 mm from finished floor level**.
2. **Windows with a sill height less than 500 mm** from the floor.
3. **Windows with a sill height less than 800 mm** where no permanent barrier prevents contact with the glazing.
4. **Bath enclosures and shower cubicles**, and any glazing immediately above and **within 1 800 mm horizontally or vertically** of a bath or shower.
5. **Glazing in walls and balustrades adjacent to stairways, ramps, landings and patios.**
6. **Glazing within 1 800 mm of a stairway pitch line or of a ramp surface.**
7. **Internal partitions glazed within 2 100 mm of floor level.**

A second commentary source adds, as trade practice: door panes larger than **1 m²** require safety glass (a door pane of 1 m² or less is permitted in ordinary glass at a minimum of **6 mm**); shopfronts larger than **1 m²**; glazing below **800 mm** from the floor at the building authority's discretion; and balustrades **less than 1.8 m above the pitch line or landing**.

> ⚠️ The two commentary sources are not perfectly consistent with one another (notably on the 800 mm threshold and on the door-pane exemption). **Read the standard.** The safe design position — and the one to adopt if the standard is not to hand — is: **use safety glass in every door, sidelight, low-level pane, bathroom, shower, balustrade and low partition, without exception.** The cost differential between 6 mm float and 6.38 mm laminate is small; the liability differential is not.

### Balustrades and changes in level

**[ZA]** SANS 10400-D: *"The edge of any balcony, bridge, flat roof or similar place more than 1 m above the adjacent ground or floor level shall be provided with a balustrade or parapet wall not less than 1 m in height"*, unless unauthorised access has been excluded by a properly erected and maintained physical barrier. Detailed requirements for screens, railings and balustrades sit in **Part M (Stairways)** and Part D.

A widely quoted practitioner rule is a **10 mm minimum glass thickness in a post-and-infill balustrade**. See `03_structural-glass-and-facades.md` for the engineering, including the requirement for post-breakage capacity.

## 3. Marking and manifestation

**[ZA]** Two distinct obligations, routinely conflated:

**Marking of the product.** Safety glazing materials must comply with **SANS 1263-1** and must be **permanently marked by the installer**. In practice, PFG's laminated safety glass carries the **SABS mark of approval** under SANS 1263 Part 1:2013 on every sheet as manufactured, and toughened safety glass is marked by the processor. The mark identifies the product as a safety glazing material and should survive cleaning and normal wear. Where an installer applies safety film to existing glass to upgrade it, the marking obligation falls on the installer.

**Manifestation of the glazing.** SANS 10400-N requires that glazing be *"obvious to anyone who approaches it"* — that is, large areas of clear glass in circulation routes must carry visual markings, transfers, frit, decals or a transom rail at the heights at which people look. This is a collision-prevention requirement, not a product-marking requirement, and it applies to glass that is otherwise fully compliant.

## 4. SANS 1263 test regimes

**[ZA]** Verified from PFG technical literature:

**SANS 1263 Part 1:2013 — Safety glazing materials.** The swing-bag test: a **lead-filled bag weighing 45 kg** is dropped through a pendulum arc from a height of **450 mm** and strikes the centre of the test pane with an impact energy of **203 joules**. To comply, the glass must break safely; for laminated glass, the impact must not rupture the interlayer sufficiently to allow a **75 mm sphere** to pass through.

**SANS 1263 Part 2:2013 — Security glazing materials.** The drop-ball test: the glass must withstand the impact of a **2.25 kg steel ball dropped five times from a height of 3 m and once from a height of 9 m**.

**SANS 1263** also covers **safety film** applied to glass. The specific part, classification and marking requirements for film were not verified in this research pass — see Open questions.

### Human-impact classification: EN 12600

**BS EN 12600:2002 — Glass in building. Pendulum test. Impact test method and classification for flat glass.** Its verified scope: it *"specifies a pendulum impact test method for single flat panes of glass for use in buildings"* and it classifies flat glass products *"in three principal classes by performance under impact and by mode of breakage."* It explicitly does **not** specify requirements for applications, nor for durability.

The classification is written as a three-part designation of the form **α(β)φ**, where the first term is the class of the highest drop height at which the pane did not break dangerously, the middle term is the mode of breakage, and the third term is the lowest drop-height class satisfied. **The specific drop heights and breakage-mode letters were not obtained from a primary source in this research pass and are deliberately not stated here.** They are commonly quoted in glazing literature; read EN 12600 or a manufacturer's declaration of performance before writing them into a specification.

Its predecessor and the standard still cited in some UK documents is **BS 6206**. The North American equivalents are **ANSI Z97.1** (updated 2020) and the mandatory US federal regulation **CPSC 16 CFR Part 1201**, which since 1977 has required safety glazing in doors and in tub and shower enclosures.

### ASTM equivalents

| Subject | ASTM |

| Air infiltration | **ASTM E783** |

The European structural-sealant reference is the ETAG 002 family, whose Part 1 residual-strength criterion (**≥ 75%** of initial tensile strength after accelerated ageing, with failure **≥ 90% cohesive**) is the working acceptance test.

## 6. Namibia

**[NA]** Namibia's building control derives from its own regulatory framework, and in practice the southern African construction industry applies the SANS suite in Namibia because it is what the professions, the suppliers and the manufacturers work to, and because SABS-marked product is what the supply chain delivers.

**This research pass did not verify Namibia's formal legal position on glazing standards.** Specifically unverified:

- whether SANS 10400-N is adopted, referenced or merely used by convention in Namibia;
- which local authority (Windhoek, Oshakati, Ondangwa, Swakopmund, Walvis Bay and the regional councils) applies which technical requirement;
- whether the Namibian Standards Institution (NSI) has published or adopted a glazing standard;
- whether SABS marking is accepted as evidence of compliance.

> ⚠️ **[NA]** For a Namibian project, confirm the applicable requirement with the relevant local authority building inspectorate **before** the glass is ordered, and specify to SANS 10400-N / SANS 1263 as the default because that is what the supply chain can actually certify against. See `18_namibia_context` and `03_codes_standards` in this knowledge base for the Namibian regulatory framework.

## 7. Energy standards and fenestration

**[ZA]** **SANS 10400-XA** (energy usage in buildings) and **SANS 204** (energy efficiency in buildings) constrain glazing.

The one figure verified from commentary: a building with **up to 15% fenestration area to nett floor area per storey** complies with the minimum energy requirements without further calculation. Above that threshold, compliance with SANS 204's fenestration provisions must be demonstrated by calculation. "Fenestration" is defined as *"any glazed opening in a building envelope, including windows, doors and skylights."*

South Africa is divided into **six climatic zones** with different envelope R-value requirements.

The detailed SANS 204 fenestration calculation — the permissible combinations of area, orientation, shading, U-value and SHGC — was **not** obtained in this research pass. It is the calculation that actually determines whether a glass selection passes, so it must be read from the standard.

## 8. A practical compliance checklist

1. **Identify every critical location** on the drawings — doors, sidelights, low sills, bathrooms, showers, balustrades, stair-adjacent glazing, low partitions — and mark them on the glazing schedule.
2. **Specify a safety glass** (laminated or toughened) in each, to **SANS 1263-1** **[ZA]**, and require the SABS mark.
3. **Require heat soak testing to EN 14179-1** for all fully toughened glass that is overhead, frameless, in a balustrade, or above a public route.
4. **Specify laminated heat-strengthened glass** rather than monolithic toughened for balustrades, floors and overhead glazing, for post-breakage retention.
5. **Check thickness against area** using the deemed-to-satisfy tables as a floor, and **calculate to EN 16612** or the local equivalent for anything tall, exposed or large.
6. **Specify manifestation** on all large clear panes in circulation routes.
7. **Specify installation to SANS 10137** **[ZA]** and require the installer's permanent marking of safety glazing.
8. **Require certificates as deliverables:** SABS/SANS 1263 conformity, EN 14179 heat-soak certificates, IGU conformity to EN 1279-5, structural sealant compatibility and adhesion reports.
9. **Check the fenestration area** against the SANS 10400-XA 15% threshold **[ZA]** early, before the elevations are fixed.

- **SANS 10137's full title and current edition are unverified**, as are its dimensional requirements for edge clearance, edge cover and setting blocks.
- **No SANS 10400-N clause numbers are cited anywhere in this domain**, deliberately. The commentary sources do not give them.
- **EN 12600 drop heights and breakage-mode letters are deliberately omitted** because no primary source was obtained. The classification structure α(β)φ is described but its values are not.
- **EN 12150-1 minimum fragment counts by thickness were not obtained** and are omitted.
- **SANS 1263 coverage of safety film** (which part, what classification, what marking) is unverified — the brief's reference to SANS 1263 for safety film could not be confirmed against a primary source.
- **Namibia's legal adoption of any glazing standard is unverified.**
- **The SANS 204 fenestration calculation method is unverified** beyond the 15% deemed-to-satisfy threshold.
- The two commentary sources on critical locations disagree in detail; the standard governs.
