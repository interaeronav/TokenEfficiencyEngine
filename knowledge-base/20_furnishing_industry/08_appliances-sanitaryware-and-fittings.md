# Appliances, sanitaryware, brassware and architectural hardware

**Summary.** This is the mechanical half of a furnished house — the parts that have moving components, water and electricity in them, and therefore the parts that determine maintenance cost over twenty years. The decisive questions are not aesthetic: **is there a local agent who stocks cartridges and seals? Is the appliance a 220–240 V/50 Hz model? Does the tap suit the local water pressure? Can the WC be serviced without demolishing the wall?** In southern Africa the answer to the first question eliminates more brands than price does.

## Key facts

- **Miele** (Gütersloh, Germany, founded 1899 by Carl Miele and Reinhard Zinkann) is still family-owned — Miele family 51.1 %, Zinkann family 48.9 %; slogan *immer besser*; competes at the Sub-Zero/Viking/Thermador level in North America.
- **Defy** (founded **1905** in Wentworth, KwaZulu-Natal; first electric stoves **1932**) is "the largest manufacturer and distributor of major domestic appliances in Southern Africa"; acquired by Turkey's **Arçelik in July 2011 for US$324 m**; two SA factories — **Jacobs (Durban)** for stoves, built-in ovens, hobs and tumble dryers, and **Ezakheni (Ladysmith)** for chest freezers and refrigerators.
- **Duravit** (Germany, founded **1817** in Hornberg, Black Forest) makes sanitary porcelain, furniture and accessories; plants in Hornberg, Schenkenzell and Meißen (DE), Bischwiller (FR), Cairo, Chongqing, Bizerte and Tarapur; active in **over 130 countries**; Philippe Starck designed its Design Center.
- **Grohe** (Germany, founded **1936**; HQ Düsseldorf, registered office Hemer) has been part of Japan's **LIXIL since 2014** (€3 bn deal); revenue **€1.35 bn (2017)**, ~5,831 employees; plants in Hemer, Lahr, Porta Westfalica, plus Thailand, Portugal and Canada.
- **Hansgrohe SE** (Schiltach, Germany, founded **1901** by Hans Grohe): brands **hansgrohe and Axor**; **Masco 68 % / Klaus Grohe family 32 %**; ~4,962 employees, 140+ countries; invented the wallbar (1953) and the adjustable hand shower (1968); 24 patents, 180 designs, 93 brands as of 2015.
- **Geberit** operates as **Geberit Southern Africa / Geberit SADC**, offering installation and flushing systems (Duofix), drainage, WCs, urinals and the AquaClean shower toilet range.

> ⚠️ **[NA] [ZA] Electrical compatibility.** Southern Africa runs **230 V / 50 Hz** with SANS 164 plug systems. US appliances (120 V / 60 Hz) are not usable. Some induction hobs and large ovens need **three-phase or a dedicated 32–40 A circuit**. Confirm the electrical requirement before the slab is poured, and require a Certificate of Compliance from a registered electrician.

**Appliance decision rules:**
1. **Serviceability first.** In Windhoek the practical hierarchy is Defy/Samsung/LG (excellent), Bosch/Siemens/Miele (good, via SA), everything else (plan on flying in a technician).
2. **Extraction is more important than the hob.** Ducted extraction, sized to the appliance, short runs, few bends.
3. **Match the aperture to the appliance data sheet, not to the cabinet catalogue.**
4. **Dishwashers and washing machines are water-hardness-sensitive** — much Namibian borehole water is hard; specify softening or expect scale.
5. **Voltage stability.** Rural supply fluctuates; specify surge protection and consider voltage stabilisation for expensive built-ins.

### WCs and flush technology

| Type | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Close-coupled** | Cistern bolted to the pan | Cheapest, easiest to service, bulkiest |
| **Back-to-wall with concealed cistern** | Pan on the floor, cistern in a frame/duct | Clean look, floor still to clean around |
| **Wall-hung on a frame** | Pan cantilevered from a steel frame (Geberit Duofix and equivalents) | Best for cleaning; **frame must be fixed to structure**; access panel via the flush plate is essential |
| **Rimless pan** | No rim channel; water directed around the bowl | Far more hygienic; now standard in good ranges |
| **Dual flush** | Typically **6/3 l or 4.5/3 l** | **[ZA] [NA]** Water restrictions make low-volume flushing important; check the drainage fall is adequate for reduced flush volumes |
| **Washdown vs. siphonic** | European washdown (open trap) vs. US/Asian siphonic (quieter, more water) | Washdown dominates in southern Africa |
| **Shower toilet / bidet seat** | Integrated washing (Toto Washlet, Geberit AquaClean, Duravit SensoWash) | Needs a **power point and a water feed inside the duct** — decide before tiling |
| **Macerator / pumped** | Where gravity drainage is impossible | Last resort; noisy, needs power |

### Baths and basins

| Material | Character | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Acrylic (sheet, reinforced) | Warm, light, repairable, cheapest | Needs a proper cradle and support; flexes if unsupported |
| Steel enamel (Kaldewei, Bette) | Very durable, thin section, cold to touch | Chips if struck; excellent life |
| Cast iron enamel | Heaviest, holds heat | **Check floor loading**; two-person lift minimum |
| Solid surface / mineral cast (Corian-type) | Seamless, warm, matt, repairable | Premium freestanding baths |
| Stone/composite | Sculptural | Very heavy |
| Ceramic (basins) | Standard, hygienic | Vitreous china; thin-ceramic ranges are premium |
| Glass / metal basins | Design-led | Show water marks badly, especially with hard water |

**Basin types:** countertop (vessel), semi-recessed, undercounter, wall-hung, integrated (moulded into the top), pedestal. **[NA] hard water note:** with hard borehole water, a countertop basin with a visible waterline ring will need daily wiping; undercounter or integrated basins are more forgiving.

### Mixer technology

| Technology | How it works | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ceramic disc cartridge** | Two polished ceramic discs shear across each other | The modern standard: quarter-turn, no washers. **Cartridge sizes (35 mm, 40 mm) are semi-standard — but always keep a spare** |
| **Compression / washer** | Rubber washer onto a seat | Obsolete for new work but infinitely serviceable |
| **Thermostatic** | Wax or bimetallic element modulates the mix to a set temperature | **Strongly recommended for showers**, especially with solar/heat-pump hot water where temperature swings are large. Includes anti-scald and flow-failure shut-off |
| **Pressure-balanced** | Maintains hot/cold ratio when pressure changes | Cheaper than thermostatic, less precise |
| **Progressive cartridge** | Single control for volume and temperature | Minimalist designs |
| **Electronic / sensor** | IR sensor, solenoid | Public and hygiene applications; needs power |
| **Aerators / flow regulators** | Restrict flow to 4–8 l/min | Big water saving; **but low-pressure gravity systems need aerator-free or low-resistance outlets** |

> ⚠️ **Pressure compatibility is the number one tap failure in southern Africa.** Many older SA/NA houses run **low-pressure gravity-fed** hot water from a roof tank while the cold is at mains pressure. High-pressure European mixers on an unbalanced supply give a trickle of hot and a torrent of cold. Confirm the system type (**high-pressure equal, low-pressure gravity, or unequal**) and specify taps rated for it. Fit a pressure-reducing valve where mains pressure exceeds the fittings' rating (commonly 400–600 kPa).

### Shower systems

| Element | Options | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Valve | Exposed bar mixer, concealed thermostatic, digital | Concealed valves need a serviceable access route — never bury a non-serviceable body |
| Outlets | Overhead rain head, hand shower on rail, body jets | Each outlet needs flow; **calculate the total demand against the geyser recovery rate** |
| Diverter | 2-way, 3-way | Specify how many outlets can run at once |
| Drainage | Point drain, linear channel, wet-room gully | Linear channels need a single-direction fall and careful waterproofing coordination |
| Screens | Frameless toughened glass 8–10 mm | Specify a hydrophobic coating with hard water |

## 4. Water heating

| System | How it works | Suitability [NA] [ZA] |
|---|---|---|
| **Electric geyser (storage cylinder)** | Immersion element, 100–250 l | Standard; high electricity cost; requires SANS-compliant installation with vacuum breakers, PRV, drip tray and overflow |
| **Solar thermal (flat plate or evacuated tube)** | Direct or indirect circulation with an electric backup element | **Excellent in Namibia** — very high solar resource. Needs freeze protection inland in winter and scale management with hard water |
| **Heat pump** | Air-source, moves heat rather than making it | High efficiency, works well in warm climates, quieter installations need thought |
| **Instantaneous gas** | LPG on demand, no storage losses | Good for guest cottages and off-grid; needs safe LPG installation and flue |
| **Instantaneous electric** | Point-of-use | Very high current draw; rarely appropriate |
| **PV-diverter to geyser element** | Uses surplus solar PV | Increasingly common with rooftop PV |

### Door and window hardware — what to specify

| Element | Specification points |
|---|---|
| Lever handles | Material (solid brass, stainless 304/316, aluminium), finish, rose vs. backplate, **spindle size (8 mm standard)**, return-to-door safety shape for fire doors |
| Locks | Mortice lock case (standard backset 45/55/60 mm), euro cylinder vs. rim; keying schedule (keyed alike, master key suite) |
| Cylinders | Security grade, key control (patented profile for master suites) |
| Hinges | 3 per leaf standard, 4 for heavy/tall doors; **stainless in coastal areas**; ball-bearing for heavy doors |
| Closers | EN 1154 power size (EN 2–7), backcheck, delayed action for accessibility |
| Panic/emergency hardware | EN 1125 (panic, public) / EN 179 (emergency, known users) |
| Sliding gear | Door weight class; top-hung preferred; soft-close both ends |
| Pivots | For heavy/tall feature doors; must be set out with the structure |
| Window hardware | Friction stays, espagnolette, restrictors; **stainless in coastal exposure** |
| Seals | Brush/drop seals — important against **dust** in Namibia, not just draughts |
| Finishes | PVD finishes (brass, bronze, black) far outlast lacquered brass; satin stainless is the safest long-term choice |

> ⚠️ Produce an **ironmongery schedule door by door** before ordering: door number, leaf size and material, fire rating, handing, lock type, cylinder and keying group, hinges, closer, seals, accessories. Ironmongery is the commonest cause of late-stage snagging because it is ordered too late and specified per item rather than per door.
