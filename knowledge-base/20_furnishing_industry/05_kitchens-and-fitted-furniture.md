# Kitchens and fitted furniture — system manufacturers, carcass standards, door finishes and worktops

**Summary.** The kitchen industry runs on a **grid**: German system manufacturers build to a 600 mm module in a highly automated flowline; Italian design houses build to a similar grid but with far more finish variety and design authorship; mass-market retailers sell a reduced version of the same grid at volume; and local joiners build to the room. What distinguishes a €50,000 kitchen from a €10,000 one is, in order: hardware, carcass and edge quality, door finish process, worktop material, appliance specification, and design/installation labour. Only the last two are visible to a casual observer.

## Key facts

- Häcker Küchen: family-owned, based in **Rödinghausen, Germany**; two main ranges — **systemat** and **concept130** — plus a "Limited Edition 1926" marking **100 years of fitted kitchens**; ISO 9001, ISO 14001 and ISO 45001 certified and certified climate-neutral.
- Bulthaup: German, founded **1949** in Bodenkirchen, third-generation family ownership; current ranges **b Solitaire, b2, b3** (historic: System 25 from 1992, System 20 from 1997); **~250 partner stores worldwide**, **~83 % of €103.8 m 2023 revenue from exports**. This is the luxury end — small revenue, huge brand.
- Quartz composite worktops are ~90–93 % quartz in 7–10 % polyester resin, standard slab **≈3040 × 1440 mm** in **12/20/30 mm**; low water absorption, but **less heat-resistant than natural stone and not UV-stable**.
- Porcelain slabs reach **1600 × 3200 mm** (specialists **1840 × 3300 mm**) with water absorption **< 0.5 %** — the basis of Dekton/Neolith/Laminam worktops.
- Standard European kitchen grid: base units **600 mm deep × 720 mm carcass height** on a **100–150 mm plinth** giving a **900 mm worktop height**; wall units **300–350 mm deep**; tall units **2000–2400 mm**.

## 1. Dimensional standards (the grid)

| Element | Standard | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Base carcass height | **720 mm** (some 760 mm systems) | Plus plinth |
| Plinth height | 100, 120 or 150 mm | 150 mm is generous and easier to clean under |
| Worktop height | **900 mm** default; ergonomic range 850–1000 mm | Set to the primary cook's elbow height minus ~150 mm |
| Base depth | **560–580 mm** carcass, **600–650 mm** worktop | 650 mm worktops allow services behind |
| Cabinet widths | 150, 200, 300, 400, 450, 500, 600, 800, 900, 1000, 1200 mm | 600 mm is the module |
| Wall unit depth | 300–350 mm (deeper on tall/island backs) | |
| Wall unit height above worktop | **500–600 mm** | 500 mm is tight with a tall appliance |
| Tall unit height | 2000, 2150, 2300, 2400 mm | Match to ceiling with a bulkhead or a top box |
| Aisle / walkway | **1000–1200 mm** single cook, **1200–1400 mm** two cooks, ≥ 1200 mm for island + opposing run | Below 1000 mm two people cannot pass |
| Island size | Minimum ~1800 × 900 mm to be useful | Add 300–400 mm overhang for seating |
| Seating overhang | 300 mm (min. 250) at 900 mm bar height; 380 mm at 750 mm table height | |
| Appliance apertures | Ovens 560–568 mm wide; dishwashers 600 mm; fridges per model | **Always take the aperture from the appliance's own data sheet** |

**What the German system model gives you:** millimetre-consistent panels, tested hardware, a documented planning system with CAD catalogues, spares availability for a decade, and a flat-packed shipment that survives freight. **What it does not give you:** a fit to a crooked wall (that is what the fitter's scribes and filler panels do) or design authorship.

## 6. Carcass construction standards

| Feature | Budget | Good | Best |
|---|---|---|---|
| Panel | 16 mm MFC | **18–19 mm MFC or MR-MFC** | 19 mm MR board or ply, moisture-resistant throughout |
| Back panel | 3 mm hardboard, pinned | 8 mm in a groove | **12–18 mm structural back, rebated and glued** |
| Edging | 0.4 mm paper | **1 mm ABS** | 2 mm ABS or laser/zero-joint |
| Joints | Cam-and-dowel only | Dowel + cam + glue | Dowelled, glued, and pressed |
| Shelves | 16 mm, 800 mm span | 18 mm, ≤ 800 mm span | 25 mm or supported at ≤ 600 mm |
| Adjustable feet | Plastic, non-adjustable plinth clips | Adjustable feet + clip-on plinth with seal | Stainless or sealed feet, gasketed plinth |
| Drawer boxes | Board sides, roller runners | **Metal box system, full extension, soft close, 40 kg** | 70 kg class, internal drawers, tool-free adjust |
| Hinges | Unbranded | Blum/Hettich/Grass 110° soft-close | Same + integrated servo/push-to-open where handleless |
| Sink base | Untreated board | Sealed edges, aluminium/PVC drip tray | Full MR board carcass and sealed cut-outs |

> ⚠️ **The sink base is where every kitchen dies.** Specify moisture-resistant board for the sink and dishwasher runs, a drip tray, sealed edges to all cut-outs, and a visible plumbing access. This is cheap at build and ruinous later.

## 7. Door finishes compared

| Finish | Construction | Look | Durability | Repairability | Heat/UV | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Melamine (MFC)** | Decor paper impregnated with melamine resin, pressed onto board | Matt to textured woodgrain | Very good scratch resistance | Not repairable | Excellent | Lowest |
| **Foil / thermofoil wrapped** | PVC or PET film vacuum-pressed over routed MDF | Shaker profiles cheaply | Good until it delaminates | Not repairable | **Poor near ovens/dishwashers — peels** | Low |
| **Laminate (HPL) on MDF** | High-pressure laminate bonded to a substrate, edged | Sharp, flat, huge decor range | Excellent | Edge repair only | Very good | Low–mid |
| **Painted MDF (2-pack polyurethane)** | Sprayed, sanded and cured lacquer, 2-component | Any colour, matt/satin/gloss | Good; chips at edges | **Repairable — can be resprayed** | Good; some whites yellow under UV | Mid–high |
| **Painted MDF (1-pack/water-based)** | Single-component | Softer sheen | Lower; marks | Repairable | Fair | Mid |
| **Timber veneer** | Sliced veneer on MDF/ply, lacquered or oiled | Real grain, matched leaves | Good | Repairable if oiled | **Fades and ambers in strong sun** | Mid–high |
| **Solid timber (frame and panel)** | Rails/stiles + floating panel | Traditional | Good | Very repairable | **Moves — must be designed to move** | High |
| **Acrylic (PMMA face)** | High-gloss or matt acrylic sheet on MDF, edged | Deepest gloss available | Scratches on gloss show badly | No | Good | Mid–high |
| **Glass (back-painted or satin)** | Toughened glass on a board core or in an aluminium frame | Very high-end flat look | Excellent surface | No | Excellent | High |
| **Stainless steel / metal-faced** | Sheet bonded to a core | Professional | Fingerprints (unless textured) | No | Excellent | High |
| **Fenix NTM / nanotech laminates** | Acrylic-resin nanotech surface | Super-matt, anti-fingerprint, thermally healable micro-scratches | Very good | **Micro-scratches heat-repairable** | Very good | Mid–high |

## 8. Worktops compared

| Material | Heat | Scratch | Stain | Seams | Outdoor/UV | Repair | Typical relative cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Laminate (postformed) | Poor | Fair | Good | Visible | No | No | 1× |
| Solid timber / butcher block | Fair (marks) | Poor–fair | Poor unless oiled | Glued, visible | No | **Sandable** | 1.5–2.5× |
| Quartz composite (Caesarstone, Silestone) | **Poor — resin scorches** | Very good | Very good | Visible but tight | **No — resin yellows** | Limited | 3–5× |
| Sintered stone (Dekton, Neolith) | Excellent | Excellent | Excellent | Fine | **Yes** | No | 4–7× |
| Porcelain slab (Laminam, Atlas Plan) | Excellent | Excellent | Excellent | Fine | Yes | No | 4–6× |
| Granite | Excellent | Excellent | Good (sealed) | Visible | Yes | Polishable | 3–5× |
| Marble | Good | Poor | **Poor — etches** | Visible | Yes | Polishable | 4–7× |
| Stainless steel | Excellent | Scratches to a patina | Excellent | Welded/seamless | Yes | Grain-sandable | 4–6× |
| Solid surface (Corian) | Poor–fair | Fair | Very good | **Invisible** | Limited | **Fully repairable** | 3–5× |
| Concrete (cast/precast) | Good | Fair | Fair (sealed) | Cast in place | Yes | Patchable | 2–4× |

**Detailing that matters more than material choice:** overhang support (any unsupported quartz/porcelain overhang beyond ~300 mm needs steel), cut-out radii (square internal corners crack — insist on radiused corners at hob and sink cut-outs), edge profile, upstand vs. tiled splashback, and the drainage fall around undermount sinks.

## 9. Appliance integration

| Issue | Rule |
|---|---|
| Aperture dimensions | Take from the appliance data sheet, never from the cabinet catalogue |
| Ventilation | Ovens, fridges and dishwashers all need specified free area; blocked plinth vents kill compressors |
| Fridge/freezer, integrated | Requires door-on-door or sliding-hinge fixing; check the appliance supports the door weight |
| Extraction | Ducted always beats recirculating; **size the duct to the appliance (usually 150 mm)** and keep bends to a minimum. Recirculating carbon filters remove smell, not moisture or grease |
| Hob clearance | Manufacturer's minimum to overhead cabinets and to side walls; gas hobs need more |
| Sink and tap | Boiling-water taps need a base-unit tank, an isolated power point and a filter change schedule |
| Electrical | Dedicated circuits for oven and hob; sockets accessible for isolation; **[ZA] [NA]** work by a registered electrician with a Certificate of Compliance |
| Service access | Every integrated appliance must be removable without cutting cabinetry |

## 10. What separates a €50k kitchen from a €10k one

In descending order of real, measurable difference:

1. **Hardware** (~€1,500–4,000 difference on a mid-size kitchen): branded soft-close box systems, servo/push-to-open, engineered corner and pantry pull-outs.
2. **Carcass and edging**: 19 mm MR board, structural backs, 2 mm laser edging vs. 16 mm standard board and 0.4 mm paper edge.
3. **Doors**: sprayed 2-pack, veneer or Fenix vs. foil-wrapped MDF; consistency of gloss and colour batch.
4. **Worktop**: sintered stone/stainless with engineered support vs. laminate.
5. **Appliances**: Gaggenau/Miele vs. entry-level (`08_appliances-sanitaryware-and-fittings.md`).
6. **Design and setting-out**: a designed kitchen has correct ergonomics, real service coordination, and a lighting plan.
7. **Installation**: scribing, levelling, sealing and snagging by a skilled fitter — often 10–15 % of the kitchen cost and the difference between "fitted" and "installed".

Note what is **not** on the list: the brand badge. A locally made kitchen using 19 mm MR board, Blum hardware, sprayed doors and a sintered-stone top will outlast an imported system kitchen built to a lower internal specification.
