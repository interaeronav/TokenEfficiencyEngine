# Textiles and soft furnishing — fibres, weaves, specification data and the fabric houses

**Summary.** A furnishing textile is specified on five axes: **fibre** (what it is made of), **construction** (how it is made), **abrasion** (how long it lasts under rubbing), **lightfastness** (how long it keeps its colour), and **fire performance** (whether it is legal in the building). In a high-UV, low-humidity, dusty climate the binding constraint is almost never abrasion — it is **lightfastness and soiling**. A 100,000-Martindale linen with a lightfastness of 4 will look worse after three Namibian summers than a 40,000-Martindale solution-dyed acrylic with a lightfastness of 7–8.

## Key facts

- Martindale guidance in general use: **10,000–15,000 domestic**, **25,000–35,000 office/commercial**, **200,000–500,000** for the most demanding applications. Higher numbers past ~50,000 stop predicting real-world domestic life.
- The **Wyzenbeek** test (ASTM D4157, double rubs) is the US equivalent; **Martindale and Wyzenbeek results do not convert** — no reliable conversion table exists, though the industry uses rough equivalences.
- Lightfastness is graded on the **blue wool scale 1–8** (ISO 105-B02); 8 is the most lightfast. **Specify ≥ 5 for any fabric in a sunlit room; ≥ 6–7 for direct sun.**
- Pilling is graded **1–5** (ISO 12945, Martindale or ICI box), 5 = no pilling. Specify **≥ 4**.
- Kvadrat: Danish, founded **1968**, family-controlled with Anders Byriel as CEO; furniture textiles, acoustic panels and the Really upcycled-textile board; collaborators include Nanna Ditzel, Raf Simons, Olafur Eliasson, Hella Jongerius and Peter Saville.
- **CAL TB 117-2013** is a smoulder test (cigarette, 45 minutes) and no longer effectively mandates flame-retardant chemicals.

## 1. Fibres

| Fibre | Origin | Strengths | Weaknesses | Hot-dry/high-UV verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cotton** | Natural cellulose | Breathable, dyes well, cheap, comfortable | Fades, soils, shrinks, weak when wet, low abrasion alone | Good in blends; expect fading. Print-friendly |
| **Linen (flax)** | Natural cellulose | Strong, cool, beautiful slub and drape | Creases badly, low abrasion, stretches and relaxes | Superb aesthetically; specify blended or loose-covered. Fades gracefully |
| **Wool** | Protein | Naturally flame-resistant, resilient, self-extinguishing, excellent recovery, good soil resistance | Cost, moth risk, can felt | **The best all-round upholstery fibre.** Kvadrat/Maharam contract wools are the benchmark |
| **Silk** | Protein | Lustre, colour depth | Very poor lightfastness, weak, water-marks | Curtains only, always lined and interlined, never in sun |
| **Viscose / rayon / lyocell** | Regenerated cellulose | Silk-like lustre, cheap, dyes brilliantly | Weak wet, pills, poor abrasion, crushes (in velvets) | Use blended only; avoid ≥ 50 % viscose in a seat |
| **Polyester** | Synthetic | Strong, cheap, dimensionally stable, good lightfastness when solution-dyed | Pills, can look flat, oil-based stains cling | The volume workhorse; solution-dyed polyester is excellent outdoors |
| **Acrylic** | Synthetic | **Solution-dyed acrylic has outstanding lightfastness**, soft hand, wool-like | Pills, lower abrasion than polyester | The outdoor/sunroom fibre of choice (Sunbrella) |
| **Olefin / polypropylene** | Synthetic | Excellent stain and moisture resistance, bleach-cleanable, cheap | Low melting point, flattens, dull | Good for high-abuse family and contract use |
| **Nylon / polyamide** | Synthetic | Highest abrasion resistance of common fibres | Poor lightfastness unless treated | Contract carpets and heavy-duty upholstery |
| **Trevira CS** | Inherently FR polyester | Permanent flame retardance built into the polymer, washable | Aesthetic limits | The default for hospitality curtains and drapes |

## 2. Constructions and weaves

| Weave / construction | How it is made | Character | Durability notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | 1-over-1 | Flat, stable, shows the yarn | The most stable, least prone to snagging |
| **Twill** | Offset floats forming diagonal ribs | Drapes well, hides soil, denim-like | Very durable per weight |
| **Satin / sateen** | Long floats on the face | Lustrous, smooth | Snags easily; curtains rather than seats |
| **Jacquard** | Each warp end individually controlled by a jacquard head | Complex figured patterns woven, not printed | Durable, expensive; long floats can snag |
| **Dobby** | Small geometric patterns via a dobby mechanism | Textured "structure" fabrics | Excellent workhorse construction |
| **Velvet / velour** | Pile fabric, woven double and cut, or warp-pile | Depth of colour, luxury | Cotton velvet crushes and "shades"; mohair and polyester velvets recover better |
| **Chenille** | Chenille yarn (caterpillar-like, with pile radiating) woven into the ground | Soft, heavy, light-reflective | Yarn can shed and "track"; avoid on pets' furniture |
| **Bouclé** | Looped novelty yarn | The signature texture of the 2020s | **Snags catastrophically with claws**; check the loop is tightly bound |
| **Epinglé / mohair frisé** | Uncut looped pile, often mohair | Extremely durable, historic contract fabric | Among the longest-lived upholstery fabrics made |
| **Flat-woven wool / felted** | Dense plain or twill wool | Kvadrat/Maharam house look | Excellent all-round |
| **Non-woven / bonded** | Fibres bonded, not woven | Cheap backing, interlinings | Not a face fabric |
| **Knits (with backing)** | Warp-knitted, backed with polyester | Stretch fabrics for moulded shapes | Only where the design requires stretch |

**Backing.** Most contract upholstery fabrics are latex- or acrylic-backed. Backing stabilises loose weaves, raises abrasion, and improves fire performance — but stiffens the hand and can crack with age. A **secondary backcloth** may also be applied for tailored upholstery.

**Railroading.** Fabrics may be woven or printed to be used **up the roll** (usual) or **railroaded** (turned 90°, pattern running along the roll) to avoid seams on long sofas. Check before ordering: railroading a non-railroaded pattern doubles the metreage.

## 3. Specification data — the numbers to demand

| Property | Test | Scale | Domestic target | Contract/hospitality target |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Abrasion (EU) | Martindale, ISO 12947 | Cycles | 20,000–30,000 | 40,000+ (heavy use 50,000+) |
| Abrasion (US) | Wyzenbeek, ASTM D4157 | Double rubs | 15,000 | 30,000+ |
| Lightfastness | ISO 105-B02 | Blue wool 1–8 | ≥ 5 | ≥ 6 (≥ 7 in direct sun) |
| Pilling | ISO 12945 | 1–5 | ≥ 4 | ≥ 4 |
| Colourfastness to rubbing | ISO 105-X12 | 1–5 dry/wet | ≥ 4 dry, ≥ 3 wet | ≥ 4 / ≥ 3–4 |
| Seam slippage | ISO 13936 | mm at load | ≤ 6 mm | ≤ 6 mm |
| Tensile / tear | ISO 13934 / 13937 | N | Per end use | Per end use |
| Fire (EU) | EN 1021-1 & -2 | Pass/fail | Pass | Pass, plus BS 5852 crib 5 where required |
| Fire (US) | CAL TB 117-2013 | Pass/fail | Pass | Plus TB 133 for assembly occupancies |
| Dimensional stability | ISO 5077 | % shrink | ≤ 2 % for loose covers | ≤ 2 % |

**Fabric width and repeat.** Standard upholstery widths: **137–142 cm** (54") for most contract fabrics; **130–150 cm** for European; curtain fabrics commonly **137 cm** or **280–300 cm** (sheers and wide-width linens, hung sideways with no seams). Always calculate metreage with the **pattern repeat** included — a 64 cm repeat on a pair of 3 m curtains can add 20–30 % to the metreage.

**Curtain making-up allowances (trade practice):**
- Fullness: **2× to 2.5×** for pleated headings; 2.5–3× for sheers; 1.5× for wave/ripplefold.
- Side hems 5 cm double; bottom hem 10–15 cm double; heading allowance 10–20 cm.
- Interlining (bump/domette) adds body, insulation and light-blocking — the single biggest quality difference in curtains, and it also **protects the face cloth from UV**.
- Always line curtains in a high-UV climate; specify a **UV-stable lining** and expect linings to be sacrificial (replaceable at 7–12 years).

## 4. Performance fabrics

| Brand | Owner / country | Technology | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sunbrella** | Glen Raven, US | Solution-dyed acrylic — pigment in the fibre, not on it | Outdoor and marine upholstery, awnings, sunlit interiors. The benchmark for lightfastness |
| **Crypton** | US | Fibre and finish system integrating stain, moisture and odour barriers into the fabric | Healthcare, hospitality, family furniture |
| **Alcantara** | Italy (Toray) | Microfibre non-woven suede-like composite | Automotive, luxury interiors |
| **Ultrasuede** | Toray, Japan | Similar microfibre suede | High-wear luxury |
| **Ultrafabrics / Yarwood / Spradling** | Various | Polyurethane coated synthetics ("performance leather") | Contract seating where cleanability rules |
| **Trevira CS branded fabrics** | Various weavers | Inherently FR polyester | Hospitality drapery |

> ⚠️ **PU-coated "vegan leather" ages by hydrolysis.** It flakes and peels, typically well before the frame is worn out, and it does so faster in heat. If you need a wipeable seat that lasts, specify a high-quality PU with a stated hydrolysis rating (e.g. tested to 5+ years in accelerated ageing) or use real pigmented leather.

**[NA] Namibia.** There is no Namibian fabric house of comparable scale; Windhoek interior shops and curtain makers buy from the SA houses on trade accounts and ship by road. Because SACU removes customs duty between the two, buying from Hertex, St Leger & Viney, Home Fabrics or Mavromac is effectively domestic procurement plus freight and Namibian import VAT handling. ## 6. Trimmings and making-up

| Item | Description | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Piping / welt | Cord covered in the face fabric or a contrast | Contrast piping is the cheapest way to lift a plain sofa |
| Flange / knife edge | Flat fabric edge instead of piping | Contemporary look; weaker seam |
| Braid, gimp, fan edge | Applied decorative edging | Traditional; hides tack lines |
| Bullion / tassel fringe | Heavy fringe at the base | Classic; collects dust |
| Studs / nails | Individual or on strip | Antique brass, pewter, chrome; strip is faster and neater but reads cheaper close up |
| Buttons | Self-covered, deep-buttoned or float-buttoned | Deep buttoning requires a properly built base or the buttons pull through |
| Leading edge trims, borders | Curtain edge details | Cheap way to add design content |
