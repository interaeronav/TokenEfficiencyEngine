# Wardrobes, closets and storage — systems, components, hardware and dressing-room planning

**Summary.** Storage is bought in four fundamentally different ways: as a **wire or rail system** hung off a wall (Elfa-type), as a **modular carcass system** (IKEA PAX, Nolte, Hülsta), as an **architectural system** of aluminium frames and doors (Raumplus, Rimadesio, Molteni Gliss), or as **bespoke joinery** built to the room. The choice is driven less by budget than by geometry: rooms with square walls, standard heights and no obstructions favour systems; old buildings, sloping ceilings and awkward returns favour bespoke. Everything else — hanging heights, drawer depths, door clearances — follows a small set of dimensions that have not changed in fifty years.

## Key facts (planning dimensions)

| Dimension | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Wardrobe internal depth (hanging on a rail across the opening) | **560–600 mm** | 600 mm carcass depth is the near-universal standard |
| Wardrobe depth with **front-to-back** pull-out rail | 350–450 mm | Used in shallow recesses; capacity per metre drops sharply |
| Hanging rail height, **long hang** (dresses, coats) | **1500–1800 mm** clear | 1600 mm is a good default |
| Hanging rail height, **short hang** (shirts, jackets, trousers folded) | **900–1100 mm** clear | Allows double-hanging in a 2100 mm+ height |
| Double-hang stack (two rails) | Lower rail ~1000 mm above floor, upper rail ~2000 mm | Needs ~2100–2200 mm internal height |
| Rail centre from back panel | **300 mm** (for a 600 mm carcass) | Keeps shoulders off the back |
| Clear space in front of a hanging rail | ≥ **50 mm** to door face | Doors that touch clothes wear them |
| Shelf spacing, folded clothes | **300–400 mm** | 350 mm is comfortable |
| Shoe shelf depth / spacing | 300–350 mm deep, **180–200 mm** spacing (flat), sloped shelves save depth | Boots need 400–500 mm |
| Drawer internal height for underwear/socks | 100–150 mm | |
| Drawer internal height for jumpers | 200–250 mm | |
| Top box / overhead storage | 400–600 mm high above 2100 mm | Seasonal only |
| **Walk-in aisle width, single-sided** | **900–1000 mm** minimum, 1100 mm comfortable | Below 900 mm you cannot open a drawer and stand aside |
| **Walk-in aisle, double-sided (both walls)** | **1100–1200 mm** minimum, 1400 mm comfortable | Add drawer extension + body depth |
| Walk-in with an island | Aisle **1000–1200 mm each side**; island 600–900 mm wide | Room needs ≥ 3600 mm width |
| Dressing bench / seat | 400–450 mm high, 400 mm deep | |
| Full-length mirror | 400 × 1500 mm min., centre ~1400 mm | Needs 1500–2000 mm standing distance |
| Hinged door swing clearance | Door width + 50 mm in front | A 600 mm door needs 650 mm clear |
| Sliding door overlap/track depth | Typically **100–130 mm** lost from the carcass depth for 2-track systems | Which is why sliding wardrobes are usually 650–680 mm deep externally |
| Lighting: rail-lit or shelf-lit LED | 2700–3000 K, **CRI ≥ 90** | Colour judgement of clothing depends on CRI — see `06_lighting-industry.md` |

**Capacity rule of thumb:** 1 linear metre of hanging rail holds roughly **50–60 shirts** or **20–25 jackets/coats**; a double-hang metre therefore holds ~100 shirts. A couple sharing typically needs **4–6 m of hanging** plus 8–12 drawers. `needs-verification` — trade practice.

**Systems vs. bespoke — the honest comparison:**

| | System | Bespoke joinery |
|---|---|---|
| Fit to irregular rooms | Poor to fair (filler panels) | Perfect |
| Price predictability | High — catalogue pricing | Depends on the joiner |
| Lead time | 6–14 weeks + freight | 4–10 weeks local |
| Interior fittings quality | Excellent (engineered range) | As good as the hardware specified |
| Future extension | Easy while the range lives | Requires the same joiner |
| Resale / reuse | PAX and Elfa are re-usable | Fixed to the building |
| Cost in southern Africa | Import freight + agent margin | No freight, local labour rates |

**[NA] [ZA] Verdict:** for southern Africa, the economically rational default is **local bespoke joinery using imported hardware** (Blum/Hettich/Häfele) and, where sliding doors are wanted, an imported aluminium door system or a locally fabricated equivalent. Full imported carcass systems only make sense at the luxury tier where the design content is the point.

## 2. Components

### Carcass
- **Material:** 16 or 18 mm melamine-faced chipboard (MFC) is the volume standard; 18 mm MDF for painted; 18 mm birch ply for exposed-edge contemporary; moisture-resistant board in any coastal or bathroom-adjacent location.
- **Backs:** 3–8 mm hardboard/MDF in a groove is standard; a **12–18 mm structural back** with the carcass rebated is far more rigid and worth the money on tall units.
- **Edging:** 0.4 mm paper-thin edge is budget; **1 mm or 2 mm ABS/PP** is correct; laser/zero-joint edging is premium. In a dry climate, thin edging lifts.
- **Assembly:** dowel + confirmat, or cam-and-dowel (RTA), or dominos/lamellos in a shop-built unit. Screw-only into board edges is the sign of a cheap unit.

### Doors
| Type | Pros | Cons | Space required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hinged** | Full access, cheapest, quietest, best seal against dust | Needs swing space | Door width + 50 mm |
| **Sliding (bottom-rolling)** | No swing space, large panels possible | Only ~half the opening accessible; track collects dust; 100–130 mm depth lost | Nil beyond the carcass |
| **Sliding (top-hung)** | Cleaner floor, no bottom track to clog | Requires a strong head fixing | Nil |
| **Bi-fold / folding** | Compromise between the two | More hardware, more failure points | ~½ door width |
| **Pocket / flush-retracting (Häfele HAWA, Salice)** | Doors disappear into the carcass — best of both | Expensive, eats internal volume | Door depth inside the carcass |
| **Open / curtained** | Cheapest, dust problem | Dust — a serious issue in Namibia | Nil |

> ⚠️ **[NA] Dust.** In Windhoek and anywhere on gravel, open shelving and open walk-ins need either doors or an annual clothes-cleaning budget. Specify **brush seals** on sliding doors and a full-height door line rather than open bays.

| Fitting | Function | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Hanging rail (oval 30 × 15 mm or round Ø25) | Hanging | Support at ≤ 900 mm centres or it sags |
| Illuminated LED rail | Hanging + light | Neat, but check driver access |
| Pull-out trouser rack | Trousers full-length without folding | Needs 500 mm+ depth |
| Pull-out shoe rack / sloping shelves | Shoes | Sloping shelves are cheaper and see better |
| Pull-out valet rod | Outfit staging | 300 mm projection |
| Pull-down wardrobe lift ("hanger lift") | Reaching high rails | Essential above 2000 mm; check load rating (usually 10–15 kg) |
| Internal drawers with glass or solid fronts | Small items | Glass fronts let you see contents — the single best upgrade |
| Jewellery/watch insert trays | Small items | Felt-lined |
| Belt/tie racks | | Side-mounted, low value per mm |
| Laundry pull-out hampers | | Ventilated |
| Corner solutions (rotating or L-carousel) | Corner walk-ins | Always lose capacity; consider a fixed shelf plus a door instead |
| Mirror (door-mounted, pull-out or full-height) | | Safety-back film on any wardrobe mirror |

### Wire vs. board

| | Wire (Elfa-type) | Board (MFC/MDF) |
|---|---|---|
| Ventilation | Excellent — good in humid climates | Sealed — better against dust |
| Cost | Lowest | Higher |
| Look | Utilitarian | Furniture-like |
| Load | Good (loads into the wall) | Good (loads to floor) |
| Adjustability | Very high, tool-free | Low once built |
| Marks on clothing | Wire shelves mark folded knitwear | None |
| Fixing | **Needs a sound wall** — masonry or properly located studs | Free-standing possible |

## 3. Hardware ecosystem

Detailed treatment is in `06_joinery_and_woodwork/07_hardware-systems.md`. For wardrobes specifically:
- **Hinges:** Ø35 mm cup hinges, 110° standard; use **155–165°** where an internal drawer must clear the door; add a hinge for doors over 600 mm wide or 2000 mm tall. Tall wardrobe doors in a dry climate can cup — use 4–5 hinges on a 2400 mm door.
- **Drawer runners:** concealed full-extension soft-close (Blum MOVENTO/TANDEM, Hettich Quadro) for internal drawers; box systems (LEGRABOX, TANDEMBOX, InnoTech Atira) where the drawer is visible.
- **Sliding gear:** Häfele Slido, Hettich TopLine, Hawa. Door-weight classes matter — a 2400 × 1000 mm mirrored door is heavy; size the gear to it.
- **Lift systems:** Blum AVENTOS for overhead top-boxes.

## 4. Planning a dressing room — a working method

1. **Inventory first.** Count hanging metres (long and short), drawers, shoe pairs, and bulky items. Design to the inventory plus 25 %.
2. **Set the aisle.** 1000 mm single-sided, 1200 mm double-sided, 1000–1200 mm each side of an island.
3. **Zone by height.** 0–600 mm: shoes and drawers. 600–2000 mm: hanging and everyday shelves. 2000 mm+: seasonal and luggage.
4. **Place the mirror** where there is standing room and, ideally, daylight — but not opposite a west window in Namibia (glare and fabric fading).
5. **Light it properly.** Vertical illumination on the clothes, not just a ceiling downlight. CRI ≥ 90, 3000 K, and shielded so it does not glare. Door-actuated switching or PIR.
6. **Ventilate.** A closed dressing room with no air movement smells. A small extract or a louvred door helps; in dry Namibian air, mustiness is less of a risk than dust.
7. **Services.** Allow for an ironing point, a charging drawer, and a safe location (bolted to structure, not to board).
8. **Doors last.** Decide hinged vs. sliding only after the internal layout works.

## 5. The southern African market

| Category | South Africa | Namibia |
|---|---|---|
| Franchised fitted-furniture retail | Easylife Kitchens, Kitchen Studio, Nobilia dealers, Blu Line, CTM/Italtile-adjacent cabinet retail | A small number of Windhoek shopfitters; some SA franchises operate branches or agents `needs-verification` |
| Bespoke joinery | Large industry, Gauteng and Western Cape concentrated | Windhoek and Swakopmund joineries — the default route |
| System imports (Raumplus, Rimadesio, Poliform, Molteni) | Available through Johannesburg/Cape Town agents | Ordered via SA agents or direct import |
| Wire systems | Elfa distributed in SA; local wire-shelving equivalents widely available | Available via SA retail and hardware chains |
| Hardware | Blum, Hettich, Häfele, Grass all agented into SA | Supplied through SA agents; stock items reasonable, specials slow |

> ⚠️ **[NA] Lead time reality.** A bespoke Windhoek wardrobe using stock hardware: **4–8 weeks**. The same wardrobe with a non-stock Blum or Häfele item: add **4–10 weeks** for the hardware alone. Order hardware before you order board.
