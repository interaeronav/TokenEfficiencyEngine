# Quality standards and testing for furniture — EN, BIFMA, SANS, emissions, certification and enforceable specification

**Summary.** Furniture quality is measurable, and the tests are published. A chair is loaded cyclically until it fails; a storage unit is pulled forward with a weight in an open drawer; a board is put in a chamber and its formaldehyde emission measured in ppm; a fabric is rubbed until it breaks. What separates an enforceable specification from a wish list is naming the **standard, the level, and the evidence required** — and putting that in the purchase order, where it becomes a contractual term rather than an opinion.

## Key facts

- **TSCA Title VI** (US EPA) formaldehyde limits for composite wood products are **set at levels identical to CARB ATCM Phase 2**; from **22 March 2019** composite wood products must be **labelled as TSCA Title VI compliant**. Covers hardwood plywood, MDF, particleboard and finished goods containing them. **The ppm limits themselves are in 40 CFR Part 770** and were not on the page fetched.
- **UL GREENGUARD** tests chemical emissions against limits for **more than 15,000 known VOCs**; **GREENGUARD Gold** applies lower thresholds and requires compliance with **California Section 01350** — the level appropriate for schools, healthcare and children's environments. Standards: **UL 2818** (building materials, finishes, furnishings), UL 2819 (electronic equipment), UL 2820 (cleaners).
- **FSC** (founded 1993) labels: **FSC 100 %**, **FSC Mix** (introduced 2004), **FSC Recycled**; **Chain of Custody (CoC)** certification is required before any company may use FSC trademarks. **PEFC** is the competing scheme, created by forest-industry stakeholders and criticised for limited local/NGO influence.
- **CAL TB 117-2013** is a 45-minute smoulder test (cigarette on fabric and foam); the 2013 revision ended the effective mandate for chemical flame retardants, and **SB 1019** introduced flame-retardant content labelling.
- **Martindale** guidance in general use: 10,000–15,000 domestic, 25,000–35,000 office/commercial, 200,000–500,000 for extreme duty.

## 1. Seating and table standards

| Standard | Scope | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| **EN 1728** | Seating — test methods for strength and durability | Seat and back cyclic loading, seat front-edge loading, arm static and fatigue, leg forward and sideways static, drop tests |
| **EN 1022** | Seating — stability | Forward, rearward and sideways overturning |
| **EN 12520** | **Domestic seating — requirements** | Sets the pass levels of EN 1728/1022 tests for household use |
| **EN 16139** | Non-domestic seating — strength, durability, safety | Contract levels; usually specified with severity level L1 or L2 |
| **EN 1729-1 / -2** | Chairs and tables **for educational institutions** | Part 1: functional dimensions (size marks by pupil height); Part 2: safety requirements and test methods |
| **EN 12521** | **Domestic tables — requirements** | Pass levels for EN 1730 tests |
| **EN 1730** | Tables — test methods | Stability, strength, durability, vertical static load, horizontal load, drop |
| **EN 15372** | Non-domestic tables | Contract-level table requirements |
| **EN 581-1/-2/-3** | **Outdoor furniture** | General safety, seating, tables — the correct family for a Namibian stoep |
| **EN 1335-1/-2/-3** | Office work chairs | Dimensions, safety requirements, test methods |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X5.1** | Office chairs (US) | Cyclic and static loading; the North American benchmark |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X5.4** | Lounge and public seating | |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X5.5** | Desk and table products | |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X5.9** | Storage units | |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X6.1** | Educational seating | |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X7.1** | Formaldehyde and TVOC emissions from furniture | Emissions, paired with e3 |
| **BIFMA e3 / LEVEL** | Sustainability certification for furniture | Multi-attribute, tiered (LEVEL 1/2/3) |

**How to use them:** for domestic work, specify **EN 12520 (seating)** and **EN 12521 (tables)**. For anything contract, hospitality or public — including a guest lodge — specify **EN 16139 (level L1 or L2)** and **EN 15372**. For office chairs specify **EN 1335 or ANSI/BIFMA X5.1**, and state the required user mass and hours of use (e.g. "suitable for users to 150 kg, 24-hour use").

## 2. Storage furniture

| Standard | Scope |
|---|---|
| **EN 14749** | **Domestic and kitchen storage units and worktops — safety requirements and test methods** — the core standard for wardrobes, kitchen cabinets and chests |
| **EN 16121 / EN 16122** | Non-domestic storage furniture: requirements and test methods |
| **EN 15939** | Shelving-type storage `needs-verification` |
| **ASTM F2057** | Clothing storage units — **tip-over stability** (US; the standard behind anti-tip requirements) |
| **ANSI/BIFMA X5.9** | Storage units (US contract) |

**Tip-over is the safety issue that matters.** Tall chests and wardrobes must be either **stable with a loaded drawer extended** or **anchored to the wall**. Specify and supply anti-tip restraints for every unit above roughly 750 mm tall, and require them to be **fitted, not just delivered in the bag**.

**[ZA] SANS.** South Africa publishes SANS standards for furniture, many of them adoptions of ISO/EN documents. **The applicable SANS numbers for seating, storage and mattresses were not verified in this research pass** — obtain the current list from the SABS standards catalogue (see `03_codes_standards/10_how-to-obtain-standards.md`). `needs-verification`.

## 3. Mattresses and beds

| Standard | Scope |
|---|---|
| **EN 1957** | Beds and mattresses — test methods for the determination of functional characteristics (durability rolling load, firmness change, height loss) |
| **EN 1725** | Beds and mattresses — safety requirements and test methods `needs-verification` (partially superseded) |
| **EN 597-1 / -2** | Mattress and bed base ignitability — cigarette (‑1) and match flame (‑2) |
| **BS 7177** | UK mattress and divan flammability hazard classes (low, medium, high, very high) |
| **16 CFR 1632 / 1633** | US mattress flammability — cigarette ignition (1632) and open-flame (1633) |
| **EN 16890** | Children's mattresses |
| **EN 747-1/-2** | Bunk beds and high beds — safety |

**Practical mattress specification:** state size and tolerance, spring type (pocket count where relevant), comfort layer materials and densities, cover/ticking, firmness, warranty period and what it covers (usually structural sagging beyond a stated depth), and the **flammability standard the local occupancy requires**. For lodges and guest houses, mattress flammability is an insurance and licensing issue, not a comfort one.

## 4. Formaldehyde emission classes

| Class / regime | Region | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | EU (EN 13986 / EN 717-1) | The mainstream European class for panel products |
| **E0** | Marketing/regional term (notably Asia-Pacific and China GB standards) | Lower than E1; **"E0" is not a single harmonised European class** — always ask which standard and which test method |
| **CARB Phase 2 (ATCM 93120)** | California | Product-specific ppm limits by panel type |
| **TSCA Title VI** | USA federal | **Set at levels identical to CARB Phase 2**; labelling as TSCA Title VI compliant required after 22 March 2019; third-party certification via EPA-recognised bodies |
| **JIS F★★★★ (F-four-star)** | Japan | The strictest common commercial class |
| **EN 16516** | EU | Test method for release of regulated dangerous substances, including formaldehyde |

> ⚠️ **Ask for the certificate, not the claim.** "E1 board" on a quotation means nothing without the mill's test certificate or the panel's stamped edge. In a hot climate emission rates rise with temperature — a board that is marginal at 23 °C test conditions performs worse in a 35 °C Namibian bedroom. Specify **E1 as a minimum and E0/CARB2/TSCA Title VI where children or bedrooms are involved.**

## 6. Timber certification

| Scheme | Note |
|---|---|
| **FSC** | Founded 1993, German-registered non-profit. Labels: **FSC 100 %**, **FSC Mix** (2004, permits controlled mixing but excludes illegally harvested wood and high-conservation-value forest material), **FSC Recycled**. **Chain of Custody** traces material from forest through processing to the final product; only CoC-certified companies may use FSC trademarks. Criticised for greenwashing, indigenous-rights cases, weak certifier oversight and cost barriers to small operators; **Greenpeace International withdrew in 2018** |
| **PEFC** | The main competing scheme, created by forest-industry stakeholders; criticised for limited local and NGO influence and for transparency |
| **EUDR / EUTR (EU Deforestation/Timber Regulation)** | Legality and deforestation-free due diligence for placing timber products on the EU market |
| **US Lacey Act** | Prohibits trade in illegally sourced plant and timber products |
| **CITES** | Restricts trade in listed species (several rosewoods/*Dalbergia*, ramin, afrormosia); **relevant to African timbers** — check before importing or exporting furniture containing them |
| **[ZA] [NA] Local** | South African plantation timber is largely FSC-certified; Namibian indigenous hardwoods (e.g. *Pterocarpus angolensis*/kiaat) require permits and provenance documentation — confirm with the Directorate of Forestry |

## 8. Warranty norms by category

| Category | Typical warranty | What is usually excluded |
|---|---|---|
| Sofa frame | 10 years–lifetime (structural) | Fabric, foam, normal softening |
| Foam / cushion interiors | 2–5 years | Loss of loft, fibre compaction |
| Upholstery fabric | 1–2 years, or none | Fading, pilling, abrasion in use, pet damage |
| Leather | 2–5 years | Natural marks, colour change, drying if not conditioned |
| Case goods / timber furniture | 2–10 years; some makers (e.g. Houtlander) offer a **lifetime structural guarantee** | Movement, checking, finish wear |
| Kitchen carcass | 5–25 years | Water damage, incorrect installation |
| Hinges and runners | Often "lifetime" from Blum/Hettich | Misuse, overloading |
| Worktops (quartz/sintered) | 10–25 years, registration usually required | Heat damage, chipping, staining from misuse, **outdoor use** |
| Appliances | 1–2 years standard, 5 years extended | Consumables, limescale, incorrect voltage |
| Taps and mixers | 5–15 years on the body; 1–5 on cartridges and finishes | Scale, incorrect pressure, aggressive cleaners |
| Sanitaryware ceramics | 5–25 years | Impact damage |
| LED luminaires | 3–5 years typical; some architectural to 5–10 | Driver failure outside Ta rating, surge damage |
| Flooring — engineered timber | 10–25 years domestic | Moisture, sunlight fading, inadequate expansion gaps |
| Flooring — LVT/SPC | 10–25 years domestic | Heat/UV distortion, indentation from point loads |
| Carpet | 5–15 years | Fading, crushing in traffic lanes, stains |
| Mattress | 5–10 years | Sagging below a stated depth (usually 20–30 mm), stains voiding the warranty |

> ⚠️ **Every warranty above is voided by the same three things**: incorrect installation, use outside the stated conditions (including outdoor use and ambient temperature), and cleaning with the wrong product. In a hot dry, high-UV climate, "outside the stated conditions" is easy to trip into — check whether the warranty even applies in direct sunlight.

## 9. What to put in a purchase order

A purchase order that gets you enforceable quality contains, per line item:

1. **Identification** — manufacturer, exact model/range name, product code, finish/colour code, size in mm.
2. **Materials** — carcass board type, thickness and emission class; frame timber and moisture content; foam density **and** hardness; fabric name, colour, batch/dye lot; metal grade and finish.
3. **Hardware** — brand and model of every hinge, runner, lift and lock; load class; soft-close specified.
4. **Standards compliance** — the standard **and level**: e.g. "seating to EN 16139 level L2", "storage to EN 14749", "board to E1 (EN 13986) minimum", "upholstery to EN 1021-1 and -2", "fabric ≥ 40,000 Martindale (ISO 12947), lightfastness ≥ 6 (ISO 105-B02)".
5. **Evidence required** — "test certificates for the exact cover/filling combination to be supplied before manufacture", "mill certificate for board emission class", "FSC CoC certificate number".
6. **Tolerances** — dimensional tolerance, panel gap and reveal tolerance (e.g. 2 mm ± 0.5), flatness.
7. **Finish** — system (e.g. two-pack polyurethane), sheen level (measured, e.g. 20 GU at 60°), number of coats, colour reference.
8. **Sample and approval regime** — control sample or prototype approved and signed before batch production; a retained approved sample held by both parties.
9. **Packing and delivery** — export packing, labelling per room, delivery window, who offloads, access constraints.
10. **Installation scope** — who levels, scribes, anchors (anti-tip!), connects, seals and snags.
11. **Spares** — a stated percentage of hardware, fabric (metres retained for repairs), touch-up finish, and LED drivers.
12. **Warranty terms** — duration, what is covered, response time, and **who performs the warranty work locally**.
13. **Payment terms tied to milestones** — deposit, sample approval, pre-shipment inspection, delivery, practical completion. **Never pay 100 % before delivery.**
14. **Remedies** — what happens if the goods fail inspection, and who pays return freight.

### Inspection checklist on delivery

- Count against the packing list before the truck leaves; photograph any damaged carton unopened.
- Check dimensions against the order, not against the drawing you remember.
- Check colour and batch consistency across all pieces **in daylight**.
- Operate every drawer, door, hinge and mechanism.
- Check panel gaps and reveals with a gauge.
- Check that anti-tip fixings, spares and warranty documents are present.
- Sign delivery notes "**received unexamined**" if you cannot inspect immediately — an unqualified signature can waive claims.

## Open questions

- **EN and BIFMA standard scopes are cited from general knowledge, not read from the standards themselves.** The BIFMA standards page returned HTTP 403 and no EN standard text was accessible. Verify every standard number and its current edition before writing it into a contract. `needs-verification`.
- **Formaldehyde ppm limits** for TSCA Title VI / CARB Phase 2 (per panel type) were not obtained — the EPA overview page does not state them. They are in **40 CFR Part 770**.
- **SANS standards for furniture, storage and mattresses in South Africa were not identified by number.** This is a significant gap for **[ZA] [NA]** work; obtain the SABS catalogue listing.
- **EN 1725, EN 15939 and the current status of superseded seating/storage standards** need confirmation against the current CEN catalogue.
- Warranty norms in section 8 are compiled industry practice, not surveyed data; only Houtlander's lifetime structural guarantee was verified from a primary source.
- The relationship between "E0" and any harmonised European class is genuinely ambiguous in the market — treat "E0" claims with suspicion unless a test standard and method are named.
