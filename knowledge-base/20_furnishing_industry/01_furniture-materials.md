# Furniture materials — timber, board, metal, polymer, glass, stone, fibre and leather

**Summary.** Every furniture material is a compromise between stiffness, mass, workability, moisture response, surface durability and cost. In a hot dry climate — Windhoek runs low relative humidity for most of the year with large diurnal swings — the dominant failure mode is **moisture-driven movement and adhesive failure**, not wear. Solid timber shrinks and checks, veneers lift, hide glue and PVA creep, leather dries and cracks, and UV through large glazed openings bleaches and embrittles polymers and dyes. This file gives the material families, the grades within each, and a hot-dry-climate verdict for each.

## Key facts

- Quartz engineered stone is typically **90–93 % silica by weight in 7–10 % unsaturated polyester resin**; standard slab about **3040 × 1440 mm**, thicknesses **12, 20, 30 mm**. It has low water absorption and good chemical durability but is **less heat resistant than natural stone and UV-sensitive — not for outdoor use**.
- **Australia banned engineered stone from 1 July 2024** over silicosis; California had ~500 diagnosed cases as of January 2026. This is a fabricator health issue, not a homeowner one, but it affects supply and pricing.
- Porcelain is defined by **water absorption < 0.5 %** (ISO 13006 / BS EN 14411); large-format porcelain slabs reach **1600 × 3200 mm**, specialists to **1840 × 3300 mm** — the same material family used for sintered-stone worktops.
- Leather grades in descending order: **full-grain → top-grain → corrected grain → split → bonded**. Bonded leather is shredded scrap bound with polyurethane or latex onto a fibre mesh, **10–90 % leather content**.
- Chrome tanning takes **less than a day** and yields "wet blue" containing **4–5 % chromium**; vegetable tanning takes **several weeks** in vats of increasing tannin concentration and yields a stiffer leather.
- Flexible polyurethane foam is **67 % of all polyurethane produced (2016)** — the single largest polyurethane application, and the heart of the seating industry.

## 1. Solid timber and engineered board

Full treatment is in domain 06 — see `06_joinery_and_woodwork/01_wood-science.md`, `02_timber-species-southern-africa.md` and `03_sheet-goods-and-boards.md`. The furniture-specific summary:

| Material | Typical use in furniture | Movement risk | Hot-dry verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solid hardwood (oak, ash, walnut, kiaat, imbuia) | Frames, legs, table tops, chair parts | High across grain | Excellent **if** properly dried to local EMC (roughly 8–10 % MC inland) and detailed to allow movement (buttons, slotted holes, breadboard ends) |
| Solid softwood (pine, saligna) | Budget frames, painted furniture | High; also resin bleed | Acceptable painted; poor for wide unrestrained tops |
| Plywood (birch, marine, bending ply) | Seat shells, frames, drawer boxes, curved work | Low | Very good — dimensionally the most stable board |
| MDF | Painted doors, curved panels, substrate for veneer/acrylic | Low, but swells irreversibly if wetted | Good indoors; never in wet areas without MR/MRMDF |
| Chipboard/particleboard (melamine faced) | Carcasses, budget case goods | Low, but poor screw-holding and edge integrity | Adequate for carcasses; fails at edges and fixings if dampened |
| Blockboard / laminboard | Long shelves, door cores | Low | Good; stiffer per kg than MDF |
| Veneer on stable core | Any figured or expensive timber look | Low if balanced | Best practice — but **must be balanced (backing veneer)** or it will cup in dry heat |

> ⚠️ In a hot dry interior, the failure is nearly always **an unbalanced panel, a fixed-width solid top screwed rigidly to a frame, or a veneer over an unstable core**. Detail for movement or expect splits within two dry seasons.

## 2. Metals

| Metal | Common forms | Finish options | Notes and hot-dry verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild steel tube | 25 mm and 19 mm round, 25 × 25 square; wall 1.2–2.0 mm | Powder coat, electroplate (chrome, nickel), painted, blackened | The workhorse of chair frames since Thonet's tubular era. Powder coat is fine indoors; **chrome fails fast in coastal air (Swakopmund, Walvis Bay)** |
| Stainless steel | 304 (indoor/general), 316 (marine/coastal) | Brushed, satin, mirror, bead-blast, PVD | 316 is the correct grade anywhere near the coast; 304 will tea-stain |
| Aluminium | Extrusion (profiles, wardrobe frames), die-cast (luminaire bodies, chair bases), sheet | Anodised, powder coat | Light, corrosion-resistant, recyclable; anodised finishes are extremely durable in UV. Rimadesio builds its whole sliding-door and wardrobe system on aluminium and glass |
| Brass / bronze | Solid bar and sheet, cast fittings, tube | Polished lacquered, unlacquered (living finish), antique, brushed | Unlacquered brass patinates fast in dry heat and handling oils. Specify explicitly which you want |
| Cast iron | Table bases, stove parts | Enamel, paint | Heavy, stable, cheap in southern Africa; good for outdoor table bases |
| Wire (welded steel rod) | Wire shelving (Elfa-type), chair shells | Epoxy, zinc + epoxy | Cheap, ventilated, light; edges wear and rust once coating chips |

**Coastal rule of thumb [NA]:** within roughly 5 km of the Atlantic coast, specify 316 stainless, marine-grade anodised aluminium, or powder coat over correctly pretreated substrate. Chrome plating and plain mild steel are a warranty claim waiting to happen.

## 3. Plastics and polymers

| Polymer | Typical furniture use | Key properties | UV / heat behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Polypropylene (PP)** | Injection-moulded chair shells (Kartell, Vitra, Magis, most stacking chairs) | Tough, chemical-resistant, cheap, recyclable, matt finish | Needs UV stabiliser for outdoors; unstabilised PP chalks and embrittles quickly in high-UV Namibian sun |
| **Polycarbonate (PC)** | Transparent chairs (Kartell Louis Ghost), lenses, diffusers | Very high impact strength, optically clear | Yellows and crazes under prolonged UV unless UV-grade; scratches easily |
| **ABS** | Appliance housings, drawer components, TV/media units | Rigid, good surface finish, paintable | Poor UV resistance; indoor only |
| **PMMA / acrylic** | Kitchen door faces (high-gloss acrylic), light diffusers, table tops | Excellent optical clarity, better UV stability than PC | Scratches; thermal expansion ~7× that of steel — allow for movement in long runs |
| **Polyethylene (LLDPE/HDPE), rotomoulded** | Outdoor furniture, planters, illuminated furniture | Seamless hollow parts, impact-resistant, no coating to fail | With UV stabilisers, the best outdoor plastic; colours still fade over years |
| **Nylon (PA), glass-filled** | Chair mechanisms, castors, connectors | High strength, good fatigue life | Absorbs moisture (less of a problem in dry climates) |
| **PU / PVC "faux leather"** | Budget upholstery | Cheap, wipeable | **PU coatings hydrolyse and flake, typically 3–7 years**; the commonest complaint in budget seating |

## 4. Glass and stone

### Glass
Types, coatings and safety standards are covered in domain 08 (`08_glass_and_facades`). For furniture:
- **Toughened (tempered)** — mandatory for table tops, shelves in reach, glass doors. Typically 8–12 mm for tops, 6–8 mm for shelves depending on span.
- **Laminated** — where the glass must remain in place after breakage; also cuts UV transmission.
- **Back-painted (lacobel-type)** — splashbacks and cabinet doors; verify the paint's UV stability, as direct sun can shift colour.
- **Acid-etched / satin** — fingerprint-prone in a "reverse" way; shows oils.
- **Low-iron (extra-clear)** — removes the green edge; specify for clear glass tops and shelves.

### Natural stone
| Stone | Character | Worktop suitability |
|---|---|---|
| Granite | Hard, low porosity, heat-tolerant | Excellent; the dependable choice |
| Marble | Calcite — etches with acid, stains | Beautiful, high-maintenance; expect patina |
| Quartzite (true) | Very hard silicate | Excellent, expensive |
| Limestone / travertine | Soft, porous | Floors and walls, not kitchen worktops |
| Onyx | Translucent, fragile | Backlit feature panels only |
| Soapstone | Soft, non-porous, heat-proof | Niche; scratches take on patina |

### Engineered surfaces (the worktop decision)

| Product | Type | Composition / process | Key strengths | Key limits |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Caesarstone** (Israel) | Quartz composite | ~90–93 % quartz in polyester resin | Consistent, non-porous, wide colour range | Not heat-proof; not UV-stable; visible seams |
| **Silestone** (Cosentino, Spain) | Quartz composite | As above; newer low-silica "HybriQ" bodies exist | As above, plus large brand support | As above |
| **Dekton** (Cosentino, Spain) | Sintered stone | Sintered mineral particles, ultra-compact | Extremely hard, heat- and UV-resistant, outdoor-capable, thin slabs | Brittle at edges during fabrication; specialist fabricator required |
| **Neolith** (Spain) | Sintered stone | Sintered porcelain-type body | As Dekton; large formats | As Dekton |
| **Laminam, Florim, Atlas Plan** | Porcelain slab | Large-format porcelain to 1600 × 3200 mm | Thin, light, printable surfaces, outdoor-safe | Edge detailing and support are critical |
| **Corian / solid surface** | Acrylic-mineral | ATH filler in acrylic resin | Seamless joints, thermoformable, repairable by sanding | Scratches and heat-marks; matt only |

> ⚠️ **Silicosis.** Dry cutting of quartz composite generates respirable crystalline silica. Australia banned engineered stone from 1 July 2024. Specify wet cutting and extraction, and require the fabricator's occupational health compliance in the purchase order. Sintered stone and porcelain carry similar dust hazards.

**Hot-dry-climate note:** for any worktop or table that sees direct sun — an island under a rooflight, an outdoor kitchen — quartz composite is the wrong choice (resin yellows and can distort). Use sintered stone, porcelain, granite or concrete.

## 5. Rattan, cane, bamboo and natural fibres

| Material | What it is | Uses | Behaviour in dry heat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rattan (pole)** | Solid climbing palm stem, steam-bent | Chair frames, bedheads | Shrinks and loosens at bindings; joints creak and eventually fail |
| **Cane (rattan skin/peel)** | Outer bark, woven into webbing sheets | Chair seats, cabinet door panels | Needs occasional humidification; brittle when bone dry — re-caning is routine maintenance |
| **Wicker** | A weave, not a material (may be rattan, willow, paper cord, PE) | Outdoor and conservatory furniture | Natural fibres degrade in UV; **PE (synthetic) wicker is the correct outdoor spec** |
| **Bamboo** | Grass; strand-woven or laminated into board | Flooring, panels, some furniture | Strand-woven bamboo is very hard and dimensionally reasonable; laminated bamboo boards move like timber |
| **Paper cord** | Twisted paper, waxed or unwaxed | Danish chair seats (Wegner CH24) | Very stable, long-lived indoors; stains and does not like water |
| **Seagrass, abaca, jute, sisal** | Woven fibre | Rugs, seat weaving | Jute and sisal become brittle and shed in very dry air; wool blends behave better |
| **Water hyacinth / kubu** | Woven dried stems | Baskets, light furniture | Cheap, short-lived, prone to insect attack |

## 6. Leather

### Grades

| Grade | What it is | Look and feel | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Full grain** | Entire grain layer intact, no sanding | Natural marks visible, develops patina | Highest; the top layer is the densest fibre |
| **Top grain** | Outer hide layer, may be lightly corrected | Uniform, fine fibre | High |
| **Corrected grain** | Buffed/sanded, then dyed and embossed with an artificial grain | Very uniform; plastic feel at the extreme | Good surface durability, less breathable |
| **Split** | Layer below the grain, after splitting | Suede/nubuck-like or coated | Weaker; often sold as "genuine leather" |
| **Bonded** | Shredded scrap + PU/latex on a fibre mesh, 10–90 % leather | Uniform, cheap | Poor — delaminates and flakes, especially in dry heat |
| **Nubuck** | Top-grain sanded on the grain side to a short nap | Velvety | Good but marks and soils easily |
| **Suede** | Split, napped on the flesh side | Soft nap | Low soil resistance |

### Finishes
- **Aniline** — dyed through with soluble dyes, no pigment topcoat. Most natural, most breathable, **least protected**: scratches, sun-fades, absorbs oils. In a sunny, dry room, aniline will change colour visibly within a year.
- **Semi-aniline** — aniline dyed plus a light pigment/protective coat. The usual best compromise for family use.
- **Pigmented (protected)** — opaque pigment layer plus clear topcoat. Most durable and colour-stable, least natural feel. The correct choice for hard-worn, sunlit, dusty environments.
- **Pull-up / waxed** — oils and waxes migrate under stress to lighten the surface; heavy character, will not stay pristine.

### Tanning
Beamhouse operations (soaking, liming, unhairing, scudding, fleshing, deliming, bating, pickling) prepare the collagen. Then:
- **Chrome tanning** — chromium(III) sulfate, crosslinks collagen, completed in under a day, produces greyish-blue "wet blue"; 4–5 % chromium in the finished leather. Soft, stretchy, the standard for upholstery.
- **Vegetable tanning** — natural tannins from oak, chestnut, quebracho bark; hides stretched on frames and immersed for weeks in progressively stronger tannin liquors; stiffer, warmer, ages beautifully; used for saddlery, some furniture, and belting leather.
- **Crusting and finishing** — waxing, rolling, oiling, splitting, shaving, dyeing, embossing, plating.

**Hot-dry care rule:** leather in a dry climate needs periodic conditioning (a light, non-silicone hide food two to four times a year) and must be kept out of direct sun. Untreated aniline in a north-facing Namibian living room will crack at the cushion folds within a few years.

## 7. Selection matrix for a hot, dry, high-UV interior

| Requirement | Choose | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Table top, sunlit | Sintered stone, porcelain, solid timber with movement detailing, powder-coated steel | Quartz composite, veneered MDF without balance, high-gloss acrylic |
| Outdoor seating | Powder-coated aluminium, teak, rotomoulded PE, PE wicker, 316 stainless | Rattan/cane, mild steel, unstabilised PP |
| Upholstery cover, sunlit | Solution-dyed acrylic (Sunbrella-type), pigmented leather, high-lightfastness wool blends | Aniline leather, undyed silk, cheap viscose blends |
| Cabinet doors | Painted MDF (2-pack), foil-free melamine, veneer with balanced backing | Thermofoil/PVC wrap near ovens; unbalanced veneer |
| Wet areas | MR MDF, marine ply, aluminium, stainless, porcelain | Standard chipboard, unsealed MDF |
| Fixings into board | Confirmat screws into chipboard, threaded inserts, dowel + glue in ply | Coarse woodscrews into MDF edges |
