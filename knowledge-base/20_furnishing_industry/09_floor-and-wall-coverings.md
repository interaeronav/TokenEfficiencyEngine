# Floor and wall coverings — timber, resilient, tile, carpet, resin and wall finishes

**Summary.** Floors are the largest single surface in a house and the one most punished by climate. In a hot dry interior with large diurnal swings, **solid timber floors shrink and gap, laminate cores dry and click-joints loosen, and vinyl expands and contracts against fixed perimeters**. Tile is dimensionally indifferent to all of this, which is why porcelain dominates southern African housing. Walls are the opposite problem: they are cheap to change, and are where texture and acoustics are won or lost.

## Key facts

- **Porcelain tile = water absorption < 0.5 %** (ISO 13006 / BS EN 14411). Wear grades run **0–5** per ISO 10545-7 / ASTM C1027. Large-format slabs reach **1600 × 3200 mm**, specialists **1840 × 3300 mm**.
- **Laminate flooring** is a multi-layer product fused by lamination: melamine-resin and fibreboard core, photographic decor layer, protective wear layer; **EN 13329** specifies requirements and test methods. Glueless click locking was invented by **Välinge (1996)** and **Unilin (1997)**.
- Polished (unglazed) porcelain is mechanically ground, which **opens microscopic pores and increases staining risk unless sealed** — a real issue with dusty, mineral-rich water.
- **Italtile** describes itself as South Africa's online home-style retailer with 50 years of experience, selling tiles plus bathroom and kitchen products; brands seen on the site include **Ceramica Sant'Agostino, Atlas Concorde and Almar**, with a showroom locator, nationwide delivery and installation in Johannesburg, Pretoria and Cape Town, and pricing in ZAR and BWP.

## 1. Timber floors

| Type | Construction | Stability | Refinishable | Hot-dry verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Solid hardwood** | 15–22 mm solid boards, T&G, nailed or glued | Lowest — moves with humidity | 4–8 times | **Risky**: expect winter gapping. Narrow boards (≤ 90 mm), acclimatisation and a controlled interior help |
| **Engineered wood** | 2.5–6 mm wear layer over a plywood or HDF core | Good — the cross-laminated core resists movement | 1–3 times (depends on wear layer) | **The correct timber choice for Namibia**; specify a ≥ 3 mm wear layer if you want to sand it later |
| **Parquet / block** | Small blocks (mosaic, herringbone, Versailles panels) laid in mastic | Good — small units move little individually | Yes | Historically the most successful timber floor in dry climates |
| **Solid strip over battens** | Traditional sprung floor | Movement absorbed at perimeters | Yes | Good but expensive |
| **Bamboo (strand-woven)** | Shredded bamboo compressed with resin | Very hard (higher Janka than most oak) | Limited | Good hardness; some products move noticeably with humidity |
| **Cork** | Cork granules bonded, in tile or click-plank form | Good | Sandable if solid | Warm, quiet, resilient, forgiving underfoot; UV-fades and dents |

**Timber floor specification points:** acclimatise on site for at least a week; leave a **10–12 mm expansion gap at every perimeter and every doorway** and never trap the floor under a fixed heavy object; specify finish type (**oil = repairable in patches, matt; lacquer/UV-cured = harder, but repairs mean sanding the whole board run**); confirm underfloor-heating compatibility if relevant; specify moisture content on delivery.

## 2. Resilient floors — laminate, LVT and SPC

| Product | Core | Wear layer | Water | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Laminate** | HDF/MDF with melamine resin | Melamine overlay with aluminium oxide, graded **AC1–AC6** (EN 13329) | **Weak** — a swollen edge is permanent | Cheap, hard-wearing surface, hollow-sounding without underlay. AC3 domestic heavy / AC4 commercial |
| **LVT (luxury vinyl tile), flexible** | PVC with plasticiser, glue-down or click | PU-coated wear layer, **0.3–0.7 mm** | Waterproof | Quiet, warm, thin (2–5 mm); **glue-down is far more stable in heat than loose-lay/click** |
| **SPC (stone plastic composite)** | Rigid limestone-powder/PVC core | 0.3–0.55 mm wear layer, often with attached IXPE underlay | Waterproof | **The most dimensionally stable resilient floor in a hot climate** — the rigid core resists thermal expansion; the usual right answer for Namibian retrofits |
| **WPC (wood plastic composite)** | Foamed PVC/wood-flour core | As above | Waterproof | Softer, warmer, slightly less stable than SPC |
| **Linoleum (true, e.g. Forbo Marmoleum)** | Linseed oil, wood flour, jute backing | Natural, needs a finish | Damp-sensitive at joints | Genuinely natural, antistatic, very durable; ambers if kept dark |
| **Rubber (Nora, Artigo)** | Vulcanised rubber | Integral | Waterproof | Contract/gym; excellent underfoot |
| **Sheet vinyl** | PVC with a backing | Wear layer | Waterproof | Cheapest; welded seams for wet areas |

> ⚠️ **Thermal expansion is the failure mode.** Vinyl and SPC planks in direct sun through a large window can grow several millimetres per metre. Specify expansion gaps at every perimeter, follow the manufacturer's maximum unbroken run, keep floors out of un-shaded direct sun where possible, and **glue down rather than float** where sun exposure is unavoidable.

## 3. Tile and stone

### Types

| Type | Body | Absorption | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Porcelain, glazed** | Vitrified body, glazed face | < 0.5 % | The workhorse — floors and walls, indoor and outdoor |
| **Porcelain, full-body / through-body** | Metallic-oxide pigments through the section | < 0.5 % | Heavy-duty; chips are less visible |
| **Porcelain, polished** | Mechanically ground | < 0.5 % | High shine; **needs sealing**, slippery when wet |
| **Ceramic (monocottura/bicottura)** | Higher-porosity body | 3–10 % | Wall tiles, light-duty floors |
| **Terracotta / quarry** | Fired clay | High | Rustic; needs sealing and maintenance |
| **Natural stone (granite, marble, travertine, slate, sandstone)** | — | Varies | Beautiful; sealing, acid sensitivity and slip performance all need checking |
| **Terrazzo (cast or tile)** | Aggregate in cement or resin | Low | Durable, seamless in poured form |
| **Large-format slab (Laminam, Atlas Plan, Florim slabs)** | Porcelain | < 0.5 % | Walls, floors, worktops, shower linings with minimal joints |

**Specification points:** slip resistance (**R-value R9–R13 per DIN 51130** or pendulum test values; specify **R11/R12 for wet external areas and shower floors**), rectified vs. pressed edges (rectified = ground to exact size, allows 2–3 mm joints; pressed = 3–5 mm joints), tone/shade variation grade (V1 uniform to V4 substantial), thickness (9–11 mm floor, 20 mm for external pedestal paving), and **adhesive class (C2TE S1 for large formats and external work)**.

> ⚠️ **Large-format tiles need a flat substrate and full-bed coverage.** A 1200 × 600 tile over a 5 mm hollow will crack. Specify **≥ 95 % adhesive coverage** for floors and external work, back-buttering, and levelling clips. Movement joints are required at perimeters, at changes of substrate, and typically every 4–6 m internally / 3–4 m externally — this matters more in a climate with large temperature swings.

## 4. Carpet and rugs

| Construction | How it is made | Character | Durability |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tufted** | Yarn punched through a primary backing, latex-locked, secondary backing added | The volume standard — cut pile, loop pile, cut-and-loop | Good; depends on density and twist |
| **Woven (Axminster)** | Pile and backing woven together; each tuft individually inserted from a limited colour frame | Patterned hotel/contract carpet | Very high |
| **Woven (Wilton)** | Continuous yarn carried through the backing | Dense, fewer colours, high quality | Very high |
| **Needlepunch / bonded** | Fibres mechanically entangled | Entrance and utility | Utility only |
| **Hand-knotted (Persian, Tibetan, Nepalese)** | Individual knots tied on a warp | Rugs — knot count drives price | Generational |
| **Hand-tufted** | Tufting gun into a canvas, latexed, backed | Rugs at a fraction of hand-knotted cost | Sheds; 10–20 year life |
| **Flatweave (kilim, dhurrie, sisal, jute)** | No pile | Textural, reversible in some | Moderate |

**Fibre comparison for carpet:**

| Fibre | Resilience | Soil/stain | Fade | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wool** | Excellent — crush recovery is unmatched | Naturally soil-shedding; stains if untreated | Good | High |
| **Wool/nylon 80/20** | Excellent | Good | Good | High |
| **Nylon (PA 6, 6.6)** | Excellent | Good with treatment | Fades unless solution-dyed | Mid |
| **Polypropylene (olefin)** | Poor — flattens | Excellent stain resistance, bleach-cleanable | Excellent (solution-dyed) | Low |
| **Polyester (PET)** | Fair | Good | Good | Low–mid |
| **Sisal/jute/seagrass** | Fair | Poor — stains permanently | Fades | Mid |

**Specification points:** pile weight (g/m²), pile height, density (the honest quality indicator — bend the sample back; visible backing means low density), backing type, underlay (a good underlay extends carpet life materially), and fire rating. **[NA] Dust note:** cut-pile carpet in a dusty region needs serious vacuuming; loop pile and flatweaves shed dust more readily but trap grit that abrades the fibre.

## 5. Resin and screed floors

| System | Composition | Thickness | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Epoxy resin** | 2-part epoxy, self-levelling or trowelled | 2–5 mm | Hard, chemical-resistant; **yellows under UV** — not for sunlit rooms |
| **Polyurethane resin** | 2-part PU | 2–6 mm | More flexible, UV-stable, better thermal shock resistance |
| **PMMA / methacrylate** | Fast-cure acrylic | 3–6 mm | Cures in hours, strong odour during installation |
| **Polished concrete** | Ground and densified structural slab | — | Requires the slab to be planned for it (aggregate, joints, no service chases) |
| **Power-float / burnished screed** | Cementitious screed | 50–75 mm | Cheap, industrial; **will craze and crack** — accept it as the aesthetic or don't use it |
| **Microcement / micro-topping** | Polymer-modified cement, 2–3 mm in layers | 2–3 mm | Seamless over existing floors and walls; **hairline cracking follows substrate movement** — insist on a decoupling membrane and a competent applicator |

## 6. Wall coverings

### Wallpaper

| Type | Construction | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Non-woven** | Cellulose/polyester fleece; paste-the-wall | The modern standard; dimensionally stable, strippable dry |
| **Paper** | Traditional | Cheap, fragile when wet |
| **Vinyl (surface-printed, blown, heavy contract)** | PVC on a paper/fleece backing | Durable, wipeable; contract grades to **EN 233/259** classes |
| **Textile / grasscloth / silk** | Natural fibres on a backing | Beautiful, no repeat match, **fades badly in sun** and shows seams |
| **Mural / digital** | Printed to a room-specific layout | Needs accurate site dimensions before printing |
| **Metallic / mica** | Reflective particles | Shows every substrate imperfection |

Major houses: **Sanderson Design Group (Morris & Co, Zoffany, Harlequin, Scion), Cole & Son, Farrow & Ball, Designers Guild, Osborne & Little, Élitis, Arte, Omexco, Phillip Jeffries (textile/grasscloth), Vescom (contract)**. In South Africa these are distributed by the fabric houses covered in `03_textiles-and-soft-furnishing.md` — Hertex, St Leger & Viney, Home Fabrics, Mavromac.

**[NA] Hot dry climate wallpaper note:** low humidity means adhesive dries very fast — a good hanger will wet-brush the substrate; plaster must be sealed and fully cured; and seams open if the substrate moves. Textile wallcoverings in a sunlit room will fade unevenly within a few years.

### Other wall finishes

| Finish | Description | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Panelling (timber, MDF)** | Applied frame-and-panel, shiplap, slat, fluted | **Must be detailed for movement** in solid timber; MDF panelling is the stable option; specify the paint system |
| **Acoustic slat panels** | Timber slats on a felt (often PET) backing | Cheap acoustic improvement and strong visual texture; check fire performance |
| **Acoustic textile panels** | Fabric-wrapped mineral wool or PET; Kvadrat supplies acoustic panels alongside its textiles | Genuine absorption; specify NRC/αw and fire class |
| **Microcement** | See above | Seamless walls and shower linings |
| **Tadelakt** | Moroccan lime plaster burnished with a stone and treated with olive-oil soap | Genuinely waterproof when correctly executed — **entirely dependent on the applicator's skill**; hairline crazing is inherent |
| **Venetian / polished plaster (marmorino, stucco veneziano)** | Lime and marble dust in layers, burnished | Depth and lustre; repairable by a specialist only |
| **Clay and lime plasters** | Breathable mineral finishes | Good with earth and masonry construction |
| **Cement/lime render, bagged brick, textured paint** | Site finishes | See `02_building_construction/07_finishes-plaster-screed-paint.md` |
| **Stone cladding / brick slips** | Thin veneer | Weight and fixing method matter; avoid "stick-on stone" without a mechanical fixing on tall areas |
| **Wall tiles and large-format slabs** | See section 3 | Slabs give near-seamless shower walls |

## 7. Selection guidance for a Namibian house

| Location | First choice | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Living/dining, sunlit | Porcelain (large format), polished concrete, engineered oak | Solid timber over 120 mm wide, floating LVT in direct sun |
| Bedrooms | Engineered timber, wool carpet, SPC | Solid timber if unconditioned |
| Bathrooms/wet | Porcelain, large-format slab, microcement (skilled applicator), tadelakt (specialist) | Laminate, natural limestone, unsealed polished porcelain |
| Kitchen floor | Porcelain, SPC, polished concrete | Laminate |
| Stoep / outdoor | 20 mm porcelain pavers on pedestals, R11+ slip, natural stone | Polished anything; timber decking unless very well specified |
| Walls, general | Painted plaster + one textured feature (slat, plaster, paper) | Grasscloth in direct sun |
| Acoustics | Acoustic slat or fabric panels, heavy curtains, rugs | Nothing — hard-surfaced dry-climate houses are acoustically brutal |
