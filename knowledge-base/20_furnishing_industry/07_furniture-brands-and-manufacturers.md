# Furniture brands and manufacturers — the significant makers by tier

**Summary.** Roughly forty companies define the vocabulary of contemporary furniture. Italy supplies the upholstery and the systems, Denmark and Switzerland the chairs and the archive, the United States the office and the ergonomic research, Sweden the volume, and — increasingly — southern Africa a serious design-maker scene that exports. This file is a register: who, where, what, signature pieces, tier, and whether you can get it in southern Africa.

## Key facts

- **Vitra** (Birsfelden, Switzerland, founded 1950 by Willi and Erika Fehlbaum) acquired manufacturing rights to Eames, Nelson and Panton designs after a 1953 New York visit; after the **1984 separation from Herman Miller** it holds European and Middle Eastern rights. It launched the Home Collection in 2004 and produced the first plastic cantilever chair — the **Panton Chair, 1967**.
- **Herman Miller** (US, founded 1905 as Star Furniture, renamed 1923) acquired **Knoll for $1.8 bn**, closing **19 July 2021** and rebranding as **MillerKnoll**. Signature: Eames Lounge Chair (1956), Noguchi table (1948), **Aeron (1987, Stumpf and Chadwick)**.
- **B&B Italia** (founded 1966 by Piero Ambrogio Busnelli) has produced **more than 1,000 designs**, reinvests **>3 % of sales in R&D**, and built a **€4 m in-house foaming department in 2010**. Now in the **Flos B&B Italia Group**, jointly owned by Investindustrial and Carlyle.
- **Carl Hansen & Søn** (Odense, Denmark, founded **28 October 1908**): the **CH24 Wishbone / Y Chair** accounts for **more than half of sales**; exports rose from 20 % (1998) to over 50 % (2008).
- **IKEA**: 504 stores in 60 countries (April 2025), **€45.1 bn of goods sold in FY2024**, and **no store in sub-Saharan Africa**.

Contract specialists differ from residential brands in three concrete ways: they publish **EN 16139 / BIFMA test evidence**, they hold **replacement parts and cover fabrics** for years, and they price in **batch quantities** with FF&E documentation.

## 6. The South African design scene

This is the most under-appreciated part of the regional market: several studios are internationally exhibited, export to Europe and the US, and — critically for Namibia — sit **inside SACU**, so there is no import duty and freight is a road trip rather than a container.

**[NA] Namibian makers.** No Namibian furniture brand of national or international profile could be verified in this research pass. What Namibia has, verifiably in practice rather than from a fetched source, is: joinery workshops in Windhoek, Swakopmund and Okahandja producing bespoke cabinetry and solid-timber furniture; steel fabricators capable of frames and bases; upholsterers who re-cover and build to order; and craft/woodcarving traditions (Okahandja market) that are craft rather than furniture manufacture. **This is a genuine gap in the knowledge base — see Open questions.**

## 7. Tier and availability summary

| Tier | Realistic route into Namibia | Typical lead time | Duty position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Italian/Nordic luxury | SA agent order or direct EU purchase + sea freight | 14–20 weeks + 4–8 weeks freight | Import duty + VAT on entry to SACU (usually cleared in SA) |
| Contract/hospitality | SA contract manufacturers or European contract brands via SA | 8–16 weeks | As above |
| Mid-market design | SA design retailers (Weylandts, Créma, Life etc.) | Stock–8 weeks | **None between SA and NA (SACU)** |
| Budget | SA volume retail chains | Stock | None (SACU) |
| SA design-maker | Direct from the studio, road freight to Windhoek | 6–12 weeks | **None (SACU)** — the best value/quality route |
| Local bespoke | Namibian joiners and upholsterers | 4–10 weeks | None |

> ⚠️ **The single most useful commercial fact in this file:** because Namibia and South Africa are in the same customs union, buying from Houtlander, Pedersen + Lennard, Meyer von Wielligh or an SA contract manufacturer avoids import duty entirely and replaces 6–10 weeks of sea freight with a 2–4 day road trip. For most houses this beats importing European mid-market furniture on both price and lead time.
