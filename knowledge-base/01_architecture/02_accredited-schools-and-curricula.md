# Accredited architecture schools and model curriculum

**Summary.** SACAP has validated **11 Architectural Learning Sites (ALSs)** in South Africa; NUST is the sole provider of the full professional architecture stream in Namibia, validated jointly by NCAQS, SACAP and the Commonwealth Association of Architects. The dominant Southern African structure is **3 + 1 + 1/2** — a three-year first degree, a one-year Honours, and a one- or two-year professional Master's — with each rung mapping to a different SACAP registration category. This file lists the institutions and programme names, then distils a model curriculum with what each year of study typically demands.

## Key facts

- **11** SACAP-validated Architectural Learning Sites (10 public universities + STADIO; Inscape holds unconditional validation for a Higher Certificate at NQF 5).
- Only the **MArch (Professional) / NQF 9** stream leads to **Professional Architect**; a three-year first degree alone leads to **Candidate Architectural Technologist**.
- **[NA]** NUST's BArch, BArch Hons and MArch all hold **unconditional validation** — the highest status obtainable. BArch and Hons validated 2016/17; MArch panel visited September 2022 and recommended unconditional validation.
- **[ZA]** Entry is competitive and two-stage almost everywhere: academic points **plus** a portfolio, drawing/aptitude test, and/or interview.

## [NA] Namibia

| Institution | Programme | Code | NQF | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Namibia University of Science and Technology (NUST), Faculty of Engineering and the Built Environment, Dept of Architecture, Planning and Construction | Bachelor of Architecture | 07BARC | 7 | Full-time. On completion: register with NCAQS as **Candidate Architectural Technologist** |
| NUST | Bachelor of Architecture Honours | — | 8 | 480 cumulative credits |
| NUST | Master of Architecture | 09MARC | 9 | Entry: BArch Hons or equivalent NQF 8 / 480 credits + portfolio + interview with the Postgraduate Selection Committee |

**NUST admission (BArch), three stages:**
1. **Academic merit** — English and Mathematics: minimum **12 points** on the NUST evaluation scale (combination of NSSCH/NSSCO, with no grade below **C** on NSSCO). Three further subjects from Technical Drawing, Physical Science, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, Geography, Fine Art, Arts and Crafts, Literature, Economics: minimum **18 points**.
2. **Selection test** — general knowledge, technical and scientific principles, freehand and technical drawing, English language.
3. **Selection interview** — shortlisted candidates only.

**Accreditation.** Bachelor, Honours and Master validated by a joint panel of **NCAQS + SACAP + CAA**. The CAA element gives the qualification Commonwealth-wide currency. No expiry date is stated on NUST's announcement; accreditation cycles are typically 5 years and should be re-checked.

> ⚠️ Choose the institution **after** deciding the target SACAP registration category. A diploma or three-year degree does not become a Professional Architect qualification retrospectively; the bridge back into the Master's stream can cost two or more additional years.

## Model curriculum

SACAP assesses candidates against **10 outcome fields** at four ascending levels (Awareness → Knowledge → Understanding → Ability). A well-formed curriculum is those ten fields distributed across the years, with **design studio as the integrating spine** — typically 40–50% of credit weight in every year.

| Strand | SACAP outcome field | Typical share of credits |
|---|---|---|
| Design studio | Architectural design; urban relationship | 40–50% |
| History and theory | Architectural history & theory | 10–15% |
| Construction technology | Construction technology | 10–15% |
| Structures | The structure of buildings | 5–10% |
| Environmental design / building science | Environmental relationship | 5–10% |
| Building services | Building services & related technologies | 5% |
| Professional practice and law | Office practice, legal aspects and ethics; contract documentation and administration | 5–10% (concentrated in final years) |
| Digital tools and representation | Computer applications | 5–10% |

### Year-by-year demands

**Year 1 — Fluency in representation and elementary space.**
Studio: abstract spatial exercises, a small single-cell building (pavilion, studio, kiosk), typically 20–80 m². Hand drawing to scale; orthographic projection; sectional thinking; physical model-making at 1:100 and 1:50. Construction: basic material families, brick coursing, simple pitched and flat roofs. History: survey from antiquity to the industrial revolution. Structures: statics, equilibrium, simple beam behaviour. Digital: 2D CAD, basic 3D. *Failure mode:* treating plan as a diagram and never cutting a section.

**Year 2 — The building as a system.**
Studio: a multi-room, multi-user building of 300–1,500 m² — a clinic, a library, a small school. Circulation, servicing and structure must now coordinate. Construction: full wall–floor–roof assemblies; damp-proofing; junctions at 1:20 and 1:5. Environmental: solar geometry, orientation, shading, natural ventilation. History: 20th-century modernism and its critiques; regional and African architectures. Structures: load paths, preliminary sizing, foundations. Digital: BIM introduced (Revit/ArchiCAD), plus SketchUp/Rhino for form-finding. *Failure mode:* a plan that resolves beautifully and a section that cannot be built.

**Year 3 — Complexity, context and technical resolution.**
Studio: an urban site with real constraints — zoning, boundaries, heritage, topography; 1,500–5,000 m². A resolved technical package is expected: at least one bay drawn from foundation to parapet. Construction: long-span structures, curtain walling, waterproofing strategy, fire compartmentation. Professional practice introduced: the Act, categories, the Code of Conduct. Regulatory: **[ZA]** SANS 10400; **[NA]** local-authority building regulations. Portfolio for graduation is compiled here. *Failure mode:* under-resourcing the technical package because the concept "feels finished".

**Honours year (NQF 8) — Consolidation and professional apprenticeship.**
Often combined with, or immediately preceded by, 12 months in practice (Wits requires ≥ 12 months of professional experience for BAS(Hons) eligibility; UKZN runs a year-long internship). Studio: a complete building at higher technical resolution; a full municipal-submission and tender-standard drawing set. Coursework: contract documentation and administration, building economics, specification writing. Research methods introduced.

**Master's (NQF 9) — Independent thesis and design research.**
A self-directed design dissertation on a self-defined site and brief, supported by a written research component, plus advanced technology and professional practice. The output must demonstrate original design judgement **and** technical credibility. This is the qualification that unlocks Professional Architect registration.

### Studio workload reality
Design studio typically consumes **20–30 hours per week** beyond scheduled contact time in years 2–3, and more in the Master's year. Crit (review) cycles run every 3–4 weeks. Expect to produce, per major project: a site analysis set, 3–6 iterations of parti, a final drawing set of 8–20 sheets, and 2–5 physical models. Budget for printing, board, timber, acrylic and adhesives — a recurring cost that students routinely underestimate.

## Open questions

- Exact NQF levels and credit totals per programme are not published consistently across SA institutions; the levels shown are inferred from the SACAP competency document's category-to-NQF mapping.
- Accreditation expiry dates for each ALS were not obtainable; SACAP publishes visit reports but not a consolidated validity table. Re-verify before relying on a specific programme's status.
- CPUT and DUT programme names have changed with the phasing out of the National Diploma/BTech structure; the SACAP list may lag the institutions' current offerings. Marked `confidence: medium` for that reason.
- The credit-share percentages in the model curriculum are a distillation of common practice, not a SACAP-prescribed weighting.
