# Pathway to registration as an architect

**Summary.** Registration is a statutory licence, not a title of courtesy. In South Africa it is granted by SACAP under the Architectural Profession Act 44 of 2000 across four professional categories with different, gazetted scopes of work; in Namibia by NCAQS under the Architects' and Quantity Surveyors' Act 13 of 1979. Both jurisdictions require an accredited qualification, a period of supervised practical experience as a registered candidate, and a prescribed examination. Realistic total elapsed time from school leaving to a professional architect's registration is **8–10 years**. RIBA/ARB (UK) and NCARB (US) follow the same three-part logic — education, experience, examination — with different sequencing.

## Key facts

| Step | **[ZA]** | **[NA]** | UK (ARB) | US (NCARB) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| School prerequisite | NSC with Mathematics + strong language; portfolio/selection test at most schools | NSSC: English + Mathematics ≥ 12 NUST points (no NSSCO grade below C); 3 further subjects ≥ 18 points | A-levels/equivalent; portfolio | High school diploma; portfolio for BArch |

## [ZA] South Africa — SACAP

### Legal framework
- **Architectural Profession Act 44 of 2000.** s.2 establishes SACAP as a juristic person. s.3: eleven members appointed by the Minister — seven registered practitioners (at least four in active practice), two State professionals, two public representatives. s.11 registration and accreditation powers. s.12 fee determination. s.13 education oversight and accreditation visits. **s.18 categories of registration.** s.19 registration requirements. **s.26 identification of work.** s.27 code of conduct. ss.28–32 discipline. s.33 appeals (Council → CBE → High Court). s.41 offences and penalties.
- SACAP sits under the **Council for the Built Environment (CBE Act 43 of 2000)**; identification of work is determined under CBE Act s.20.
- **Code of Conduct: Board Notice 7 of 2021, 19 February 2021.** Eight rules — integrity, competency, promotion, administrative functions, practice establishment, environmental protection, foreign professionals, disciplinary procedures.

### The four registration categories and their scopes
Scope is set by **Board Notice 901 of 2026 (27 March 2026)**, which replaced Board Notice 27 of 2021. Complexity is rated **A (high) / B (medium) / C (low)** against seven factors: Utility, Structure, Building Technologies, Building Services, Urban Context, Occupational Health and Safety, and Existing Buildings. Building types are grouped into ten categories: AUM (agriculture, utility, miscellaneous), AE (assembly and entertainment), COM (commercial), BUS (business), WFW (warehousing, factories, workshops), MED (medical and veterinary), CIV (community/civic), EDU (education), ACC (accommodation and residential), TRA (transport).

| Category | Designation | Minimum NQF | Complexity permitted | Typical work |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Professional Architect | PrArch | 9 (Master's) | A, B and C — unrestricted | All building types; multi-storey, multi-functional and complex buildings |
| Professional Senior Architectural Technologist | PrSArchT | 8 | A and B | Simple multi-storey buildings, long-span structures; offices, hotels, health centres, FET colleges, transport facilities |
| Professional Architectural Technologist | PrArchT | 6–7 | B and C | Simple double-storey buildings; shops, single/double-storey offices, workshops, consulting rooms, pre-schools, single and double-storey dwellings |
| Professional Architectural Draughtsperson | PrArchDraught | 5 (Higher Certificate) | C only | Single dwelling units of simple single-storey design; barns, carports, boundary walls, surface parking, minor works, substations, general-purpose shops, single-storey offices, standard storage |

> ⚠️ A candidate in **any** category may perform architectural work only under the supervision and control of a registered professional. Practising outside your registered category's scope is improper conduct under the Code and may constitute an offence under s.41.

### Candidacy
On graduating, register immediately as a **Candidate** (CandArch, CandSArchT, CandArchT, CandArchDraught). Only registered candidacy time counts.

Competency is assessed across **10 outcome fields**: office practice/legal aspects/ethics; computer applications; urban relationship; architectural history and theory; architectural design; environmental relationship; contract documentation and administration; the structure of buildings; construction technology; building services and related technologies. Each is assessed at four ascending levels — **Awareness, Knowledge, Understanding, Ability**.

Logged via **Monthly Training Records (MTRs)** accumulating **value units**:

| Candidate category | Minimum candidacy | MTRs | Value units | PPE papers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate Architect | 2 years | 24 | 400 | Papers 1 and 2 |
| Candidate Senior Architectural Technologist | 2 years | 24 | 400 | Papers 1 and 2 |
| Candidate Architectural Technologist | 2 years | 24 | 400 | Papers 1 and 2 |
| Candidate Architectural Draughtsperson | 3 years | 36 | 400 | Paper 1 only |

### Professional Practice Examination (PPE)
- **Two papers, each 3 hours, each marked out of 150. Pass mark 50% per paper.**
- **Paper 1 — closed book.** Content: (1) the architectural professional as practitioner; (2) the professional and their office; (3) the provision of architectural services; (4) dispute resolution.
- **Paper 2 — open book** (approved study material permitted). Same four areas plus construction procurement and contracting, weighted heavily.
- Historically held annually (e.g. 11–12 October 2023); SACAP's 2023/24 annual report records **two sittings** in that year — May 2023 (244 candidates, 208 passed, 37 failed) and October 2023 (296 candidates, 224 passed, 72 failed). Pass rates ≈ 85% and ≈ 76% respectively.
- Fees at the 2023 sitting: **preliminary application R390; examination fee R2,377** (2023 quote). Current fees must be confirmed with SACAP.

### Realistic durations and costs **[ZA]**
- BAS 3 yr + BAS(Hons) 1 yr + MArch(Prof) 2 yr = **6 years of study**; + 2 years candidacy + PPE = **8+ years minimum**. 9–10 years is common with a gap year in practice between Honours and Master's (widely encouraged).
- University tuition varies widely by institution; budget separately for studio materials, model-making, printing and site visits, which are a substantial and often underestimated cost.
- SACAP application and annual registration fees are determined under Act s.12 and gazetted; obtain the current schedule from SACAP directly — **not verified here.**

## [NA] Namibia — NCAQS

### Legal framework
- **Architects' and Quantity Surveyors' Act 13 of 1979.**
  - **s.2** — establishes the Namibia Council for Architects and Quantity Surveyors as a body corporate.
  - **s.3** — composition: four members nominated by the **Namibia Institute of Architects**, four by the **Institute of Namibian Quantity Surveyors**, two public-service representatives, one public-interest member; appointed by the Minister.
  - **s.7** — Council prescribes registration and annual renewal fees, published in the *Gazette*.
  - **s.11** — four registration classes: **Architects; Quantity Surveyors; Architects in Training; Quantity Surveyors in Training.**
  - **s.11(2)** — registration requirements: minimum age 21; prescribed examinations; documented work experience "of sufficient variety and of a satisfactory nature and standard"; institute membership.
  - **s.13** — reserved work: performing work reserved for architects or quantity surveyors for gain while unregistered, or holding oneself out as registered, is an offence (penalty stated in the 1979 text as up to one thousand rand; the current penalty level **needs verification**).
  - **ss.15, 18** — disciplinary powers: fines, removal from the register, suspension from practice.
- NCAQS address: 8 Koch Street, Klein Windhoek; tel +264-61-401813.

### Practical route **[NA]**
1. NSSC with the NUST entry points (see file `02`), NUST selection test and interview.
2. **Bachelor of Architecture** (NUST, 07BARC) — on completion the graduate may register with NCAQS as a **Candidate Architectural Technologist**.
3. **Bachelor of Architecture Honours** — NQF 8, 480 cumulative credits.
4. **Master of Architecture** (NUST, 09MARC) — entry requires the BArch Hons or an equivalent NQF 8 / 480-credit qualification, plus portfolio and interview with the Postgraduate Selection Committee.
5. Register as **Architect in Training** with NCAQS; complete the prescribed practical training under s.11(2)(c).
6. Sit the prescribed examination under s.11(2)(b) and satisfy the institute-membership requirement — in practice, membership of the **Namibia Institute of Architects (NIA)**.
7. Register as **Architect**. Annual renewal fees under s.7.

Namibia's professional culture is closely coupled to South Africa's: **PROCSA** client/consultant agreements are promoted in Namibia by ACEN, and NUST's architecture programmes were validated by a joint NCAQS / SACAP / **Commonwealth Association of Architects (CAA)** panel — meaning a NUST graduate's qualification is recognised for the purposes of both councils and, via the CAA, across the Commonwealth.

## International reference models

### UK — ARB and RIBA
The historical route is **Part 1** (3-year undergraduate) → 1 year out in practice → **Part 2** (2-year postgraduate) → 1 year in practice → **Part 3** (professional practice examination). Minimum **24 months of professional practical experience**, of which at least **12 months in the UK** for the outgoing Part 3, recorded on **PEDR** (Professional Experience and Development Record) sheets. Total typically **7 years**.

**Registration with ARB is what makes you an architect** in UK law (protected title); RIBA Chartered membership is voluntary and additional.

Following the *Tomorrow's Architects* consultation (2023), ARB approved a competency-based framework that replaces Parts 1/2/3 with **two accredited qualification levels**: a Master's-level qualification assessing **Academic Outcomes**, and a separate **practice-level qualification** assessing **Practice Outcomes**. ARB is **ceasing accreditation of undergraduate (Level 6 / Scottish Level 9) degrees**. The 24-month practical-experience minimum has been retained. From **spring 2027** a **Registration Assurance Process** replaces the Competency Standards Group route for applicants whose Part 3 is more than two years old.

**RIBA Plan of Work 2020 stages** (the UK project-stage vocabulary; compare **[ZA]** PROCSA stages in file `07`):
0 Strategic Definition · 1 Preparation and Briefing · 2 Concept Design · 3 Spatial Coordination · 4 Technical Design · 5 Manufacturing and Construction · 6 Handover · 7 Use.

### US — NCARB
Three requirements, which may be pursued concurrently: an **NAAB-accredited degree**, the **AXP**, and the **ARE 5.0**. Licensure is granted by the individual state board; the **NCARB Certificate** enables reciprocity between states and, under mutual-recognition agreements, with SACAP.

**AXP — 3,740 hours total:**

| Experience area | Hours |
|---|---|
| Practice Management | 160 |
| Project Management | 360 |
| Programming & Analysis | 260 |
| Project Planning & Design | 1,080 |
| Project Development & Documentation | 1,520 |
| Construction & Evaluation | 360 |
| **Total** | **3,740** |

At least half (**1,870 hours**) must be earned in **Setting A** — employed by an architecture firm legally practising architecture, under the supervision of an architect. From November 2025 the five-year reporting rule was replaced: experience reported within one year earns full credit; older experience earns **75% credit with no age limit**. Setting O was expanded to include HSW-related continuing education from pre-approved providers.

**ARE 5.0 — six divisions:**

| Division | Items | Test duration |
|---|---|---|
| Practice Management (PcM) | 65 | 2 h 40 min |
| Project Management (PjM) | 75 | 3 h (3 h 40 min appointment incl. 30 min break) |
| Programming & Analysis (PA) | 75 | 3 h |
| Project Planning & Design (PPD) | 100 | 4 h 5 min |
| Project Development & Documentation (PDD) | 100 | 4 h 5 min |
| Construction & Evaluation (CE) | 75 | 3 h |

From **27 April 2026** NCARB made minor adjustments to 12 of the 91 exam objectives and reformatted case studies with fewer resources per case study. Per-division ARE fees are not stated on the pages fetched — **needs verification.**

There is a **mutual recognition arrangement between NCARB and SACAP**, allowing qualified architects registered in one jurisdiction a streamlined route to the other.
