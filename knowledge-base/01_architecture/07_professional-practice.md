# Professional practice — services, fees, contracts and liability

**Summary.** The architect's commercial life is organised around six work stages, a fee calculated from either project cost or time, a written appointment agreement, and — during construction — the role of **principal agent** with power to certify. This file covers the stages, the **[ZA]** gazetted fee guideline (Board Notice 672 of 2024), appointment agreements (SACAP CPPA, SAIA Client–Architect, PROCSA), contract administration under **JBCC**, certificates and payment timing, variations, defects liability, professional indemnity insurance, the Code of Conduct, duty of care and the common routes to a claim. **[NA]** Namibian practice follows the same architecture: PROCSA agreements are actively used and promoted in Namibia by ACEN, and JBCC contracts are common.

## 1. The work stages

**[ZA]/[NA]** The six-stage model is shared by SACAP, SAIA, PROCSA and the other built-environment councils, which is what makes multi-consultant appointments coordinate.

| Stage | Name | Fee % | Cumulative | Core deliverable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Inception | 2% | 2% | Confirmed brief, site appraisal, consultant recommendations, programme, budget parameters |
| 2 | Concept and Viability | 15% | 17% | Design concept — layouts, elevations, materials, cost check against budget |
| 3 | Design Development | 20% | 37% | Developed design, coordinated with consultants, specifications begun |
| 4.1 | Documentation — statutory/authority submission | 10% | 47% | Municipal submission set; approval obtained |
| 4.2 | Documentation — procurement | 20% | 67% | Tender documentation; contractor procured |
| 5 | Construction | 30% | 97% | Contract administration, site inspections, certificates, instructions |
| 6 | Close-out | 3% | 100% | Defect rectification, record drawings, O&M manuals, final certificates |

> Note: the **SAIA client guide** publishes a slightly different split — Part A Design 35% (Stage 1 at 5%, 2 at 15%, 3 at 20% — note this sums to 40%), Part B Documentation 40%, Part C Contract Administration 25% (Stage 5 at 27%, Stage 6 at 3%). Where the two differ, the **gazetted SACAP guideline governs** as the reference; the actual split is whatever the appointment agreement says.

Mapping to the **RIBA Plan of Work 2020** (see file `01`): Stage 1 ≈ RIBA 0–1; Stage 2 ≈ RIBA 2; Stage 3 ≈ RIBA 3; Stage 4 ≈ RIBA 4; Stage 5 ≈ RIBA 5; Stage 6 ≈ RIBA 6.

## 2. Fees

### Method 1 — project cost based **[ZA]**
The gazette gives three complexity tiers (**Low, Medium, High**) and a table of project-cost bands. For each band: a **primary fee** (a fixed rand amount) plus a **secondary fee** (a percentage applied to the amount by which the project cost exceeds the band threshold).

`Fee = Primary fee (Column C) + [(Project cost − band threshold) × Secondary % (Column D)]`

Selected entries from **Board Notice 672 of 2024** (Gazette 51352, 4 October 2024):

| Complexity | Lowest band (R1 – R1,2 m) | Highest band (> R1,04 bn) |
|---|---|---|
| Low | R11 341,85 + 17,53% | R84 483 711,59 + 7,28% |
| Medium | R13 570,07 + 20,96% | R101 081 351,13 + 8,71% |
| High | R15 798,28 + 24,41% | R117 678 990,65 + 10,16% |

The percentage falls as project value rises — the classic regressive fee scale. Complexity is assessed on the same factors used for identification of work (utility, structure, technologies, services, urban context, OHS, existing buildings).

### Method 2 — time based **[ZA]**
Used where scope is not definable, for partial services, or for work beyond the agreed scope.

| Category | BN 91 of 2020 (Aug 2020) | BN 672 of 2024 (Oct 2024) |
|---|---|---|
| Principal/Specialist | R2 520 | R3 313 |
| Principal > 10 years | R2 025 | R2 663 |
| Principal < 10 years | R1 515 | R1 991 |
| Associates / Managers | R1 080 | R1 420 |
| Registered architectural professionals | R730 | R960 |
| Architectural professionals (directed) | R435 | R571 |
| Supporting staff | R310 | R408 |

*(Rates per hour, excluding VAT. Reproduced from the gazettes as quoted at those dates.)*

### Other methods
- **Deliverables based** — a schedule of specific activities with a price against each; usual for partial services.
- **Square-metre rate** — for additions and alterations where a project cost cannot be predetermined.
- **Combination** — time-based for Stages 1–2, cost-based thereafter. Common and sensible.

### Surcharges and additions
The SACAP CPPA provides for surcharges of **25%–40%** for additions and alterations, heritage work, and taking over an incomplete project from another professional. Disbursements — travel, printing, authority fees, courier — are reimbursed separately at cost.

> ⚠️ **The published fee guideline is a guideline, not a tariff.** Fees are negotiable and competitive tendering for professional services is normal. But quoting substantially below the guideline without adjusting scope is the most reliable predictor of an under-serviced project and a professional-indemnity claim.

## 3. Appointment agreements

Never work without a signed written appointment. **[ZA]** the Code of Conduct (BN 7 of 2021) requires registered persons to enter written agreements specifying **scope, fees and insurance details**.

| Agreement | Use |
|---|---|
| **SACAP Client/Professional Project Agreement (CPPA)** | Any architectural appointment; published by the regulator |
| **SAIA Client–Architect Agreement** | Residential and medium-scale projects |
| **PROCSA** suite | Medium to large projects with a full professional team. **Edition 4.0 (October 2017)** is the edition promoted in Namibia by ACEN; a later edition exists in South Africa. Fifteen agreement types: development manager, principal agent, project manager, architect, quantity surveyor, structural/electrical/civil/mechanical/wet services engineers, fire consultant, landscape architect, construction health & safety specialist, interior designer, and a blank user-defined form |

**Key CPPA terms:**
- Invoices **due and payable on presentation**; late-payment interest after 30 days.
- If payment is not received within **7 days of notice**, the professional may **suspend** work; work resumes within 2 working days of full payment.
- Disputed invoices must be contested **within 14 days**, without delaying undisputed amounts.
- **Liability cap**: the lesser of available PI insurance proceeds, a specified agreed amount, or an amount equal to the fees payable.
- **Termination**: 14 days' written notice for material breach. A client may terminate or postpone at any time; the professional invoices for services rendered plus a **10% surcharge** where termination is not the professional's fault. Work postponed **12 months or more is deemed terminated**.
- **Disputes**: mediation first (costs shared equally), then arbitration; arbitrator appointed by agreement or by the SACAP president under Association of Arbitrators rules.

## 4. Contract administration and the principal agent

Under **JBCC** the architect is usually appointed **principal agent** — the employer's agent with contractual authority to instruct, value, certify and determine time. This is a distinct appointment from the architectural services appointment and should be documented as such (PROCSA has a separate Principal Agent agreement).

**The principal agent's dual role.** Acting for the employer when instructing and administering, but required to act **fairly and impartially between the parties** when valuing, certifying and determining extensions of time. Certifying to please the client is the fastest route to personal liability.

**Core duties during Stage 5:**
- Regular site inspections (frequency set in the appointment — "periodic" ≠ "supervision"; the architect does not supervise the contractor's means and methods).
- Issue **contract instructions** in writing; verbal instructions confirmed in writing within a stated period.
- Answer **RFIs**; review contractor shop drawings and samples (review for conformity with design intent — do not "approve" in a way that assumes the contractor's responsibility).
- Value work and issue **payment certificates**.
- Assess and determine claims for **extension of time** and, where the contract allows, additional cost.
- Certify **practical completion**, **works completion** and **final completion**.
- Maintain the site instruction register, minutes, drawing issue register and photographic record. These are the documents that decide disputes.

## 5. Certificates and payment — JBCC Principal Building Agreement

| Certificate | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Interim payment certificate** | Values work done to date, less retention/guarantee reduction. Issued regularly by the due date stated in the contract data (cl. 25.2) |
| **Certificate of Practical Completion** | The works can be used for their intended purpose. Starts the **90-day defects liability period** (cl. 21.1); ends penalties for late completion; risk and insurance transfer; reduces the withholding percentage from 10% to **2,5%** (cl. 11.4.1) |
| **Certificate of Works Completion** | Outstanding items listed at practical completion have been completed |
| **Certificate of Final Completion** | Defects notified during the defects liability period have been made good. Starts the **5-year latent defects period** (cl. 22.1) |
| **Final Payment Certificate** | Final account settled |

**Payment timing:** the employer pays within **14 calendar days** of the date for issue of the payment certificate (cl. 25.10). Where the certificate is in the employer's favour, the contractor pays within **21 calendar days** (cl. 25.11). The contractor must pay subcontractors within **7 calendar days** of the employer's due date (cl. 25.13).

**Security:** a variable construction guarantee of **10%** of the contract sum, or a fixed guarantee of **5%** with a corresponding payment reduction (cl. 11.1.1–11.1.2); alternatively the employer may withhold **10%**, reducing to **2,5%** at practical completion (cl. 11.4.1).

**Penalties** for late completion are a stated amount per calendar day in the contract data (cl. 24.1) — a genuine pre-estimate of loss, not a punishment.

## 6. Variations

A variation is any change to the scope, quality, quantity or sequence of the works. Process:
1. Identify the change and its origin (client request, design development, site condition, statutory requirement, error).
2. **Instruct in writing** before the work is done. An instruction after the fact is a ratification, and a weak one.
3. Obtain a priced quotation, or agree a valuation basis (contract rates → pro-rata rates → fair valuation → daywork, in that order of preference).
4. Assess time impact separately from cost impact.
5. Obtain the client's **written** authorisation where it affects the budget.
6. Record it in the variation register and reflect it in the next payment certificate.

> ⚠️ Design errors and coordination failures are **not** variations. Instructing them as variations, without disclosing the cause to the client, is a conduct problem and can become a fraud problem.

## 7. Defects liability

- Defects appearing during the **90-day** JBCC defects liability period are made good by the contractor at its cost.
- **Latent defects** — not reasonably discoverable at final completion — remain the contractor's liability for **5 years** from certified final completion (JBCC cl. 22.1).
- Statutory prescription of claims runs separately and is longer for some causes of action; take legal advice on limitation before assuming a claim is time-barred.
- Do not certify final completion while known defects are outstanding — the certificate is the trigger that shortens the client's remedies.

## 8. Professional indemnity insurance

- **[ZA]** The Code of Conduct requires that registered persons "take adequate and appropriate professional indemnity insurance" and provide evidence to SACAP on request.
- PI is written on a **claims-made** basis: the policy in force *when the claim is made* responds, not the policy in force when the work was done. Consequence: **run-off cover** must be maintained after retirement or closure of a practice, typically for at least the latent-defects/prescription period.
- Disclose to the client: policy number, limit per claim and in the aggregate, renewal date, and the excess/deductible (a CPPA requirement).
- Set the limit against project value and consequence, not against fee income. A R2 m limit on a R150 m project is decorative.
- Notify the insurer of **circumstances that may give rise to a claim** as soon as they are known. Failing to notify, or admitting liability without the insurer's consent, commonly voids cover.

## 9. Ethics and duty of care

**[ZA]** Code of Conduct, **Board Notice 7 of 2021 (19 February 2021)** — eight rules:

1. **Integrity** — honesty; nothing detrimental to the profession.
2. **Competency** — perform with skill; keep current with developments; work only within your registered category.
3. **Promotion** — promote services honestly, limited to your actual registration categories; credit work done in previous practices correctly.
4. **Administrative functions** — proper documentation; written client agreements; keep clients informed of progress; **issue certificates based on correct facts on site**; comply with building regulations.
5. **Practice establishment** — register the practice; maintain supervision.
6. **Environmental protection** — minimise adverse environmental impact; **advise the client in writing how best to conserve and enhance the quality of the environment**.
7. **Foreign professionals** — non-South African practitioners must be registered.
8. **Disciplinary procedures** — as set out in the Act (ss.28–32; appeal under s.33).

**Conflict of interest:** declare to any potential client any business interest that could raise doubt about impartiality — property development, contracting, trading. Any commission or remuneration for recommending a product requires **written** notification to the client.

**Duty of care.** The architect owes the client a contractual duty of reasonable skill and care measured against the standard of the ordinary competent architect, and owes third parties a duty in delict/tort where harm is reasonably foreseeable. The duty is *not* a guarantee of a perfect result — but it is also not discharged by good intentions.

## 10. Common legal exposures

1. **Certifying incorrectly** — over-certifying payment, or certifying practical completion prematurely. Direct financial loss to the client, directly traceable to the architect.
2. **No written appointment** — scope disputes are unwinnable without one, and it breaches the Code.
3. **Scope creep without a fee variation** — unrecoverable work and eventual under-servicing.
4. **Practising outside the registered category** — a conduct offence **[ZA]**, and a statutory offence in both jurisdictions if unregistered altogether.
5. **Building regulation non-compliance** — fire, accessibility and energy provisions are the most frequently missed.
6. **Failure to warn** about budget, programme or buildability risk. Verbal warnings do not exist; write them down.
7. **Copyright and moral rights** — the architect generally owns copyright in the drawings; licence terms for reuse and for completing the building with another architect must be in the agreement.
8. **Non-disclosure of a conflict of interest.**
9. **Lapsed PI cover or missed notification** — turns a covered claim into a personal one.
10. **Uncontrolled drawing revisions** — building to a superseded revision (see file `04`).

## Open questions

- The current PROCSA edition is ambiguous: ACEN promotes **Edition 4.0 (October 2017)**, while an ASAQS circular refers to **Edition 5**. Confirm the current edition before issuing an agreement.
- The SAIA client guide and the gazetted SACAP guideline give **different stage percentages**; the SAIA figures as fetched also do not sum cleanly. Use the gazette.
- Whether a **Board Notice later than 672 of 2024** has updated the SACAP fee guideline for 2025/2026 was not established; check the current gazette before quoting rates.
- **[NA]** No Namibian gazetted fee guideline was located. Namibian practice appears to reference the SACAP guideline and PROCSA; this should be confirmed with NCAQS and the NIA.
- JBCC edition: the contract data fetched corresponds to Edition 6.2 (May 2018) clause numbering; confirm against the edition actually incorporated in any given contract, as clause numbers move between editions.
- The exact interim payment certificate **issue** interval is set in the contract data, not fixed by the agreement; monthly is conventional but not automatic.
