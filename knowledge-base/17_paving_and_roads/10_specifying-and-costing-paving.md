# Specifying and costing paving — quantities, waste, plant, bill of quantities and a reusable specification

**Summary.** A paving job is priced on six quantities: area of paving, volume of excavation, volume of each layerwork course, volume of bedding sand, length of edge restraint, and number of blocks. Get those right and the estimate is right within a few per cent. The two things that wreck estimates are **waste** (which is a function of pattern and cutting, not of carelessness) and **layerworks** (which is where a cheap quotation hides its savings). This file gives the measurement conventions, the per-m² quantities, the waste factors, the plant list, a bill-of-quantities structure, the tolerance and warranty clauses, and a specification template that can be dropped into a contract.

## Key facts

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Bedding sand, compacted | 25 mm → **0,025 m³/m²** compacted; allow **~0,030–0,035 m³/m² loose** (surcharge) |
| Bedding sand mass | ~40–55 kg/m² at 25 mm compacted (`needs-verification` for density) |
| Jointing sand | ~2–5 kg/m² for a 3 mm joint on a 60 mm block, plus top-up at 3 months (`needs-verification`) |
| Subbase, 100 mm compacted G5 | 0,100 m³/m² compacted; allow **1,25–1,35× loose/bulking factor** for the borrow volume |
| Excavation depth for 60 mm block + 25 mm sand + 100 mm subbase | ~185 mm below finished level, plus subgrade preparation |
| Plate compactor, blocks ≤ 80 mm | 7–16 kN centrifugal force, 65–100 Hz, plate 0,2–0,4 m² (covers ≥ 10 units) |
| Plate compactor, blocks > 80 mm | 16–20 kN, 65–100 Hz, plate 0,35–0,5 m² (covers ≥ 12 units) |
| Heavy-duty plate for lock-up | 300–600 kg, plate 0,5–0,6 m², 30–65 kN centrifugal force |
| Roller | Light 2–4 t vibratory, or heavy pneumatic-tyred; **never heavy steel** |
| Compaction of depression fill | 98 % modified AASHTO, subbase material only |
| Gap-filling concrete | ≥ 15 MPa at 24 h, 9,5 mm max aggregate, cured 24 h |
| Depression fill in stabilised subbase > 50 mm deep | Concrete ≥ 5 MPa at 7 days |

## Blocks per square metre

The single most useful table in estimating. All figures **exclude cutting and waste**.

|---|---|---|---|---|

| Corobrik Cobble clay paver | 108,5 × 108,5 × 50 | **80** | 1,2 kg | 864 per pallet |
| Corobrik Piazza clay paver | 220 × 52 × 50 | **82** | 1,2 kg | 1 064 per pallet |
| Technicrete Double Zig-Zag® | 220 × 97 | **48** | — | 50/60/80 mm |
| Technicrete Conleaf® | 200 × 200 | **42** | — | 55/60/80 mm |
| Technicrete Concobble® Square | 116 × 116 | **74** | — | 55 mm (60 on request) |
| Technicrete Concobble® Wedge | 116 × 112/67 | **92** | — | 55 mm (60 on request) |
| Technicrete Trojan® Slab | 240 × 160 | **26** | — | 60 mm |

**Rule of thumb for any rectangular unit:** units/m² = 1 000 000 / (L × W) where L and W are the **work sizes plus the joint** (add 3 mm to each dimension). For a 220 × 110 mm paver: 1 000 000 / (223 × 113) = 39,7 → order on 41/m² to be safe, which is what the manufacturers publish.

**Mass per m²** matters when haulage dominates cost (as it does in northern Namibia):
- 50 mm concrete paver: roughly 105–125 kg/m²
- 60 mm concrete paver: roughly 125–150 kg/m²
- 80 mm concrete paver: roughly 170–200 kg/m²
- 50 mm clay Modular: 41 × 2,6 kg = **107 kg/m²** (verified from Corobrik data)

These figures let you convert an area into truckloads before you ask for a delivered price.

## Waste factors

Waste in block paving is almost entirely **cutting waste**, and it is a function of geometry, not workmanship.

| Situation | Add |
|---|---|
| Large rectangular area, stretcher bond, straight edges | **3–5 %** |
| Large area, 90° herringbone, straight edges | **5–7 %** |
| Large area, 45° herringbone (a cut on every edge block) | **7–10 %** |
| Residential driveway with kerbs, curves and a manhole | **10–15 %** |
| Narrow path with curved edges | **12–18 %** |
| Circles, fans, radial patterns | **15–25 %** |
| Cobble-format units (small, many per m²) | add a further 2–3 % for breakage |
| Long-distance road haulage (unsealed final approach) | add **2–5 %** for transit breakage |

`needs-verification` — these are estimating conventions, not sourced figures. Two structural rules reduce them: reorient the pattern into a soldier course along edge restraints (which converts sliver cuts into whole blocks), and never accept cuts smaller than 25 % of a full block.

**Always order the full quantity in one delivery** where colour matters. Mould wear and pigment-batch variation mean a second delivery will not match. If phasing is unavoidable, either stockpile the whole order or separate the phases with an edge restraint or a contrasting band of rectangular units — this is explicit CMA guidance.

## Layerwork quantities

| Item | Measurement | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Site clearance | m² | Strip topsoil and vegetation |
| Bulk excavation | m³ | To formation level; state whether measured in-situ or loose |
| Cart away spoil | m³ | Bulked volume; typically 1,2–1,3 × in-situ. State the tip and the haul distance |
| Subgrade preparation and compaction | m² | State the compaction standard (e.g. 93 % mod. AASHTO) |
| Selected layer (G7/G9) | m³ or m² per stated thickness | State material class and compacted thickness |
| Subbase (G5/G6/C4) | m³ or m² per stated thickness | State material class, compacted thickness and compaction standard |
| Cement for stabilisation | tonne or kg/m³ | Where a C-class layer is specified |
| Bedding sand | m² (at stated compacted thickness) | Never measured by volume delivered |
| Blocks — supply | m² or per 1 000 units | State product, thickness, colour, type (S-A/S-B/S-C) |
| Blocks — lay | m² | State pattern |
| Jointing sand | m² or tonne | Include the 3-month top-up |
| Edge restraints | linear metre by type | Include excavation and concrete haunch |
| Kerbs and channels | linear metre by section | To SANS 927 / SANS 1200 MK |
| Anchor beams | linear metre | Where slope > 12 % |
| Gap-filling concrete | m³ or number | Should be near zero |
| Cutting around manholes, gullies, columns | number | Or absorbed in the laying rate — but say which |

**Measurement conventions.** Paving is measured **net over the paved area**, deducting voids over a stated size (commonly 0,5 m²) — manholes, tree pits, planters. Cutting is normally included in the laying rate, not measured separately, which is why the waste factor and the pattern must both be stated in the bill. Edge restraints are measured along their centre line. Layerworks are measured to the neat dimensions on the drawing, not to the excavated dimensions — over-excavation is the contractor's risk unless the ground is proven unsuitable.

## Plant requirements

| Plant | Purpose | Specification |
|---|---|---|
| **Plate compactor** (vibrating plate, high frequency, low amplitude) | Bedding-in the blocks, settling joint sand | Blocks ≤ 80 mm: 7–16 kN, 65–100 Hz, plate 0,2–0,4 m². Blocks > 80 mm: 16–20 kN, 65–100 Hz, plate 0,35–0,5 m² |
| **Heavy plate compactor** | Final lock-up | 300–600 kg, plate 0,5–0,6 m², 30–65 kN |
| **Roller** | Layerworks compaction; proof-rolling and lock-up on heavy pavements | Light 2–4 t vibratory; heavy pneumatic-tyred for ≥ 5 passes where wheel loads exceed 30 kN. Heavy steel rollers must not be used on paving — they break blocks |
| **Block splitter (guillotine)** | Cutting closures | Hydraulic or mechanical; no power or water needed |
| **Power saw / angle grinder with diamond blade** | Visible cuts, curves, manhole closures | Water suppression or dust extraction mandatory (silica) |
| **Screed rails and screed board** | Striking the bedding sand | Board cut to the surcharge depth below the edge restraints |
| **String lines, pegs, level or laser** | Setting out and level control | Two perpendicular string lines minimum |
| **Wheelbarrows, clamp trolleys, trolley and stools** | Moving blocks to the face | Keeps the laying face fed |
| **Brooms** | Brooming in joint sand | Sand is **broomed**, not washed, into joints |
| **Plastic sheeting** | Covering sand stockpiles | Moisture uniformity is a specification requirement (6 ± 2 %) |
| **Boards/planks** | Protecting uncompacted new paving from plant crossing it | Required by SANS 1200 MJ 5.4 |
| **Compression testing machine or laboratory access** | Block strength verification | Only if manufacturing locally or verifying an unmarked supply |

## Building up a rate

Do not accept or offer a single all-in m² rate. Build it up:

```
Blocks:        units/m² × (1 + waste%) × unit price ex works
             + haulage (mass/m² × rate per tonne-km × distance)
             + offloading and handling
Bedding sand:  0,033 m³/m² loose × delivered sand price
Jointing sand: allowance per m² + top-up visit
Layerworks:    compacted thickness × bulking factor × delivered material price
             + compaction plant and water
Labour:        m² ÷ (team output m²/day) × team day-rate
Plant:         compactor, splitter, saw — day rate × days
Preliminaries: setting out, protection, cleaning
Overheads and profit
```

The two inputs you must not guess are **team output** (see `07_paving-contractors-and-craft.md` — the estimates there are `needs-verification`) and **delivered material price**, which in northern Namibia is dominated by haulage.

**The only source-backed absolute cost benchmark in this domain** is the CMA's independently commissioned July 2009 Gauteng costing: 60 mm concrete block paving over 100 mm G5 in a 3 000 m² car park came to **R175–207/m²** including estimated 10-year maintenance, against **R266–294/m²** for 25 mm premix asphalt over G4/G5. That establishes the ratio (CBP roughly 60–80 % of premix), not present-day money.

## Tolerance and quality clauses

Adopt these directly (Degree of Accuracy I, SANS 1200 MJ Table 6.2), measured **3 months after opening to traffic**:

| Item | Permissible deviation |
|---|---|
| Unit length / width | ±2 mm |
| Unit thickness | ±3 mm |
| Unit squareness | ±2 mm |
| Top of subbase from designated level | ±10 mm |
| Smoothness of subbase, 3 m straightedge, any direction | ±10 mm |
| Thickness of 25 mm compacted bedding sand | ±10 mm |
| Line of pattern, deviation from any 3 m straight line | 10 mm max |
| Line of pattern, deviation from any 20 m straight line | 20 mm max |
| Vertical deviation from 3 m straightedge at kerbs, channels, gullies, manholes and edge restraints | +3, −0 mm |
| Vertical deviation from 3 m straightedge elsewhere | +10, −15 mm |
| Surface level difference between adjacent units | 3 mm |
| Deviation of finished surface level from designated level | +10, −15 mm |

Supporting clauses worth writing in:
- **Trial section**: first 20 m × ~6 m from a permanent edge restraint, approved before full production; unapproved trial sections removed at the contractor's cost.
- **Smoothness check frequency**: roads per SANS 1200 M 6.3; other areas one check per 300 m² maximum.
- **Ponding test**: the engineer may order the surface flooded; the contractor rectifies at his own cost if ponding occurs, the employer bears the cost if it does not.
- **Subbase tolerance amendment**: the standard SANS 1200 and CASRA tolerances on subgrade and subbase are *not* adequate for good paving; the specification must amend them (the ±10 mm above is the amended figure).
- **Non-relaxation clause**: tolerances on the units themselves and other SANS 1058 requirements shall not be relaxed under any circumstances.
- **Material certification**: blocks to carry the SANS 1058 mark; sand gradings tested and certified against SANS 1200 MJ 3.3(a) and (b); soluble salt content of both sands reported.
- **Prohibition**: no cement in the jointing sand; no cut units smaller than 25 % of a full unit; no vehicular traffic until all joints are filled; no heavy steel rollers.

## Warranty terms

Distinguish two warranties and write both:

1. **Product warranty** from the manufacturer, covering the block: strength, dimensional compliance, abrasion resistance and (where offered) colour. Bosun, for example, offers a five-year guarantee on paving manufactured to SANS 1058:2021 at its Midrand, Brits and PE plants. Note explicitly that efflorescence is **not** a product defect — it is a natural, temporary phenomenon that rain clears in 1–2 years.
2. **Workmanship warranty / defects liability period** from the installer. A reasonable residential term:
   - 12 months from practical completion.
   - Covers: settlement or dishing exceeding the stated tolerances; loss of joint sand; movement or failure of edge restraints; blocks becoming loose; ponding.
   - Excludes: damage by vehicles heavier than the stated design case; trenching or excavation by others; root heave from trees planted after completion; efflorescence; normal colour weathering; damage from chemical spills.
   - Includes the **3-month joint top-up visit** as an obligation, not a favour.
   - Requires the owner to keep joints full and not to pressure-wash the joints out — the single most common owner-caused failure.

## Reusable specification template

```
SPECIFICATION: SEGMENTAL CONCRETE BLOCK PAVING

1.  STANDARDS
1.1 Materials and workmanship shall comply with SANS 1200 MJ (Segmented paving),
    SANS 1200 DM (Earthworks), SANS 1200 ME (Subbase), SANS 1200 MK (Kerbing)
    and SANS 1058 (Concrete paving blocks), except as amended herein.
1.2 Where this specification conflicts with a referenced standard, this
    specification governs.

2.  UNITS
2.1 Type: <S-A / S-B / S-C>.  Class: <25 / 35>.  Thickness: <50/60/80> mm.
2.2 Product: <manufacturer and product name>.  Colour: <___>, to approved samples.
2.3 Units shall carry the SANS 1058 mark and be certified to SANS 1058:2021.
2.4 Units shall be free of cracks; no chip exceeding 15 mm or covering more than
    3 % of the exposed periphery; no protuberance exceeding 3 mm; colour to
    penetrate at least 5 mm below the wearing surface and be integrally bound.
2.5 Dimensional tolerance: +/-2 mm length and width, +/-3 mm thickness,
    +/-2 mm squareness.
2.6 Reference samples showing the agreed colour and texture range shall be held
    by the Employer for the duration of the Contract.
2.7 The full quantity shall be delivered in a single consignment, or matching
    deliveries stockpiled, to avoid colour banding.

3.  SANDS
3.1 Bedding sand: coarse, clean, non-plastic, free of silt and soluble salts,
    grading to SANS 1200 MJ 3.3(a): 9,52 mm 100 %; 4,75 mm 95-100 %;
    2,36 mm 80-100 %; 1,18 mm 50-85 %; 0,600 mm 25-60 %; 0,300 mm 10-30 %;
    0,150 mm 5-15 %; 0,075 mm 0-10 %.  Angular sands preferred.
3.2 Jointing sand: passing 1,18 mm with 10-50 % passing 0,075 mm, to
    SANS 1200 MJ 3.3(b).  A small clay or silt content is acceptable and desirable.
3.3 No cement shall be added to jointing sand, save immediately adjacent to
    downpipes and stormwater outlets where directed.
3.4 Both sands shall be tested for soluble salt content and results submitted.

4.  LAYERWORKS
4.1 Subgrade prepared and compacted to <__> % modified AASHTO, extending to the
    rear face of all edge restraints.
4.2 Subbase: <___ mm> compacted <G5 / G6 / C4> to SANS 1200 ME.
4.3 Top of subbase: minimum 1 % longitudinal and 2 % transverse fall; level
    +/-10 mm; smoothness +/-10 mm on a 3 m straightedge in any direction.
4.4 Depressions shall be filled with subbase material compacted to 98 % modified
    AASHTO.  Bedding sand shall not under any circumstances be used for this purpose.
    Shallow depressions require scabbling to give a minimum 50 mm fill.
4.5 All service trenches within the paved area shall be backfilled to subgrade
    level with approved granular material and compacted, before subbase construction.

5.  EDGE RESTRAINTS
5.1 Edge restraints shall be constructed on the subbase and shall achieve design
    strength before any units are laid.
5.2 Type: <cast in-situ concrete edge beam / SANS 927 kerb Fig ___ / soldier
    course on concrete haunch>.
5.3 Anchor beams to be provided where the slope exceeds 12 %, at spacings of
    30 m (12 %), 20 m (15 %) and 15 m (20 %), 150 x 150 mm, with 25 mm diameter
    weepholes at 1,0 m centres, cast 7-10 mm below the surrounding blockwork.

6.  BEDDING SAND LAYER
6.1 Compacted thickness 25 mm +/- 10 mm.  Moisture content at spreading 6 % +/- 2 %.
6.2 The surcharge shall be established on site by a test area laid on 30 mm loose
    sand and compacted with three passes of the plate compactor to be used.
6.3 Sand shall be screeded loose, only as far ahead as can be paved the same day,
    and protected from wetting and drying.  Accidentally compacted sand shall be
    raked and re-screeded loose.

7.  LAYING
7.1 Pattern: <45 degree herringbone>, unless otherwise shown.  Where herringbone
    is not possible the long axis shall be at right angles to the line of traffic.
7.2 Joint widths 2-6 mm, averaging 3 mm over any 20 blocks.
7.3 Whole units laid first; closures cut and fitted thereafter.
7.4 No cut unit smaller than 25 % of a full unit.  Where necessary the pattern
    shall be reoriented into a soldier course along edge restraints.
7.5 Gap-filling concrete shall be avoided; where unavoidable it shall be full
    depth, minimum 15 MPa at 24 hours, 9,5 mm maximum aggregate, adjacent units
    pre-wetted, cured 24 hours, with no compaction within 1 m for 24 hours.
7.6 Paving shall be laid 5-10 mm proud of channels, gullies and manhole covers.
7.7 On slopes, laying shall proceed uphill.  Over undulating ground, laying shall
    commence at the low point and proceed in both directions.
7.8 Boards shall be laid where plant must cross uncompacted new paving.

8.  COMPACTION AND JOINTING
8.1 Minimum two passes of an approved plate compactor before any jointing sand,
    stopping not closer than 1 m from any free edge at the end of each day.
    A temporary restraint shall be installed against free edges before compaction.
8.2 Plate compactor: units <= 80 mm - 7-16 kN, 65-100 Hz, plate 0,2-0,4 m2;
    units > 80 mm - 16-20 kN, 65-100 Hz, plate 0,35-0,5 m2.
8.3 Damaged units shall be replaced before joint filling.
8.4 Jointing sand shall be broomed (not washed) into the joints until full,
    compacted, and the process repeated until joints remain full.
8.5 Where wheel loads exceed 30 kN, final lock-up shall be at least five passes
    of a heavy pneumatic-tyred roller over the entire paved area.
8.6 No vehicular traffic shall be permitted until all joints are filled.
8.7 Heavy steel rollers shall not be used on the paving.

9.  TOLERANCES
9.1 As SANS 1200 MJ Table 6.2, Degree of Accuracy I, measured three months after
    opening to traffic. <insert the table above>
9.2 Tolerances on the units and other requirements of SANS 1058 shall not be relaxed.

10. TESTING AND ACCEPTANCE
10.1 Trial section: the first 20 m length by approximately 6 m width, commencing
     from a permanent edge restraint, shall be laid as part of the permanent works
     and approved before full production.  Unapproved trial sections shall be
     removed at the Contractor's cost.
10.2 Smoothness checks: roads to SANS 1200 M 6.3; other areas one check per
     300 m2 maximum.
10.3 The Engineer may order the surface flooded to test for ponding.  Rectification
     is at the Contractor's cost if ponding occurs; the Employer bears the cost of
     the test if it does not.
10.4 Block wet strength testing to SANS 1058 as directed.

11. COMPLETION AND MAINTENANCE
11.1 Excess sand shall be broomed off and removed on completion.
11.2 The Contractor shall return at three months to top up the joints.
11.3 Defects liability period: 12 months from practical completion, covering
     settlement, loss of joint sand, edge restraint movement, loose units and
     ponding.  Efflorescence is excluded, being a temporary natural phenomenon.
```

## Open questions

- **Waste factors are estimating conventions, not sourced data** — `needs-verification`.
- Bedding sand and jointing sand mass per m² depend on the sand's bulk density, which was not sourced; the kg/m² figures are estimates.
- Bulking factors for excavation and for compacted layerworks are conventional values and unverified.
- No current labour rates, plant hire rates or material prices for South Africa or Namibia were obtained. The only absolute cost data in this file is the CMA's July 2009 Gauteng comparison.
- Whether SANS 1200 MJ has been revised since 1984 (which would change the clause numbers used in the template) is unverified.
- The template assumes the South African SANS framework. **[NA]** In Namibia these standards apply only by contract, not by law — which is precisely why the specification must be written out in full.
