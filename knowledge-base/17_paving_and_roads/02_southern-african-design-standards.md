# Southern African road and paving design standards — the actual documents

**Summary.** Southern African road engineering runs on four document families. **TRH** (Technical Recommendations for Highways) are the design guidelines — what to build. **TMH** (Technical Methods for Highways) are the test and assessment methods — how to measure it. **COLTO/COTO Standard Specifications** are the contract specifications — how to build it and what the contractor is paid for. **SANS** standards cover the products and the smaller civil works (the SANS 1200 series). Sitting alongside them are the **UTG** urban transport guidelines, the CSIR "Blue Book" township services guidelines and the CMA's own block-paving manuals. Namibia has no parallel design-guideline series: the **Roads Authority** issues its own manual set (Construction, Drainage, Economic, Environmental, Geometrics, Materials, Procedures, Standard Drawings, Structures, Survey — all dated October 2014) and otherwise Namibian practice uses the South African documents directly, as the road-safety research literature confirms for TRH 17 and TRH 26.

## Key facts

| Family | Publisher over time | Status |
|---|---|---|
| TRH | NIRR → NITRR (CSIR) → CSRA → COLTO → COTO / SANRAL | Guidelines, not law; adopted by reference into contracts |
| TMH | NITRR (CSIR) → COLTO → COTO | Test and assessment methods |
| COLTO / COTO Standard Specifications | Committee of Land Transport Officials → Committee of Transport Officials | Contract specification; COLTO 1998 still widely used, COTO 2020 draft standard released |
| SANS 1200 series | SABS | Standardized specification for civil engineering construction |
| SANS product standards | SABS | SANS 1058 (concrete paving blocks), SANS 1575 (clay pavers), SANS 927 (kerbs) |
| UTG | Committee of Urban Transport Authorities | Urban roads and township work |
| **[NA]** RA manuals | Roads Authority of Namibia | The Namibian design/maintenance set, Oct 2014 |

> ⚠️ Do not cite a TRH number without its edition. TRH 4 has 1980, 1985 and 1996 editions with materially different catalogues; TRH 14 dates from 1985; TRH 1 exists in a 1970 and a 1986 version with different titles. Getting the edition wrong changes layer thicknesses.

| Document | Title | Publisher / year |
|---|---|---|

| **TRH 1:1986** | Prime coats and bituminous curing membranes | NITRR, CSIR, Pretoria |
| **TRH 3:1971** | Bituminous surface treatments for newly constructed rural roads | NIRR, Pretoria |
| **TRH 4:1996 (draft)** | Structural design of flexible pavements for interurban and rural roads | COLTO, Department of Transport, Pretoria |
| **TRH 12:1997 (draft)** | Flexible pavement rehabilitation investigation and design | COLTO, Department of Transport, Pretoria |
| **TRH 14:1985** | Guidelines for road construction materials | NITRR, CSIR, Pretoria |
| **TRH 17:1988** | Geometric design of rural roads | CSRA, Pretoria |
| **TRH 20:2009** | The structural design, construction and maintenance of unpaved roads | NITRR, CSIR, Pretoria |
| **TRH 26:1988** | Road classification and access management | CSRA, Pretoria |

**TRH 4** is the central document: it defines the four road categories (A–D) with approximate design reliabilities of 95 / 90 / 80 / 50 %, and contains the pavement design catalogue that most southern African flexible pavements are still built from. The 1994–95 revision expanded the catalogue to include category D and introduced reliability into the design method.

**TRH 14** is the material specification that everything else references: the G1–G10 granular classes, C1–C4 cemented classes, BC/BS bituminous bases, S1–S9 seals, WM waterbound macadam. See `01_pavement-engineering-fundamentals.md` for the full table.

**TRH 3** in its modern form covers the design and construction of surfacing seals — single, double, Cape, sand and slurry seals. The 1971 edition is the one verified here; a later revision under SANRAL is widely referenced in practice but its number and date could not be verified from a primary source and is marked **needs-verification**.

**TRH 20** governs unpaved roads: gravel wearing-course material selection, dust, ravelling, erosion, gravel loss, blading and regravelling cycles. This is the operative document for most of Namibia's 25 710 km of gravel road.

## The TMH series

| Document | Title | Publisher / year |
|---|---|---|
| **TMH 1:1986** | Standard methods of testing road construction materials | NITRR, CSIR, Pretoria |
| **TMH 6:1984** | Special methods for testing roads | NITRR, Pretoria |
| **TMH 9:1992** | Pavement management systems: standard visual assessment manual (flexible pavements) | COLTO / NITRR |

TMH 1 is the test-method reference cited throughout: e.g. Method A21T for saturated paste conductivity, Method A22T for soluble salts. Much of TMH 1 has been progressively superseded by the **SANS 3001** series of test methods (referenced in current asphalt training curricula), so laboratory schedules increasingly cite SANS 3001-xx rather than TMH 1 methods.

## COLTO and COTO standard specifications

**COLTO:1998 — Standard specifications for road and bridge works for state road authorities** is the long-serving contract specification for road works in South Africa and, by adoption, in much of the region. It is a purchase item, not a free download.

Its successor, the **COTO Standard Specifications for Road and Bridge Works, Draft Standard version October 2020**, *is* freely downloadable from SANRAL as 17 files (25,17 MB total):

## SANS standards for paving work

| Standard | Covers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SANS 1058** | Concrete paving blocks | 1985 edition specified Class 25 (average 25 MPa, individual minimum 20 MPa) and Class 35 (35/30 MPa) compressive strength on the wearing face, measured **on the day of despatch, not at 28 days**. **The current edition is SANS 1058:2021**, which moves the strength requirement to **tensile splitting** and adds an abrasion-resistance requirement plus tightened dimensional tolerances |
| **SANS 1575** | Burnt clay paving units | Classes **PA** (higher uniformity: individual tolerances 5/3/3 mm on length/width/thickness; nominal modulus of rupture 4 MPa, individual minimum 2,5 MPa) and **PB** (6/4/4 mm; 3 MPa nominal, 2 MPa minimum). | **SANS 1200 MJ** | Segmented paving — the standardized specification for laying block paving | 1984 edition. Sets bedding sand grading, jointing sand grading, joint widths 2–6 mm, compaction plant requirements, trial section, tolerances |

## Guidelines for urban and township work

- **UTG 2 (draft): Structural design of segmental block pavements for southern Africa** — the block-paving design catalogue. Classifies by road category (ER, UB, UC…), traffic class (E0–E4) and climatic region, and returns block thickness and type, 20 mm sand ("SND") and subbase. Its limitation is that it accommodates only subgrade CBR 10 % or 15 %. The CMA reproduces a representative catalogue page in Book 2.
- **Guidelines for the provision of engineering services in residential townships** — the CSIR "Blue Book"; superseded in practice by the **"Red Book" / Neighbourhood Planning and Design Guide** (CSIR / national Department of Human Settlements). Covers residential road geometry, parking, stormwater and services reticulation. *The Red Book's chapter list and download location could not be verified in this pass — marked* **needs-verification**.
- **South African Pavement Engineering Manual (SAPEM)** — a multi-chapter practitioner's manual covering the whole pavement lifecycle, published by SANRAL. It is widely cited in industry, but no live download location or chapter list could be verified from a primary source in this pass. Treat SAPEM chapter numbers and content as **needs-verification** until confirmed against SANRAL.

## Industry technical documents (free)

- **CMA Concrete Block Paving Books 1–5** (Introduction; Design Aspects; Specification and Installation; Site Management and Laying; Training Manual) plus a Facilitator's Guide — all free PDFs from cma.org.za/publications/paving. Book 2 is 4th edition, 2004. Alongside them: technical notes on Drainage of Concrete Block Paving, Steep Slopes, Good Earthwork Practice, Efflorescence, Permeable Paving, Township Roads, Cost Comparison with Premix Asphalt, and the correlation between compressive and tensile splitting strength. The CMA also distributes the **Lockpave** and **Permpave** block-pavement design software.
- **Sabita** publishes the bituminous-products manual series and the **TG** technical guidelines. TG 4 (Water quality for use in test laboratories) is downloadable free from asphaltacademy.co.za; TG 1 (bitumen binder testing) and TG 2 (bitumen-stabilised materials) are the widely used ones but are distributed through Sabita rather than an open link.

## The Namibian position **[NA]**

**Statutory framework.** The 1999 road-sector reform created three bodies by statute:

- **Roads Authority Act 17 of 1999** — establishes the Roads Authority as a juristic person "to manage the national road network of Namibia". Most provisions commenced 25 October 1999; ss 15(1)(a) and 16 on 1 April 2000. The national road network is defined as "every trunk road, main road and district road proclaimed in terms of Chapter III of the **Roads Ordinance**". Statutory objective: "To manage the national road network so that it is safe and efficient."
- **Road Fund Administration Act 18 of 1999** — assented 6 October 1999; establishes the Road Fund Administration as a juristic person to run the road user charging system and secure funding for road expenditure. Amended by the Public Enterprises Governance Act, 2006.
- The **Roads Contractor Company** — the state-owned road construction and maintenance contractor, with a head office in Windhoek and regional offices in Otjiwarongo and Keetmanshoop.

**Technical documents.** The Roads Authority's downloads page lists a complete manual set, all dated **October 2014**:

| Manual | Scope |
|---|---|
| Construction Manual | Execution of road works |
| Drainage Manual | Hydrology, culverts, stormwater |
| Economic Manual | Economic appraisal of road projects |
| Environmental Manual | Environmental management of road works |
| Geometrics Manual | Horizontal/vertical alignment, cross-sections |
| Materials Manual | Material classification, testing and use |
| Procedures Manual | Project procedures |
| Standard Drawings | Detail drawings |
| Structures Manual | Bridges and major culverts |
| Survey Manual | Survey standards |

Also listed: **BRMM Manual, March 2003** (bridge routine maintenance), and "Road Summary Network 11 (Latest)" plus a road summary by road region — the network statistics documents.

The titles and dates are verified; the contents are not. Request them directly from the Roads Authority (pr@ra.org.na, +264 61 284 7000, corner of Mandume Ndemufayo Avenue and David Hosea Meroro Road, Southern Industrial Area, Windhoek).

**What Namibia borrows.** Peer-reviewed work on Namibian road safety states that the Namibian national rural road network is divided into functional classes using **TRH 26 (CSRA, 1988)** — R1 rural principal arterial, R2 rural major arterial, R3 rural minor arterial (high-order); R4 rural collector, R5 rural local, R6 rural walkway (low-order) — and that design compliance is assessed against **TRH 17 (CSRA, 1988)**. Netterberg's Namibian experiments classify materials to COLTO (1998) G3/G4 and use TMH 1:1986 test methods and Weinert's N-value / Thornthwaite Moisture Index for climatic zoning. In short: for anything the RA manuals do not cover, the South African stack applies.

**For private paving work in Namibia there is no statutory standard at all.** Outside proclaimed local-authority areas — which includes communal land in Ohangwena Region — there is no building control and no plan approval. SANS 1058 and SANS 1200 MJ have force only because the client, the specifier or the supplier's guarantee says so. That makes the written specification the only control mechanism: see `10_specifying-and-costing-paving.md`.
