# The training pathway — how you actually become a psychologist

**Summary.** Four national routes are set out below. The structural facts that matter most: **[ZA]** the bottleneck is not the degree but the **selection interview for the professional master's**, where single-figure intake numbers against three-figure applicant pools are normal; the UK bottleneck is the funded doctorate place; the US bottleneck is cost and the licensing examination. Everywhere, the pattern is the same shape — a broad accredited undergraduate degree, a filtering postgraduate year, a fiercely competitive professional programme built around supervised practice, a period of internship, and an examination before independent registration.

## 1. **[ZA]** South Africa — the HPCSA route

### 1.1 Undergraduate: BA or BSc with a psychology major

Three years, at any of the universities offering an accredited major. Psychology is one of the largest undergraduate subjects in South Africa by headcount — first-year classes of well over a thousand are routine at UCT, Wits, UP, UJ, Stellenbosch and UNISA. This is the first place the funnel narrows: the overwhelming majority of psychology majors will never register as psychologists, and this should be said plainly to anyone entering the degree. The undergraduate degree is a **general social-science education**, not a professional qualification, and is worth having on its own terms.

Two variants exist at the four-year level:

- **BPsych** — a four-year professional bachelor's degree incorporating a supervised practicum, designed as the qualifying route to the **Registered Counsellor** category. Not all universities offer it.
- **BA/BSc (3 yrs) + Honours in Psychology (1 yr)** — the standard academic route.

### 1.2 Honours in Psychology — the first hard filter

A one-year coursework-plus-research-project degree. Entry is already selective (typically a strong average in third-year psychology modules), and intakes are usually in the low tens per department where hundreds apply. Honours is where research methods and statistics get serious, where psychometrics is formally taught, and where the student produces a supervised mini-dissertation.

Honours alone confers **no** right to practise. Its function in the system is to be the pool from which master's selection draws.

### 1.3 The professional master's — the real bottleneck

The professional (as opposed to research) master's is the qualification that leads to registration as a psychologist. For educational psychology the awards are typically:

- **MEd (Educational Psychology)** — housed in a Faculty of Education
- **MA (Psychology) / MA (Educational Psychology)** — housed in a Faculty of Humanities or Arts
- **MEdPsych** or similar local variants

Parallel programmes exist for **Clinical**, **Counselling**, **Industrial/Organisational** and **Research** psychology; UCT, for example, lists MA in Clinical Psychology, MA in Psychological Research and MA in Neuropsychology among its master's offerings.

Structure: two years. Year 1 is coursework — psychopathology, assessment, intervention/therapy, professional ethics, research methods — plus a practicum with observed and supervised casework, plus the start of a dissertation. Year 2 is the **internship** (below), usually with the dissertation running alongside and frequently finishing late.

**Selection.** This is the defining experience of the South African route and the thing prospective students most consistently underestimate. Programmes typically admit **6 to 12 students a year**. Applications routinely run into the **hundreds**. Selection involves a written application, academic transcript screening, references, often a written test or essay under exam conditions, a group selection exercise, and one or more panel interviews. Many successful candidates apply **two, three or four times**, working as assistant psychologists, research assistants, teachers, HR officers or NGO workers in between. Applying to a single programme is close to futile; applying to six or eight in one cycle is normal practice.

Selection panels are also, explicitly, applying transformation and equity criteria under South African higher-education policy, and language competence in an African language other than English or Afrikaans is a genuine and increasing advantage — it is also a genuine professional competence, given the assessment issues in file `04`.

### 1.4 The 12-month accredited internship

Registration as an **intern psychologist** with the HPCSA precedes and covers this period. The internship must be served at an **accredited** internship site — a hospital, a psychiatric facility, a university student counselling service, a school district office, a specialised school, an NGO, or an accredited private practice — under a registered psychologist supervisor in the same category. Educational psychology internships are commonly served at schools, district-based support teams, remedial and special schools, university student support services, and children's hospitals.

Internship supply is itself a bottleneck: the number of accredited posts constrains the number of students programmes can admit, and this — more than teaching capacity — is why intakes are small.

### 1.5 Community service

**[ZA]** Compulsory 12-month community service in the public sector applies to **clinical psychologists**. Educational, counselling, industrial and research psychologists are, on current understanding, not subject to it. *This has been proposed for extension at various times — verify current position.*

### 1.6 The National Board Examination

Before independent registration the candidate must pass the HPCSA's **National Board Examination** for their category. It is a written examination covering professional practice, ethics, the statutory framework, assessment and intervention within the category's scope. Separate board examinations apply to **Registered Counsellors** and **Psychometrists**. Pass rates and the exact format are not publicly summarised in a source reachable from this environment — `needs-verification`.

### 1.7 Independent registration and after

On passing, the practitioner registers in the relevant category and may practise independently, using the protected title. Thereafter: **annual fees**, **continuing professional development** (a points-based system with an ethics component), and liability under the HPCSA's ethical rules and disciplinary machinery (file `07`).

### 1.8 The two other statutory categories

- **Registered Counsellor.** Four-year BPsych or equivalent accredited degree including a supervised practicum, plus a board examination. Scope: psychological screening, basic supportive and preventive counselling, psycho-education, and referral. Explicitly **not** psychotherapy, diagnosis, or independent psychological assessment beyond permitted screening instruments. The category was created to put a large, cheap, appropriately-scoped workforce into schools, clinics, NGOs and workplaces where psychologists were never going to be available. Its practical difficulty has always been that the labour market and the medical schemes did not build posts and reimbursement to match, so many Registered Counsellors struggle to find work in the role.
- **Psychometrist (Independent Practice).** Trained to administer and score psychological tests and, within a defined scope, report on them. Central to school assessment, career guidance and workplace selection. Again, the boundary is interpretation: complex clinical interpretation and diagnosis sit with psychologists.

### 1.9 Realistic cost **[ZA]**

Public-university fees vary enormously by institution and are revised annually; a professional master's is generally in the **tens of thousands of rand per year**, with the internship year typically carrying a **stipend** rather than a salary. Add the opportunity cost of seven or eight years out of full earnings. **Do not quote a figure without checking the institution's current fee schedule** — no verified figures are given here.

## 2. **[NA]** Namibia — the Health Professions Councils of Namibia route

The regulator is the **Health Professions Councils of Namibia (HPCNA)**, which oversees five professional boards: the Allied Health Professions Board, the Medical and Dental Board, the Nursing Board, the Pharmacy Board, and the **Social Work and Psychology Board of Namibia**. Psychologists register with the last of these. The board publishes, on the HPCNA site, its own **Act**, **Minimum Requirements**, **Registration**, **Scope of Practice**, **Additional Examinations**, **Conditions to Practice**, **CPD** and **Guidelines and Regulations** pages, and maintains an **E-Register** of practitioners.

The registration categories, minimum qualifications and any additional examination requirement must be confirmed directly with HPCNA (+264 61 555 800; 36/37/46 Schonlein Street, Windhoek West).

### 2.1 The practical Namibian reality

Namibia has no large domestic professional-master's pipeline in psychology. In practice:

- **UNAM** (University of Namibia) offers psychology at undergraduate level and postgraduate provision in psychology and in education; **NUST** (Namibia University of Science and Technology) offers human-sciences and applied provision. *The precise current programme names and whether either institution offers an HPCNA-recognised professional master's leading to registration as a psychologist could not be verified from this environment — `needs-verification`.*
- The dominant route to becoming a registered psychologist in Namibia is therefore to **qualify in South Africa** — undergraduate and honours at UNAM or a South African university, then a South African professional master's and internship — and then to apply to HPCNA for registration on the basis of that qualification. The Social Work and Psychology Board's "Additional Examinations" page exists precisely because foreign-qualified applicants may be required to sit one.
- The consequence is a **very small profession**: a few dozen registered psychologists for a population of roughly 2.6 million, heavily concentrated in Windhoek, with almost no coverage in the northern regions. Educational psychology services in Namibian government schools are delivered predominantly by the Ministry of Education's regional support structures, school counsellors and teachers rather than by registered psychologists.
- Cross-border practice matters: a South African-registered psychologist is **not** thereby entitled to practise in Namibia, and vice versa. Registration is national.

## 3. UK — the BPS/HCPC route to educational psychologist

### 3.1 Accredited degree and Graduate Basis for Chartered Membership

Step one is a **British Psychological Society (BPS) accredited** undergraduate degree in psychology, achieved at 2:1 or above, which confers **Graduate Basis for Chartered Membership (GBC)**. Applicants whose first degree is in another subject take a **BPS-accredited conversion course** (typically a one-year MSc) to obtain GBC.

### 3.2 Relevant experience

At least **one year's full-time experience working with children** in educational, childcare or community settings is required before the doctorate; some courses require two. Typical routes in: teaching assistant, learning support assistant, assistant psychologist, residential childcare, youth work, speech and language assistant, or teaching itself. **Teaching experience is no longer an entry requirement** in England, though it remains common and is viewed favourably.

### 3.3 The doctorate

Three years full-time: the **Doctorate in Educational Psychology (DEdPsy)** or **Doctorate in Educational and Child Psychology (DEdChPsy)**, accredited by the BPS and approved by the **Health and Care Professions Council (HCPC)**. It combines taught content (developmental and learning theory, assessment, consultation, systemic and organisational work, SEND legislation, therapeutic approaches), **supervised placements in local authority educational psychology services**, and a doctoral thesis. In **Scotland** the qualifying route is an accredited **master's** in educational psychology plus a supervised probationary year — a genuine structural difference from England, Wales and Northern Ireland.

Funding in England has for years been administered centrally with a bursary/salary arrangement for trainees; the arrangements change and should be checked with the AEP and the current commissioning body. Competition for places is severe — small cohorts per course, hundreds of applicants.

### 3.4 Registration

To use the title **educational psychologist** in the UK you must be registered with the **HCPC**, which requires completion of an HCPC-approved programme. "Practitioner psychologist" and the specific titles under it are protected in law; "psychologist" on its own is not. Chartered status (CPsychol) with the BPS is a separate, voluntary professional standing.

## 4. US — the school psychology route

### 4.1 Two degree levels

- **Specialist level (EdS or equivalent)** — "3–4 years of graduate training including a 9-month (1200 hour) internship in a school setting". This is the **entry-level credential for practice** as a school psychologist and covers the large majority of practitioners.
- **Doctoral level (PhD, PsyD, EdD)** — "5–7 years of graduate training", with more psychology coursework, advanced statistics, research involvement, a dissertation, and a **one-year (1500+ hour) internship**, which may be in schools or in clinics and hospitals.

**NASP (National Association of School Psychologists) programme approval** operates alongside APA accreditation (APA accredits doctoral programmes only). NASP-approved specialist programmes are generally built to a 60-graduate-semester-hour minimum with a 1200-hour internship of which at least 600 hours are in a school. *Verify the current credit-hour and internship figures against the NASP Standards for Graduate Preparation — the NASP site returned a server error from this environment.*

### 4.2 Credentialing — a two-track system that confuses everyone

- **Most states credential school psychologists through their state education agency** — a school-based credential, not a psychology licence. Texas and Hawaii are the noted exceptions; Texas uses the **LSSP (Licensed Specialist in School Psychology)**, issued through the state psychology board.
- Some states additionally offer, or require, licensure through the **state board of psychology**, which is generally doctoral-only for the title "psychologist".
- The **NCSP (Nationally Certified School Psychologist)** credential from NASP is a **national, non-practice credential** — holding it does not by itself permit service delivery; the state requirement still governs. Its value is portability: many states accept the NCSP as satisfying part of their own requirements.

### 4.3 The EPPP

The **Examination for Professional Practice in Psychology**, administered by ASPPB, is required for psychology licensure in essentially all US and Canadian jurisdictions and is computer-administered.

**Part 1 (Knowledge)** covers eight domains with published weightings: biological bases of behaviour (10%), cognitive-affective bases (13%), social and multicultural bases (11%), growth and lifespan development (12%), **assessment and diagnosis (16%)**, treatment/intervention/prevention/supervision (15%), research methods and statistics (7%), and **ethical, legal and professional issues (16%)**.

**Part 2 (Skills)** covers six domains — scientific orientation (6%), **assessment and intervention (33%)**, relational competence (16%), professionalism (11%), ethical practice (17%), collaboration/consultation/supervision (17%) — and uses multiple-choice/multiple-response, scenario-based, point-and-click and drag-and-drop items. Not every jurisdiction has adopted Part 2; check the specific state board.

State licensure also typically requires a defined number of **supervised postdoctoral hours** and a **state jurisprudence examination**. Specialist-level school psychologists credentialed through education agencies frequently do not sit the EPPP at all — another reason the two tracks must not be conflated.

### 4.4 Cost

US doctoral training in psychology is the most expensive route in this file by a wide margin. Funded PhD places (tuition remission plus stipend) exist and are competitive; PsyD programmes are frequently unfunded and produce six-figure debt in US dollars. EdS programmes are cheaper and shorter. No specific figures are given here — they vary by institution and year and must be checked.

## 5. Cross-cutting realities

1. **The internship, not the coursework, is where the profession is actually learned.** Every route in this file is organised around supervised contact with real cases. Programmes that shorten or dilute supervised practice produce practitioners who cannot function.
2. **Attrition is front-loaded and brutal.** In South Africa the ratio of first-year psychology students to eventual registered psychologists is of the order of hundreds to one. Anyone advising a school-leaver should say this.
3. **Second and third applications are normal**, not a signal of failure. Selection panels expect it and often prefer applicants with intervening real-world experience.
4. **The qualifications do not port cleanly across borders.** A South African professional master's is a master's; the UK and increasingly the US expect a doctorate. Recognition applications are slow, sometimes require bridging study or additional examinations, and should be started long before relocation.
5. **Registration is annual and revocable.** CPD, fees and ethical compliance are ongoing conditions of the licence, not one-off hurdles.
