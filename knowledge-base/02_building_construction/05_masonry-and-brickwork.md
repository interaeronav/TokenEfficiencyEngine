# Masonry and brickwork

**Summary.** Masonry in southern Africa is governed by **SANS 10400-K** (deemed-to-satisfy rules for walls) and built to **SANS 2001-CM1** (masonry walling construction standard), with mortar strength classes I and II defined in that standard. For a single-storey house the critical numbers are: maximum wall panel length and height for the unit thickness you are using, Class II mortar at 50 kg cement to 200 ℓ sand, control joints at the correct spacing for the unit type, lintels with 150 mm minimum bearing, and roof anchors of the correct type embedded to the correct depth. Get those six right and the wall will not crack.

## 1. Units

### Clay bricks

Standard **imperial brick 222 × 106 × 73 mm**. Other manufactured combinations (length × width × height, mm) include 222×90×73, 222×40×73, 222×90×114, 222×140×114, 220×110×73, 190×90×90, 190×106×90, 290×90×90, 290×150×190 and 390×190×90.

Classes (SANS 227): **FBX** face brick extra (tightest tolerance), **FBS** face brick standard, **FBA** face brick aesthetic, **NFP** non-facing plastered, **NFX** non-facing extra. Compressive strengths range from about **7 MPa for NFP to over 50 MPa** for FBX and engineering brick.

Tolerance on work sizes:

| Class | Length | Width | Height |
|---|---|---|---|
| FBX, individual units | ±5 mm | ±3 mm | ±3 mm |
| FBS, individual units | ±7 mm | ±4 mm | ±4 mm |
| FBX, average of 32 units | ±2,5 mm | ±1,5 mm | ±1,5 mm |
| FBS, average of 32 units | ±3,5 mm | ±2 mm | ±2 mm |
| NFP, NFX, average of 32 | ±3,5 mm | ±2 mm | ±2 mm |

Warpage: FBX individual units ≤ 5 mm, and in not more than three units of a sample may warpage exceed 3 mm; FBS and engineering ≤ 5 mm individual; FBA and NFP no requirement.

**Irreversible moisture expansion** is the property that drives control-joint spacing in clay masonry: burnt clay units generally must not exceed **0,20 %**, most of it occurring in the first six months after firing.

**Efflorescence** is graded nil / slight / moderate / heavy / serious under SANS 227 Table 4, with limits on how many units of a sample may show each degree, differing for special and normal grade.

### Concrete masonry units

Made to SANS 1215. Manufactured from cement, coarse sand (max particle ~5 mm) and stone of maximum size **13 mm for bricks or solid blocks, 10 mm for hollow blocks**. Concrete units are more porous than clay and exhibit **reversible** moisture movement of 3–6 mm per 10 m of walling against clay's ~1 mm per 10 m — which is why concrete masonry needs closer control joints and why plastered-and-painted concrete block walls need re-decorating more often.

> ⚠️ **Do not wet concrete bricks and blocks before laying. Do wet burnt clay bricks before laying** (Cement & Concrete SA). Mixing clay and concrete units in the same wall is a reliable way to crack it, because the two move in opposite directions.

Adjustments: for **15 mm joints** multiply mortar by 1,5; for **20 mm joints** by 2. For hollow units reduce mortar by 20 % (90–110 mm wide), 30 % (140 mm), 40 % (190–220 mm). For perforated units add 15 %. For frogged units laid frog-up (as required for structural walls) add 15 %. Ready-mixed mortar delivered into watertight containers: reduce by 20 % against site-mixed. **Then add 20–50 % for wastage.**

## 2. Mortar

### Classes and mixes (SANS 2001-CM1:2012)

| Class | Preliminary lab strength | Works test strength | Sand per 50 kg common cement | Sand per 50 kg MC 22,5X | Bags common cement per m³ | Sand m³ per m³ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | 14,5 MPa | 10 MPa | **130 ℓ** | 80 ℓ | 10,0 | 1,25 |
| II | 7 MPa | 5 MPa | **200 ℓ** | 130 ℓ | 7 | 1,35 |

Sand estimated at 5 % moisture content. **Class II is the general-purpose mortar for all brickwork.**

Use classes as follows:
- **Class I** — highly stressed masonry with high-strength structural units: multi-storey loadbearing buildings, reinforced masonry.
- **Class II** — normal loadbearing applications, parapets, balustrades, retaining structures, freestanding and garden walls, and any wall exposed to possible severe dampness.

Volume batching in barrows: **200 ℓ of sand is 3 wheelbarrows** (65 ℓ each) per 2 × 50 kg bags for Class II; **130 ℓ is 2 barrows** for Class I. Small batches: Class II = 1 part cement : 5 parts building sand; Class I = 1 : 3½.

To lay **1 000 bricks** you need approximately: Class II — 150 kg cement + 0,6 m³ sand; Class I — 200 kg cement + 0,55 m³ sand.

### Lime and admixtures

Use only **type A2P or A3P building lime to SANS 523**. Never quicklime, limewash or agricultural lime. Lime improves workability, plasticity and water retention, particularly with single-sized or coarse sands that lack fines. A 25 kg bag of lime has a nominal volume of 40 ℓ. Adding lime does not change the mix proportions; it increases yield by about 5 %. **Never use lime, or an air-entraining "mortar plasticiser", with masonry cement.**

### Sand

Sand should comply with SANS 1090 for mortar sand, or at least: no organic material, nothing retained on a 5 mm sieve, and it must pass the **water-demand field test** — mix 5 kg cement with 25 kg dry sand; if 5 ℓ of water brings it to laying consistence the sand is *good*; 5 + 1 ℓ is *average*; 5 + 1 + 1,5 ℓ is *poor*; more than 7,5 ℓ is *very poor*. Use only good or average sand for mortar, and for walling below DPC use good or average only.

River, dune and beach sands are often too uniform or too fine and need blending with a coarser sand (for example a river sand of particle size < 4 mm) to comply.

### Mixing and use

Mix on a clean hard surface — a smooth concrete floor or a steel sheet. Mix sand, cement (and lime) dry to uniform colour before adding water in small amounts. Small batches may be mixed in a wheelbarrow provided the batch is no more than half the barrow's capacity. If you are working alone or with one assistant, mix several small batches rather than a one-bag batch.

**Mortar must not be used after it has started to set — about two hours after mixing.** Discard mortar that has stiffened so far that workability cannot be restored without adding water. Cover mortar left standing in the sun with plastic sheeting or a wet sack. A joint that is too thick is wasteful and cracks.

Output benchmark from Cement & Concrete SA: *"One man — particularly if he is a weekend builder — can probably lay a little more than 60 bricks an hour."* Treat that as the floor, not the target, for a trade bricklayer.

## 3. Bonds, coursing and gauge

**Stretcher bond** — every unit laid stretcher-face out, perpends staggered by half a unit. The default for 90/110 mm single-leaf walls and for each leaf of a cavity wall.

**English bond** — alternating courses of headers and stretchers. Strongest traditional bond for a solid 222 mm wall.

**Flemish bond** — headers and stretchers alternating within each course. Decorative; slightly weaker than English.

**Stack bond** — no overlap; has no structural bond and must be reinforced. Avoid unless designed.

**Collar-jointed wall** — two parallel single-leaf walls with a gap **not exceeding 25 mm** filled solidly with mortar and tied with crimp wire ties. Behaves as one thick wall; commonly built as 190 mm or 220 mm.

**Cavity wall** — two leaves with a cavity **≥ 50 mm and ≤ 110 mm**, tied with metal wall ties. The cavity must be kept clean of mortar droppings, extended 150 mm below DPC, and drained with weepholes at ≤ 1 m centres in the course below the DPC.

### Coursing and gauge

Set a **gauge rod** at the start of every wall: a straight batten marked with the course heights, allowing for the 10 mm bed joint. For the imperial brick (73 mm high) the gauge is **83 mm per course**. Mark on the rod the DPC level, sill level, head-of-opening level, wall plate level. Every corner, every day, is checked against the gauge rod, not against yesterday's work.

Working practice: build the corners first (a "lead") to 5–6 courses, plumb and gauge them, then run the line between them and fill in. Never build more than about 1,2–1,5 m of wall height in a day in fresh mortar — the wall will sink and bulge.

## 4. Wall thickness and panel size rules (SANS 10400-K)

The deemed-to-satisfy provisions apply to dwellings, dwelling houses, educational buildings, hospitals, hotels, other institutional occupancies and general offices where the **imposed floor load does not exceed 3,0 kN/m²** and the building is not exposed to severe wind from a ridge, crest or escarpment.

**Single-storey (or upper storey of a double-storey) limits:**
- Roof truss/rafter span between supporting walls: **≤ 6,0 m** for a single-leaf wall thinner than 110 mm; **≤ 8,0 m** for 140 mm or greater single-leaf walls and all cavity and collar-jointed walls.
- Nominal height of masonry above openings: **≥ 0,4 m**.
- Unit strength: hollow ≥ 3,0 MPa, solid ≥ 4,0 MPa.
- Class II mortar to SANS 2001-CM1.
- Mass of roof covering other than a concrete slab: **≤ 80 kg/m²**.
- Concrete roof slab span between supporting walls: **≤ 6,0 m**; slab thickness ≤ 255 mm solid.
- Foundation walls not thinner than the walls they support; foundation wall height **≤ 1,5 m**.

**Lower storey of a double storey** additionally requires hollow units ≥ 7,0 MPa and solid ≥ 10 MPa; height from ground floor to the top of an external gable ≤ 8,0 m; storey height floor to wall-plate or to underside of first floor ≤ 3,0 m; walls supporting floor elements of cavity construction or ≥ 140 mm nominal thickness.

### Maximum wall panel dimensions (SANS 10400-K Table 1, solid units, horizontal + vertical support)

L = maximum length, H = corresponding maximum height, in metres. Two alternative pairs per panel type; interpolate linearly between the pair but not between wall types.

| Nominal thickness | Wall type | No openings L / H | Openings ≤ 15 % L / H | Openings > 15 % L / H |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90 mm | single leaf | 3,2 / 2,4 · 2,8 / 3,4 | 2,7 / 2,4 · 2,5 / 3,4 | 2,7 / 2,4 · 2,3 / 3,4 |
| 90-90 | cavity | 5,5 / 2,7 · 5,5 / 3,9 | 5,5 / 2,7 · 5,0 / 3,9 | 5,5 / 2,4 · 4,5 / 3,9 |
| 110 mm | single leaf | 4,5 / 2,7 · 4,0 / 3,6 | 4,0 / 2,7 · 3,5 / 3,6 | 3,5 / 2,7 · 3,0 / 3,6 |
| 110-110 | cavity | 7,0 / 3,3 · 6,0 / 4,4 | 7,0 / 2,4 · 5,5 / 4,4 | 6,5 / 2,4 · 5,0 / 4,4 |
| 140 mm | single leaf | 7,0 / 3,3 · 6,0 / 4,3 | 6,5 / 2,4 · 5,2 / 4,3 | 6,0 / 2,7 · 5,0 / 4,3 |
| 190 mm | collar jointed | 8,0 / 4,6 (all cases) | 8,0 / 4,6 | 8,0 / 4,0 · 7,5 / 4,6 |
| 220 mm | collar jointed | 9,0 / 4,6 (all cases) | 9,0 / 4,6 | 9,0 / 4,6 |

Hollow-unit panels are smaller (e.g. 140 mm single leaf: 5,5 / 2,4 · 4,5 / 4,2 with no openings) **unless** reinforced with truss-type brickforce of main wires ≥ 3,55 mm diameter at vertical centres ≤ 400 mm, plus two 5,6 mm rods (90/110 mm walls) or a single Y8 in a bond block (140/190 mm walls) in the bed joint immediately above window level, extending the full panel length into the supports — in which case the solid-unit values may be used.

Panels incorporating full-height doors, or doors with fanlights, are treated as **supported on one side only**.

## 5. Control and movement joints

Masonry moves. Clay expands irreversibly and permanently after firing; concrete shrinks as it dries and then moves reversibly with moisture. South Africa's ~20 °C daily temperature swing adds thermal movement on top. Coefficients of linear thermal movement (×10⁻⁶ per °C): clay brick **4–8**, concrete brick **7–14**, concrete block similar to concrete brick but larger units magnify the movement.

**Maximum spacing between vertical control joints (SANS 10400-K Table 19):**

| Unit type | Moisture expansion | Free-standing wall | Building |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burnt clay | < 0,05 % | 16 m | 18 m |
| Burnt clay | 0,05–0,10 % | 10 m | 14 m |
| Burnt clay | 0,11–0,20 % | 6 m | 10 m |
| Concrete | — | 5,0–7,0 m | 8 m |
| *With bed joint reinforcement at ≤ 450 mm vertical centres:* | | | |
| Burnt clay | < 0,05 % | 16 m | 18 m |
| Burnt clay | 0,05–0,10 % | 12 m | 16 m |
| Burnt clay | 0,11–0,20 % | 8 m | 12 m |
| Concrete | — | 10 m | 12 m |

In hollow-unit construction, a single Y8 bar in bond beams at centres ≤ 1 200 mm (typically below slabs, below sills, above windows and doors, and in the top course) may be treated as equivalent to bed joint reinforcement.

Additional rules:
- A vertical control joint is required **where there is a storey-height change in the external walling** and where setbacks produce a return on plan of **less than 800 mm**.
- Vertical control joints in free-standing walls extend to the **top of the foundation**.
- Control joints are butt joints across the full width of the masonry.
- Bed joint reinforcement must be **discontinuous across a tied control joint**.
- Vertical control joints in external walls must be detailed to resist rain penetration — that means a backing rod and an elastomeric sealant, not an open gap.
- A **10 mm horizontal control joint** is required between the top of internal and external clay masonry walls and the frame in framed buildings.

## 6. Wall ties, brickforce and reinforcement

- Cavity walls are tied with metal wall ties; collar-jointed walls with **crimp wire ties**, typically located in bed joints at **5 per m²**.
- **[ZA]/[NA] coastal and corrosive environments** (within 30 km of the coastline, or 1 km of the shoreline, or 3 km of industry discharging corrosive pollutants): ties require a minimum galvanizing of **750 g/m²**; brickforce from pre-galvanized wire to SANS 935 grade 2; rod reinforcement galvanized to SANS 935 grade 2 or SANS 121. **In tidal and splash zones use stainless steel (grade 316).** Okongo is far inland, so standard galvanised ties are adequate — but the same is not true of a coastal job at Swakopmund or Walvis Bay.
- Truss-type brickforce: main wires **≥ 3,55 mm** diameter, built into courses at vertical centres **≤ 400 mm** where it is being used to upgrade hollow-unit panels.

## 7. Lintels

Three deemed-to-satisfy lintel types in SANS 10400-K clause 4.2.9: **bed-joint reinforced**, **bond-block**, and **precast prestressed concrete to SANS 1504**.

Key rules common to all:
- Lintels are required **above all window and door openings**.
- Minimum bearing when set in mortar: **150 mm** supporting masonry only; **150 mm** for trusses spanning ≤ 1,5 m; **250 mm** for trusses spanning between 1,5 m and 2,5 m.
- Secondary bed joint reinforcement is required in the uppermost bed joint of the lintel.
- Where the pier between openings is **less than 750 mm wide**, primary reinforcement must also be provided in the uppermost bed joint.
- Lintels must be **adequately supported (propped) for not less than 7 days** after completion.
- In hollow-unit walls, the cores immediately adjacent to openings are reinforced with a single Y10 bar from floor level to the top of the lintel and filled solidly with grade 25 infill concrete.
- Lintels supporting concrete floors or roofs, or timber floors, fall **outside** SANS 10400-K and must be designed to SANS 10400-B.

## 8. Roof fixing

Anchor types (SANS 10400-K 4.2.11): **Type A** = two strands of 2,4 mm diameter galvanized steel wire; **Type B** = 30 × 1,2 mm galvanized steel strap; **Type C** = 30 × 1,6 mm galvanized steel strap.

| Roof slope | Max truss/rafter spacing | Anchor required (light roof) |
|---|---|---|
| < 15° | 760 mm | A, B or C |
| < 15° | 1 050 mm | B or C |
| < 15° | 1 350 mm | C |
| 15–30° | 760 mm | A, B or C |
| 15–30° | 1 050 mm | B or C |
| 15–30° | 1 350 mm | C |
| > 30° | any | A, B or C |
| Heavy roof, 15–30° | — | A for all applications |

Wire is not permitted for lightweight roof coverings unless truss/rafter spacing is 760 mm maximum.

Minimum embedment depth (Table 30, solid units): **heavy roof 300 mm; light roof 600 mm**. Hoop-iron straps must be embedded in mortar in bed joints to a depth of **not less than 70 mm**.

> ⚠️ A light sheeted roof on a low pitch generates the greatest uplift and therefore needs the *deepest* anchor embedment — 600 mm, roughly seven courses of brickwork. This is the detail most often skimped on and it is the reason roofs leave buildings in a squall.

## 9. Rain penetration and DPC

SANS 10400-K Annexure C sets rain-penetration acceptance criteria by building category. A **Category 1 building** is one of class A3, A4, F2, G1, H2, H3 or H4 occupancy, with no basements, a maximum of 6,0 m between intersecting walls or lateral supports, and a floor area not exceeding 80 m². For Category 1 the criterion is that moisture penetrating the wall is of insufficient intensity to run down the wall onto the floor; for all other categories, **no damp patches may be visible on the inside of the wall**.

DPC placement rules are in `03_groundworks-and-foundations.md` §5.
