# Construction health and safety

**Summary.** **[ZA]** Construction health and safety is governed by the **Construction Regulations, 2014** made under the Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993: a client-side **construction work permit** above defined thresholds, written **appointments** of a construction manager and competent persons, a **health and safety file** kept on site, **risk assessments** by a competent person, a **fall protection plan**, and prescribed regimes for excavation, scaffolding and plant. **[NA]** Namibia's framework is the **Regulations relating to the Health and Safety of Employees at Work, GN 156 of 1997** under the Labour Act: 30 days' notice before building work commences, appointment of supervisors and safety officers, workplace safety representatives, and detailed prescriptive rules for scaffolding, roof work, excavation, welfare and first aid.

## 1. **[ZA]** Construction Regulations 2014 — the structure

### Regulation 3 — Construction work permit

A **client** who intends to have construction work carried out must apply to the provincial director in writing **at least 30 days** before the work starts, for a construction work permit, if the intended construction work will:
- **(a)** exceed **180 days**; or
- **(b)** involve more than **1 800 person days** of construction work; or
- **(c)** be a works contract of a value **equal to or exceeding thirteen million rand**, or cidb grading level 6.

The provincial director must issue the permit within 30 days of receiving a complete application and must assign a **site-specific number** for each construction site, which must be conspicuously displayed at the main entrance and is not transferable. **No construction work may be commenced before the permit and number have been issued.** The principal contractor keeps a copy in the health and safety file.

The permit is granted only if the client's health and safety specification and the baseline risk assessment have been submitted, and proof is provided of the principal contractor's registration and good standing.

### Regulation 4 — Notification of construction work

A **contractor** carrying out construction work below the permit threshold must notify the provincial director in writing **at least 7 days** before starting, where the work will include:
- excavation work;
- working at a height where there is a risk of falling;
- demolition of a structure; or
- use of explosives.

And specifically — relevant to almost every house build — a contractor who intends to carry out construction work involving **construction of a single-storey dwelling for a client who is going to reside in it** must notify at least 7 days before the work starts.

### Regulation 5 — Duties of the client

The client must, among other things, prepare a **health and safety specification** and a **baseline risk assessment**, provide these to designers and to every tendering principal contractor, ensure sufficient health and safety funds are allowed in the tender, appoint a principal contractor in writing, and ensure that a health and safety file is kept.

### Regulation 6 — Duties of the designer

The designer must consider the health and safety of persons during construction, maintenance and eventual demolition, and provide the client with all relevant information about the design.

### Regulation 7 — Health and safety plan and file

The principal contractor must provide a **documented health and safety plan** based on the client's specification, and must **open and keep on site a health and safety file** containing all documentation required by the Act and Regulations, available on request to an inspector, the client, the client's agent or a contractor. On completion the principal contractor hands over a **consolidated health and safety file** to the client, including a record of all drawings, designs and materials used.

The principal contractor must also:
- Give tendering contractors the relevant sections of the health and safety specification;
- Satisfy himself that any contractor appointed has the necessary competencies and resources;
- Ensure, **before work starts**, that every contractor is registered and in good standing with the compensation fund or a licensed compensation insurer (COIDA);
- Appoint each contractor **in writing** for their part of the work;
- Conduct **periodic site audits and document verification at least once every 30 days**;
- **Stop** any contractor whose work does not accord with the health and safety plan or poses a threat to persons;
- Ensure all his employees hold a valid **medical certificate of fitness** for the construction work, in the form of Annexure 3.

Every other contractor must likewise produce a health and safety plan and open and keep a health and safety file on site.

### Regulation 8 — Management and supervision

The principal contractor must appoint, in writing, **one full-time competent person as the construction manager** responsible for managing all construction work on a single site, including OHS compliance, with an alternate for their absence. Assistant construction managers must be appointed for different sections as the size of the project requires; an inspector may direct that more be appointed. **A construction manager may not manage construction work on any site other than the one for which they are appointed.**

A **construction health and safety officer** must be appointed in writing, full- or part-time, after consultation with the client, having considered the size of the project and the degree and accumulation of hazards.

### Regulation 9 — Risk assessment

Before and during construction work, the contractor must have **risk assessments performed by a competent person appointed in writing**, forming part of the health and safety plan, and including:
- (a) identification of the risks and hazards to which persons may be exposed;
- (b) analysis and evaluation of those risks based on a **documented method**;
- (c) a documented plan and applicable **safe work procedures** to mitigate, reduce or control them;
- (d) a **monitoring plan**; and
- (e) a **review plan**.

Ergonomic hazards must be analysed as far as reasonably practicable. All employees must be **informed, instructed and trained by a competent person** about hazards and control measures before work commences and thereafter as the monitoring/review plan requires. The principal contractor must inform all contractors of the hazards. Copies must be available on site for inspection. The risk assessment must be **reviewed** where design or construction changes alter the risk profile, or **when an incident has occurred**.

### Regulation 10 — Fall protection

The contractor must designate a competent person to prepare a **fall protection plan**, ensure it is implemented, amended when necessary and maintained, and take steps to ensure continued adherence.

The plan must include:
- (a) a **risk assessment of all work carried out from a fall risk position**, and the procedures and methods used to address every risk identified per location;
- (b) processes for evaluating employees' **medical fitness** to work at a fall risk position, and the records;
- (c) a **training programme** for employees working from a fall risk position, and the records;
- (d) procedures for **inspection, testing and maintenance** of all fall protection equipment; and
- (e) a **rescue plan** detailing the procedure, personnel and suitable equipment to rescue a person after a fall, implemented immediately following the incident.

The construction manager must hold the most recently updated version of the plan.

Additionally, all unprotected openings in floors, edges, slabs, hatchways and stairways must be adequately guarded, fenced or barricaded; fall prevention and fall arrest equipment must be approved as suitable and of sufficient strength and securely attached to a structure of sufficient strength and stability; and **fall arrest equipment is used only where it is not reasonably practicable to use fall prevention equipment**.

No employee may work on roofs during inclement weather or where the wind makes it unsafe.

### Regulation 13 — Excavation

See `03_groundworks-and-foundations.md` §3 for the detail. In summary: a competent person appointed in writing supervises all excavation work and evaluates ground stability before work starts; shoring or bracing is required unless the sides are sloped to at least the maximum angle of repose or the material is stable with the written permission of the competent person (with a professional engineer's decision decisive where uncertainty remains); no load, material, plant or equipment may be placed or moved near the edge where it could cause collapse; safe access must be no further than **6 m** from any worker in the excavation; services must be located before excavation; every excavation including bracing and shoring must be inspected **daily prior to each shift, after every blasting operation, after an unexpected fall of ground, after damage to supports, and after rain**, with results recorded in a register kept on site.

### Regulations 16 and 17 — Scaffolding and suspended platforms

A competent person must be appointed **in writing** to ensure all scaffolding work operations are carried out under their supervision and that all scaffold erectors, team leaders and inspectors are competent. Access scaffolding in use must comply with the safety standards incorporated into the Regulations — **scaffolds must comply with SANS 10085**.

For suspended platforms: a competent person appointed in writing; a copy of the design must be submitted; outriggers meeting prescribed requirements; **each person on a suspended platform must be provided with and wear a body harness**; and the installation and all working parts must be inspected by a competent person.

### Regulation 30 — Construction employees' facilities

In addition to the Facilities Regulations, 2004, the contractor must provide at or within reasonable access of every construction site, **clean, hygienic and maintained**:
- (a) **shower facilities** after consultation with the employees or their representatives, or at least **one shower per 15 persons**;
- (b) at least **one sanitary facility for each sex for every 30 workers**;
- (c) **changing facilities** for each sex; and
- (d) **sheltered eating areas**.

The contractor must also provide **reasonable and suitable living accommodation** for workers who are far removed from their homes where adequate transport or other accommodation is not available. This is directly relevant to a remote site such as Okongo.

### Other regulations of note

Reg 11 structures; reg 12 temporary works (a temporary works designer appointed in writing); reg 14 demolition; reg 18 rope access; reg 19 material hoists; reg 20 bulk mixing plant; reg 22 driven machinery; reg 23 construction vehicles and mobile plant; reg 24 electrical installations (with the Electrical Installation Regulations); reg 25 use and storage of flammable liquids; reg 26 water environments; reg 27 environmental/hazardous substances; reg 28 stacking of articles; reg 29 fire precautions — including all appropriate measures to avoid fire, suitable and sufficient fire-extinguishing equipment at strategic locations inspected by a competent person, a sufficient number of workers trained in its use, marked escape routes, and a siren sounded in the event of a fire.

## 2. **[NA]** Namibia — GN 156 of 1997 under the Labour Act

The Namibian regulations are older and more prescriptive but cover the same ground. Structure:

- **Chapter 1 — Rights and duties of employers.** Employer's general duties (reg 2); **health and safety policy and programme** (reg 3); appointment of **supervisors** (reg 4); duties of the executive officer (reg 5); **safety officers** (reg 6) — appointed in relation to the number of employees and the safety risk, to monitor and evaluate compliance with the safety policy, the Act and the regulations, and furnished with sufficient information, training, facilities and assistance; duties to persons other than employees (reg 7); duties of self-employed persons (reg 8); duties of designers, manufacturers, importers and suppliers (reg 9); **workplace safety representatives** (reg 10) — who inspect **monthly** and carry out a thorough examination **at least once every 12 months**; removal from the workplace (reg 11); prohibition (reg 12).
- **Chapter 2 — Administration.** Approved codes of practice; incorporation of health and safety standards; registration of factories; **commencement of building or construction work (reg 20) — not less than 30 days' notice to the Chief Inspector**, stating the names of the main contractor and any subcontractors, the address, the nature of the work, the expected dates of commencement and completion, and the total number of employees; notification of accidents and dangerous occurrences (reg 22); notification of occupational diseases (reg 23); posting of notices; keeping of documents; inquiries.
- **Chapter 3 — Welfare and facilities.** Floor space, ventilation, natural lighting, **sanitary conveniences (reg 35)**, **washing facilities (reg 36)**, cleanliness, change-rooms, rest and dining rooms, ergonomics, heating and cooling, fire precautions, storage of volatile flammable substances, smoking and meals, and **drinking water (reg 46)** — an adequate supply of clean drinking water, kept **cool and easily accessible in a hot environment**, with taps or pipes carrying non-potable water clearly and conspicuously marked.
- **Chapter 4 — Safety of machinery.** General machinery protection, controls, guards, specific machinery (abrasive wheels, machine tools, presses, rollers and conveyors), welding and flame cutting, **woodworking machinery** (circular saws, band saws, planing and moulding machines), goods hoists, cranes and lifting appliances, explosive-powered tools, pressure vessels, boilers.
- **Construction-specific regulations 246–256.**
  - **Reg 246 Scaffolding** — where work cannot be done safely from the ground or from part of the permanent structure, the employer must provide and maintain a safe and suitable scaffold. No builder may permit scaffolding to be constructed, dismantled or substantially altered except **under the supervision of a competent person and by competent employees with adequate experience**. No crane may be installed on scaffolding until a competent person has tested it and certified in writing that it is suitably constructed and stable enough. All scaffolding must be inspected by a competent person whenever additions or alterations are made.
  - **Reg 247 Gear used in connection with structural work** — no gear that is not of adequate strength, free from patent defects and in good working order; hoisting machines and tackle inspected and tested by an inspector before use; chains, rings, hooks, shackles, swivels, pulley blocks, levers, claws and dogs periodically inspected by competent and responsible persons.
  - **Reg 248 Roof work** — protection against falling from a roof; **suitable roof ladders, duck-boards or crawling boards** for any roof with a pitch steeper than **one to four**, or with a slippery surface, or covered with material through which a person could fall — unless suitable safety belts attached to the structure prevent a fall through the roof.
  - **Reg 249 Demolition and excavation** — see the excavation summary in the Key facts table and in `03_groundworks-and-foundations.md`. Note also the **asbestos** provisions: demolition of a structure containing asbestos, removal of asbestos, or work that may make asbestos airborne may only be undertaken by employers approved by the Chief Inspector, and the employer must identify all possible sources of asbestos before starting and draw up a written plan covering protection of employees, limiting release of asbestos dust into the air, and safe disposal of asbestos waste.
  - **Reg 253 Scaffold framework** — standards properly attached, secured vertically on firm foundations and against displacement (putlog scaffolds inclining slightly towards the structure); **steel standards** for "heavy", "medium", "light" and "very light" platform loading, which must not exceed **320, 240, 160 and 80 kg/m²** respectively, spaced not more than **1,8 m, 2,0 m, 2,5 m and 3,0 m** apart respectively; **wooden standards spaced not more than 3 m apart**; **ledgers spaced vertically not more than 2,1 m apart**; putlogs or transoms supporting a platform of solid timber boards **not more than 1,25 m apart**; any part of a wooden scaffold frame at least **75 mm** in diameter. Scaffolds must be securely braced in all directions, secured to the structure unless designed free-standing, constructed with a **factor of safety of not less than two**, and **inspected not less than once a week and after any turbulent weather**, with findings recorded in a register or report book. A scaffold with a **supporting wooden framework must not exceed 10 m in height**, and scaffolds must be erected, altered or dismantled by or under the supervision of a person with the necessary training and experience **appointed in writing**.
  - **Reg 254 Scaffold platforms** — planks or boards of a solid wooden scaffold platform **not less than 275 mm wide and not less than 38 mm thick**; supported at distances not exceeding **1,25 m**; ends projecting **not less than 70 mm and not more than 200 mm** beyond the last prop; every plank firmly secured against displacement.
  - **Regs 255–256** suspended and trestle scaffolds.
- **Protective equipment (reg 210 and Chapter section C)** and **First aid and emergency arrangements (regs 231–242)** — the employer must provide and maintain a readily accessible **first aid unit or station** containing a first aid box of durable material (strong plastic or metal or equivalent), marked with a **green cross on a white background**, fitted with straps and/or a handle, containing the supplies and equipment specified in Annexure G(3)(a); plus first aid attendants with valid certificates, a first aid room where required, transportation arrangements for injured employees, and reporting and recording of injuries.

## 3. Practical site safety — what actually kills and maims

In order of frequency and severity on small building sites:

1. **Falls from height** — roofs, scaffolds, ladders, unprotected edges and openings.
2. **Trench collapse** — the fastest and least survivable.
3. **Struck by falling objects** — materials from scaffolds, unsecured loads, collapsing stacks.
4. **Electrocution** — temporary supplies, damaged leads, contact with overhead or buried services, unqualified people working live.
5. **Plant and vehicles** — reversing vehicles, concrete mixers, angle grinders, cut-off saws.
6. **Manual handling** — the biggest cause of chronic injury and lost days.
7. **Heat illness** — in northern Namibia this belongs high on the list, not as an afterthought.
8. **Silica dust** — from cutting, chasing and grinding masonry and concrete; a long-latency killer that no one takes seriously enough.

### PPE minimum on a house site

| Item | When |
|---|---|
| Safety boots with steel toe and midsole | Always, everyone, including visitors |
| Hard hat | Wherever there is work overhead or a risk of falling objects |
| High-visibility vest | Where plant or vehicles operate |
| Eye protection | Cutting, grinding, chipping, drilling overhead, mixing chemicals |
| Hearing protection | Cut-off saws, grinders, hammer drills, compactors, generators |
| **P2/FFP2 or better respirator** | All dry cutting, chasing, grinding and sweeping of masonry/concrete |
| Gloves | Handling masonry, steel, timber, chemicals — not near rotating machinery |
| Full body harness with a double lanyard and a suitable anchor | Any fall risk position where fall prevention is not practicable |
| Sun protection: wide-brim hat or brim on hard hat, long sleeves, sunscreen | Outdoors, all day, at this latitude |

Wet-cut or dust-extract wherever possible. Dry-cutting a chase in concrete without extraction produces respirable crystalline silica concentrations far above any occupational limit.

### Scaffolding checklist (both jurisdictions)

- Erected, altered and dismantled only under the supervision of a competent person appointed in writing.
- Base on a firm, level foundation with sole plates and base plates. On sand, use timber sole boards.
- Standards vertical, spaced within the limits for the platform loading class (**[NA]** 1,8 / 2,0 / 2,5 / 3,0 m for heavy / medium / light / very light steel; 3,0 m for timber).
- Ledgers at ≤ 2,1 m vertical centres; transoms supporting timber board platforms at ≤ 1,25 m.
- Braced in all directions; tied to the structure unless designed free-standing.
- Boards ≥ 275 mm wide, ≥ 38 mm thick, supported at ≤ 1,25 m, ends projecting 70–200 mm, secured against displacement.
- Guard rails and toe boards on all working platforms.
- Safe access: a proper ladder tied off, extending at least 1 m above the platform, at ~75° (1:4).
- **Inspect at least weekly, after turbulent weather, and after every alteration**, and record it.
- Timber-framed scaffolds not more than 10 m high.
- Never load a scaffold beyond its class rating. A single pallet of bricks on a "light" platform exceeds 160 kg/m² easily.

### Excavation checklist

- Locate services before you dig. Every time.
- Batter, bench or shore anything over 1,5 m where collapse is possible; never work in an unsupported vertical face in sand.
- Keep spoil, plant and materials back from the edge.
- Safe access within 6 m **[ZA]** / at ≤ 50 m intervals in long trenches **[NA]**.
- Barrier or fence ≥ 1 m high where the public can reach it; red warning lights at night **[NA]**.
- Inspect daily before each shift and after rain; record it.
- Never leave an open trench unattended and unfenced overnight.

### Working at height checklist

- Prefer **fall prevention** (guard rails, scaffolds, working platforms) to **fall arrest** (harnesses) — the Regulations require it.
- Ladders: correct angle, tied at the top or footed, extending 1 m above the landing, on a firm level base, not used as a working platform for anything more than short-duration light work.
- Roof work above 1:4 pitch, or on a slippery or fragile roof: roof ladders, duck-boards or crawling boards.
- Never step on fibre-cement or translucent sheeting.
- Anchor points must be rated and independent of the thing being built.
- **A harness with no rescue plan is not a safety system** — a suspended person can suffer suspension trauma within minutes.
- Stop roof work in wind or rain.

## 4. Toolbox talks

A toolbox talk is a short (5–10 minute), specific, task-related safety briefing at the start of the shift or before a new activity. Record the date, topic, presenter and attendance — the record is required and it is also the evidence that the "informed, instructed and trained" duty was met.

A 20-topic rotation for a house build:

1. Site rules, emergency procedures and the assembly point
2. PPE: what, when, why, and how to inspect it
3. Manual handling and team lifting
4. Heat stress: symptoms, hydration, rest and shade
5. Ladders: selection, angle, footing, tying off
6. Scaffold: what to check before you climb on it
7. Excavation: why sand collapses, and never entering an unsupported trench
8. Buried and overhead services
9. Hand and power tool safety; guards; the danger of a disc-cutter kickback
10. Silica dust: wet cutting, extraction, respirators
11. Working at height and the rescue plan
12. Roof work: crawling boards, fragile materials, edge protection
13. Housekeeping: trip hazards, nails in timber, material stacking
14. Concrete and cement: dermatitis, burns, eye protection
15. Electrical: leads, plugs, earth leakage, no live work
16. Fire: extinguisher types and locations; hot work (torch-on waterproofing) precautions
17. Reversing vehicles and mobile plant
18. First aid: who the attendants are, where the kit is, how to call for help
19. Reporting incidents and near misses
20. Alcohol, fatigue and medication

## 5. Minimum paperwork for a small builder

Even on a one-house site, keep:

- Written appointments: construction manager (**[ZA]**), competent persons for excavation, scaffolding and fall protection, safety officer, first aid attendants
- Health and safety plan / policy
- Risk assessments with safe work procedures, monitoring and review plans
- Fall protection plan including the rescue plan
- Health and safety file (**[ZA]**) / documents register (**[NA]**)
- Notice of commencement (**[NA]** 30 days) / notification or permit (**[ZA]** 7 or 30 days)
- Compensation fund / Social Security registration and good standing
- Medical certificates of fitness (**[ZA]**)
- Induction records for every person on site
- Toolbox talk register
- Scaffold inspection register
- Excavation inspection register
- Plant and lifting-tackle inspection records
- PPE issue register
- Incident and near-miss register
- Emergency contact list and route to the nearest clinic/hospital — at Okongo, work out and write down the actual travel time to Okongo District Hospital or Eenhana before someone is bleeding.

> ⚠️ On a remote site the **rescue and medical evacuation plan is the safety system that matters most**. A fall arrested by a harness, a trench collapse, or a serious cut is survivable in a city and often not survivable two hours from a hospital. Plan for it: trained first aiders, a stocked and maintained kit, a vehicle that is always available and fuelled, a written route, and a phone that works.
