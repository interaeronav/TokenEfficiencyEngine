# Builder competency roadmap — trade to contractor

**Summary.** There are two separate ladders a builder climbs: a *personal* ladder of trade competence certified by a trade test, and a *corporate* ladder of contractor registration that decides what work the business is legally allowed to take on. In Namibia the personal ladder runs through the Namibia Training Authority (NTA) with qualifications registered on the NQF by the Namibia Qualifications Authority (NQA); in South Africa it runs through QCTO occupational qualifications with the trade test moderated by NAMB. On the corporate side South Africa has two hard gates — cidb grading for public-sector work and NHBRC registration for building homes for sale — while Namibia has no equivalent statutory contractor register, which shifts the burden of proving competence onto references, membership bodies and the client's own due diligence.

## Key facts

| Item | **[NA]** Namibia | **[ZA]** South Africa |
|---|---|---|
| Vocational qualification body | Namibia Training Authority (NTA) | QCTO (occupational qualifications) |
| Qualification register | Namibia Qualifications Authority (NQA), 10-level NQF | SAQA / NQF |
| Trade certification | NTA National Trade Testing & Certification (NTTC) | Trade test at accredited Trade Test Centre; certificate recommended by NAMB, issued by QCTO |
| Typical construction trade levels | National Vocational Certificate Levels 1–3 (bricklaying & plastering, joinery & cabinetmaking, plumbing & pipefitting, electrical general) | NQF 4 occupational certificate + trade test |
| Contractor register | No statutory national grading register | cidb Register of Contractors, grades 1–9 |
| Home-builder warranty body | None equivalent | NHBRC (Housing Consumers Protection Measures Act, 1998) |
| Credit definition | 1 credit = 10 notional learning hours | 1 credit = 10 notional learning hours |

## 1. The personal ladder — becoming a certified artisan

### **[NA]** Namibia

Vocational training is delivered through NTA-accredited Vocational Training Centres. The centres relevant to Ohangwena and the wider north are **Valombola VTC (Ongwediva, Oshana)**, **Nakayale VTC (Outapi, Omusati)**, **Eenhana VTC (Ohangwena)** and **Zambezi VTC**.

Construction-trade offerings at Valombola VTC, as an indicative example:

| Programme | NQF level | Indicative fee (2025/26) |
|---|---|---|
| Bricklaying & Plastering | 1–3 | N$16 000 |
| Joinery & Cabinet Making | 1–3 | N$16 000 |
| Plumbing & Pipe Fitting | 1–3 | N$16 000 |
| Electrical General | 2–3 | N$16 000 |

Typical entry requirements are a Grade 10 (JSC) pass with 23 points over six subjects including an E in English, Mathematics and Physical Science; or Grade 11 (NSSCO) / Grade 12 with 20 points and F symbols in the same subjects.

Structure of the pathway:
1. **Level 1** — basic hand skills, safety, tools, materials. Registered NQA unit standards for bricklaying and plastering include items such as working safely on scaffolding, platforms and ladders as part of bricklaying operations.
2. **Level 2** — production work under supervision: setting out, coursing, bonding, plastering to a screed.
3. **Level 3** — independent work, setting out from drawings, quality control, small-team supervision.
4. **Trade test** — administered under the NTA's National Trade Testing & Certification system. The NTA also operates Competency-Based Assessment (CBA) for occupational areas within the NQF, and registers assessors and moderators.

Recognition of Prior Learning (RPL) is the route for the self-taught builder: an experienced person is assessed against the registered unit standards rather than sitting the full programme. Confirm current RPL arrangements directly with the NTA (Windhoek, +264 61 207 8200).

### **[ZA]** South Africa

The QCTO occupational qualification has three components that must all be completed:
- **Knowledge component** (theory, usually at a TVET college or accredited provider),
- **Practical component** (simulated workshop),
- **Workplace experience component** (real site work under a qualified artisan).

These are integrated and then tested in an **External Integrated Summative Assessment (EISA)**, and for the artisan trades a **trade test** at an accredited Trade Test Centre. NAMB (the National Artisan Moderation Body) moderates the trade test and recommends the issue of the trade certificate to the QCTO within 21 working days of receiving documentation; the certificate should reach the learner within three months of trade testing. A person is legally an *artisan* only once the trade test is passed and the trade certificate issued.

Funding and placement typically run through a **SETA learnership or apprenticeship** — for building work, the **CETA** (Construction Education and Training Authority). Relevant trades: bricklayer, plasterer, carpenter and joiner, plumber, electrician, painter, roof tiler.

> ⚠️ **[ZA]** Electrical work is different in kind. Only a *registered person* (electrical tester for single phase, installation electrician, or master installation electrician) registered with the Department of Employment and Labour may issue an electrical Certificate of Compliance. A builder may never sign one. See `10_electrical-fundamentals.md`.

## 2. The corporate ladder — contractor registration

### **[ZA]** cidb Register of Contractors

Any contractor tendering for South African public-sector construction work must be registered on the cidb Register of Contractors in the relevant **class of works** at a **grading designation** matching the tender value.

Tender value ranges per grading designation (upper limit, inclusive of VAT), per cidb Best Practice Guideline #A6, July 2021:

| Grade | Upper tender value limit |
|---|---|
| 1 | R 500 000 |
| 2 | R 1 000 000 |
| 3 | R 3 000 000 |
| 4 | R 6 000 000 |
| 5 | R 10 000 000 |
| 6 | R 20 000 000 |
| 7 | R 60 000 000 |
| 8 | R 200 000 000 |
| 9 | No limit |

Classes of works (principal codes): **GB** general building, **CE** civil engineering, **EB** electrical engineering (buildings), **EP** electrical engineering (infrastructure), **ME** mechanical engineering, and the **SB–SQ** range of specialist works (asphalt, glazing, piling, structural steelwork, waterproofing, landscaping and others).

Practical reading of the ladder:
- **Grade 1** is the entry point: no financial qualifying criteria, effectively an administrative registration. It caps you at small works.
- **Grades 2–4** require demonstrated financial capability (available capital / turnover) *and* a track record of completed contracts of a given value in the same class. This is where most small residential builders sit.
- **Grade 6 and above** triggers **[ZA]** the Construction Regulations 2014 construction work permit threshold — see below.

> ⚠️ Verify current tender value ranges against the cidb Register before relying on them; they are set by regulation and revised periodically.

### **[ZA]** NHBRC home builder registration

Under the Housing Consumers Protection Measures Act 95 of 1998 a person who builds a home for a housing consumer must be registered with the NHBRC as a home builder, and **each new home must be enrolled with the NHBRC at least 15 days before construction starts**. Enrolment triggers NHBRC inspections during construction and the statutory warranty cover:

| Cover | Period from date of first occupation | Notified to |
|---|---|---|
| Minor defects / maintenance | 3 months, in writing | Home builder (not NHBRC-warranted) |
| Roof leaks | 1 year, in writing | Home builder |
| Major structural defects | 5 years, in writing | Home builder |

The three-month snag period is the builder's own liability and sits outside the warranty fund.

### **[ZA]** Construction work permit threshold

A **client** must apply to the provincial director for a **construction work permit** at least 30 days before work starts where the intended construction work will exceed **180 days**, involve more than **1 800 person days**, or where the contract value is **equal to or exceeding thirteen million rand**, or cidb grading level 6. Below that threshold, a **contractor** must give **7 days' notice** to the provincial director where the work includes excavation work, working at height with a risk of falling, demolition, or use of explosives; and specifically for construction of a **single-storey dwelling for a client who will live in it**. See `14_health-and-safety.md`.

### **[NA]** Namibia

Namibia has no statutory national contractor grading register equivalent to the cidb, and no NHBRC-equivalent home warranty scheme. In practice the following substitute for it:

- **Registration of the business**: BIPA company/close corporation registration, a NamRA tax good-standing certificate and VAT registration, and Social Security Commission registration for employees.
- **Construction Industries Federation of Namibia (CIF)** membership: a voluntary industry body that functions as a de facto quality and credibility signal, and which has publicly campaigned for the sector to be formally regulated. Membership is commonly asked for in private and parastatal tenders.
- **Public procurement**: tenders are run under the Public Procurement Act 15 of 2015 through the Central Procurement Board and public entities, with bidder eligibility criteria set per tender rather than by a national grading.
- **Affirmative Action and local content**: the Affirmative Action (Employment) Act 29 of 1998 requires *relevant employers* (a threshold based on number of employees) to register with the Employment Equity Commission and file affirmative action reports and plans. Public tenders commonly carry local-content, local-employment and subcontracting-to-locals conditions, and preference points for Namibian-owned and previously disadvantaged-owned businesses. For a builder at Okongo this usually means: employ locally, keep proof, and be ready to demonstrate Namibian ownership and SSC compliance.
- **OHS notification**: **[NA]** a person who intends to commence building or construction work must give **not less than 30 days' notice** to the Chief Inspector, stating the names of the main contractor and subcontractors, the address, the nature of the work, the expected start and completion dates and the total number of employees (GN 156 of 1997, reg 20).

> ⚠️ Namibian requirements around contractor registration, procurement preference and affirmative action change with policy; treat the above as orientation and verify against BIPA, NamRA, the Ministry of Labour and the CIF before tendering.

## 3. The self-taught competence checklist

If you are not going through a VTC or an apprenticeship, this is the honest audit. You are competent when you can do each of these unaided, repeatedly, to tolerance.

**Setting out and levels**
- [ ] Establish a site datum and transfer it accurately around a building with a dumpy or laser level
- [ ] Set profiles and building lines; square a rectangle with 3-4-5 and prove it with diagonals within 10 mm
- [ ] Read a site plan, a floor plan, a section and a foundation layout, and reconcile them

**Groundworks**
- [ ] Dig a trial hole, describe the profile and recognise loose sand, calcrete, clay and made ground
- [ ] Set foundation width and depth against SANS 10400-H limits and know when to stop and call an engineer
- [ ] Compact fill in layers and test it (DCP or proof-rolling)

**Concrete**
- [ ] Batch a 2-bag mix accurately by wheelbarrow for 15, 25 and 30 MPa
- [ ] Run a slump test and read it
- [ ] Cast, label and cure a set of 150 mm cubes
- [ ] Place, compact and cure a slab in 35 °C wind without plastic shrinkage cracks

**Masonry**
- [ ] Set out and build a corner, plumb and gauged, without a profile
- [ ] Mix Class II mortar to 1:5 (or 50 kg cement : 200 ℓ sand) and know why not 1:3
- [ ] Place brickforce, wall ties, lintels and control joints in the right places

**Roof**
- [ ] Set out a roof: pitch, rafter length, batten gauge, purlin spacing
- [ ] Specify and check a nail-plate truss delivery against the drawing
- [ ] Fix IBR or corrugated sheeting with correct laps and screw pattern
- [ ] Fit roof anchors, flashings and gutters

**Finishes and services**
- [ ] Plaster a wall to 6 mm under a 2 m straight edge
- [ ] Lay a screed to fall with no hollow areas
- [ ] Set out drainage falls and inspection eyes; test a drain
- [ ] Coordinate first-fix electrical and plumbing so nothing is chased after plastering

**Commercial**
- [ ] Take off quantities for a 100 m² house from drawings and be within 5 % of the actual
- [ ] Build up a rate from labour, material, plant, overhead and profit
- [ ] Produce a bar chart programme with lead times and a cash-flow forecast
- [ ] Run a site diary, a snag list and a handover pack

**Safety and law**
- [ ] Write and use a risk assessment and a fall protection plan
- [ ] Erect and inspect a scaffold to the legal spacing and platform rules
- [ ] Know the excavation shoring trigger depth (1,5 m) and the inspection regime
