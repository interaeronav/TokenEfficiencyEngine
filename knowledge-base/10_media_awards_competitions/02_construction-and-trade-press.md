# Construction and trade press

**Summary.** The trade press is where contractors, engineers, quantity surveyors and product manufacturers get their commercial and technical information. It is structurally different from the architecture press: circulation is often **controlled and free to qualifying industry readers** (funded by advertising rather than subscription), the content mixes news, tender and project intelligence, product launches and technical articles, and the same publisher usually runs an awards programme on top. For southern Africa the two indispensable titles are *Engineering News* (Creamer Media) and *Construction World* (Crown Publications); the two indispensable institute journals are *Civil Engineering* (SAICE) and *SA Builder* (Master Builders orbit).

## South African trade press in practice

### Creamer Media (*Engineering News*, *Mining Weekly*)
Creamer Media is the most useful single source of **project intelligence** in South Africa. Beyond the weekly magazine it maintains research reports and a projects database, sells tiered subscriptions, and publishes Real Economy Reports as video and audio. Sectors covered run from agriculture and automotive through construction, electricity, energy, environment, manufacturing, metals, mining, renewables, trade, transport and water. For a contractor tracking where public and private capital is actually going, this is the primary feed.

### Crown Publications (*Construction World* and stablemates)
Crown is a business-to-business publisher with a portfolio spanning construction, mining, electrical, welding and chemical engineering. *Construction World* has been published since 1982 and is the general contractor-facing title. Crown's **Best Projects** awards programme (covered in file `05`) is one of the most accessible awards for a South African contractor or consultant to enter, because it is judged from a written submission rather than requiring a live presentation.

For a joinery, glass or steel subcontractor, the Crown stable is disproportionately useful: *African Fusion* for welding and fabrication practice, *Capital Equipment News* for plant procurement decisions, *Construction World* for main-contractor relationships.

### Institute journals
- **SAICE** publishes the monthly *Civil Engineering* magazine (practice-facing) and the peer-reviewed *Journal of the SAICE* (research). The *Journal* is carried on **SciELO South Africa** and is genuinely open access — this is one of the best free technical resources in the region and is treated in detail in file `08`.
- **Concrete Society of Southern Africa** publishes *Concrete Beton* and runs the Fulton Awards.
- **Master Builders South Africa** is the national representative body for the building industry; *SA Builder* is the long-running title in its orbit, carrying MBSA congress coverage, the national safety competition, and contractor economics.
- **SAFCEC** (South African Forum of Civil Engineering Contractors) publishes a **State of the Industry Report**, newsletters and a **Tender Bulletin** for members. Membership is tiered: *Emerging* (cidb grades 1–5 and/or turnover under roughly R1–5 m), *Established* (cidb grades 6–9 and/or turnover above roughly R6 m), and *Associate* (suppliers of goods and services).

## How to use the trade press

1. **Register for every free controlled-circulation title you qualify for.** In **[ZA]** that is most of the Crown stable plus *SA Builder*. It costs an email address and delivers product, price and project intelligence.
2. **Read the rankings, not the articles.** *ENR*'s Top 400/Top 250 lists and *Building*'s Top 150 Consultants are the fastest way to understand market structure and who is actually delivering work.
3. **Trade press is the cheapest PR channel there is.** A product launch, a completed project, a new appointment or a technical opinion piece will be published by a trade title far more readily than by an architecture magazine, and the readership is the one that hires contractors. See file `07`.
4. **Beware advertorial.** Much trade-press "technical" content is manufacturer-supplied. Cross-check any performance claim against a standard or an institute publication (file `08`) before specifying from it.
5. **Institute journals outrank commercial titles for technical accuracy.** The SAICE *Journal*, *Concrete Beton*, AAAMSA codes of practice and SAISC publications are peer- or committee-reviewed; the commercial monthlies are not.
