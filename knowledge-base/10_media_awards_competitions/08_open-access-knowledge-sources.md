# Open-access construction and architecture knowledge sources

**Summary.** A very large proportion of genuinely useful construction and architecture knowledge is free. National building-science institutes, standards bodies' free tiers, materials institutes, government technical libraries, university repositories, manufacturer technical libraries and open-access journals together cover most of what a practitioner needs — the gap being the *binding national standards themselves*, which almost always cost money. This file is a tested link table: every row marked **tested** was successfully retrieved on 2026-08-25; rows marked **untested** are included on the basis of known reputation but were not retrieved in this pass (several sites block automated access via robots.txt, which does not affect human browsing).

## The five categories, and how to use each

### 1. National building-science institutes and government technical libraries

These are the highest-quality free technical sources in existence, because they are publicly funded and written to be authoritative rather than to sell anything.

| Body | Country | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| **NIST** | US | Fire research, structural failure investigations, building-envelope moisture research |
| **WBDG / National Institute of Building Sciences** | US | UFC/UFGS federal criteria, design objectives (accessibility, aesthetics, cost-effectiveness, functionality, historic preservation, productivity, security, sustainability) |
| **BRE** | UK | BREEAM methodology; BRE Digests and Information Papers (mostly paid via the bookshop, but methodology documents free) |
| **CSIR** | **[ZA]** South Africa | Built environment research; historically the source of much South African building-science guidance — **needs verification** of current free-publication portal |
| **CSIRO** | Australia | Building physics, bushfire construction |
| **NRC-IRC / National Research Council** | Canada | Cold-climate building science; some free technical reports |
| **Fraunhofer IBP** | Germany | Building physics research; WUFI moisture-modelling background material |
| **BAM / DIBt** | Germany | Materials testing and approvals |

**[NA]**/**[ZA]** Practical caveat: nearly all of this material is written for temperate or cold climates. The physics transfers; the assumptions about heating-dominated loads, vapour-drive direction and insulation strategy frequently do not. Read the method, re-derive the numbers.

### 2. Standards bodies' free tiers

> ⚠️ **Binding national standards are almost never free.** **[ZA]** SANS standards must be purchased from the SABS; **[NA]** Namibian building regulations reference SANS/SABS material. Budget for the standards you are legally required to comply with — do not attempt to work from summaries.

What *is* free:

- **ASHRAE** — read-only preview access to popular standards, plus a substantial library of free guidance documents (full list in the key-facts table). This is the single most generous free tier from any standards body.
- **NBS** — free specification guidance, sample specifications and embodied-carbon guidance.
- **Eurocodes** — the standards themselves are paid, but a very large volume of free explanatory and worked-example material exists from universities and national annex authorities.
- **ISO / IEC** — a small number of standards are published free of charge; the online browsing platform gives free access to terms-and-definitions clauses of many standards.

> ⚠️ **ASHRAE AI policy note.** ASHRAE's site states that it prohibits entering content from ASHRAE publications or related ASHRAE intellectual property into AI tools. If you are building an AI-assisted workflow, treat ASHRAE material as read-and-cite, not as ingestible corpus.

### 4. University open repositories and open-access journals

Every university with an architecture or civil engineering faculty operates an institutional repository containing **theses, dissertations and often full-text staff publications**, free and without registration.

| Route | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| **DOAJ** (23 356 journals; 14 424 with no publication fee) | The master index for open-access journals across every discipline |
| **SciELO South Africa** (ASSAf) | **[ZA]** The *Journal of the SAICE* and other South African scholarly journals, free to read and free to publish |
| **University institutional repositories** — UCT (OpenUCT), Wits, UP (UPSpace), Stellenbosch (SUNScholar), NMU, UKZN, and **[NA]** NUST | Masters and doctoral theses on local building science, materials, housing and urbanism that exist nowhere else |
| **CORE, BASE, OpenDOAR** | Aggregators that search across thousands of repositories at once |
| **Google Scholar** | Use the "All versions" link to find the free preprint or repository copy of a paywalled paper |
| **arXiv / EngrXiv** | Preprints in structural and materials engineering |

**[ZA]**/**[NA]** The single most under-used resource in this whole file is the **South African university thesis corpus**. Doctoral and masters research on earth construction, low-cost housing, thermal performance of local walling systems, informal settlement upgrading and township urbanism is extensive, locally specific, methodologically sound and entirely free.

### 5. Manufacturer technical libraries

Manufacturers publish detailed, dimensionally accurate technical information free because they want to be specified. Used critically this is the fastest route to buildable detail.

| Type | Examples of what is free |
|---|---|
| Facade and glazing systems | Full system sections in DWG, thermal performance data, installation manuals |
| Insulation | U-value calculators, condensation-risk guidance, fixing details |
| Waterproofing | Junction and upstand details, substrate preparation, warranty conditions |
| Roofing and cladding | Profile geometry, spans, fixing centres, flashing details |
| Cement and admixtures | Mix design guidance, curing regimes, technical data sheets |
| Ironmongery and fixings | Load tables, pull-out values, fire ratings |
| BIM objects | Revit families and IFC objects, usually via NBS Source or the manufacturer's own portal |

> ⚠️ **Read manufacturer literature as a specification, not as physics.** Performance figures are obtained under stated test conditions that may not resemble your building. Details are drawn to protect the manufacturer's warranty, which is not identical to protecting your building. Cross-check anything load-bearing, fire-rated or waterproofing-critical against a standard or an institute publication.

## How to build a practice technical library from free sources

1. **Start from the problem, not the source.** "How do I detail a parapet in a hot dry climate with high diurnal swing?" leads to better material than browsing.
2. **Download and keep.** Free resources disappear — manufacturers withdraw PDFs, institutes reorganise sites, journals change platforms. Save a local copy, with the URL and access date in the filename.
3. **Record provenance.** Every saved document should carry its source and date. A detail with no provenance is unusable when someone asks why.
4. **Prefer institute over manufacturer, and standard over institute**, for anything that could fail.
5. **Use the Wayback Machine** when a cited technical page is dead — it very often has the withdrawn PDF.
6. **Check climate assumptions on everything.** Almost all free building-science material is written for northern-hemisphere temperate or cold conditions.
7. **Budget separately for the standards you are legally bound by.** SANS purchases are a compliance cost, not a library cost.
