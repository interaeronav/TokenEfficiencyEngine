# Architecture magazines and journals

**Summary.** The international architecture press splits cleanly into three functions: **technical** (transferable construction information — *Detail* above all), **cultural/critical** (argument about what architecture means — *AR*, *Log*, *AA Files*, *Casabella*), and **documentary/fast** (project publication at volume — ArchDaily, Dezeen, Divisare, Designboom). Most of the cultural and monographic titles are paywalled and expensive; most of the fast digital titles are free and advertising-funded. Ownership has consolidated sharply: NZZ Mediengruppe owns ArchDaily, Architonic and Designboom; BNP Media owns *Architectural Record*; Metropolis International owns *The Architectural Review*.

## The technical titles, and why *Detail* is the outlier

*Detail* is structurally different from every other title on this list. Each issue is built around a **construction theme** — timber, concrete, roofs, refurbishment, facades, building simply, translucent materials — and each featured project is presented with the same apparatus: plans and sections at building scale, then a run of **measured construction details, typically 1:5 to 1:20, with the layers labelled**. The detail drawings are redrawn to a house standard, so they are legible and comparable across issues and across decades. The magazine is published in German and English editions.

What that means practically:

- It is the only mainstream architecture magazine whose drawings can be **reasoned from as construction information**. Everything else publishes drawings as illustration.
- Details are climate- and code-specific to central Europe. A *Detail* facade section assumes European insulation levels, vapour-control logic and rainscreen conventions. **[NA]**/**[ZA]** In hot, dry southern African conditions the thermal layer thicknesses and vapour strategy are usually wrong and must be re-derived; the *geometry*, sequencing and junction logic transfer well.
- The back-catalogue is the asset. *Detail* has been published since the 1960s; the value is in accumulated themed issues, not in the current one.
- *DETAIL inspiration* is the searchable online database of published projects and details. It is a paid subscription and is normally the more useful purchase for a practice than the print magazine alone.

Secondary technical titles worth knowing: *Architectural Record*'s continuing-education articles (free to read, free CEU quizzes), *The Plan*'s technical sheets, and — for structures and materials rather than architecture — the trade and institute journals in file `02`.

## The cultural / critical titles

These publish few projects, at length, with essays. Do not buy them expecting details.

- **The Architectural Review** — founded 1896, the oldest title here. Thematic monthly issues with sustained criticism; historically the most consistently intelligent English-language architecture writing. Publisher Metropolis International acquired it in June 2017 as part of a £23.5 m purchase of 13 heritage titles. Runs the **AR Emerging Architecture Awards** (established 1999) and the **AR House** and **AR Future Projects** awards.
- **Casabella** — Milan, founded 1928 as *La Casa Bella*. Edited over its history by Giuseppe Pagano, Ernesto Nathan Rogers, Vittorio Gregotti and, currently, Francesco Dal Co. Sober, drawing-led, historically serious.
- **Domus** — founded 1928 by Gio Ponti (with Giovanni Semeria). Eleven issues a year, Italian/English, with Russian and Chinese editions. Domus rotates a high-profile guest editor, which makes the editorial line change sharply from year to year — a feature, not a fault.
- **Log** — a small New York theory journal from Anyone Corporation. Text-driven; where North American architectural theory is currently argued.
- **AA Files** — the Architectural Association's scholarly journal. Architectural history, archival research, essays of a kind no commercial magazine would carry.
- **Volume** — the Archis/AMO/C-Lab experiment in "beyond architecture" discourse. Its regular run appears to have ended; back issues remain valuable for the 2005–2015 period of architectural-political writing.

## The monographic titles

**El Croquis** and **2G** and **AV Monografías** all do the same job: exhaustive documentation of one practice or one theme, with drawings and photography reproduced at a standard nothing else matches. *El Croquis* issues typically cover a defined date range of one office's work (for example, a four-year window), with plans, sections, models, sketches and new photography commissioned for the issue.

For a student or a young practice, **one *El Croquis* on an office you actually want to learn from is worth ten general-interest magazines**. They are expensive — individual issues run into the tens of euros and out-of-print issues considerably more on the secondary market. University libraries in **[ZA]** (UCT, Wits, UP, UKZN, NMU, UFS, TUT, CPUT, DUT, UJ) and **[NA]** (NUST) generally hold runs of *El Croquis*, *a+u* and *Detail*; using the library run is the rational route.

## The fast digital titles

| Title | Reach signal | Submission route | Editorial character |
|---|---|---|---|
| ArchDaily | ~17.9 m monthly readers, ~283 m monthly page views (2022 figures) | Free submission form; built projects **completed after 2014**; region-specific submission packs | High volume, low editing, standardised format. Publishes plans and sections — unusual for a fast site |
| Dezeen | >3 m monthly uniques and >6.5 m social followers at time of 2021 acquisition; ~50 staff across London, New York, Shanghai | Free submission; heavily selective | Strong news instinct, headline-driven, will run critical stories |
| Designboom | Founded 1999, Milan | Free submission | Design/art crossover; runs partner-sponsored competitions |
| Architizer | Firm and product directory + A+Awards | Free firm profile; paid awards entry | Directory-first; publication is largely a funnel to the awards |
| Divisare | Curated, subscription-supported, explicitly ad-free | Submit project or photo essay | Slowest and most curated of the group; organised by theme, not date |

The trade-off is exact: **free reach in exchange for no editorial care**. ArchDaily will republish your press kit close to verbatim. That is why the quality of the press kit — file `07` — determines the quality of the publication.

Consolidation matters for two reasons. First, a single submission to one group can be syndicated across several titles. Second, editorial independence from advertisers is weaker where a single owner runs both the magazine and its awards programme — which is now the norm (*AR*, Dezeen, Architizer, *The Plan*, WAF all run awards on the back of their own editorial reach).

## Practical guidance for a southern African practice

1. **Free first.** ArchDaily, Dezeen, Designboom and Architizer cost nothing to read and nothing to submit to. Start there.
2. **One paid technical subscription.** *DETAIL inspiration* if you detail buildings; otherwise put the money into materials-institute literature (file `08`).
3. **Use the university library** for *El Croquis*, *a+u*, *AA Files* and *Log* rather than buying.
4. **Divisare for precedent research.** Its thematic organisation makes it far more efficient than searching ArchDaily when you need, for example, "brick screen facades" or "rammed-earth housing".
5. **Watch what the paywalled titles award, not what they publish.** *AR* Emerging Architecture and *AR* House shortlists are free to read online and are a compressed summary of the critical consensus.
