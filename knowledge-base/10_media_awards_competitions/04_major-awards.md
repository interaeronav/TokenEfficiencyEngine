# Major international architecture awards

**Summary.** International awards divide into three kinds: **honour awards** for a lifetime body of work, decided by nomination and closed jury with no entry fee (Pritzker, RIBA Royal Gold Medal, AIA Gold Medal); **peer-and-jury project awards** where the profession selects from its own award-winners (RIBA Stirling Prize, EU Mies Award); and **paid-entry commercial awards** where anyone may enter for a fee (WAF, Architizer A+, Dezeen Awards, AZ Awards, THE PLAN Award). The Aga Khan Award for Architecture sits apart: it is nomination-based, triennial, carries US$1 000 000, and is the most consequential award in the world for African and Islamic-world work. The Holcim Foundation Awards — for two decades the leading sustainable-construction prize — **concluded after the 2025 cycle**.

## The honour awards — how they actually work

### Pritzker Architecture Prize
Sponsored by the **Hyatt Foundation**, awarded annually to a living architect or architects "whose built work demonstrates a combination of those qualities of talent, vision and commitment". The **2026 laureate is Smiljan Radić** (Chile). There is no entry fee and no submission portal in the conventional sense; the process is nomination followed by a closed jury.

What it rewards: a **sustained body of built work with an identifiable intellectual position**. It does not reward a single building, commercial success, or scale of practice. The recent pattern has broadened the geography sharply — Diébédo Francis Kéré (Burkina Faso) in 2022 was the first African laureate, and the prize has repeatedly gone to practices working with modest budgets and local materials.

### RIBA Royal Gold Medal
Approved personally by the reigning monarch and awarded to a person or group for a body of work with lasting influence. No cash. It is the closest British equivalent to the Pritzker in prestige and has been awarded since 1848.

### AIA Gold Medal
The American Institute of Architects' highest honour to an individual, recognising a lifetime of distinguished practice and lasting influence on architectural theory and practice. Nomination-based, annual.

**Practical relevance to a small practice: none directly.** These awards are not entered; they are conferred. Their value to a practitioner is as a **reading list** — the laureates' work and the jury citations are a compact statement of what the profession currently considers excellent.

## The peer-selected project awards

### RIBA Stirling Prize
The route is a **funnel**, and this is the important structural point:

```
RIBA Regional Awards  →  RIBA National Awards  →  RIBA Stirling Prize shortlist  →  winner
```

To be eligible for the Stirling Prize a building must be a **new building in the UK**, designed by a RIBA Chartered Architect or RIBA International Fellow, and must already have won a **RIBA National Award**. National Awards are drawn from the Regional Awards. Judging criteria across the funnel include design vision, innovation, sustainability, accessibility, functionality, client satisfaction, and the building's capacity to engage and delight users.

2026 is the prize's **30th anniversary**; the winner is announced on **15 October 2026** at Old Billingsgate, London. The 2026 shortlist: A House at Fairmead (Epping Forest, Essex); BEAM (Hertfordshire); Lion Green Road (south-east London); Paddington Square (west London); Pembroke, Mill Lane (Cambridgeshire); River Wing, Clare College, Cambridge.

RIBA also runs **RIBA Awards for International Excellence** and the **RIBA International Prize**, which are the routes open to work outside the UK.

### EU Mies Award
A partnership of the European Commission, the European Parliament and the Fundació Mies van der Rohe in Barcelona, established in **1987** and awarded **biennially**. Eligibility is works **completed in Europe** within the two-year period preceding the award. Crucially, **you cannot enter yourself**: nominations come from independent experts, national architecture associations and the prize's advisory committee. A jury shortlists five finalists, visits them, and selects one main winner and one **Emerging Architect** winner. Since **2016** the **Young Talent Architecture Award (YTAA)** has run alongside for final-year degree projects. The 2024 winner was the Palais des Expositions in Charleroi (AgwA with architecten jan de vylder inge vinck).

## The Aga Khan Award for Architecture — the one that matters most for African work

Established in **1977** by Aga Khan IV, governed by a steering committee chaired by the Aga Khan under the Aga Khan Trust for Culture. Fifteen cycles have been completed; the award has documented over **9 000 projects** and selected **128** for the prize. Each cycle runs **three years** and shares **US$1 000 000** among the winning projects.

**Eligibility and character.** The award recognises projects that serve the needs and aspirations of **societies with a significant Muslim presence** — which in practice means much of North, West and East Africa, plus South Asia, the Middle East, Central Asia and Muslim communities elsewhere. Categories are broad: contemporary design, social housing and community development, restoration and reuse, area conservation, landscape design and environmental improvement. It rewards **projects, teams, clients, craftspeople and institutions**, not only architects. A new steering committee is constituted for each cycle and sets that cycle's thematic direction and eligibility detail.

**What juries reward.** The consistent thread across five decades is: use of **local materials and appropriate technology used inventively**, demonstrable **social effect**, **cultural continuity** without pastiche, and evidence that the project works for the people who use it. Budget scale is explicitly not a criterion — some of the most celebrated winners are very small buildings.

## The paid-entry commercial awards

These are businesses. Entry fees fund the programme and the media property behind it. That is not a criticism — the exposure is real — but it does mean you should treat entry as a **marketing spend with a measurable target**, not as a professional obligation.

### World Architecture Festival (WAF)
Organised by **EMAP Publishing Limited**. WAF 2026 runs **18–20 November 2026 at the Broward County Convention Center, Fort Lauderdale, Florida** — the second consecutive US edition. The 2026 shortlist comprises **416 projects**.

Its distinguishing feature is that **all finalists present live** to a jury panel and an audience of peers at the festival: "the only architectural awards programme where all finalists present their projects LIVE." Programme strands include the main WAF Awards (Creative Re-Use, Housing, Hotel, Health, Culture and more), **WAF Interiors** (nine categories), **WAFX** (projects addressing global challenges), and special prizes such as Best Use of Natural Light, Sustainability Prize, Best Use of Stone, Best Use of Colour and Small Project of the Year.

**Cost reality:** entry fee **plus** travel and delegate pass for at least one presenter. For a southern African practice, budget realistically for entry, flights, accommodation and a delegate pass before entering. The live-presentation format rewards practices that can present well as much as it rewards the building.

### Architizer A+Awards
Described by Architizer as the world's largest architecture awards programme, established by Marc Kushner in **2013**. Entry is a four-step online process: entrant details; project name, information and description; high-resolution images and/or video; payment by card or PayPal. Submissions remain editable until the final deadline. Each category produces a **Jury Winner** and a **Popular Choice** winner (public vote), which is a meaningful distinction — Popular Choice rewards the practice's ability to mobilise an audience, not the building.

### Dezeen Awards
Launched **2018**, now **49 categories** across architecture, interiors and design, with more than 4 300 entries in the 2026 cycle. Fees are **explicitly tiered by studio size and by deadline**, which is unusually fair to small practices:

| Tier | Small studio (1–10 employees) | Large studio (11+) |
|---|---|---|
| Early entry (to 25 Mar 2026) | £105 per category | £210 |
| Standard entry (to 27 May 2026) | £130 | £260 |
| Late entry (to 3 Jun 2026) | £270 | £540 |
| Regional Showcases | £25 | £50 |

2026 cycle dates: opened 11 February 2026; shortlists late September; public vote opens 5 October; winners mid-November. Judges for the 2026 cycle included Norman Foster, Corey Damen Jenkins and Nike's chief design officer Martin Lotti.

**The Regional Showcases strand at £25 is the cheapest credible international exposure available to a small southern African practice.**

### AZ Awards
Run by **AZURE** magazine (Toronto) since **2011**, with **31 categories** including Architecture, Design, Interiors, Landscape Architecture, Urban Design, Concepts, Experiential Graphic Design, Social Good, Environmental Leadership, an **Emerging** category, and the **A+ Award for Student Work**. 2026 cycle dates: entries opened 10 November 2025; early-bird deadline 23 January 2026; extended deadline 27 February 2026; finalists announced 17 April 2026; people's-choice voting 17 April – 8 May 2026; gala in Toronto 28 May 2026.

## The award that closed: Holcim Foundation Awards

For twenty years the **Holcim Foundation Awards for Sustainable Construction** were the leading global prize for sustainability in the built environment. Over **eight cycles from 2004 to 2025** they distributed **USD 14 million**, received **33 230 entries from 166 countries**, involved **81 413 participants** and **260 jury members**, and selected **362 winning projects** from **79 countries**. Entries were assessed against four goals — **Healthy Planet, Thriving Communities, Uplifting Places, Viable Economics** — and three design principles — **Holistic, Transformational, Transferable**.

**The programme has concluded.** The Holcim Foundation states it is entering a new phase with a sharper focus on responsible urban development. Anyone planning around a "next Holcim cycle" is planning around something that no longer exists.

Two things survive and remain valuable: the **published archive of 362 winning projects** with full documentation, which is one of the best free sustainable-construction case-study libraries in existence; and the **four goals / three principles framework**, which is a usable evaluation rubric for any project regardless of the award.

## Choosing what to enter — a decision rule

| Situation | Enter |
|---|---|
| Small practice, first published project, tight budget | Dezeen Awards **Regional Showcases** (£25/£50); ArchDaily Building of the Year (free, member-voted); local institute awards (file `05`) |
| Strong single completed building, want peer credibility | **[ZA]** SAIA Award of Merit → Award of Excellence; RIBA Awards for International Excellence |
| Public/community project with local materials, African context | **Aga Khan Award** nomination route; AZ Awards Social Good |
| Want maximum international press | Dezeen Awards or Architizer A+ (Popular Choice drives shares) |
| Have budget and a presenter who performs well | **WAF** — but only with the full travel budget committed |
| Student or recent graduate | AZ Awards Student Work; YTAA (Europe); Corobrik-SAIA student award **[ZA]** (file `05`); competition platforms (file `06`) |
| Sustainability-led project | Holcim is closed — use its rubric, then enter AZ Environmental Leadership, WAFX, or BREEAM Awards |

> ⚠️ Do not enter an award for a building you cannot photograph properly. Every one of these programmes is judged from images. Professional photography is a precondition, not an enhancement — see file `07`.
