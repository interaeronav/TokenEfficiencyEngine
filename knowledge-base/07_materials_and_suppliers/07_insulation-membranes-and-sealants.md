# Insulation, membranes, DPC/DPM and sealants

**Summary.** Insulation in southern Africa is specified by **R-value** (m²K/W) against the SANS 10400-XA / SANS 204 climatic-zone requirements. Two mechanisms are in play — bulk resistance (glass wool, polyester, EPS, XPS, PIR, cellulose, spray foam) and radiant reflection (foil barriers, which only work with an air gap). Damp-proofing is by polyethylene DPC and DPM sheeting in 250/375/500 micron grades; waterproofing by torch-on bitumen membranes or liquid-applied systems. Sealants are classified by **ISO 11600** on movement capability, which is the only sensible basis for specifying them.

## Key facts

- R-value = thickness (m) ÷ k-value (W/mK). A 100 mm blanket with a tested k of 0,046 gives R ≈ 2,17.
- The **product R-value is not the system R-value.** Roof covering, ceiling, air gaps and surface resistances all add. Safintra quotes system R-values roughly **0,4 m²K/W higher** than product R for their blanket range.
- Reflective foil **reflects up to 97 % of radiant heat** but requires an **air gap** and **150 mm overlaps** to perform.
- DPM/DPC polyethylene sheeting: **250, 375, 500 micron** (range extends 150–500), roll widths 3 m and 6 m, lengths 30/50/60/100 m, colours black, green and orange.
- ISO 11600 classes **25, 20, 12,5 and 7,5** correspond to movement capability of **25 %, 20 %, 12,5 % and 7,5 %**.

### Applying this in a hot semi-arid climate **[NA]**

The critical heat flow is **downward summer gain through the roof**, not winter loss. The most cost-effective assembly is a light-coloured reflective roof surface, a **ventilated roof space**, a **radiant barrier under the sheeting with an air gap**, and bulk insulation **at ceiling level**. Putting all the money into a thick blanket while leaving the roof space unventilated and the sheet dark is a common and expensive mistake. Ceiling-level bulk insulation at R ≈ 2,5–3,4 (135–145 mm) plus a radiant barrier is a sensible target.

## 2. Reflective insulation — foil and sisalation

Reflective barriers work by reflecting radiant heat and by presenting a low-emissivity surface to an air space. They provide almost no benefit when in contact with the material whose heat they are meant to block.

### Installation rules that determine whether it works

**Residential (over rafters):**
1. Unroll horizontally across the rafters, printed side up.
2. Overlap **150 mm** at all joins (guideline markings are printed on the rolls).
3. **An air gap is required** between the barrier and the roof sheeting.
4. Fix between rafters and battens.
5. Pull hand-taut — do not over-stretch.
6. Do not leave exposed to sun or wind for long periods.

**Industrial/commercial (over purlins):**
1. Straining wire above the purlins, evenly tensioned; first wire **75 mm from the gable end**, then at **275 mm** centres.
2. Printed side up; **150 mm** overlaps; air gap maintained.

Secondary benefits claimed: vapour barrier, dust proofing (a genuine benefit on a dusty northern Namibian site), and reduced radiant heat transmission of up to 97 %.

> ⚠️ "Sisalation" is a generic name in southern African usage for reinforced foil laminate. Insist on a product with a stated R-value, a stated fire classification and a stated construction — the cheap unbranded foils vary enormously and some tear on installation.

## 3. Damp proof course and damp proof membrane

| Grade | Typical application |
|---|---|
| **250 micron** | Standard residential slab-on-ground; light-duty moisture protection; standard DPC in walls |
| **375 micron** | Heavier residential and light commercial |
| **500 micron** | Heavy-duty commercial and industrial slabs requiring increased puncture and tear resistance under reinforcement |

Available thickness range **150–500 micron**. Standard roll widths **3 m and 6 m**; roll lengths **30, 50, 60 and 100 m**. Colours black, green and "hyperplastic orange". DPC is supplied in narrow rolls sized to wall widths — commonly 225 mm, 300 mm and 375 mm × 40 m.

Installation points that matter:

- Lap DPM generously (150 mm minimum) and tape the laps. A DPM with untaped laps under a slab is decorative.
- Protect the DPM from puncture by reinforcement chairs and by aggregate — this is the argument for 375 or 500 micron under a reinforced slab.
- DPC in a wall must be **continuous and correctly stepped** at changes of level, and must be lapped with the DPM at the slab edge. The most common damp defect in southern African housing is a DPC that stops at the plaster line.
- Do not leave polyethylene exposed to UV. In this climate it embrittles in weeks.

## 4. Waterproofing membranes

| System | Description | Where used | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Torch-on bitumen (APP/SBS modified)** | Rolls of polymer-modified bitumen with a polyester or glass carrier, heat-welded with a gas torch; mineral-chip or foil finish | Concrete flat roofs, gutters, parapets, balconies | Robust and repairable. Needs a competent applicator, a primed and dry substrate, and correct upstand heights. Foil-faced ("Alutrix"-type) grades resist UV; plain grades must be covered or coated |
| **Liquid-applied acrylic + membrane** | Acrylic waterproofing dressed over a reinforcing fabric | Roof sheets, gutters, small flat roofs, refurbishment | Cheap, easy, forgiving of complex shapes; limited life in high UV; needs recoating |
| **Liquid-applied polyurethane** | Single or two-component PU | Trafficked roofs, balconies, complex detail | Higher performance and elongation than acrylic; more expensive; substrate preparation critical |
| **Cementitious slurry / crystalline** | Cement-based coating brushed or sprayed | Water-retaining structures, basements, planters, negative-side applications | Bonds to concrete, tolerates hydrostatic pressure, non-toxic — usable in potable water tanks |
| **Bituminous emulsion / DPC coating** | Brush or spray applied bitumen | Below-ground walls, foundation tanking, isolation of aluminium from concrete | Cheap; needs protection |
| **Single-ply sheet (TPO, PVC, EPDM)** | Mechanically fixed or adhered sheet | Large flat roofs | Fast, light; specialist installers; less common in SA domestic work |

**Detailing rules:** upstands 150 mm minimum above finished level; falls to outlets designed and checked, not assumed; overflow provision on every enclosed flat roof; and full compatibility check between the membrane, the primer, the sealant and any insulation below it (solvent-based bitumen primers dissolve polystyrene).

## 5. Sealants and movement capability

### ISO 11600 classification

**Types:**
- **Type G** — glazing sealants, for glazing joints.
- **Type F** — construction sealants, for building joints other than glazing.

**Classes** (by ability to perform in a joint with the stated movement):

| Class | Test amplitude | Movement capability | Applies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| **25** | ± 25 % | 25,0 % | Types G and F |
| **20** | ± 20 % | 20,0 % | Types G and F |
| **12,5** | ± 12,5 % | 12,5 % | Type F only |
| **7,5** | ± 7,5 % | 7,5 % | Type F only |

**Subclasses:**
- Classes 25 and 20 are sub-classified by secant tensile modulus: **LM (low modulus)** or **HM (high modulus)**. A sealant is HM if the secant modulus exceeds **0,4 N/mm² at +23 °C** or **0,6 N/mm² at −20 °C**.
- Class 12,5 is sub-classified by elastic recovery: **E (elastic)** if recovery ≥ 40 %, **P (plastic)** if < 40 %.
- Classes 25, 20 and 12,5E are **elastic** sealants; classes 12,5P and 7,5P are **plastic** sealants.

So a designation such as **F 25 LM** means a construction sealant with 25 % movement capability and low modulus — the right general choice for a wide expansion joint where the substrate is weak and you do not want the sealant to tear the arris off the concrete.

### Sealant chemistries

| Chemistry | Typical ISO class | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Neutral-cure silicone** | 25 LM / 25 HM, Type G and F | Outstanding UV and temperature resistance, excellent to glass and metal, long life | Poor paintability; can stain porous stone; limited adhesion to some substrates without primer |
| **Acetoxy silicone** | 20–25 | Cheap, fast cure, good for glazing and sanitary | Acetic acid cure corrodes metals and attacks cementitious substrates — **not for concrete, masonry or bare metal** |
| **Polyurethane (PU)** | 25 LM / 25 HM, Type F | Excellent adhesion to concrete, paintable, abrasion and tear resistant | Degrades under prolonged UV unless formulated for it; slower cure |
| **MS polymer / hybrid** | 20–25 | Paintable, primerless adhesion to most substrates, no isocyanates, low VOC, UV stable | Higher cost; lower movement capability than the best silicones |
| **Acrylic (water-based)** | 7,5–12,5 P | Cheap, paintable, easy | Low movement capability; shrinks; interior and low-movement joints only |
| **Butyl** | low | Excellent for lap joints and bedding; never fully cures | Not for exposed movement joints |
| **Polysulphide** | 25 | Chemical and fuel resistance | Specialist, expensive |

### Joint design

- **Width:** the joint width must be at least 4× the expected movement for a 25 % sealant. Undersizing the joint is the commonest cause of sealant failure.
- **Depth:** for elastic sealants in a movement joint the usual rule is depth ≈ half the width, with a minimum of about 6 mm and a maximum of about 12–15 mm.
- **Two-sided adhesion only.** Use a closed-cell backing rod or bond-breaker tape so the sealant adheres to the two joint faces and not to the back. Three-sided adhesion tears the sealant.
- **Prime** where the manufacturer's data sheet requires it. This is not optional.
- **Substrate must be clean, dry and sound.** Sealant applied over laitance, dust or a curing compound will fail regardless of quality.

**[NA] Hot-climate notes:** apply sealant when the joint is at mid-range width (mid-morning, not at peak afternoon heat when the joint is at its narrowest); check the product's application temperature range — many have an upper limit around 40 °C; and store cartridges out of the sun, because heat shortens both shelf life and tooling time.

## Open questions

- **The Aerolite and Isotherm R-value tables come from an installer's website, not from the manufacturers' own datasheets.** They are internally consistent and match published trade practice, but should be re-verified against Isover/Knauf and Isotherm primary datasheets before being used in a compliance calculation.
- **Lambdaboard R-values per thickness were not retrieved** and are therefore not tabulated. IsoBoard's specific R-value-by-thickness table was also not extracted.
- The SANS 204 climatic zone assignments shown ("zones 1 & 4 & 6" etc.) are as published by the installer and relate to **South African** climatic zones. **Namibia is not covered by SANS 204 zoning** — the applicable Namibian requirement, if any, needs separate verification.
- The sealant joint width/depth rules given are general good practice; they are **not quoted from ISO 11600** (which classifies products, not joint design) and should be checked against the specific manufacturer's data sheet.
- Den Braven's and Alcolin's Namibian distribution was not verified.
