# Steel, reinforcement, sheet metal and fixings

**Summary.** Reinforcing steel in southern Africa is specified under **SANS 920** (grades 250 "R" mild and 450 "Y" high-tensile) with bending schedules to **SANS 282**. Welded mesh is specified by a reference number that equals its mass in kg/m² × 100. Structural sections are supplied to **SANS 50025 / EN 10025 grade S355JR**. Sheet steel is defined by its base metal thickness plus a metallic coating (galvanised Z275, or aluminium-zinc AZ150/AZ200) plus, optionally, a factory paint system (Chromadek, Colorplus, Colorbond). Corrosion protection is chosen against an atmospheric corrosivity category, and inland Namibia sits at the benign end of that scale.

## Key facts

- **Y** = high tensile deformed bar, SANS 920 grade **450 MPa**. **R** = mild steel smooth round bar, SANS 920 grade **250 MPa**.
- Standard rebar lengths: **12 m and 13 m**; 6 m also stocked in the smaller diameters.
- Mesh reference number ÷ 100 = mass in **kg/m²**. Ref 193 = 1,93 kg/m².
- Standard mesh sheet: **6 m × 2,4 m**; several references also come in rolls.
- Hot dip galvanising to **SANS 121:2011 (ISO 1461:2009)**.
- Roofing fasteners to **SANS 1273:2011 (AS 3566-2-2002)**, classified C3 or C4 by environment.
- Inland Namibian corrosivity: **C1 (desert)** — uncoated mild steel < 5 µm/year, zinc loss < 0,5 µm/year.

**Indented / deformed wire (coil and straightened)**, also SANS 920: Y3,5 · Y4 · Y5 · Y5,6 · Y6 · Y8 · Y9 · Y10 · Y12, supplied in coils of 1–2 tonne or cut lengths from 1 m. Used for links, mesh manufacture and light reinforcement.

**Bending schedules** are prepared to the **SANS 282:2011** shape codes. Rules that keep a schedule buildable:

- All dimensions in a bending schedule are **external (outside) dimensions**.
- Radii of all bends are standard unless an "R" is given in the schedule.
- Use **shape code 99** for anything non-standard and supply a dimensional sketch.
- Cut-and-bend from a merchant is almost always cheaper and more accurate than site bending — but on a remote site it also freezes the design. Order cut-and-bend only from a drawing that has been checked, and carry a stock of straight bar and a bending machine for the inevitable variations.

## 4. Sheet steel, galvanising and coatings

### Terminology that must be got right when comparing quotes

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **BMT** — Base Metal Thickness | The steel substrate only, excluding metallic and paint coatings |
| **TCT** — Total Coated Thickness | Steel + metallic coating + paint system |
| **Z275** | Hot-dip galvanised: 275 g/m² of pure zinc, both surfaces combined |
| **AZ150 / AZ200** | Aluminium-zinc alloy: 150 or 200 g/m² of 55 % Al / 43,5 % Zn / 1,5 % Si |
| **G550 / ISQ550** | High-strength substrate, 550 MPa |
| **ISQ300 / G275** | Commercial/lower-strength substrate |

> ⚠️ A quote in "0,5 mm" is ambiguous unless it says BMT or TCT. A 0,50 mm TCT sheet may have a BMT nearer 0,47 mm. Always compare on **BMT**, and ask for the coil markings (brand, TCT, BMT, coating class, quality standard, coil number, production date) to verify.

### Typical BMT applications

| BMT | Application |
|---|---|
| 0,47 mm | Light residential |
| 0,53 mm | Standard residential and light commercial |
| 0,58 mm | Heavier residential / light industrial |
| 0,80 mm and above | Industrial and high-wind |

### Metallic coating choice

- **Galvanised Z275** performs adequately in dry inland environments and deteriorates faster in coastal and high-humidity regions.
- **Aluminium-zinc AZ150** — the aluminium forms a barrier layer while the zinc protects cut edges. Field performance is reported at **two to four times the service life** of galvanised steel under comparable conditions.
- **AZ200** is specified for severe coastal and industrial exposure; projects within roughly 100–200 m of open ocean exposure should generally specify AZ200.
- **[NA]** For an inland Namibian site (C1 desert), Z275 or AZ150 are both technically adequate. For Walvis Bay or Swakopmund, AZ200 is the sensible minimum.

### Pre-painted products

| Brand | Substrate | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Chromadek®** (ArcelorMittal South Africa) | Pre-painted **galvanised** (zinc-coated) steel | The best-known SA name. Suitable for inland applications; generally lower corrosion resistance than aluminium-zinc based painted products in coastal environments |
| **Colorplus®** (Safal Steel) | AZ150 or AZ200 | Developed for southern African conditions; high reflectivity and colour stability |
| **Clean Colorbond®** (BlueScope) | AZ150 | Multi-layer coatings resisting fade, chalking and pollutant build-up |
| **Zincal®** (Safal) / **Zincalume®** (BlueScope) | Unpainted AZ | The bare aluminium-zinc product |
| **Color-Tech / painted aluminium** | Aluminium | For the most aggressive environments and where weight matters |

**Understand the substrate beneath the paint.** "Chromadek" and "Colorplus" are paint systems on different substrates; comparing them on colour and price alone hides a real durability difference.

### Hot dip galvanising of fabricated steelwork

Fabricated steel (balustrades, trusses, gates, brackets) is hot dip galvanised to **SANS 121:2011 (ISO 1461:2009)**. Practical points from the Hot Dip Galvanizers Association Southern Africa:

- Small bare spots of about 5 mm² are excluded from the standard and are adequately protected by the sacrificial action of surrounding zinc; **gross uncoated areas are cause for rejection**.
- Coating thickness varies with steel chemistry. Silicon-killed and high-phosphorus steels react more and produce thicker, more brittle coatings; aluminium-killed steel less. **Fabricate a component from a single steel** to avoid mismatched coatings on welded assemblies.
- **Wet storage stain** (white zinc hydroxide) forms when freshly galvanised items are stored in close contact in the presence of moisture. It is not a rejection cause if the coating thickness under it still meets specification, but it is a real risk on a site where steel sits stacked for weeks — separate and ventilate stored galvanised steel.
- Repair of damaged galvanising is by specified methods (zinc-rich paint, zinc metal spray, low-melting-point zinc alloy). A brush of ordinary silver paint is not a repair.

## 5. Corrosion protection classes

### Atmospheric corrosivity by distance from the sea (Safintra)

| Category | Description | Severity | Uncoated mild steel loss | Zinc coating loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C5M** | Usually less than 50 m from high water mark | Severe marine | +300 µm/year | > 30 µm/year |
| **C5** | Usually 50 m to 1 km from HWM | Marine | 200–300 µm/year | 15–30 µm/year |
| **C4/C5** | 1 km to 40 km from sea | Average marine | 20–200 µm/year | 2–15 µm/year |
| **C3** | 40 km to 100 km from sea | Mildly marine | 10–20 µm/year | 1–2 µm/year |
| **C3** | Urban inland | Industrial | 10–30 µm/year | 1–3 µm/year |
| **C2** | Inland | Rural | < 10 µm/year | < 0,8 µm/year |
| **C1** | Inland | **Desert** | **< 5 µm/year** | **< 0,5 µm/year** |

### Fastener classes

| Class | Outdoor environment | Indoor environment | Steel mass loss | Zinc mass loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **C3** | Moderate airborne pollution; coastal areas with low salt; urban and industrial | Moderate humidity and some airborne pollution from production processes — laundries, breweries, dairies | > 200–400 g/m²/yr | > 5–15 g/m²/yr |
| **C4** | Discernible airborne pollution; industrial areas; coastal areas with moderate salt | High airborne pollution — chemical plants, swimming pools, dockyards | > 400–650 g/m²/yr | > 15–30 g/m²/yr |

Corrosion classification under SANS is based on the **fastener heads**, not the shanks. Where the shank may be exposed to a harsher microclimate (heavy condensation, intensive animal farming), ask the supplier about the fastener's porosity rating.

> ⚠️ Fasteners containing **stainless steel, lead, copper or copper-containing alloys (e.g. Monel) must not be used** with aluminium-zinc coated or pre-painted steel. Washers must be **carbon-black-free** for use with aluminium-zinc alloy coated and pre-painted products.

## 6. Fixings and anchors

| Type | Mechanism | Typical use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Expansion (wedge/sleeve) anchor** | Mechanical friction against the hole wall | Structural fixings into sound concrete | Needs edge distance and spacing; not for hollow or cracked substrate |
| **Drop-in anchor** | Internally expanded, flush with surface | Threaded rod suspensions | Requires setting tool |
| **Screw anchor / concrete screw** | Cuts a thread in the hole | Removable and adjustable fixings | Fast; lower ultimate load than resin |
| **Chemical / resin anchor** (vinylester, epoxy, polyester) | Bonded stud or rebar in a drilled hole | Rebar starter bars, high loads, close to edges, cracked concrete | The only reliable choice in hollow or weak substrate; hole cleaning is everything |
| **Cast-in socket / anchor channel** | Cast into the concrete | Façade brackets, lifting | Requires coordination with the concrete programme |
| **Nylon / plastic plug** | Friction | Light fixings into masonry | Not structural |
| **Toggle and cavity fixings** | Mechanical behind a board | Plasterboard, hollow blocks | Load limited by the board, not the fixing |
| **Roofing fastener** — self-drilling screw with bonded washer | Drills and taps in one action | Sheet to purlin | To SANS 1273:2011; select Class 3 or Class 4 by environment |
| **Stitching screw** | Sheet-to-sheet | Side laps at ≤ 500 mm centres | Typically #14 × 22 mm |

Practical rules: match the anchor's corrosion class to the environment and to the material it passes through; observe the manufacturer's minimum edge distance, spacing and embedment; and for resin anchors, follow the hole-cleaning procedure exactly — brushing and blowing out dust is not optional and is the most common cause of anchor failure. On a remote site, keep resin cartridges out of the heat: shelf life and gel time both collapse above about 30 °C, and a cartridge that has cooked in a bakkie is worthless.
