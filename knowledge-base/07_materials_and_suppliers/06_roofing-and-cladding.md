# Roofing and cladding — profiles, coatings, spans and manufacturers

**Summary.** Southern African metal roofing splits into **pierced fix** (corrugated, IBR and its wider relatives) and **concealed fix / standing seam** (Klip-Lok, Saflok, Newlok, Zip-Tek). Each profile has a published effective cover width, a minimum pitch, and a purlin spacing table that varies with gauge. Concrete and clay tiles remain common in domestic work. This file gives the profile data, the coating and gauge conventions, minimum pitches, fixing requirements, and the manufacturer landscape — with the practical detail a specifier needs to write an unambiguous roof specification.

## Key facts

- **IBR** = Inverted Box Rib, introduced to the South African market in **1958**; the most widely used commercial and industrial profile.
- Standard IBR: **5 trapezoidal ribs at 171 mm centres, 686 mm effective cover, 737 mm overall, 36,5 mm rib height**.
- Standard corrugated: **76 mm pitch, 17,5 mm rib height, 762 mm effective cover, 838 mm overall**.
- Maximum practical sheet length for road transport: **13,2 m**; longer needs special arrangement or on-site rolling.
- Dimensional tolerance on straight sheets: length **+10 / −0 mm**, width **±5 mm**.
- Side laps stitched at **500 mm centres** between purlins on all pierced fix profiles.
- Side and end laps to be sealed **in accordance with SANS 10400-L:2020** using a butyl product or neutral-cure silicone.

## 1. Pierced fix profiles — geometry and pitch

| Profile (Safintra name) | Rib pitch | Rib height | Effective cover | Overall width | Min pitch (slopes < 15 m) | Min pitch (slopes > 15 m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Classicorr® corrugated** | 76 mm | 17,5 mm | 762 mm | 838 mm | **10°** | **15°** |
| **Tufdek® IBR** | 171 mm (5 ribs) | 36,5 mm | 686 mm | 737 mm | **5°** | **7,5°** |
| **Widedek®** | 191 mm (5 ribs) | 29 mm | 760 mm | 810 mm | **7,5°** | **10°** |
| **Fluteline®** | — | — | 889 mm | — | — | — |
| **Versatile®** (tile profile) | 185 mm | 25,5 mm | 740 mm | 825 mm | **10°** | **10°** |

Widedek gives roughly **10 % more cover** than IBR with shallower flutes — fewer sheets, less labour, but a shallower profile and therefore a steeper minimum pitch. Fluteline is the widest-cover trapezoidal profile at 889 mm and is not rolled with stiffener ribs. All profiles except Fluteline and Versatile are rolled with stiffener ribs in the pan unless otherwise specified, which suppresses oil canning in the broad flute.

### Versatile® tile profile (0,40 mm only)

End span 600 mm; internal/double span 600 mm; cantilever 50 mm; mass 3,79 kg/m². Minimum pitch 10°. Sold as a complete system with its own roll-top ridges. Rolled in commercial-quality steel only. Handed sheet with a capillary break on one side.

**Reading the tables.** Corrugated needs roughly **half** the purlin spacing of IBR at the same gauge. That is the real cost comparison: corrugated sheet is cheaper per m², but you buy nearly twice as many purlins. On a remote site where the purlins are freighted in, IBR or Widedek usually wins on delivered cost as well as on labour.

## 3. Concealed fix and standing seam

| System | Type | Effective cover | Min pitch | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Saflok 700®** (Safintra) | Concealed fix, double interlocking, angular | **700 mm** | **2°** | Rib height 43 mm at 233 mm rib pitch (5 ribs). Clip 21 and Clip 35 options; ~1,5 clips/m² at ~330 g per clip. Sheet slides on the clip, which absorbs thermal movement. Can be curved/bullnosed. On-site rolling recommended above 13,2 m |
| **Saflok 410®** (Safintra) | Concealed fix | **410 mm** | — | Rib pitch 205 mm |
| **Newlok™** (Safintra) | **Standing seam** — mechanically seamed | — | — | The seam is closed by a seaming machine; ideal for long-length sheets on industrial and commercial buildings |
| **Klip-Lok 700** (GRS) | Concealed fix | 700 mm | — | The best-known concealed fix name in South Africa |
| **Klip-Tite, Zip-Tek 420, Brownbuilt** (GRS) | Concealed fix / standing seam | various | — | |

**Concealed fix vs standing seam.** In a concealed-fix system (Saflok, Klip-Lok) a clip is fastened to the purlin and the sheet ribs snap over it; the sheet is free to slide, which is what allows very low pitches and very long sheets. In a true standing-seam system (Newlok) the seam is mechanically folded closed after laying. Neither has fasteners penetrating the weather surface, which removes the commonest leak path.

## 4. Fixing requirements

| Profile | Roof fastener into steel | Roof fastener into timber | Cladding into steel | Side stitching |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Classicorr corrugated | #12 × 38 mm hex head metal | #12 × 65 mm hex head timber | #12 × 25 mm hex head metal | #14 × 22 mm metal stitching |
| Tufdek IBR | #12 × 65 mm hex head metal | #12 × 85 mm hex head timber | #12 × 25 mm hex head metal | #14 × 22 mm metal stitching |
| Widedek | #12 × 65 mm hex head metal | #12 × 85 mm hex head timber | #12 × 25 mm hex head metal | #14 × 22 mm metal stitching |

Rules:

- Drive fasteners **perpendicular to the sheeting and in the centre of the rib**.
- Corrugated: fix **every second rib** at eaves, ridges and apex. IBR and Widedek: fix **every rib** at eaves, ridges and apex; every second crest at intermediate purlins.
- Stitch side laps at **500 mm centres** between purlins.
- Seal side and end laps per **SANS 10400-L:2020** with butyl or neutral-cure silicone.
- All sheets are to be **tanked (turned up) at the ridge end** and **lipped (turned down) at the eave end**.
- Clean the roof daily during installation — swarf, pop rivets and offcuts left on an AZ-coated sheet will rust into the coating and start a corrosion cell.
- Select fastener corrosion class (C3 or C4) against the environment, to **SANS 1273:2011**, with the fastener service life warranty matched to the sheeting warranty. See `04_steel-and-reinforcement.md` for the class table and the compatibility restrictions.

| Material option | Available gauges (mm) |
|---|---|

| Aluminium, unpainted or pre-painted | 0,80 |
| Zinc-coated steel Z200 / Z275, ISQ550 / ISQ300, unpainted or pre-painted | 0,50 · 0,58 |

\* 0,80 available in G275/ISQ300 only. Material and coating thickness vary regionally — confirm availability with the branch.

**[NA] Colour choice matters more here than in South Africa.** In a hot semi-arid climate with intense insolation, a light, high-solar-reflectance roof colour reduces roof surface temperature substantially, which reduces heat gain into the roof space, reduces thermal movement, and extends coating life. Dark roofs in this climate are an aesthetic decision paid for in cooling load and in coating degradation.

## 7. Tiles

### Concrete tiles (Marley Double Roman — representative)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Minimum pitch | **17,5°** (with 100 mm headlap) |
| Maximum pitch | 45° (above this, consult manufacturer) |
| Minimum headlap | 75 mm at pitch ≥ 26°; **100 mm at 17,5°–25°** |
| Maximum gauge (batten spacing) | 320 mm (100 mm headlap, 17,5°–26°); 345 mm (75 mm headlap, ≥ 26°) |
| Cover width | 301 mm nominal |
| Covering capacity | 10,42 tiles/m² at 100 mm headlap; 9,66 tiles/m² at 75 mm headlap |
| Weight of tiling | ≈ **43,76 kg/m²** at 100 mm headlap; ≈ 40,57 kg/m² at 75 mm |
| Battens required | 3,13 lin.m/m² at 100 mm headlap |
| Batten size | 38 × 38 mm for rafters ≤ 760 mm centres; 38 × 50 mm flat for ≤ 900 mm; 38 × 50 mm on edge for ≤ 1 000 mm |
| First batten: top edge to outside of fascia | 335 mm |
| Top batten: top edge to rafter apex | 25 mm |
| Underlay | Radiant barrier **compulsory below 26°**; radiant barrier or undertile membrane compulsory above 26° |
| Coating guarantees | M11 coated finish — 7-year guarantee; M22 superior coated finish — 22-year guarantee (registration required) |

Battens are graded SA Pine complying with the softwood brandering and battens standard; verge counterbattens at gable ends are always 38 × 50 mm on edge. Counterbattens are required at valleys and hips to support cut tile ends.

**The weight is the design driver.** At roughly 44 kg/m², a concrete tile roof is about **eight times** the mass of 0,53 mm IBR (5,42 kg/m²). That flows into the truss design, the wall loading and the foundations. Deciding tile-versus-sheet after the structure is designed is expensive.

### Clay tiles

Clay roof tiles are available in southern Africa in Marseille, Roman and flat profiles. They are more durable and colour-stable than concrete but more expensive and heavier to freight. In Namibia they are an import item.

## 8. Other roof coverings

### Thatch

**[NA]** Thatch is the traditional roof of northern Namibia and remains a live option, particularly for lodge and community buildings.

| Factor | Note |
|---|---|
| Material | Locally harvested grass (in southern Africa most often *Hyparrhenia* / *Cymbopogon* species) on a timber or gum-pole frame |
| Thermal | Excellent — thick thatch is a very effective insulator and gives a cool interior in high heat |
| Sourcing | Local, low cash cost, high labour content; supports local economy |
| Pitch | Steep pitch is essential for a thatch roof to shed water; the traditional forms reflect this |
| **Fire** | The dominant risk. Fire regulations, lightning protection, spark arrestors and separation distances are all engaged. **Requires a rational fire design in most jurisdictions** |
| Insurance | Thatch attracts materially higher insurance premiums and some insurers decline it |
| Maintenance | Periodic re-ridging and eventual re-thatching; needs a skilled thatcher |

> ⚠️ Do not specify thatch without addressing fire (rational design, lightning protection, fire-retardant treatment where required) and confirming insurability. The fire requirements are the reason most thatch schemes fail at approval stage, not the material.

### Translucent sheeting

Matched to the metal profile so it can be laid in the same run.

| Material | Characteristics |
|---|---|
| **Polycarbonate** | Highest impact resistance, good UV stability with a co-extruded UV layer, higher cost. Available in the standard IBR, corrugated and Klip-Lok/Saflok profiles |
| **GRP / fibreglass** | Cheaper; yellows and fibre-blooms with UV exposure; shorter life in a high-UV climate |
| **PVC** | Cheapest; poorest UV and heat performance; not recommended for a high-insolation site |

Fixing: translucent sheets expand far more than steel. Safintra supplies a **polyslider** and saddle-washer arrangement for polycarbonate and high-impact sheeting, which allows large thermal movement and uses a 3 mm EVA seal to prevent water ingress. Use oversized holes and never over-tighten.

**[NA]** In a high-insolation climate, translucent panels are a heat-gain liability as much as a daylight benefit. Use sparingly, on the south-facing slope, and consider tinted or opal grades.

### Insulation-backed and composite panels

| System | Description |
|---|---|
| **Spacer system + blanket** | A spacer bar lifts the outer sheet off the purlin, creating a void for polyester or glass wool blanket over a liner sheet. Safintra's Roof Spacer System is the reference. Also used for **over-roofing** an existing (e.g. asbestos) roof |
| **Composite / sandwich panel** | Factory-bonded metal-insulation-metal panel (PIR, PUR, mineral wool or EPS core) | 
| **Insulation-backed sheet** | Sheet supplied with insulation bonded to the underside |

Sandwich panels give the best thermal performance per millimetre and the fastest erection, but they are expensive, must be ordered to length, and are effectively unrepairable. Check the core's reaction-to-fire classification — PIR and mineral wool cores behave very differently in fire from EPS.
