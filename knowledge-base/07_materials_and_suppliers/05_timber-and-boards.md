# Timber, treatment classes and board products

**Summary.** Structural timber in South Africa and Namibia is South African Pine graded to **SANS 1783-2** in three stress grades — **S5, S7 and S10** — where the number is roughly the allowable bending stress in MPa. Every piece must carry a mark showing manufacturer, grade, grading method and, if treated, the H class. Preservative treatment is specified by **hazard class H2 to H6** under **SANS 1288**, with process guidance in **SANS 10005**. Getting the H class right is the single most consequential timber decision on a hot, termite-active site.

## Key facts

- Structural stress-graded lumber must be at least **38 mm thick and 50 mm wide**. 25 mm and 16 mm stock cannot carry a structural grade.
- S5 has a **characteristic** bending strength of **11,5 MPa**; the *allowable* value (÷2,22) is about 5,2 MPa — hence the name.
- Minimum density: **S5 > 360 kg/m³, S7 > 425 kg/m³, S10 > 475 kg/m³**.
- **S10 availability is limited** in the South African market.
- All treatment plants in South Africa must be approved by the NRCS and monitored through an approved third-party product certification scheme; treated timber must show a certification mark, the **H class** and the SANS standard.
- **In certain magisterial districts of South Africa it is illegal to use untreated timber.**

### Characteristic strength and stiffness (MPa; from draft SANS 10163-1 and SANS 1707-1)

| Grade | Bending f_b | Tension ∥ f_t | Tension ⊥ f_tp | Compression ∥ f_c | Compression ⊥ f_cp | Shear ∥ f_s | Rolling shear f_sr | E_min | E_mean |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S5** | 11,5 | 6,7 | 0,36 | 18,0 | 4,7 | 1,6 | 0,36 | 4 875 | 7 800 |
| **S7** | 15,8 | 10,0 | 0,51 | 22,8 | 6,7 | 2,0 | 0,51 | 6 000 | 9 600 |
| **S10** | 23,3 | 13,3 | 0,73 | 26,2 | 9,1 | 2,9 | 0,73 | 7 500 | 12 000 |
| **EH** (green Eucalyptus) | 20,8 | 14,1 | 0,30 | 19,3 | 3,9 | 2,2 | 0,51 | 7 041 | 9 826 |

These apply to visually, mechanically or proof-graded timber complying with SANS 1783-2 (grades 5, 7, 10), SANS 1460 (grades 5, 7, 10) or SANS 1707 as appropriate.

> ⚠️ **Characteristic strength is a statistical value.** It is the 5th-percentile strength of a large sample. You cannot test one board and conclude that it does or does not meet a grade.

### Density and knot criteria (SANS 1783-2 visual grading)

| Grade | Minimum density |
|---|---|
| S5 | > 360 kg/m³ |
| S7 | > 425 kg/m³ |
| S10 | > 475 kg/m³ |

The other principal visual criterion is knot cover: the maximum circumferential distance of knot cover at the worst section is the sum of one face width plus 1,5 edge widths.

### Reading the grade mark

The mark must show: manufacturer name or trade mark; the stress grade and grading method; for mechanically graded lumber the date or batch number; for finger-jointed lumber the letters **FJ** in a colour representing the adhesive exposure class plus date and batch (SANS 10096); and for pressure-treated lumber, the **H class** (SANS 1288).

The position of a dot on the mark encodes the grading method:

| Method | Mark form |
|---|---|
| Visual | `S •` certification mark `•5` |
| Mechanical | `S` certification mark `• 5` |
| Proof | `S •` certification mark `5` |

Untreated, unmarked or "black cross" / XXX second-grade timber does not comply with SANS structural requirements and must not be used structurally, whatever the yard says.

## 2. Preservative treatment — hazard classes H2 to H6

South Africa uses five hazard-class levels under the SANS standards (H2, H3, H4, H5, H6).

| Class | Name | Where required | Typical applications | Usual chemicals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **H2** | Internal (low hazard) | Interior, dry, protected from weather | Roof trusses, laminated beams, internal structural timber, ceiling boards, flooring, panelling, doors, cupboards, skirting, window frames, plywood | Waterborne CCA and boron; LOSP |
| **H3** | Exterior above ground (moderate hazard) | Outdoors, not in ground contact | Balustrades, fencing bearers and slats, decking and beams, garden furniture, laminated beams, weatherboard, steps, cladding, stairs, log homes, gates, fascia boards, plywood, droppers; spacers and cross-arms for poles | CCA, creosote |
| **H4** | Ground contact (high hazard) | In the ground | Agricultural posts, landscaping structures, playground structures, fencing, pergolas, carports, flower boxes, decking, bridges, stakes, electrical/distribution/telephone/lighting poles, garden edging | CCA, creosote |
| **H5** | Fresh water and heavy wet soils (high hazard) | Continual wetting or planted in wet soil | Jetties, drains, walkways, retaining walls, slipways, agricultural poles under frequent irrigation and fertiliser, piling and foundation poles for permanent structures including timber homes | CCA, creosote |
| **H6** | Marine (high hazard) | Sea water | Jetties, slipways, retaining walls, walkways | **Dual treatment**: CCA *with* creosote — only this combination is recommended against marine borers |

Treatment must be by an impregnation process (vacuum/pressure cycles) — **brushed-on preservative does not achieve a hazard class**. Boron may under specific conditions be applied by dip-diffusion at elevated moisture content.

### What this means on a northern Namibian site **[NA]**

- **Roof trusses and internal structure: H2 minimum.** Termite pressure in Ohangwena is high and the standard SA convention of H2 for trusses assumes an enclosed, protected roof space. If the roof space is open, dusty and periodically damp, treat as H3.
- **Anything touching the ground: H4, no exceptions.** Fence posts, carport posts, pergola legs, garden edging, formwork stakes left in place. H3 timber in ground contact will fail within a few seasons.
- **Fascias, barge boards, exposed pergola and shading members: H3.**
- **Do not "upgrade" by painting.** A coating is not a preservative treatment.
- **CCA-treated timber has disposal implications** — do not burn it, and treat offcuts as controlled waste.

## 3. Standard sizes available in South Africa and Namibia

The stocked cross-sections follow the SANS 1783 dimension families:

| Thickness (mm) | Widths commonly stocked (mm) | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| 38 | 38, 50 (brandering/battens); 76, 114, 152, 228 (structural) | Trusses, rafters, ceiling brandering, tile battens |
| 50 | 38, 50 (battens); 76, 152, 228 | Purlins, joists, framing |
| 76 | 76, 152, 228 | Heavy beams, posts |
| 25 | 76, 114, 152, 228 | Industrial/appearance grade only — **not structural** |

Lengths are typically supplied from about 1,8 m up in 300 mm increments; 6,0 m and 6,6 m are the usual maxima in standard stock, with longer lengths by order. Confirm the length range with the specific yard — northern Namibian branches often carry a shorter length range than Windhoek.

**Roof battens for concrete tiles:** Marley's fixing specification calls for battens of graded SA Pine complying with the softwood brandering and battens standard, with minimum sizes of **38 × 38 mm** for rafters/supports not exceeding 760 mm centres, **38 × 50 mm laid flat** for rafters not exceeding 900 mm centres, and **38 × 50 mm on edge** for rafters not exceeding 1 000 mm centres. Verge counterbattens at gable ends must always be 38 × 50 mm on edge.

## 4. Roof trusses

Nail-plated timber trusses are the default roof structure in southern African domestic and light commercial work.

- Design and fabrication are controlled through the **ITC-SA** (Institute for Timber Construction South Africa) system, using proprietary connector-plate design software (MiTek, Multinail and others). SANS 10400 Part L covers the regulatory requirement.
- **Timber grade:** S5 is standard, S7 where spans or loads require it. The grade must be stamped on every member.
- **Treatment:** H2 as a minimum; specify explicitly.
- **What to insist on:** an ITC-SA (or equivalent) certificate for the truss design, an erector's certificate on completion, and correct bracing. Trusses fail from missing bracing far more often than from undersized members.
- **Practical note for remote sites:** trusses are bulky and awkward to freight. Options are (a) fabricate in Windhoek or Oshakati and freight complete, (b) fabricate on site from a supplied cutting list and plates, or (c) design a site-built rafter roof in graded timber. Option (b) requires a press or a competent hand-nailing method and is the usual compromise. BUCO describes itself as South Africa's largest roof truss manufacturer and offers design, site visit, manufacture and delivery.

**[NA] Namibia has no significant commercial softwood plantation resource**; structural pine and treated timber are effectively all imported from South Africa. This has three consequences: timber is a long-lead item relative to cement and blockwork; the grade and H-class mark is the only practical way to verify what you received; and substitutions at the merchant counter (an untreated or H2 board supplied where H3 was ordered) are a real and common risk. Check the stamps on delivery, not later.
