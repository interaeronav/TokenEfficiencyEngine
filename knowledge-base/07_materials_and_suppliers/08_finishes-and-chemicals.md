# Plaster, screeds, tile adhesives, paints and construction chemicals

**Summary.** Cement plaster is governed by **SANS 2001-EM1** with sand to **SANS 1090**; screeds and toppings follow the Cement & Concrete SA guidance and are batched at a fixed proportion rather than designed for strength. Tile adhesives are classified by the **C/S/T/F/E** system (adopted into SANS from EN 12004) — C1 or C2, with T for slip resistance, F for fast set, and S1/S2 for deformability. Paint is a system, not a product: substrate preparation plus primer plus topcoat. This file gives the mixes, thicknesses, classifications and the brand landscape in South Africa and Namibia.

## Key facts

- Plaster sand to **SANS 1090:2009**; the standard is "no more than a guide" — sands that comply may still plaster badly and sands that fail may plaster well.
- Screed batching: **130 ℓ of moist loose sand : 50 kg cement**, water to a plastic workable consistence. Two barrows + one bag ≈ **115 ℓ of compacted screed**.
- Screed batch must be used within **45 minutes** of mixing.
- Builder's wheelbarrow capacity is taken as **65 ℓ**.
- Plaster: single coat **10–16 mm**, never thicker than 16 mm.
- **Karoo sands must not be used for plastering** — excessive swelling and shrinkage cause shrinkage cracking.
- **Crusher sands are generally unsuitable for plaster** (angular particle shape), except in rich mixes for special applications.

## 1. Plaster

### Sand — SANS 1090:2009 grading for plaster sand

| Sieve | % passing by mass |
|---|---|
| 4,75 mm | 100 |
| 2,36 mm | 90–100 |
| 1,18 mm | 70–100 |
| 600 µm | 40–90 |
| 300 µm | 5–65 |
| 150 µm | 5–20 |

Some coarser material may be acceptable, or desirable, for textured decorative work. For conventional smooth plaster **all the sand should pass a 2,36 mm sieve**.

Sand quality rules:
- Must be free of organic matter — roots, seeds, twigs, humus. Absolutely essential for white or pigmented plaster.
- Lumps that cannot be broken between the fingers indicate a sand that is not ideal; sieve them out.
- High clay content causes high water demand and high drying shrinkage. Diagnostic: the fraction passing 0,075 mm can, when moistened, be rolled into a thread about 3 mm or less in diameter.
- **[NA]** Northern Namibian dune sands are fine and single-sized; they will usually need lime (or a plasticiser) to give workability, and the mix should be checked by trial panel before the main work starts.

### Mix proportions (SANS 2001-EM1:2007, Table 2)

| Type | Common cement (kg) | Lime (kg) | Masonry cement MC 22,5X or MC 12,5X (kg) | Masonry cement MC 12,5 (kg) | Sand, loose damp (ℓ) | Sand (barrows) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **External plaster** | 50 | 0–25 | — | — | 150 | 2,5 |
| **External plaster** | — | — | 50 | — | 130 | 2 |
| **Internal plaster** | 50 | 0–25 | — | — | 200 | 3 |
| **Internal plaster** | — | — | 50 | — | 150 | 2,5 |
| **Internal plaster** | — | — | — | 50 | 100 | 1,5 |

**Below DPC / permanently moist positions:** the preferred proportions are 50 kg common cement — **preferably CEM II B-V or CEM II B-W** (fly ash) — with 2 barrows (130 ℓ) of plaster sand.

**Weak substrates (Mix C, for poorly burnt clay brick or other very weak substrate):** 50 kg cement + 0–50 kg hydrated builder's lime + 300 ℓ loose damp sand; or 50 kg masonry cement + 200 ℓ sand.

> ⚠️ Builder's lime and air-entraining agents **must not be used with masonry cement**.

### Thickness

| Coat | Thickness |
|---|---|
| First undercoat | 8–16 mm |
| Second undercoat (if any) | 6–10 mm |
| Finish coat | 5–8 mm |
| **Single coat** | **10–16 mm, never more than 16 mm** |

Where the overall thickness exceeds the recommendation, mechanically anchor the plaster to the substrate (e.g. stainless steel studs). Do the same on dense non-absorbent substrates.

### Substrate preparation

Assess absorptiveness by throwing about a cupful of water at the wall:

| Category | Behaviour | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | No water absorbed (e.g. hard-burnt clay face brick, dense concrete) | Needs a bonding key — spatterdash or bonding agent; mechanical anchoring for thick work |
| **II** | Some absorbed, most runs off | Normal preparation |
| **III** | Most absorbed | Damp the wall before plastering to prevent the plaster drying out |

The substrate must be **rough** (positive key), **absorbent to a limited extent**, and **strong and clean** — free of dust, oil, paint or any film that impairs bond. The substrate's strength must be greater than or equal to the hardened plaster's. Complete all chases and fix all conduits and boxes before plastering.

**[NA] Hot-climate plastering.** The leaflet's warning is directly applicable: cements with slow early strength development should be used only if the plaster can be protected against sun and wind. In a dry, windy site, plaster loses water to evaporation before it gains tensile strength and crazes. Work in the shade of the day, damp the wall, and cure — a single wetting is not curing.

## 2. Screeds and toppings

### Definitions

- **Screed** — a layer of well compacted material, usually cement and fine aggregate, applied to a base at the appropriate thickness with a surface suitable for receiving a floor finish.
- **Topping** — a layer of high-strength concrete to provide a dense abrasion-resistant surface, or to increase the structural depth and strength of a base.

### Three types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Monolithic** | Applied while the base concrete is still plastic |
| **Bonded** | Applied to hardened concrete |
| **Unbonded** | Separated from the base by insulation boards or an impervious membrane, where a good bond cannot be assured |

Screeds and toppings should be specified **only where placing and finishing the concrete floor to acceptable standards is impracticable**. A power-floated slab is better than a slab plus a screed, and cheaper.

### Screed batching

| Component | Quantity |
|---|---|
| Sand (moist, loose state) | 130 ℓ |
| Cement | 50 kg |
| Water | Sufficient for a plastic, workable consistence |

Two barrow loads of sand plus one bag of cement produce approximately **115 ℓ of compacted screed**. Never batch more than can be used within **45 minutes**. Batching containers must be filled flush to the rim.

### Toppings

- Concrete for a topping: characteristic 28-day compressive strength appropriate to the required abrasion resistance, **at least 20 MPa** where abrasion is not a consideration.
- Coarse aggregate nominal size: **6,7 mm** for monolithic toppings of 25 mm nominal thickness; **not exceeding 9,5 mm** for separate bonded toppings of 30 mm nominal thickness. Above 40 mm thickness, increase the aggregate to one quarter of the topping thickness, maximum 19 mm.
- Aggregates to **SANS 1083**.
- **Bonded topping panels should not exceed 9 m²**.

### Panels, joints and tolerances

- Lay screeds in areas **as large as practicable** in one operation. Large areas may crack at random as they dry, but random cracks are more acceptable than the **curling** that occurs at vertical butt joints when screeds are laid in small panels.
- Joints in the base concrete must be **continued through the screed and line up exactly**.
- Where the screed sits on precast elements and joint continuation is impossible, reinforcing mesh in the screed can control cracking, provided panels do not become excessive.
- Permissible deviation from datum: **±15 mm** for large areas for normal purposes; tighter in small rooms and where a thin flooring is applied.
- Screed strength can be checked with the **BRE screed tester** — a dropped mass on a standard foot, indentation after four impacts read against acceptance limits by category. Agree the acceptance criterion **before** the job starts to prevent disputes afterwards.

## 3. Tile adhesives and grouts

### Classification

Adhesives are designated by a letter code (adopted into SANS from EN 12004):

| Code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **C** | Cementitious adhesive |
| **D** | Dispersion (ready-mixed) adhesive |
| **R** | Reaction resin adhesive (epoxy, PU) |
| **1** | Normal tensile adhesion strength |
| **2** | Improved tensile adhesion strength |
| **F** | Fast setting |
| **T** | Slip resistant (reduced slip) |
| **E** | Extended open time |
| **S1** | Deformable |
| **S2** | Highly deformable |

Worked example from the TAL Goldstar 6 data sheet:

| Mixed with | Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Water | **C1TF** | Cementitious, normal adhesion, slip resistant, fast setting |
| TAL Keycoat or TAL Screedbinder (partial water replacement) | **C2TFS1** | Improved adhesion, slip resistant, fast setting, **deformable** |
| TAL Keycoat or TAL Screedbinder (total water replacement) | **C2TFS2** | Improved adhesion, slip resistant, fast setting, **highly deformable** |

This is the key practical insight: **the same bag of powder gives a different classification depending on the gauging liquid.** A specification that says "C2S1 adhesive" is only met if the correct additive is actually used on site.

### Choosing an adhesive

| Situation | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Standard ceramic wall/floor tile on plaster or screed, internal, dry | C1 |
| Porcelain / low-porosity tile | **C2** minimum (porcelain has near-zero absorption; the adhesive must bond chemically, not by suction) |
| Large-format tile (> ~600 mm side) | C2, plus back-buttering and a notched trowel sized for full bed coverage |
| Wet areas, showers, external | C2 plus a waterproofing membrane behind |
| Substrate that will move — new concrete, timber floors, heated screeds, external in direct sun | **S1 or S2** deformable |
| Natural stone | Check for staining; use a rapid-set or white adhesive for light stone |
| Swimming pools, chemical exposure | R (epoxy) adhesive and grout |
| Vertical work, wall tiling | **T** (slip resistant) |

### Grouts

| Type | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cementitious grout, unsanded | Joints ≤ 3 mm | |
| Cementitious grout, sanded | Joints 3–12 mm+ | |
| Polymer-modified cementitious | General | Better colour retention and water resistance |
| Epoxy grout | Chemical exposure, food areas, pools, heavy hygiene requirements | Expensive, unforgiving application window |

Movement joints are required in tiling regardless of adhesive: at all perimeters, at changes of plane, over structural joints, and at intervals within large fields. Fill them with a sealant of appropriate ISO 11600 class, **not** with grout.

## 4. Paints and coatings

Paint is a **system**: substrate preparation → primer → intermediate/undercoat → topcoat. Skipping the primer is the commonest cause of coating failure in southern Africa.

### Primers by substrate

| Substrate | Primer |
|---|---|
| New cement plaster (alkaline) | Alkali-resistant plaster primer; allow the plaster to cure and dry — new plaster is highly alkaline and will saponify an unsuitable coating |
| Chalky / powdery masonry | Bonding liquid / masonry stabiliser |
| Bare ferrous metal | Zinc phosphate or red-oxide primer; zinc-rich primer where sacrificial protection is wanted |
| Galvanised / Zincalume | **Etch primer or a dedicated galvanised-iron primer** — ordinary metal primers do not adhere to zinc |
| Bare timber | Wood primer; knotting solution over resinous knots |
| Previously painted, sound | Wash, degloss, spot-prime bare areas |

### Coating types

| Type | Binder | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **PVA / acrylic emulsion** | Water-based acrylic | Interior walls and ceilings — the volume product |
| **Acrylic exterior wall coating** | Water-based acrylic, higher binder and pigment loading | Exterior masonry; look for UV and dirt-pickup resistance |
| **Enamel — water-based** | Acrylic / hybrid | Trim and joinery; low odour, non-yellowing |
| **Enamel — solvent-based (alkyd)** | Alkyd | Trim, doors, metal; harder film, yellows over time |
| **Roof coating** | Acrylic or PU | Metal and tile roofs; must be compatible with the substrate coating |
| **Epoxy** | Two-pack | Floors, plant rooms, chemical exposure |
| **Polyurethane** | Two-pack | High-durability topcoats, floors, marine |
| **Bituminous** | Bitumen | Below ground, isolation, damp-proofing |
| **Textured / thick-film coating** | Acrylic with aggregate | Exterior masonry; bridges hairline cracks |

**[NA] High-UV specification notes.** In this climate, exterior coating life is dominated by UV and heat, not by moisture. Specify: high-quality **100 % acrylic** exterior coatings rather than PVA; light and mid-tone colours (deep organic pigments fade fastest); a full system from one manufacturer so the warranty is intact; and a realistic maintenance cycle. Do not paint in direct sun on a hot wall — the film skins before it flows, and adhesion suffers.

## 5. Construction chemicals

| Category | What it does | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete admixtures | Plasticisers, superplasticisers, retarders, accelerators, air entrainers | See `02_cement-concrete-aggregates.md` for dosages and cautions |
| Curing compounds | Form a film that retards evaporation | Essential in hot dry conditions; check compatibility with subsequent finishes — most must be removed or scarified before tiling or coating |
| Bonding agents / SBR latex | Improve bond of new to old cementitious material | Also used to gauge repair mortars |
| Repair mortars | Polymer-modified cementitious mortars for concrete repair | Specify structural vs cosmetic; match modulus to the parent concrete |
| Grouts (structural) | Non-shrink cementitious or epoxy grouts under baseplates | Specify by strength and flow characteristic |
| Waterproofing admixtures / crystalline | Integral water resistance in concrete | See `07` |
| Release agents | Prevent formwork adhesion | Chemical release agents give a better concrete surface than oil |
| Anchoring resins | Vinylester, epoxy, polyester chemical anchors | See `04` |
| Floor hardeners and sealers | Sodium/lithium silicate densifiers, acrylic sealers | For power-floated slabs |
| Joint fillers | Semi-rigid epoxy or PU for saw-cut floor joints | Different product class from movement-joint sealants |

**[NA] Availability in Namibia.** Plascon, Dulux, Duram, TAL, Sika, a.b.e. and Alcolin products are all carried by the major Namibian merchants (Pupkewitz Megabuild, Build it, Cashbuild, BUCO, Agrimark). What the northern branches will *not* reliably hold is the specialist end: two-pack epoxies, S2 deformable adhesives, specific torch-on membrane grades, and colour-matched systems. Order those from Windhoek or South Africa with lead time.
