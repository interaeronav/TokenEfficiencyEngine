# How to legally obtain building standards, Acts and regulations

**Summary.** Acts, regulations, government gazettes and municipal by-laws are free in both countries. SANS and NAMS standards are copyright, sold per document, and are the substantial cost. A full SANS 10400 set costs roughly **R16 400** at 2026-08-25 prices; the loading and material design codes add several thousand rand more. There are four legitimate ways to reduce that cost: buy only the parts you need, use an employer's or institution's subscription, read a standard free at a Namibian council office under Local Authorities Act s 94B(4), or use a public/university library. There is no legitimate free download of a current SANS part.

> ⚠️ Copies of SANS parts circulate on file-sharing sites, on Internet Archive and on document-sharing platforms. They are infringing copies, they are almost always **superseded editions**, and using one for a design that is submitted for approval or relied on commercially exposes you to both an infringement claim and a professional-negligence claim if the edition has changed. Use them, if at all, only to orient yourself before buying the correct current edition.

## Buying SANS standards — SABS **[ZA]**

### The webstore

**https://store.sabs.co.za/** is the SABS's Magento webstore. Structure and features observed on 2026-08-25:

- Standards are browsable by sector; the relevant tree is **Construction → Building Materials / Cement & Concrete / Building Construction / Site Preparation / Demolition / Construction & Maintenance**.
- Search works best with the form `SANS 10400`, `SANS 10160`, `SANS 2001-CM` — i.e. the word "SANS" plus the number. Bare numbers frequently return nothing.
- Each product page offers a **PREVIEW** (normally the cover, contents and scope pages) before purchase — useful for confirming the clause structure and edition before committing.
- Features include **COLLECTIONS**, **BUY ISO STANDARDS**, **CLAIM TOKEN**, **CREDIT RECHARGE** and a **Free Downloads** section. The free downloads observed were ISO/IEC document-format standards (SANS 29500 series) — **no building standards are free**.
- The store does not support Internet Explorer and requires JavaScript.

### Sensible purchase order for a residential practice

If you cannot buy everything at once, buy in this order:

1. **SANS 10400-A** (R1 202,90) — the keystone; occupancy classification, competent persons, forms, category 1 definition. Nothing else makes sense without it.
2. **SANS 10400-K** (R1 282,25) — wall panel tables. The most-used deemed-to-satisfy content in masonry housing.
3. **SANS 10400-T** (R1 373,10) — fire. The part most likely to be checked hard by a BCO and the part with the most liability.
4. **SANS 10400-XA** (R1 124,70) — mandatory energy compliance on every new building.
5. **SANS 10400-H** (R1 124,70) and **-B** (R915,40) — foundations and structural framing.
6. **SANS 10400-L** (R1 282,25) — roofs.
7. **SANS 10400-P** (R1 124,70), **-O** (R954,50), **-C** (R431,25), **-M** (R484,15), **-N** (R523,25) — drainage, lighting/ventilation, dimensions, stairs, glazing.
8. **SANS 10400-S** (R1 373,10) — accessibility, once you do anything other than a private house.
9. The rest as needed.

Add, for anyone doing structural work: **SANS 10160-1** (R1 282,25) and **-3** (R1 282,25) at minimum; then **-2** (R876,30), **-4** (R1 007,40) and **-5** (R954,50). For masonry execution: **SANS 2001-CM1** (R1 124,70) and **SANS 2001-CM2** (R601,45). For electrical: **SANS 10142-1** (R1 649,10).

### Subscriptions and multi-user licences

SABS operates a **standards subscription / collections** service and a token-and-credit system on the webstore. Larger firms, universities and government departments typically hold a subscription giving networked access to a defined collection rather than buying single-user PDFs. Terms, collection definitions and pricing are not published on the store and must be requested.

*Subscription pricing and terms were not verified from a public source.* Contact SABS Standards Sales via the store's Help section or via https://www.sabs.co.za/Standard-Sales/.

### Practical notes

- The single-user PDF is normally **watermarked and non-printable or print-limited**. Check the licence terms before assuming you can circulate it to a site team.
- SABS re-issues parts frequently. Five SANS 10400 parts carry a **2026** date and four a **2025** date. Budget for re-purchase, or hold a subscription.
- The **PREVIEW** function is the cheapest way to confirm the edition, scope and clause structure of a part before buying — and to confirm whether the clause you are looking for still exists.

The NSI online shop (https://nsi.com.na/shop/) sells **284 NAMS standards** priced in Namibian dollars, observed in the range **N$75,00 to N$428,00** on 2026-08-25.

**What NAMS standards actually cover.** The catalogue is dominated by:

- **Agricultural and food standards** — table grapes, dates, milk products, onions, potatoes, cabbages, butternut.
- **NAMS/EN and NAMS/IEC adoptions** of European and international standards, e.g.:
  - **NAMS/EN 81-21:2019** — Safety rules for the construction and installation of lifts, Part 21: new passenger and goods passenger lifts in existing buildings (**N$428,00**);
  - **NAMS/IEC 60364-5-53:2016** and **:2015** — Electrical installations of buildings, Part 5-53: selection and erection of electrical equipment, isolation, switching and control.
- **NAMS 0001:2016** — *Requirements for Dry Sanitation Technologies in Namibia*. **This is directly relevant to rural Namibian construction** and is the closest thing Namibia has to a national sanitation building standard. It is the Namibian counterpart to SANS 10400-Q.

**What NAMS does not include: any national building code.** There is no NAMS equivalent of SANS 10400. Namibian designers buy SANS from the SABS webstore.

**ISO standards** are sold to Namibian buyers through the ISO portal linked from the NSI site (https://www.isolutions.iso.org), not through the NSI shop directly.

### The free inspection right — LAA s 94B(4)

> ⚠️ **This is the most under-used route in Namibia.** Where a local authority's building regulations incorporate a standard publication by reference under Local Authorities Act s 94B, that council **must keep copies of the standard publication, any supplementary standard incorporated by reference in it, and any amendments, open to inspection free of charge at the council's offices during official office hours** (s 94B(4)).

Practically: identify which standards the council has incorporated, then go to the council offices and read the clause you need. You may not copy it, but you can verify a value at zero cost — and you get the *edition the council has actually incorporated*, which under s 94B(3) may not be the latest edition. That is the edition you are legally held to in that jurisdiction.

## Cost planning — a realistic standards budget

**A small residential architectural practice, South Africa, starting from zero (2026-08-25 prices):**

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| SANS 10400 parts A, B, C, H, K, L, M, N, O, P, S, T, XA | ≈ R13 000 |
| SANS 10160-1, -2, -3 | ≈ R3 440 |
| SANS 10142-1 | R1 649 |
| SANS 2001-CM1, CM2 | R1 726 |
| SANS 10137, 1263-1 | R2 132 |
| **Total** | **≈ R22 000** |

Plus periodic re-purchase as editions change. A subscription is likely to be cheaper for anyone buying more than about 15 documents.

**A Namibian practice** needs the same SANS documents (bought in rand from the SABS webstore), plus **NAMS 0001** for dry sanitation if working rurally (N$75–N$312 band), plus nothing else — but should first establish, at zero cost, which standards the relevant council has incorporated under s 94B, and read those free at the council office.
