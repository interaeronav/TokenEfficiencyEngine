# South Africa — National Building Regulations and Building Standards Act 103 of 1977

**Summary.** Act 103 of 1977 is the single national statute governing the erection of buildings in South Africa. It makes it an offence to erect a building without the prior written approval of the local authority, requires every local authority to appoint a Building Control Officer, empowers the Minister to make the National Building Regulations, and creates the occupancy certificate as the legal precondition for occupying a completed building. The Regulations made under s 17 are *functional*: they state the performance a building must achieve. Compliance is demonstrated either by following the deemed-to-satisfy rules in the SANS 10400 series, or by a rational design or rational assessment prepared by an approved competent person appointed under Regulation A19.

## Key facts

| Item | Provision | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition on unapproved erection | s 4(1) | No person may erect a building without the prior written approval of the local authority |
| Application form and content | s 4(3) | Written application on the local authority's form, with the required plans and specifications |
| BCO appointment | s 5 | Every local authority must appoint a building control officer; prescribed qualifications unless the Minister approves otherwise; authorities may appoint jointly |
| BCO functions | s 6 | Make recommendations on plans; ensure instructions carried out; inspect erection; report non-compliance |
| Approval decision period | s 7(1) | 30 days for buildings under 500 m²; 60 days for 500 m² and larger |
| Grounds for refusal | s 7(1)(b)(ii) | Non-compliance with the Act or other law; or the building will be unsightly or objectionable, or will derogate from the value of adjoining or neighbouring properties, or be dangerous to life or property |
| Appeal | s 9 | Appeal against a local authority decision (Review Board) |
| Occupancy certificate | s 14 | Local authority must issue within 14 days of being satisfied the building complies; occupation without it is an offence |
| Completion certificates | s 14(2A) | Certificates for the structural, fire protection and fire installation systems from the competent persons (Form 4) |
| Exemption of buildings | s 13 | Minister/local authority may exempt buildings from the regulations — the "minor building work" route |
| Prohibition/demolition order | s 21 | Magistrate may order work stopped or authorise demolition on application by the local authority or the Minister |
| Deviation and exemption | s 18 | Deviation from the national building regulations |
| General penalty | s 24 | Where no specific penalty is provided: fine not exceeding R4 000 or imprisonment not exceeding 12 months |

> ⚠️ The R4 000 general penalty in s 24 is nominal today, but it is not the operative deterrent. The operative deterrents are the s 21 demolition order, the refusal of an occupancy certificate (which blocks occupation, bond registration and insurance), and the local authority's power under s 22 relating to rates, fees and other moneys.

## Plan submission and approval

**What must be submitted.** Regulation A2 governs plans and particulars: a site/block plan, layout drawing showing occupancy classification, floor plans, sections, elevations, drainage layout and specification, at prescribed scales, plus the declarations described below.

**Who signs.** Regulation A1(1) makes the designing, planning and supervision of erection subject to any law requiring registration — the Architectural Profession Act 44 of 2000, Engineering Profession Act 46 of 2000, Natural Scientific Professions Act 27 of 2003 and Professional and Technical Surveyors' Act 40 of 1984. Section 3 of the Act imposes duties on the draftsman of plans.

**Forms** (SANS 10400-A Annex D):

- **Form 1** — Declaration by the person responsible for preparing an application for approval of the erection of the building in terms of s 4 of the Act. Submitted with all applications prepared under Regulation A2.
- **Form 2** — Application for acceptance as an approved competent person in terms of Regulation A19. The competent person declares acceptance of the duties and responsibilities.
- **Form 3** — Declaration by a competent person appointed to design a component or element of a system.
- **Form 4** — Certificate of completion of the structural, fire protection or fire installation system in terms of s 14(2A) of the Act.

**Decision timeline.** s 7(1): 30 days for buildings under 500 m²; 60 days for buildings of 500 m² and over. Refusal must be for the reasons in s 7(1)(b), and is appealable under s 9.

**Building on a neighbour's structure.** Regulation A1(3): no person may erect a building structurally supported by an existing building, or extend an existing building, unless an approved competent person has judged the existing building capable of carrying the additional load and has informed the local authority in writing. The notification accompanies the s 4 application.

**Repairs.** No plans are required for repairs necessitated by ordinary wear and tear or undertaken in normal maintenance — but the local authority may require drawings where the repair affects structural loading or is a repair to part of the structural system.

## Minor building work (Regulation definition, s 13 route)

Application is made to the **building control officer** for authorisation, without full plans. The defined list:

- poultry house ≤ 10 m²
- aviary ≤ 20 m²
- solid fuel store ≤ 10 m² and ≤ 2 m high
- tool shed ≤ 10 m²
- child's playhouse ≤ 5 m²
- cycle shed ≤ 5 m²
- greenhouse ≤ 15 m²
- open-sided car, caravan or boat shelter, or carport, ≤ 40 m²
- free-standing wall of masonry, concrete, steel, aluminium or timber, or wire fence, ≤ 1,8 m high at any point above ground level and not retaining soil
- pergola
- private swimming pool
- change room ≤ 10 m² at a private swimming pool
- replacement of a roof or part with the same or similar material
- conversion of a door into a window or window into door without increasing the opening width
- making an opening in a wall that does not affect structural safety
- partitioning or enlarging a room by erecting or demolishing an internal wall that does not affect structural safety
- solar water heater ≤ 6 m² on a roof, or ≤ 12 m² elsewhere
- any other building where, in the BCO's opinion, full plans are unnecessary

> ⚠️ "Minor building work" removes the *full plan* requirement, not the *authorisation* requirement, and not the substantive functional regulations. A 1,8 m boundary wall still has to stand up.

## Rational design vs deemed-to-satisfy

**Deemed-to-satisfy.** Follow the relevant SANS 10400 part. SANS 10400-A defines a deemed-to-satisfy requirement as a "non-mandatory requirement, the compliance with which ensures compliance with a functional regulation". SANS 10400-A itself carries the deemed-to-satisfy requirements for Parts B, C, D, F, G, H, J, K, L, M, N, O, P, Q, R, S, T, V and W by pointing to the individual parts.

**Rational design / rational assessment.** Required (and therefore triggering a Regulation A19 appointment) where the design relies on Regulation AZ4(1)(b)(ii), A1(3), A23(4), G1(3), O4, P2(2), Q3, R3, T1(2) or W4, or where a part of SANS 10400 requires it, or where a geotechnical investigation is required under Regulation F3.

**Regulation A19 — appointment of persons responsible for design, inspection and assessment duties.** Key mechanics:

1. The **owner** appoints and retains one or more approved competent persons. Not the contractor, not the architect — the owner.
2. If the person cannot fulfil the duties, the owner must appoint a replacement who takes over responsibility for work already designed or built *and* the balance.
3. The local authority may exempt minor building work, or foundations to an addition to a single-storey building where the applicant is satisfied the existing foundations accord with SANS 10400-H and existing damage and deformation are within tolerable limits.
4. The terms of appointment must state that the person undertakes all duties required by the Regulations; the person declares acceptance on **Form 2**.
5. One person may take responsibility for more than one system if the local authority accepts their competence.
6. On an extension, the local authority may require rational assessments of the adequacy of the *existing* systems in combination with the extension, before approval. If it declines to accept an assessment it must consult the competent person first and give written reasons, subject to appeal to the Review Board.
7. Where any element of the structural, fire protection, artificial ventilation, stormwater disposal, non-water-borne sanitary disposal, fire installation or drainage system is the subject of a rational design or assessment, the appointed competent person **assumes responsibility for satisfying that functional regulation in its entirety** — not just their own element.

**Who is a competent person.** Registration in an appropriate category under the Architectural Profession Act 44 of 2000, Engineering Profession Act 46 of 2000, Natural Scientific Professions Act 27 of 2003, or another relevant Act, *plus* recognised experience and training in the specific field. SANS 10400-B distinguishes competent person (structures), competent person (civil engineering) and competent person (dolomite land), the last requiring either Professional Engineer registration or a BSc in geology/engineering geology with Natural Scientific Professions Act registration, plus dolomite-specific experience.

**Record retention.** SANS 10400-B requires the competent person to retain design documentation and produce it on request for **10 years after completion**.

## Enforcement

- **Inspection.** s 15 gives BCOs and certain other persons the power to enter buildings and land.
- **Reports.** s 16 requires reports on the adequacy of certain measures and building projects.
- **Stop and demolish.** s 21 — a magistrate, on application by the local authority or the Minister, may prohibit commencement or continuation of erection, or authorise the local authority to demolish, where satisfied the erection is contrary to or does not comply with the Act.
- **Occupancy.** s 14 — occupying without a certificate of occupancy is an offence. The certificate is the practical enforcement chokepoint.
- **Money.** s 22 gives local authorities powers relating to rates, taxes, fees and other moneys.
- **Penalty.** s 24 — R4 000 or 12 months where no specific penalty is stated. Several regulations create their own offences (e.g. Part E on demolition; Part T; Regulation A18 on plumbing control).
- **Presumption.** s 25 creates evidential presumptions easing proof of offences.
- **Immunity.** s 23 exempts the local authority and its officers from liability — approval of plans does not transfer responsibility for the design to the local authority.

## Adjacent statutes that bind the same project **[ZA]**

- **Housing Consumers Protection Measures Act 95 of 1998** — NHBRC registration of home builders and enrolment of homes. Enrolment 15 days before commencement, with soil classification certified by a competent person. Warranty periods: 3 months for general non-compliance with technical requirements, 1 year for roof leaks, 5 years for major structural defects.
- **Occupational Health and Safety Act 85 of 1993 and the Construction Regulations** — client, principal contractor and designer duties, health and safety file, construction work permit.
- **Spatial Planning and Land Use Management Act 16 of 2013 (SPLUMA)** and the municipal land use scheme — zoning, coverage, building lines, height.
- **National Water Act 36 of 1998** and **National Environmental Management Act 107 of 1998** — water use authorisations, environmental authorisation for listed activities.
