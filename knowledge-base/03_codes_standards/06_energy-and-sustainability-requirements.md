# Energy and sustainability — SANS 10400-XA and SANS 204 in practice

**Summary.** **[ZA]** Part X of the National Building Regulations is the environmental sustainability part; regulation XA governs energy usage in buildings and is mandatory. SANS 10400-XA is its deemed-to-satisfy standard, currently **SANS 10400-XA:2026 (Ed. 3.00)** (R1 124,70 on the SABS webstore, 2026-08-25). SANS 204, *Energy efficiency in buildings*, was published as a voluntary standard in 2008 and updated in 2010 to support XA; the 2011 edition of XA referred out to it extensively, while the 2021 and 2026 editions are much more self-contained. **[NA]** Namibia has **no mandatory building energy standard**; XA is applied voluntarily, by client or donor requirement, or by an engineer's or architect's own standard of care.

> ⚠️ The 2021 edition changed the climatic zone system, the compliance routes and the envelope requirements substantially. **A number from the 2011 edition is not a number from the current edition.** Both are given below, clearly separated, because the 2011 values remain in wide circulation in guidance documents, insulation manufacturers' literature and older municipal handouts.

## Key facts

| | 2011 edition | 2021 / 2026 editions |
|---|---|---|
| Zone system | 6 climatic zones | Energy zones **1–7 plus 5H** |
| Compliance routes | Deemed-to-satisfy envelope, or SANS 204 route, or rational design by simulation | Clause 4.3 reference-building route (certified thermal calculation software); clause 4.4 performance-based route across orientation, shading, fenestration, floors, walls, roofs, sealing and services |
| Fenestration rule | ≤ 15 % fenestration area to nett floor area per storey deems compliance | Clause 5.3, Table 4: area-weighted maximum whole-fenestration performance by fenestration-to-floor ratio band (≤20 % through >60 %) and orientation |
| Hot water | Regulation XA2 | Regulation XA2 — unchanged in substance |
| Standard status | Superseded | **SANS 10400-XA:2026 (Ed. 3.00)** current |

**Regulation XA2 — the single most-quoted requirement:** "At least **50 % (volume fraction)** of the annual average hot water heating requirement shall be provided by means other than electrical resistance heating."

## What a designer must actually demonstrate **[ZA]**

Regulation XA sets three functional requirements: XA1 (general — the building must be designed and constructed so that it uses energy efficiently), XA2 (hot water), XA3 (the alternative compliance route). A submission must show, on the plans and in an accompanying schedule:

1. **The energy zone** of the site, established from the standard's town table (Annex C in the 2021 edition; Annex A Table A.1 in 2011).
2. **Orientation and shading.** The reference orientation is true north. NHBRC guidance notes that the best orientation is true north **± 15°**; where a building is east- or west-orientated, the design is subject to rational design using thermal software certified by Agrément South Africa. Living spaces should be on the northern side with the long axis north–south; most windows north, then south, fewest east and west. Shading is dimensioned using the P, H and G geometry (horizontal distance from glass face to the shadow-casting edge; vertical distance from the base of the glazing to the same edge; vertical distance from the head of the glazing to the shadow-casting edge), with sun angles per zone latitude.
3. **The building envelope** — roof/ceiling assembly R-value, external wall R-value or CR-value, floor construction, and the fenestration performance.
4. **Fenestration** — total glazed area (including frames) as a percentage of nett floor area per storey, and where required the area-weighted U-value and SHGC of the whole fenestration element, plus permissible air leakage per **SANS 613** (the fenestration manufacturer must provide the performance data).
5. **Services** — hot water provision demonstrating XA2 compliance, hot water pipe sizing and insulation, and (in the 2021 edition, clause 6) other services requirements.
6. **A competent person's certification** where the rational-design or reference-building route is used.

## The climatic zone system

### The 2011 six-zone system (still widely quoted)

| Zone | Description | Representative towns |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold interior** | Johannesburg, Bloemfontein |
| 2 | **Temperate interior** | Pretoria, Polokwane |
| 3 | **Hot interior** | Makhado, Nelspruit, Graskop, Middelburg |
| 4 | **Temperate coastal** | Cape Town, Port Elizabeth |
| 5 | **Sub-tropical coastal** | East London, Durban, Richards Bay |
| 6 | **Arid interior** | Upington, Kimberley |

### The 2021/2026 system

Energy zones **1 to 7 plus 5H**, defined by Figure 1 and Table C.1, which lists South African towns alphabetically with longitude, latitude and assigned energy zone. Examples: **Johannesburg zone 1; Cape Town zone 4; Durban zone 5H**.

> ⚠️ The 2021 zone numbering is **not** a simple renumbering of the 2011 zones, and a town's zone may have changed. Look the town up in Table C.1 of the purchased current edition. Do not map 2011 zone numbers onto 2021 requirements.

## Deemed-to-satisfy building envelope

### Roof and ceiling assemblies — 2011 edition (verified, two independent sources)

SANS 10400-XA:2011 Table 7, clause 4.4.5.1. Minimum required **total R-value** of the roof assembly, in m²·K/W:

| Zone | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Min. total R-value (m²·K/W) | **3,7** | **3,2** | **2,7** | **3,7** | **2,7** | **3,5** |
| Direction of heat flow | Up | Up | Down & up | Up | Down | Up |

The *roof assembly* is the roof or ceiling system, or both, measured from the outer skin exposed to the environment to the inside of the inner skin exposed to the interior, excluding glazing such as roof lights and skylights. In a typical pitched metal roof with a horizontal ceiling, the total R-value is the sum of the outside air film, roof sheet, ventilated cavity, insulation, ceiling board and inside air film. Typical air-space and air-film R-values were given in **Table F.2 of SANS 204**, and typical roof assembly R-values in **Table F.3**.

### External walls — 2011 edition (verified)

**Non-masonry (low-density) walls**, minimum total R-value:

| Zones | R-value (m²·K/W) |
|---|---|
| 1 (Johannesburg, Bloemfontein) and 6 (Upington, Kimberley) | **2,2** |
| 2 (Pretoria, Polokwane), 3 (Makhado, Nelspruit), 4 (Cape Town, Port Elizabeth), 5 (East London, Durban, Richards Bay) | **1,9** |

**Masonry walls** are dealt with on a **CR-value** basis (combined thermal capacity and resistance, expressed in hours) rather than R-value alone. The Clay Brick Association's Technical Note 15 records that Table 3 of SANS 204 specifies minimum CR-values for external masonry walling by occupancy type and climatic zone **ranging from 60 to 120 hours**.

Two masonry constructions that NHBRC guidance identifies as complying without added insulation under the 2011 regime:

- a **double-skin masonry wall with no cavity**, plastered internally and rendered externally (thin premixed sand/cement/lime plaster, e.g. bagwash); and
- a **single-leaf masonry wall of nominal thickness ≥ 140 mm**, plastered internally and rendered externally.

### The 2021 edition envelope requirements

From SANS 10400-XA:2021 Edition 2:

- **Roof assembly — clause 5.6, Table 8.** Minimum total R-values reported as **3,7 m²·K/W** for the main zones and **2,7 m²·K/W** for zone 5H, with direction of heat flow specified per zone.
- **External walls — clauses 5.5.1 to 5.5.3.** High-density walls (**≥ 270 kg/m²**) are governed by Table 6, with R-values reported in the range **0,4–0,6 m²·K/W**. Low-density walls (**< 270 kg/m²**) are governed by Table 7, requiring R-values of **1,9–2,2 m²·K/W** *or* CR-values of **80–100 hours**.
- **Fenestration — clause 5.3, Table 4.** Area-weighted maximum whole fenestration element performance depends on the fenestration-to-floor-area ratio band (≤20 %, and bands up to >60 %) and on orientation. Reported ranges: **U-values 2,0–5,20 W/m²·K; SHGC 0,22–0,66.**
- **Roof lights — clause 5.3.8, Table 5.** Acceptable where roof lights are **≤ 1,5 %** of the floor area served; stricter limits apply above that threshold.

> ⚠️ **Confidence on the 2021/2026 numeric values above is medium.** They were extracted from a single circulating copy of the 2021 edition and the zone/value mapping in that extract was partially ambiguous. The 270 kg/m² high/low-density wall threshold, the 1,5 % roof light threshold and the existence of Tables 4–8 are reliable; the specific R-value and U-value figures are **not verified to a confidence sufficient for design**. Consult the purchased SANS 10400-XA:2026 (Ed. 3.00).

## Fenestration — the practical rule

**2011 route (clause 4.4.4.1):** "Buildings with up to **15 % fenestration area to nett floor area per storey** comply with the minimum energy performance requirements." Exceeding 15 % required compliance with SANS 204 — that is, an area-weighted U-value and SHGC calculation, or the simulation route.

NHBRC guidance sets out the practical method: sum the area of all fenestration *including the frame*, divide by the nett floor area of the storey, and if the result is ≤ 15 % the fenestration complies. At that level, clear glass sized per **SANS 10400-N** normally suffices. Permissible air leakage of external vertical glazing is determined per **SANS 613**, and the fenestration manufacturer must supply the performance data.

**2021/2026 route:** the flat 15 % deemed-to-satisfy threshold has been replaced by the banded Table 4 approach, where higher glazing ratios are permitted but must be offset by better whole-element U-value and SHGC performance. This is a significant liberalisation for glazed architecture and a significant tightening for cheap aluminium windows.

## Hot water heating

**Regulation XA2** requires at least **50 % (volume fraction)** of the annual average hot water heating requirement to be provided by means other than electrical resistance heating. Compliant means include:

- solar water heating (flat plate or evacuated tube, direct or indirect);
- heat pumps;
- heat recovery from other systems;
- gas water heating;
- solid-fuel or biomass water heating.

The **annual average hot water heating requirement is calculated in accordance with tables of SANS 10252-1** — the 2011 edition of XA cited "tables 2 and 5 of SANS 10252-1:2004" at clause 4.1.1. SANS 10252-1 is currently at the 2018 edition (Ed. 3.02), so the table numbers may have moved.

Associated requirements in the SANS 10400-XA / SANS 204 family and in NHBRC guidance:

- **Hot water pipe insulation** — hot water distribution pipework must be insulated to a specified minimum, and the geyser positioned to minimise pipe runs.
- **Geyser position** — placement affects both standing losses and the length of the dead leg to the furthest fixture.
- Installation of the storage water heater itself is governed by **SANS 10254** (fixed electric storage water heating systems), and the electrical connection by **SANS 10142-1**.

## Compliance routes

### 1. Deemed-to-satisfy (prescriptive)

Meet each envelope and services requirement in clause 5 and clause 6 (2021 numbering). Simplest, cheapest, and adequate for the great majority of houses and small commercial buildings. Requires no simulation.

### 2. Reference building (clause 4.3, 2021)

Demonstrate, using certified thermal calculation software, that the proposed building's energy performance **matches or exceeds** that of a reference building of the same geometry and occupancy built to the deemed-to-satisfy requirements. This is the route for a building that deliberately trades one element against another — more glass offset by better shading and a better roof.

### 3. Performance-based / rational design (clause 4.4, 2021; XA3)

A rational design by a competent person meeting specified requirements across orientation, shading, fenestration, floors, walls, roofs, sealing and services. NHBRC guidance notes that a building not orientated within ±15° of true north falls to rational design using thermal software **certified by Agrément South Africa** — the certification of the software, not just the designer, is the control.

> ⚠️ The competent person who signs an XA rational design takes responsibility for satisfying regulation XA **in its entirety** under Regulation A19(7), not just for the element they modelled.

## Where SANS 204 fits

SANS 204 was "published as a voluntary National Standard for *Energy Efficiency in Buildings* in October 2008 and updated in August 2010 to fulfil the requirements of" SANS 10400-XA. It is the *technical* standard; XA is the *regulatory* one. In the 2011 regime the two were tightly coupled: XA set the thresholds and referred out to SANS 204 for the calculation methods, the R-value and CR-value tables, the air-space and air-film data (Table F.2), the roof assembly data (Table F.3) and the shading geometry (clause 4.3.5.2).

The 2021 and 2026 editions of XA absorb much of this. **SANS 204's current edition did not return in the SABS catalogue search performed on 2026-08-25**, which may indicate withdrawal or restructuring. Verify with SABS before citing SANS 204 as a current requirement.

## What the equivalent expectation is in Namibia **[NA]**

**There is no mandatory building energy standard in Namibia.** Specifically:

- Namibia has no equivalent of NBR Part X/XA. The Local Authorities Act s 94(1)(ag) power is broad enough to permit a council to make energy regulations, but no Namibian council is known to have done so.
- The Standards Act 18 of 2005 s 20(6) makes Namibian standards voluntary unless declared compulsory by the Minister. No building energy standard is known to have been so declared.
- The NSI's long-running national building code project has contemplated a Eurocode-based framework, which would eventually bring EN 15251/EN ISO 52000-family energy performance methods rather than SANS 10400-XA. This has not been promulgated.

**What actually drives energy performance on a Namibian project:**

1. **Client and donor requirements.** Development-financed, institutional, mining and lodge projects routinely specify SANS 10400-XA, EDGE, or an internal green building standard.
2. **Green Building Council of South Africa / Namibia Green Building Council rating tools**, where a project seeks certification.
3. **The economics of off-grid and weak-grid supply.** In much of northern Namibia the grid is constrained and diesel backup is expensive, so envelope performance and solar water heating are justified commercially rather than by regulation.
4. **Climate.** Namibia is hotter and much more arid than most of South Africa. The relevant design problem is **summer heat exclusion and night-time re-radiation**, not winter heat retention. The SANS 10400-XA zones do not cover Namibia at all — a Namibian designer using XA must choose an analogous zone by climate similarity, and should document the reasoning.

**Practical mapping guidance (engineering judgement, not a standard):** central and northern interior Namibia is climatically closest to the SANS 10400-XA **hot interior / arid interior** conditions (2011 zones 3 and 6); the Namib coast is a distinct cool-foggy maritime climate with no good SANS analogue. Treat any zone assignment for a Namibian site as an assumption to be stated on the drawings, not as compliance.

Practical priorities for a hot, arid, high-diurnal-range Namibian site:

- Roof insulation first — the roof is the dominant heat gain path. Aim at or above the highest SANS 10400-XA roof R-value rather than the lowest.
- Thermal mass in walls, exploiting the large diurnal swing (the CR-value concept, not the R-value concept, is the right mental model).
- Aggressive shading of all glazing, especially east and west; deep overhangs, verandas, screens.
- Small, well-shaded openings on east and west; larger openings north with calculated overhangs.
- Solar water heating is almost always economic; the 50 % XA2 threshold is a low bar in Namibian irradiation.
- Light-coloured, high-emissivity roof surfaces.
- Cross-ventilation and night purge, with SANS 10400-O's 5 % openable area as a floor, not a target.
