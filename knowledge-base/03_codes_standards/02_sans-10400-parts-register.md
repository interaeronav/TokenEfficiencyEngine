# SANS 10400 parts register — A to XA

**Summary.** **[ZA]** SANS 10400, *The application of the National Building Regulations*, is the SABS standard series that provides the deemed-to-satisfy requirements for the National Building Regulations made under Act 103 of 1977. Each part carries the same letter as the corresponding NBR part. **[ZA]** Compliance with a part is a safe harbour, not a legal obligation: the functional regulation is the law, and a rational design by an approved competent person is the alternative route. This register lists every published part, its subject, the current edition and price as listed on the SABS webstore on 2026-08-25, and a substantive summary of what the part regulates.

> ⚠️ **Copyright and accuracy.** SANS parts are sold by SABS and their full text is not public. Editions change frequently — five parts were re-issued in 2025 or 2026. Where a clause number or value appears below it is tagged with the edition it came from. **Always verify against the purchased current edition before relying on a number.**

## Part-by-part register

### Part A — General principles and requirements

**Current: SANS 10400-A:2025 (Ed. 4.01).** The keystone part. It establishes the general requirements for satisfying the NBR and states the deemed-to-satisfy route for Parts B, C, D, F, G, H, J, K, L, M, N, O, P, Q, R, S, T, V and W by directing the reader to the individual parts.

Structure (2010 edition): 1 Scope; 2 Normative references; 3 Definitions; 4 Requirements (4.1 General, 4.2 Deemed-to-satisfy requirements, 4.3 Category 1 buildings); Annex A (normative) National Building Regulations for which there are no deemed-to-satisfy requirements; Annex B (informative) Commentary on the National Building Regulations; Annex C (informative) Performance-based building regulations and their usage in South Africa; Annex D (normative) Forms.

What you go to Part A for:

- The **occupancy classification table** (Regulation A20, classes A1–J4).
- The **design population** table (Regulation A21) used to size escape routes and sanitary fixtures — e.g. 1 person per 10 m² for C1, E2, F1, F2; 1 person per 15 m² for B1, B2, B3, D1, D2, D3.
- **Regulation A19**, the appointment of approved competent persons.
- The definition of **category 1 building**: class A3, A4, F2, G1, H2, H3 or H4; no basement; maximum 6,0 m between intersecting walls or members providing lateral support; floor area not exceeding 80 m². Table C.1 sets out the performance differences between category 1 buildings and other buildings of the same occupancy.
- The definition of **minor building work** (see `01_south-africa-nbr-act.md` for the full list).
- **Forms 1–4** (declaration by the preparer of the application; application for acceptance as an approved competent person; declaration by a component designer; certificate of completion of the structural, fire protection or fire installation system under s 14(2A)).
- Definitions used across the series: *habitable room*, *dwelling unit*, *dwelling house*, *floor area*, *inspection*, *inspection chamber* (not deeper than 750 mm), *manhole* (deeper than 750 mm), *functional regulation*, *deemed-to-satisfy requirement*.

### Part B — Structural design

**Current: SANS 10400-B:2025 (Ed. 4.01).** Satisfies functional regulation B1 (Design requirement). Structure (2012 Ed. 3): Scope; Normative references; Definitions; Requirements (General; Rational design and rational assessment; Agrément certification; Dolomite land); Annexes on condensation, performance tests and masonry design guidance.

Verified from Edition 3 (2012) — confirm in Ed. 4:

- Design working life: not less than **30 years** for the structural system; **15 years** for replaceable components (**10 years** for category 1 buildings). Clause 4.2.1.1.
- Loads in accordance with the relevant part of **SANS 10160**. Clause 4.2.1.2.
- Minimum design wind pressure: **0,37 kPa** for dwelling houses; **0,45 kPa** for elements. Clause 4.2.1.3.
- Serviceability for category 1 buildings: settlement **20 mm** (vs 10 mm otherwise); tilt **1:100** (vs 1:200); roof deflection **1:175** (vs 1:250).
- Recognised material design codes: SANS 10100 (concrete), SANS 10162 (steel), SANS 10163 (timber), SANS 10164 (masonry), or recognised engineering principles. Clause 4.2.2.1.
- Novel materials: demonstrate by engineering first principles, research, applicable standards, verifiable experience and appropriate test results. Clause 4.2.3.1.
- Documentation retention: **10 years** after completion, producible on request.
- Competent person categories: competent person (structures), competent person (civil engineering), competent person (dolomite land).

### Part C — Dimensions

**Current: SANS 10400-C:2016 (Ed. 3.01).** Satisfies C1 (Rooms and buildings). Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Plan dimensions; 4.3 Room height; 4.4 Floor area; Annex C on category 1 differences.

Verified from the 2010 edition — **re-verify against Ed. 3.01 (2016) before use**:

- Plan dimensions are measured between **unplastered** wall surfaces; floor area excludes built-in cabinets/cupboards and dividing walls or partitions erected under 4.2.4.
- Minimum room area: any habitable room other than a kitchen, scullery or laundry — **6 m², with no linear dimension less than 2 m**.
- Change rooms and dining rooms in class B, D or J: population 1–15, 0,8 m²/person but not less than 6 m²; 16–100, 0,6 m²/person but not less than 12 m²; over 100, 0,5 m²/person but not less than 60 m².
- Two spaces are one room if the dividing wall or partition (including any door) occupies **less than 60 %** of the separating plane.
- Room heights: bedroom **2,4 m** above at least 6 m² of floor area, with clear height at least 1,8 m at any point more than 0,75 m from the edge of the floor space; other habitable room in a dwelling house or unit **2,4 m over at least 70 %** of the floor area and not less than **2,1 m** over the remainder; all other habitable rooms **2,4 m**; passage or entrance hall **2,1 m**; bathroom, shower-room, laundry or toilet **2,1 m** where a person would normally stand; open mezzanine not exceeding 25 % of the floor below, **2,1 m** above and below.
- Structural members projecting below the ceiling: minimum **2,1 m** clear beneath the projection.
- Minimum overall plan area of a dwelling house: **15 m²** temporary, **27 m²** permanent category 1, **30 m²** any other permanent building.

### Part D — Public safety

**Current: SANS 10400-D:2022 (Ed. 4.01).** Satisfies D1–D5. Covers changes in level, guarding and balustrades, parking areas, ramps for public use, swimming pool safety (fencing/barriers to prevent unsupervised access by small children), and the deemed-to-satisfy rules for each. This is the part that governs balustrade heights and pool enclosures on a typical house.

*Specific balustrade heights and pool barrier dimensions in the 2022 edition were not verified from a public source — consult the purchased standard.*

### Part E — Demolition work

**No SANS 10400-E is listed in the SABS catalogue as at 2026-08-25.** NBR Part E (E1–E4) governs demolition procedures, safeguarding of basements, prohibited methods and penalties, and appears to have no separate deemed-to-satisfy standard. Demolition is in practice governed by NBR Part E read with the OHS Act Construction Regulations. Confirm with SABS.

### Part F — Site operations

**Current: SANS 10400-F:2026 (Ed. 3.02).** Satisfies F1–F11. Covers protection of the public, prevention of damage to adjoining property, soil conditions and geotechnical investigation (Regulation F3 triggers a competent person appointment under A19), site preparation and clearing, control of dust and noise, disposal of waste and building rubble, sanitary facilities on site, hoardings and site enclosure, and the notice board. The geotechnical investigation requirement under F3 is the trigger point for site classification of the sort the NHBRC uses.

### Part G — Excavations

**Current: SANS 10400-G:2026 (Ed. 3.02).** Satisfies G1 (General stability requirement) and G2. Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Excavations (4.2.1 temporary, 4.2.2 permanent, 4.2.3 blasting).

Verified from the 2011 edition:

- Temporary excavations must not extend below a line drawn from the bottom edge of an adjacent building's foundation (figure 1).
- Permanent excavations: cutting slope not steeper than **1:2**, except in sound intact rock where it may be vertical. No ponding of water within **3 m** of the toe. Surface water must be diverted from the cut.
- Blasting: minimum distance from blast hole to a building per table 1 — 10 m for a maximum charge of 0,19 kg per relay; 20 m for 0,76 kg; 30 m for 1,7 kg; 50 m for 4,7 kg; 100 m for 18,9 kg. Detonating relays at least **20 ms** delay interval. Levels are set to keep peak particle velocity at buildings below **50 mm/s**.

### Part H — Foundations

**Current: SANS 10400-H:2026 (Ed. 3.01).** Satisfies H1 (General requirement): foundations must safely transfer loads to the ground without detrimental movement. This is the part that carries the deemed-to-satisfy strip footing rules for simple masonry buildings on competent soils, and the trigger points at which a rational design (and therefore a geotechnical investigation and site classification) becomes mandatory — notably on heaving clays, collapsible sands, dolomite and made ground.

*Strip footing widths, depths and reinforcement tables in Ed. 3.01 were not verified from a public source — consult the purchased standard.* Cross-reference SANS 10400-B on dolomite land and the NHBRC Home Building Manual site classification.

### Part J — Floors

**Current: SANS 10400-J:2026 (Ed. 3.02).** Satisfies J1. Structure (2010 Ed.): 4.1 General; 4.2 Water-resistant floors; 4.3 Suspended timber floors not exposed to the elements; 4.4 Floors supported on ground or filling. Covers damp-proof membranes under slabs, compaction and preparation of fill, joist and bearer sizing and ventilation of underfloor voids for suspended timber floors, and water-resistant construction in wet areas. SANS 10400-B establishes the actions and structural response; Part J the construction detail.

### Part K — Walls

**Current: SANS 10400-K:2025 (Ed. 3.02).** Satisfies K1–K5 (structural strength and stability, resistance to water penetration, fixing of the roof to the walls, behaviour in fire, deemed-to-satisfy). The largest and most-used part for masonry housing.

Structure (2011 edition): 4.1 General; 4.2 Masonry walls — 4.2.1 General, 4.2.2 Masonry walling in single-storey and double-storey buildings, 4.2.3 Infill masonry panels in framed buildings, 4.2.4 Free-standing boundary, garden and retaining walls, 4.2.5 Balustrade and parapet walls, 4.2.6 Control joints, 4.2.7 Articulation joints, 4.2.8 Corbelling, 4.2.9 Lintels, 4.2.10 Masonry arches, 4.2.11 Roof fixing; 4.3 Timber-framed walls; 4.4 Fixing of roofs to concrete elements; 4.5 Water penetration (4.5.1 Condensation, 4.5.2 Rain penetration, 4.5.3 Rising damp); 4.6 Behaviour in fire; Annex B Design of lintels; Annex C (normative) Rain penetration tests for walls.

The heart of the part is a set of **wall panel tables (tables 1–6)** giving maximum panel length × height for a given masonry unit width and support condition, with three panel categories: no openings, openings less than 15 %, and openings greater than 15 %. Table 7 governs maximum opening lengths and minimum distances between supports and openings and between successive openings. The worked example in the 2011 edition shows a 190 mm hollow-unit single-storey building with a limiting no-opening panel of **7,5 m × 2,7 m**.

*The panel tables themselves are the commercial core of the standard and are not reproduced here.* Note also 4.2.2.2: panels incorporating full-height doors or doors with fanlights are treated as panels supported on one side only.

### Part L — Roofs

**Current: SANS 10400-L:2020 (Ed. 4.00).** Satisfies L1–L3 (general requirements, fire resistance and combustibility, deemed-to-satisfy).

Structure (2011 edition): 4.1 General; 4.2 Roof coverings and waterproofing systems; 4.3 Drainage and waterproofing of flat roofs; 4.4 Timber roof construction (by far the longest section, ~30 pages); 4.5 Fire resistance and combustibility; Annex A Principal waterproofing details.

Normative references include SANS 457-2 and 457-3 (wooden poles), SANS 542 (concrete roofing tiles), SANS 1288 (preservative-treated timber), SANS 1460 (laminated timber). Timber roof construction is the deemed-to-satisfy route for conventional purlin-and-rafter and pre-nailed truss roofs; anything outside it requires a rational design, which for prefabricated trusses is normally supplied by the truss plate system supplier's engineer.

### Part M — Stairways

**Current: SANS 10400-M:2025 (Ed. 3.02).** Satisfies M1–M3. Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Dimensional requirements; 4.3 Prevention against falling; 4.4 Masonry stairways; 4.5 Timber stairways.

Verified from the 2011 edition — **re-verify against Ed. 3.02**:

- Headroom not less than **2,1 m** measured vertically from the pitch line; width not less than **750 mm** measured to any enclosing wall or balustrade.
- Landing serving two flights in the same straight line: length not less than **900 mm**, width not less than the flights.
- No flight with a vertical rise greater than **3 m** between landings.
- Maximum riser **200 mm**; minimum going and tread width **250 mm**; where risers are open, treads overlap by not less than **25 mm**.
- Variation in riser and going dimensions within a flight not more than **6 mm**.
- **Going + 2 × riser between 570 mm and 650 mm.**
- Tapered treads (not winders, not spiral): going measured 400 mm from the narrow end must comply with 4.2.6; minimum going **125 mm**; constant angle between successive risers.
- Winders permitted only in dwelling houses and individual dwelling units; not more than **three successive winders**, turning not more than **90°**.
- Spiral stairways: width not more than **800 mm**; may not form part of any emergency route in occupancies A1, A2, A3, A4, A5, C1, E1, E2, E3, E4, F1, G1, H1, H2 or H5.
- Any flight with more than three risers requires protection on both sides by a secure wall, screen, railing or balustrade not less than 1 m high (4.3.1).

### Part N — Glazing

**Current: SANS 10400-N:2026 (Ed. 3.02).** Satisfies N1 (Type and fixing of glazing). Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Glazing installations; 4.3 Transparent glazing; 4.4 Safety glazing. Normative references include SANS 613 (fenestration products — mechanical performance criteria), SANS 727 (rolled mild steel windows and doors) and SANS 1263-1 (safety and security glazing materials — safety performance under human impact).

Verified from the 2012 edition. **Safety glazing complying with SANS 1263-1 is required, and must be permanently marked by the installer so the marking is visible after installation, where:**

- doors and sidelights form part of any entrance, up to **2 100 mm** above finished floor level;
- a window has a sill height **less than 500 mm** above floor or external ground level;
- a window has a sill height **less than 800 mm** without a permanent barrier and is placed so that persons on normal traffic routes are likely to move directly toward it;
- a bath enclosure or shower cubicle is glazed, or glazing occurs immediately above and within **1 800 mm** horizontally or vertically of a bath or shower;
- glazing is in a shop front or display window within **2 100 mm** of finished floor level;
- glazing is in any wall or balustrade to, or immediately adjacent to, a stairway, ramp, landing, pathway, patio, veranda or balcony;
- glazing is within **1 800 mm** of the pitch line of a stairway or the surface of a ramp, landing, pathway, patio, veranda or balcony;
- glazing is sloped or horizontal;
- a mirror faces a cupboard door less than **800 mm** above floor level with no solid backing;
- glazing is around swimming pools and ice rinks;
- glazing is in internal partitions within **2 100 mm** of floor level.

All glazing in occupancy classes A3, E1, E2, E3 and in H2 associated with A3/E1/E2/E3 must be SANS 1263-1 safety glazing (4.4.3). Glass in balustrades must be toughened safety glass unless rigidly supported on all sides, and is subject to impact and line loads determined per **SANS 10160-2** (4.4.4).

### Part O — Lighting and ventilation

**Current: SANS 10400-O:2025 (Ed. 4.00).** Satisfies O1–O7. Structure (2011 edition): 4.1 General; 4.2 Lighting (4.2.1 natural lighting, zones of space); 4.3 Ventilation (4.3.1 natural, artificial); 4.4 Designated smoking areas and smoking rooms; Annex B on category 1 differences.

Verified from the 2011 edition — **re-verify against Ed. 4.00**:

- **Natural lighting.** Openings must be in an external wall or a suitable roof position with a qualifying *zone of space* outside. Total opening area, inclusive of frames and glazing bars, not less than **5 % of floor area for category 1 buildings of occupancy F2, H3 or H4**, and **10 % of floor area for other buildings, or 0,2 m², whichever is greater**. Glazing must be transparent or approved translucent material per SANS 10400-N.
- **Natural ventilation.** Openable area not less than **5 % of the floor area of the room**, or **0,1 m² for category 1 buildings of occupancy E4, F2, H3, H4 or H5** and **0,2 m² for other buildings**, whichever is greater. Where ventilation is by an opening in the ceiling or at the top of an internal wall connected to a vertical ventilating flue, the area is not less than **2 % of the floor area**.
- The standard notes explicitly that in holiday homes and chalets in hot humid areas, closed windows over long periods lead to damp and mould, and recommends supplementary permanent ventilation such as air bricks plus a roof vent.
- Ventilated improved pit toilets are excluded from the natural lighting requirement.

### Part P — Drainage

**Current: SANS 10400-P:2010 (Ed. 3.00)** — the oldest part in the series still in force. Satisfies P1–P7 (compulsory drainage, design, discharge control, industrial effluent, disconnections, inspection and testing).

Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Materials, pipes, fittings and joints; 4.3 Sanitary fixtures; 4.4 Toilet pans; 4.5 Urinals; 4.6 Connectors for toilet pans; 4.7 Sewage lifts; 4.8 Conservancy tanks, septic tanks and french drains; 4.9 Discharges from washing areas; 4.10 Discharges from swimming baths, pools, fountains or reservoirs; 4.11 Provision of sanitary fixtures; 4.12 Hydraulic loading of drainage installations; 4.13 Drainage systems; 4.14 Sizing of discharge pipes; 4.15 Sizing of drains; 4.16 Protection of water seals; 4.17 Sizing of ventilating pipes; 4.18 Installation of discharge pipes and ventilating pipes; 4.19 Access to the drainage installation; 4.20 Provision of traps; 4.21 Provision of gullies; 4.22 Installation of drains; 4.23 onward — testing and commissioning.

Clause **4.11 (provision of sanitary fixtures)** is the table designers reach for most: minimum numbers of toilet pans, urinals, wash basins and showers per occupancy class and population. Clause 4.8 governs on-site treatment where there is no municipal sewer — conservancy tanks, septic tanks and french drains, including the percolation test method that SANS 10400-Q cross-references.

*Fixture-count tables and pipe sizing tables were not verified from a public source — consult the purchased standard.*

### Part Q — Non-water-borne means of sanitary disposal

**Current: SANS 10400-Q:2021 (Ed. 4.00).** Satisfies Q1–Q3. **[NA]** This is also the part Namibian rural practice reaches for, alongside NAMS 0001:2016 (dry sanitation technologies), even though it has no legal force there. Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Closets; 4.3 Chemical toilets; 4.4 Ventilated improved pit toilets; Annex B (informative) Ventilated improved pit toilets.

This is the part that matters wherever there is no sewer — including most of rural Namibia. Verified from the 2011 edition:

- VIP toilets must minimise odours and fly attraction by air movement, solar heating and light; must **not penetrate the water table**; may be constructed only where the percolation rate measured per SANS 10400-P is **not more than 50 mm/h and not less than 2,5 mm/h**; must not be built under or near trees.
- Where the pit is to be emptied by vacuum tanker: lined per 4.4.12, tanker able to approach within **30 m**, and the tanker standing surface not more than **2,0 m** above the top of the pit.
- Siting: downstream of, or more than **30 m** from, a well or water source; at least **3 m** from buildings; where permanent, at least **2,75 m** from the erf boundary for maintenance access. The standard recommends at least **5 m** from habitable buildings to avoid odours.
- Effective pit volume for a typical rural household to be emptied: at least **2,5 m³**; for temporary toilets at least **3,5 m³**; plus **0,5 m** freeboard above the effective volume. Round pits 1,0–1,5 m diameter, square pits 1,0–1,5 m wide.
- Pits in unstable soils must be fully lined with seepage provision. **Unlined pits must be circular and not exceed 1,5 m diameter.**
- Regulation Q2 prohibits construction of any pit toilet without the local authority's permission.

### Part R — Stormwater disposal

**Current: SANS 10400-R:2012 (Ed. 3.00).** Satisfies R1 (disposal requirement) and R2 (saving clause). Governs the disposal of stormwater from roofs and paved areas so that it does not discharge onto neighbouring property or undermine foundations: gutter and downpipe sizing, surface drainage, discharge to the municipal system or to an approved point. Regulation R3 rational design is one of the triggers for a Regulation A19 competent person appointment.

### Part S — Facilities for persons with disabilities

**Current: SANS 10400-S:2025 (Ed. 4.00).** Satisfies S1–S3. Structure (2011 edition): 4.1 General; 4.2 Signage; 4.3 Parking; 4.4 External and internal circulation; 4.5 Floor or ground surfaces; 4.6 Doorways, doors and door handles; 4.7 Changes in level; 4.8 Ramps; 4.9 Stairways; 4.10 Handrails; 4.11 Lifts; 4.12 Toilet facilities; 4.13 Auditoriums, grandstands and halls; 4.14 Controls, switches and power points; 4.15 Warning signals; 4.16 Lighting.

Verified from the 2011 edition — **re-verify against Ed. 4.00**:

- **Parking (4.3).** At least one accessible employee parking space. Where more than 50 vehicles are parked, at least one accessible space per 25 spaces or part thereof; at least 20 % of spaces at rehabilitation and medical facilities. Access surface not steeper than **1:50**; within **50 m** of an accessible entrance; clear height at least **2,4 m** on entry and routing; clearly demarcated and signed with the international symbol.
- **Doorways (4.6).** Clear opening at least **750 mm** when approached perpendicular (800 mm recommended); for two-leaf doors the leading leaf must give at least 750 mm; a nib of at least **450 mm** on the handle side where the door opens toward the wheelchair.
- **Ramps (4.8).** Gradient along the centre line not steeper than **1:12**; landings at top and bottom at least **1,2 m** long clear of any door swing and at least the ramp width; handrails both sides (or a central handrail where wider than 2,4 m) where the gradient is steeper than **1:15**; ramps in the same direction for a vertical rise greater than **600 mm** must be staggered by the ramp width. Where total rise exceeds 2 m, alternative means should be considered.
- **Refuges** for evacuation are dealt with in SANS 10400-T 4.16.8 and cross-referenced here.

### Part T — Fire protection

**Current: SANS 10400-T:2024 (Ed. 5.01).** Satisfies T1–T2. The most substantial part in the series — 59 numbered requirement clauses plus a normative annex on rational designs. See `08_fire-safety-requirements.md` for the detail. Structure verified from the 2020 Ed. 4: 4.1 General; 4.2 Safety distances; 4.3 Different occupancies in a building; 4.4 Division area; 4.5 Fire performance; 4.6 Fire resistance of occupancy-separating and division-separating elements; 4.7 Fire stability of structural elements; 4.8 Tenancy-separating elements; 4.9 Partition walls; 4.10 Protection of openings; 4.11 Raised access and suspended floors; 4.12 Roof assemblies and coverings; 4.13 Ceilings; 4.14 Floor coverings; 4.15 Internal finishes; 4.16 Provision of escape routes; 4.17 Exit doors; 4.18 Feeder routes; 4.19 Emergency routes; 4.20–4.21 Dimensions and width of escape routes; 4.22 Basements; 4.23 Stairways and changes of level; 4.24–4.25 Ventilation and pressurization; 4.26–4.28 Openings in floors, external stairways, lobbies; 4.29 Marking and signposting; 4.30 Emergency lighting; 4.31 Fire detection and alarm; 4.32–4.38 Fire-fighting equipment, water reticulation, hose reels, hydrants, fixed systems, portable and mobile extinguishers; 4.39–4.41 Fire-stopping, service shafts, services in separating elements; 4.42–4.43 Smoke control and HVAC; 4.44–4.47 Lift shafts, lifts, firemen's lift, stretcher lift; 4.48–4.53 Stage areas, seating, parking garages, operating theatres, fuel dispensing, other tanks; 4.54 Access for fire-fighting and rescue; 4.55 Presumed fire resistance of building materials and components; 4.56 Building materials; 4.57 Single-storey category 1 buildings for H3 and H4; 4.58 Guest houses and B&Bs; 4.59 Health care facilities; Annex B (normative) Rational designs.

### Part U — Refuse disposal

**No SANS 10400-U is listed in the SABS catalogue as at 2026-08-25.** NBR Part U (U1–U3) governs refuse storage areas, access for collection, and refuse chutes, and appears to have no separate deemed-to-satisfy standard. Municipal solid waste by-laws usually carry the operative detail. Confirm with SABS.

### Part V — Space heating

**Current: SANS 10400-V:2018 (Ed. 3.01).** Satisfies V1 (design, construction and installation). Structure: 4.1 General; 4.2 Flue pipes; 4.3 Chimneys; 4.4 Hearths and fireplaces. A short part governing solid-fuel and gas space heating appliances, flue construction and termination, chimney construction, and hearth dimensions and non-combustible surrounds. It is the part that governs the fireplace and braai chimney on a domestic plan.

### Part W — Fire installation

**Current: SANS 10400-W:2026 (Ed. 3.01).** Satisfies W1–W4 (installations, water supply, design, deemed-to-satisfy). Governs the water supply for fire-fighting: fire mains, hydrant provision and spacing, static water storage where the municipal supply is inadequate, pump and booster requirements, and fire-hose reel water supply. SANS 10400-T 4.33 defers to Part W for all water reticulation conveying water solely for fire-fighting. Rational design under Regulation W4 is a Regulation A19 trigger.

*Hydrant spacing, flow and pressure values in Ed. 3.01 were not verified from a public source — consult the purchased standard, and confirm the local authority's fire department requirements, which often exceed the standard.*

### Parts X and XA — Environmental sustainability, energy usage in buildings

**Current: SANS 10400-XA:2026 (Ed. 3.00).** Part X of the NBR is the environmental sustainability part; XA is the energy usage regulation within it (XA1 general, XA2 hot water, XA3 alternative compliance). See `06_energy-and-sustainability-requirements.md`.

Structure of the 2021 Ed. 2: 1 Scope; 2 Normative references; 3 Terms and definitions; 4 Requirements (including 4.3 the reference-building route and 4.4 the performance-based route); 5 Building envelope (5.1–5.7, including 5.3 fenestration, 5.5 external walls, 5.6 roof assemblies); 6 Services (6.1–6.3); Annex C with the town/energy-zone table.

The 2021 edition replaced the six climatic zones of the 2011 edition with **energy zones 1–7 plus 5H**. Regulation XA2 requires that at least **50 % (volume fraction)** of the annual average hot water heating requirement be provided by means other than electrical resistance heating.
