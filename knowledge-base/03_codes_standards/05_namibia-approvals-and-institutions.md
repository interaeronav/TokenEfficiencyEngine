# Namibia — approvals, permits and the institutions that issue them

**Summary.** **[NA]** A Namibian building project touches more separate institutions than a South African one, because there is no single building act to consolidate them. Land tenure comes from either the Deeds Office (freehold) or a Traditional Authority and Communal Land Board (communal). Planning comes from the local authority or the Minister under the Urban and Regional Planning Act. Building approval, where it exists, comes from the local authority. Environmental clearance comes from the Environmental Commissioner at MEFT. Water comes from NamWater and the local authority or the Directorate of Rural Water Supply. Electricity comes from a regional electricity distributor licensed by the Electricity Control Board. Professional competence is regulated by NCAQS and the Engineering Council of Namibia. Occupational health and safety sits under the Labour Act. This file maps each.

> ⚠️ Confidence on this file is **medium** overall. Several Namibian institutional websites publish little detail, and some Acts could not be retrieved in full. Section numbers and process detail marked *not verified* must be confirmed with the institution before a project relies on them.

## Key facts — who issues what

| Approval / permit | Institution | Legal instrument | Applies where |
|---|---|---|---|

## Professional registration

### NCAQS — Namibia Council for Architects and Quantity Surveyors

- A statutory council established by the **Architects' and Quantity Surveyors' Act 13 of 1979**.
- Registers **Professional Architects** and **Professional Quantity Surveyors**, regulates the professions in the public interest against unprofessional conduct, and establishes the route to registration for practice in Namibia.
- Contact: 8 Koch Street, Klein Windhoek, Windhoek; +264 61 401813; www.ncaqs.org.
- Practical significance for building control: the City of Windhoek accepts building plans from the property owner, or from a **registered architect, surveyor or professional engineer quoting their registration number** — so the registration number is a field on the submission form.

*The registration categories, the reserved-work provisions and the qualification/experience routes under Act 13 of 1979 were not verified from a public source — obtain them from NCAQS.*

### Engineering Council of Namibia (ECN)

Established by the **Engineering Profession Act 18 of 1986** (successor to the South West African Engineering Council) as a juristic person. Council composition: nine appointed members — seven nominated by professional engineering associations, one government engineer, one public-interest representative and one academic from an engineering faculty (ss 2–3).

Registration categories:

| Category | Basis |
|---|---|
| Professional Engineer | Four-year bachelor's degree in engineering sciences (s 11) |
| Engineer in Training | Qualification met, experience not yet complete (s 11) |
| Incorporated Engineer | Diploma in engineering technology (s 12) |
| Engineering Technician | Three-year national diploma (s 13) |

Each category has a corresponding "in training" classification.

**Reserved work** falls into two bands:
- Work in connection with projects, undertakings or services of an engineering nature, as determined by regulation — reserved to **professional engineers** (s 7(3)(b));
- Work "specially pertaining" to the engineering disciplines listed in Schedule II Parts A and B — reserved to **professional and incorporated engineers**.

Offences relating to the unlawful use of titles are in ss 16–17; improper conduct in s 18; penalties in ss 15–20.

> ⚠️ The ECN has been the subject of governance disputes, including a challenge to a decision to dissolve the council. Confirm the council's current constitution and operating status before relying on a registration.

Related bodies: the **Association of Consulting Engineers of Namibia (ACEN)** and the **Engineering Professions Association of Namibia (EPA)** are voluntary professional associations, not statutory registrars.

## Environmental clearance — the permit that applies everywhere

The **Environmental Management Act 7 of 2007** came into force with its regulations on **6 February 2012**: the **EIA Regulations (GN 30 of 2012)** and the **Listed Activities (GN 29 of 2012)**.

### Who does what

- The **proponent** (project owner) initiates the application and appoints, at their own cost, a qualified **Environmental Assessment Practitioner (EAP)** to run the assessment.
- The **Environmental Commissioner**, in the Department of Environmental Affairs at the Ministry of Environment, Forestry and Tourism, is the decision-maker and issues the Environmental Clearance Certificate.
- The **Competent Authority** is the ministry responsible for the sector — for example the Ministry of Mines and Energy for prospecting and mining.

### The process

1. EAPs register the project on the online portal at https://eia.meft.gov.na/.
2. A screening questionnaire determines whether a full EIA or baseline information is required.
3. **Public participation:** stakeholder identification and notification via notice boards, written notices and newspaper advertisements in **two consecutive weeks**, with a **14-day** initial comment period after newspaper publication. A Background Information Document (BID) is circulated with focus group and public meetings. Initial public participation must complete within **21 days**.
4. Draft scoping and ESIA reports with an **Environmental Management Plan (EMP)** are released for **14 days** of stakeholder review. All comments are recorded in an **Issues and Responses Report (IRR)**.
5. Final reports are uploaded to the DEA website with a further **14 days** for online comment.
6. The Environmental Commissioner reviews and decides.

**Fees:** application **N$300**; transfer of a certificate **N$1 000**; appeal **N$1 000**.

**Validity: an ECC is valid for three years.** Renewal requires satisfactory reporting against the EMP conditions, and competent authorities may conduct site inspections.

### Listed activities relevant to construction and land development

From the Listed Activities under GN 29 of 2012:

- **5.1** Rezoning of land from residential to industrial or commercial use; from light industrial to heavy industrial; from agricultural to industrial use.
- **5.2** Establishment of resettlement schemes.
- **5.3** Construction of protective fences.
- **8.1** Ground or surface water abstraction for industrial or commercial purposes.
- **8.4** Construction of canals and channels, including diversion of the normal flow of water.
- **8.6** Construction of industrial and domestic wastewater treatment plants and related pipeline systems.
- **8.7** Irrigation schemes for agriculture.
- **8.9** Construction within catchment areas.
- **10.1(a)** Oil, water, gas, petrochemical and other bulk supply pipelines.
- **10.1(b)** Public road construction.
- **10.2** Route determination for roads exceeding 30 m reserves.
- **3.2** Other forms of mining or extraction of any natural resources, whether regulated by law or not — **this catches borrow pits, sand winning and quarrying for construction materials**.
- **3.4** Gas extraction from landfill sites.

> ⚠️ **Township establishment and the construction of a building are not, in themselves, listed activities.** A single house does not need an ECC. But a project that opens a borrow pit, abstracts groundwater commercially, builds a wastewater treatment plant, constructs a road, or rezones land almost certainly does. On a remote rural project the borrow pit and the borehole are the two most commonly-missed triggers.

## Water

### NamWater — Namibia Water Corporation Ltd

- Established under the **Namibia Water Corporation Act 12 of 1997**; s 2 creates the company, s 3 incorporates it as a public company under the Companies Act with the **State as sole shareholder**, represented by the Minister. Registered as a company on **9 December 1997**.
- Function: supply **bulk water** to customers in adequate quantity and suitable quality by cost-effective, environmentally sound and sustainable means, plus related services and facilities.
- It supplies **in bulk** — to industries, municipalities and the **Directorate of Rural Water Supply**. It does **not** do household reticulation.
- Regional offices: Okahandja, Swakopmund, **Oshakati**, Keetmanshoop, Grootfontein. Oshakati is the relevant office for Ohangwena.
- The Act transferred bulk water supply operations, waterworks and related assets from the Department of Water Affairs.

### Where your water actually comes from

| Location | Bulk | Reticulation / connection |
|---|---|---|
| Proclaimed town | NamWater | Local authority (LAA s 30(1)(a), Part VI) |
| Settlement area | NamWater | Regional council |
| Communal / rural | NamWater or local groundwater | Directorate of Rural Water Supply (Ministry of Agriculture, Water and Land Reform), community water point committees, or a private borehole |

Water tariffs charged by a local authority are subject to any tariff determined by the **Water Regulator** under the **Water Resources Management Act 11 of 2013** (LAA s 30(1)(u) as substituted by Act 11 of 2013). Borehole drilling and abstraction are regulated under the same Act.

## Electricity

### Electricity Control Board (ECB)

The economic and technical regulator of the Namibian electricity supply industry, operating under the **Electricity Act 4 of 2007** (which replaced Act 2 of 2000). Its stated mandate is to regulate the industry "in the interest of all stakeholders with regard to efficiency, affordability, safety and accessibility". It issues licences for generation, transmission, distribution, supply, trading and import/export.

Address: 35 Dr Theo-Ben Gurirab Street, Windhoek; +264 61 374 300; info@ecb.org.na.

*The full licence taxonomy and the technical/safety conditions attached to a distribution licence were not verified from a public source.*

### NamPower

The national utility: generation and transmission, and bulk supply to distributors and large customers.

### Regional Electricity Distributors (REDs)

Namibia's distribution was restructured into regional distributors jointly owned by NamPower and the local authorities/regional councils in their area:

- **NORED** — Northern Regional Electricity Distributor. Serves the northern regions; its own publications reference **Ohangwena** and Oshikoto among its service areas. Toll-free 0800 000 100. New connections are applied for through its Customer Service "New Connections Applications" process. **This is the distributor for the Okongo area.**
- **CENORED** — Central-North Regional Electricity Distributor. Otjozondjupa and adjoining areas.
- **Erongo RED** — the Erongo Region.
- Elsewhere, local authorities or NamPower distribute directly.

> ⚠️ Getting a connection is normally the long-lead item on a remote Namibian project. Budget for the distributor's own application, wayleave, line construction and connection-fee process in parallel with — not after — building approval. The distributor will require a compliant electrical installation and an electrical certificate before energising; **SANS 10142-1** is the reference wiring standard used in practice.

## Ministry of Works and Transport

The Ministry of Works and Transport is the custodian of State-owned buildings and infrastructure and the procuring authority for government building projects through its Department of Works. It also carries transport regulation functions (roads, civil aviation, meteorological services).

*The Ministry's specific statutory role in building standards, and whether government buildings are exempt from local authority building control, were not verified from a public source.* By analogy with the South African position (NBR Regulation A1(2), where State buildings are accompanied by a departmental certificate rather than full local authority approval) it is likely that Namibian government buildings follow an internal approval route, but this must be confirmed with the Ministry.

Related: the **Public Procurement Act 15 of 2015** governs the tendering of government construction work, and the **Namibia Institute of Public Administration** and **Roads Authority** carry adjacent mandates.

## Occupational health and safety

- **[NA]** The **Labour Act 11 of 2007** carries the employer's general duty to provide and maintain a safe working environment.
- The **Regulations relating to the health and safety of employees at work, GN 156 of 1997**, made under the labour legislation, are the operative detailed rules. The NSI lists them among the instruments it administers, alongside the Labour Act 2007 and its regulations.
- These regulations are the Namibian counterpart to the **[ZA]** OHS Act 85 of 1993 Construction Regulations, but they are **general workplace regulations, not construction-specific**. Namibia has no direct equivalent of the South African Construction Regulations with their client duties, construction work permit, principal contractor appointment and health and safety file regime.

*The chapter structure and the construction-specific content of GN 156 of 1997 (scaffolding, excavations, working at height, PPE, health and safety representatives, first aid, notification of construction work) were not verified from a public source — obtain the Government Notice from the NSI or the Ministry of Labour.*

Practical consequence: on a Namibian site, the safety regime is usually driven by the contract (a donor's or a client's HSE specification, often modelled on the South African Construction Regulations or on ISO 45001) rather than by a detailed statutory construction code.
