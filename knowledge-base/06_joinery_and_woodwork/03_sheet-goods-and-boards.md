# Sheet goods and boards — MDF, chipboard, melamine, plywood, HPL and edging

**Summary.** Southern African fitted joinery is built almost entirely from two board families sold in one dominant sheet format: **2750 × 1830 mm** (nominally 9 ft × 6 ft) and **3660 × 1830 mm** (12 ft × 6 ft). PG Bison's BisonBord (particleboard) and SupaWood (MDF) are the reference substrates, MelaWood is the melamine-faced product built on both, and Sonae Arauco's Novogold/Novowood range is the main alternative. Imported Egger board arrives in the European **2800 × 2070 mm** format, which does not nest with local cutting lists — never mix the two formats in one cutting plan.

## Key facts

| Product | Substrate | Sheet sizes (mm) | Thicknesses (mm) | Standard |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **BisonBord** (PG Bison) | particleboard | 2750 × 1830; 3660 × 1830 | 9, 12, 16, 18, 22, 25, 32 | SANS 50312:2015 (EN 312:2010) |
| **SupaWood** (PG Bison) | MDF | 2750 × 1830; 3660 × 1830 | 3, 6, 9, 12, 16, 18, 22, 30 | EN 622-5:2009 |
| **MelaWood** (PG Bison) | BisonBord or SupaWood | 2750 × 1840 | 12, 16, 18 (on BisonBord); 12, 16, 18, 22, 30 (on SupaWood) | — |
| **Formica LifeSeal worktop** | BisonBord | 3660 × 600 | 32 | SANS 50312:2015, SANS 929:2008, EN 438-1/-2 |
| **Egger Eurodekor MFC** | particleboard | 2800 × 2070 | 8, 12, 15, 16, 18, 19, 22, 25, 28, 30, 38 | EN 312 P2 |

- BisonBord is offered as **Supreme** (A-grade), **Select** (commercial grade), **MR** (moisture resistant) and **E1** (low formaldehyde). It is available in **E1 (Class 1)** or **E2 (Class 2)** emission classes.
- Sonae Arauco South Africa: **Novogold** (A-grade particleboard), **Novogold MR** (moisture-resistant), **Novowood** (MDF), **Novowood DR** (deep-routing MDF for profiled doors), **Novolite** (thin MDF, faced or unfaced).

> ⚠️ MelaWood sheets are supplied at **2750 × 1840 mm** while unfaced BisonBord/SupaWood are nominally **2750 × 1830 mm**. The extra 10 mm is trim allowance on the faced product. Cutting lists must state which figure they assume.

## 1. Particleboard (chipboard) — BisonBord, Novogold

Three-layer, resin-bonded wood particles with fine surface layers and a coarse core. Cheap, flat, dimensionally stable in the plane, and the default carcass substrate for melamine-faced fitted furniture.

**Behaviour to design around:**
- **Poor edge screw-holding.** Never screw into the raw edge of 16 mm particleboard for a load-bearing connection; use confirmats with proper pilot holes, dowels, or a Lamello/knock-down connector.
- **Catastrophic on water.** Standard particleboard swells irreversibly. Anything under a sink, in a scullery, or in a laundry must be **MR grade** and every cut edge must be sealed or edged.
- **Blunts tooling fast** because of resin and residual mineral content — carbide only, and expect shorter tool life than on solid wood.

**Moisture-resistant grades** (BisonBord MR, Novogold MR) are usually dyed green in the core so they can be identified after cutting. MR is *moisture resistant*, not waterproof: it buys you time against splashes and humidity, not against a leaking trap.

## 2. MDF / HDF — SupaWood, Novowood

Fibre, not particle. Homogeneous, no grain, no voids, machines to a crisp edge, takes paint superbly, and routs into profiles — which is why it dominates painted door and moulding work.

- **Density** is much higher than particleboard (typically ~700–800 kg/m³ for standard MDF), so a full 18 mm sheet is heavy: a 2750 × 1830 × 18 mm SupaWood sheet is 0.0906 m³, so at ~750 kg/m³ it weighs roughly **68 kg**. Plan handling.
- **Edge sealing before painting** is mandatory: MDF edge is absorbent end-grain fibre. Use a dedicated MDF edge sealer or two coats of thinned primer with a light sand between.
- **Deep-routing grades** (Novowood DR) have a denser, more uniform core specifically so that routed profiles do not show core texture.
- **HDF** (higher density, usually 3–6 mm) is used for drawer bottoms, cabinet backs and door panel infill.
- **Dust is the hazard.** MDF dust is fine, respirable and carries resin; extraction plus P2/FFP2 respirators are not optional.

## 3. Melamine-faced board — MelaWood, Egger Eurodekor

A melamine-impregnated décor paper fused to the substrate under heat and pressure. It is the finished surface — there is no site finishing. MelaWood is offered in Linear, Peen, Fusion and Natural Touch surface textures across a large décor range (Esperanza Oak, Coimbra, Cannero, Camden, Normandy, Monument Oak, Lanza Oak, Verzasca Oak and others).

**Working rules:**
- **Chipping is the whole game.** Use a scoring saw on a panel saw, or a high-tooth-count triple-chip / Hi-ATB blade with a zero-clearance insert on a table saw. Score cuts on a plunge saw. Cut face-down on a portable circular saw, face-up on a table saw.
- **Every exposed edge must be edged.** PG Bison's guidance is explicit: exposed edges "must be properly edged using a suitable glue and edging material" or the substrate will take up moisture.
- Décor-matched edging is available for most MelaWood décors — specify by décor name and finish code, not by colour description.
- Textured (synchronised-pore) décors must be **grain-direction consistent** across a run of doors; the texture is directional even when the pattern looks random.

## 4. Blockboard, veneered board and "supawood"

- **Blockboard / laminboard:** a core of edge-glued softwood strips faced with veneer or ply. Light, stiff along the core direction, good for wide doors and long shelves. Less used now; check core-strip alignment before use as a door.
- **Veneered board (veneered MDF/chipboard):** real wood veneer pressed to a board core. Buy **balanced** panels (equal veneer/backing both faces) or they will bow. Veneer thickness is typically 0.5–0.6 mm — one careless sanding pass goes through.
- **"Supawood"** in South African trade usage means PG Bison SupaWood, i.e. plain MDF. In Namibia the term is used generically for MDF; confirm what is meant before ordering.

## 5. Plywood

Cross-laminated veneers, odd number of plies. Far better edge screw-holding and impact resistance than particleboard, and the only sheet material that behaves structurally.

**Grading you will meet:**
- **Bonding (EN 314-2):** Class 1 dry/interior; Class 2 humid, protected exterior; Class 3 exterior, weather exposure. The older UK term **WBP** roughly corresponds to Class 3; **MR** to Class 1/2.
- **Service class (EN 636):** EN 636-1 dry interior; EN 636-2 humid; EN 636-3 exterior.
- **Face veneer grades:** birch ply is sold as **B / BB / CP / C** (B = premium, clear; BB = small sound knots and oval patches; CP = paint grade with visible patching; C = utility). Common combinations are B/BB, BB/BB, **BB/CP (the standard cabinet grade)** and CP/CP for painted work. EN 635 uses the numeric scale E, I, II, III, IV for the same idea.
- **Marine plywood** to **BS 1088** — gaboon or okoumé faces, WBP bond, no core voids. Sold locally in 2440 × 1220 mm at 6, 9, 12, 18, 25 mm. **[ZA]** SANS 929 is the South African plywood and blockboard standard and is cited on PG Bison's Formica LifeSeal worktop.
- **Standard sheet sizes:** imported birch ply in 2440 × 1220, 1525 × 1525 and 1250 × 2500 mm, thicknesses 3–24 mm; local pine and hardwood ply usually 2440 × 1220.

**When to specify plywood over board:** drawer boxes, shelving over 800 mm span, anything screwed repeatedly, anything that could get wet, and any carcass being shipped long distance on Namibian gravel roads.

## 6. HPL / Formica and worktops

High-pressure laminate is kraft paper and décor paper impregnated with phenolic and melamine resins and pressed at high pressure — classified under **EN 438**. Nominal thicknesses are typically 0.7–1.2 mm for horizontal and vertical grades; postforming grades are thinner and can be wrapped to a radius.

- **Formica LifeSeal worktops** (PG Bison): **3660 × 600 × 32 mm**, HPL over a BisonBord core, square profile with a tight radius, supplied with a free matching edging strip.
- **Installation rule:** *all* exposed edges — including the back edge against the wall, butt joints, and sink/hob cut-outs — must be sealed with a suitable sealant, because the core is particleboard.
- **Balance the panel.** HPL bonded to one face of a substrate must have a backing laminate on the reverse or the panel will bow.
- **Cut-outs:** radius every internal corner (minimum ~10 mm, larger is better) — a square internal corner in HPL is a guaranteed crack initiator. Keep cut-outs at least 50–75 mm from any joint.
- Condition HPL sheets flat, in the shop, at the shop's temperature and humidity, for at least 48 hours before bonding.

## 7. Edging

| Type | Thicknesses | Widths | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PVC** | 400 µm, 600 µm, 1.0, 1.3, 1.5, 2.0 mm | to order | Cheapest impact-resistant option; large décor range |
| **ABS** | 800 µm, 1.0, 1.3, 1.5, 2.0 mm | 16–105 mm | Halogen-free, better for recycling; the specification default |
| **Wood veneer** | 0.5, 1.0, 2.0 mm | 22, 38, 44 mm (custom available), 100 m rolls | Pre-glued or unglued |
| **Melamine** | (paper) | 19, 20, 22, 25, 28, 34, 38, 50 mm | Budget/DIY; pre-glued or unglued |

**Selection rules:**
- **0.4–0.8 mm** for non-contact edges (cabinet interiors, shelf backs, hidden edges).
- **1.0 mm** for general visible cabinet edges.
- **2.0 mm** for door and drawer-front edges and anything that gets knocked — and it is the only thickness that can be radiused properly by an edgebander's profile trimmer.
- Match edge décor and *texture* to the board; a smooth edge on a synchronised-pore board reads as a mistake.

## 8. Formaldehyde and emissions

- **E1 / E2** are the European emission classes; E1 is the standard specification for interior furniture. **E0** and **Super-E0** are lower, non-harmonised marketing classes.
- **[ZA]/[NA]** Local particleboard is available as E1 or E2 — **always specify E1**, particularly for bedroom wardrobes and any built-in unit in a room with limited ventilation.
- For export or for clients specifying US compliance, look for **CARB Phase 2 / TSCA Title VI** certification; Egger's TSCA-compliant grades are marked E1 E05 TSCA.

## 9. Cutting, edging and handling requirements

1. **Store flat, fully supported, off the floor.** Board stored on edge for weeks will take a permanent bow.
2. **Acclimatise 48 hours** in the shop before cutting; board is far less hygroscopic than solid timber but is not inert.
3. **Cut sequence:** rip the long dimension first, then crosscut, so that offcuts stay usable; keep grain/texture direction consistent across a batch.
4. **Blade choice:** see `05_machinery-and-tooling.md` — 80–100 tooth TCG or Hi-ATB for melamine and HPL on a 250–300 mm blade; scoring blade set 0.1–0.2 mm wider than the main blade kerf.
5. **Edgeband before drilling** system holes where possible, so that the boring reference is the finished edge.
6. **Glue line:** EVA hot-melt at 170–200 °C for general work; PUR at 120–140 °C for kitchen, scullery and bathroom work — see `08_finishes-and-adhesives.md`.
7. **Protect faces.** High-gloss and super-matt boards ship with a protective film; leave it on until final installation, and never slide sheets face-to-face.
