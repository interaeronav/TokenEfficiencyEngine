# Cabinetmaking and fitted furniture — systems, dimensions and installation

**Summary.** Fitted furniture is a dimensional system before it is a craft. Get the grid right — 32 mm hole spacing, 37 mm front setback, a fixed carcass height and depth, hardware chosen before carcass sizes are frozen — and the rest is production. Get it wrong and every door reveal, every drawer runner and every appliance aperture becomes a site problem. This file fixes the grid, gives the standard dimensions for kitchens, sculleries, wardrobes and dressing rooms, and sets out how to scribe and install.

## Key facts

| Element | Dimension |
|---|---|
| System hole diameter / spacing / depth | Ø5 mm / 32 mm centres / 12–14 mm deep |
| System row setback from front edge | **37 mm** (and 37 mm from the rear edge) |
| Kitchen base carcass height (excl. plinth and top) | **720 mm** |
| Kitchen base carcass depth | **560–570 mm** |
| Plinth / kickboard height | **150 mm** (100–200 mm range) |
| Finished worktop height | **870–910 mm** (900 mm typical) |
| Worktop depth / thickness | 600 mm / 32–40 mm |
| Worktop to underside of wall unit | **450–500 mm** |
| Wall unit depth (excl. door) | **300 mm** (330–350 mm for a deeper look) |
| Wall unit heights | 575, 720, 900 mm |
| Tall unit heights | 1960–2300 mm, commonly **2140 mm** |
| Standard cabinet widths | 150, 300, 400, 450, 500, 600, 800, 1000, 1200 mm |
| Appliance aperture (oven, dishwasher, washing machine, standard fridge) | **600 mm** wide |
| Work-triangle rule | sum of the three legs **≤ 6500 mm** |
| Wardrobe internal depth | **530 mm** (600 mm including a stud wall) |
| Walk-in robe aisle | **900 mm** comfortable; minimum WIR 1430 × 1500 mm |

## 1. The 32 mm system

Post-war European cabinet production standardised on a **Ø5 mm hole at 32 mm centres**, drilled **12–14 mm** deep, in vertical rows set **37 mm** from the front edge of the carcass side and 37 mm from the rear edge. The 32 mm interval originated in the gear spacing achievable on early line-boring machines and became the module that hinge plates, drawer runners, shelf supports, hanging rails and carcass connectors are all built around.

**Rules that follow from it:**

- Distance from the first hole to the bottom of the panel equals the distance from the last hole to the top — the panel is symmetrical, so left and right sides are interchangeable and cannot be fitted upside down.
- Rows between the front and rear rows sit at multiples of 32 mm.
- **Front and back reveals:** door and drawer-front heights are set out in 32 mm increments minus the gap, e.g. `32 × 5 − 3 = 157 mm`, so that fronts stack up the grid regardless of how many drawers.
- **Registration:** either *system registration* (front edges centred on a system hole) or *shifted registration* (edges centred between holes). Pick one and use it across the whole job.
- **Construction holes** may be separate (dowels or confirmats) or the system holes themselves may be used with cam/Rafix-type connectors, which removes all end-boring.
- Shelf pins are 15–16 mm long with a ~7 mm flange.

A shop without a line borer or CNC can work the system with a shelf-pin jig (Festool LR 32 type) referenced off the panel's front edge. What must never happen is measuring hole positions with a tape.

## 2. Carcass construction systems compared

| System | Speed | Strength | Knock-down | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dowels (Ø8 × 30 mm)** | fast with CNC/line borer, slow by hand | good in shear, hidden | no | production melamine carcasses, glued |
| **Confirmat 7 × 50 mm** | fast, no clamps | very high pull-out in particleboard | semi (limited re-use) | site-built, one-offs, garage stores |
| **Lamello Clamex P-14** | fast with Zeta P2 | 1070–1210 N in board | yes, repeatedly | site-assembled, transportable, mitred, large units |
| **Cam-and-dowel (Rafix / minifix)** | fast | moderate | yes | flat-pack, bedroom units |
| **Housing/dado + screws** | slow | very high | no | plywood shop furniture, heavy shelving |

**Confirmat practice:** a 7 × 50 mm confirmat needs a **Ø7 mm clearance hole** in the through-member and a **Ø5 mm** (in practice 5.0–5.4 mm) core hole in the receiving edge, or a matching step drill. Clamp the parts together and drill both in one operation, or drill separately with a jig — never freehand. Keep confirmats at least ~36 mm from a panel end. They are designed for particleboard and MDF, not for veneer-core plywood, which splinters.

**Rule of thumb:** for a 720 × 560 mm base cabinet, three fixings per joint at roughly 50 / 280 / 510 mm from the front edge.

**Backs.** A 3–6 mm back in a rebate or groove, glued and pinned, provides all the racking resistance a cabinet needs. A back nailed onto the rear edge does not. For wall-hung units, use a **16–18 mm back** or a full-width hanging rail — the back carries the load into the hanging bracket.

## 3. Kitchen and scullery dimensions

**Base units.** 720 mm carcass on a 150 mm plinth, plus a 32–40 mm worktop = 902–910 mm finished height. If a different worktop height is wanted, change the plinth, not the carcass. Depth 560–570 mm carcass, 600 mm worktop, giving a 30–40 mm overhang plus a 20–30 mm services void at the back.

**Ergonomic worktop height.** The standard 850–900 mm suits a user of roughly 1.65–1.75 m. The practical rule is **elbow height minus 100–150 mm** for general prep, and roughly 150–200 mm lower for a hob (so a pot's contents are visible) or for kneading. In a scullery, raising the sink run by 50 mm to ~950 mm markedly reduces back strain, because a sink's working plane is the bottom of the bowl.

**Wall units.** 300 mm deep, 450–500 mm above the worktop. Heights 575 / 720 / 900 mm; a 720 mm unit with its top at 2140 mm aligns with a 2140 mm tall unit — the alignment that makes a run look designed. Reach limit for a 1.6 m user is about 1900 mm to the top *usable* shelf; anything above that is seasonal storage.

**Tall units and appliance housings.** 560–570 mm deep, 1960–2300 mm high; 2140 mm is the common module. Standard apertures: single oven 600 × 590 mm high (verify against the specific appliance), dishwasher 600 mm wide × 820 mm high, washing machine 600 mm, integrated fridge 540–600 mm wide × 1770 or 1900 mm high. **Always take the aperture from the appliance's own installation drawing** — the "600 mm standard" hides 596, 597 and 598 mm variants and no two brands agree on the height.

**Drawer heights** in a base unit typically stack as 3 drawers (140 / 280 / 280 mm fronts) or 4 drawers, on a 720 mm carcass with a 150 mm plinth. Set them out on the 32 mm grid: `32n − 3`.

**Clearances.**
- Galley/parallel runs: **1100–1200 mm** between fronts; 900 mm absolute minimum, 1500 mm if two people work and a dishwasher door and an oven door can be open opposite each other.
- Island to run: 1000–1200 mm.
- Work triangle (sink–hob–fridge): each leg 1200–2700 mm, total ≤ 6500 mm.
- Landing space: 400 mm minimum beside a hob, 450 mm beside a fridge on the handle side, 600 mm beside a sink.

**Scullery.** Treat as a working room, not a display: 600 mm deep base units, no wall units above the sink, open shelving or 300 mm wall units elsewhere, MR-grade board throughout, PUR-glued edges, a 100 mm upstand or full-height splashback, and a removable plinth panel over the trap.

## 4. Wardrobes and dressing rooms

**Depths.** Internal carcass depth **530 mm** minimum for hanging on a front-to-back rail (600 mm including the framing/wall). A garment on a hanger occupies 550–600 mm; anything under 530 mm forces a side-to-side (pull-out) rail. Shelving-only runs can be **450 mm** deep, and 350 mm is comfortable for folded knitwear.

**Hanging heights (clear internal height under the rail):**

| Garment | Clear height | Rail height above floor (typical) |
|---|---|---|
| Shirts, jackets, folded trousers (short hang) | 950–1100 mm | ~1050 mm over a lower zone |
| Skirts, trousers full length | 1200–1400 mm | — |
| Dresses, long coats (long hang) | 1500–1700 mm | 1600–1700 mm |
| Full-length gowns | 1800–2000 mm | — |

**Double hang** is the standard efficiency move: an upper rail at ~1950–2050 mm and a lower rail at ~1000–1050 mm, each with 950–1000 mm clear. A common wardrobe layout is a full-height long-hang bay (600–900 mm wide) beside two double-hang bays.

**Other dimensions.**
- Rail: Ø25–30 mm oval or round, supported at maximum **900 mm** centres — an unsupported 1200 mm rail will sag under a full load of coats.
- Clearance below a rail to the top of a drawer or shelf: **50 mm** minimum for hanger clearance and knuckles.
- Shelf spacing for folded clothes: 300–350 mm; for shoes 200 mm (flats) and up to 500 mm (boots); heels and flats around 250 mm.
- Internal drawers: 400–900 mm wide, 100/150/200 mm internal depths; keep drawer fronts below ~1250 mm so the contents are visible.
- Top shelf: 2100–2150 mm to the shelf surface — beyond comfortable reach, so use it for luggage and seasonal storage.
- **Walk-in dressing room:** 900 mm aisle for comfortable use, 1200 mm if two people pass or if drawers open into the aisle; minimum footprint about 1430 × 1500 mm for a single-sided WIR; a double-sided WIR needs ~2400 mm width (600 + 900 + 600, rounded up).
- Sliding doors need **at least 600 mm** clear in front to stand and dress; hinged doors need 900 mm swing clearance.

**[NA]** In a dry, dusty climate, prefer sealed carcasses with full backs and either soft-close hinged doors or good-quality sliding gear with brush seals. Open-front dressing rooms look excellent and collect dust.

## 5. Setting out — shop-built vs site-built

**Shop-built (preferred).** Take site dimensions after plaster and before floor tiling; build carcasses to a fixed grid; deal with all site variation in *filler panels, scribe strips and adjustable feet*. Rules:

- Leave a **20–30 mm scribe strip** on the end panel of every run that meets a wall, and 10–15 mm on the top of tall units meeting a ceiling.
- Leave a **10–20 mm filler** at any internal corner so that two runs of doors do not collide.
- Never build a carcass to a measured wall dimension. Build to the module and scribe.
- Take three measurements of every wall length (at 100 mm, 900 mm and 2000 mm above floor) and use the **smallest**.
- Take the floor level across the whole run with a laser and mark the **highest** point; all cabinet tops are set from there.

**Site-built.** Only where access, transport or geometry demand it (deep alcoves, curved walls, remote sites with no transport). Use confirmats or Clamex, a plywood or MDF sub-base scribed to the floor, and a laser line for the top edge. Accept that quality control is harder and price accordingly.

## 6. Scribing to uneven walls, floors and ceilings

1. **Adjustable legs first.** Set every cabinet to the datum height with adjustable feet (typically 100–150 mm range with an M10 thread); do not pack with timber.
2. **Scribe the panel, not the wall.** Set the panel plumb and hard against its high point, run a compass or scribing block set to the largest gap along the wall, and cut to the line.
3. **Back-bevel** the cut by 5–10° so only the front arris touches; the joint closes tight.
4. **Cutting method:** jigsaw with a down-cut blade for melamine (or score the line first), belt sander or plane for the final fit.
5. **Ceilings:** scribe a separate cornice or infill piece; never try to scribe a full-height door or a carcass side to a ceiling.
6. **Worktops:** scribe the back edge of the worktop, not the wall; leave a 3–5 mm shadow gap and seal it if a splashback will cover it.
7. **Internal corners:** the two walls are never 90°. Cut a loose corner post/filler and scribe both edges.

## 7. Installation sequence

1. Confirm services: water, waste, gas, power and extract positions against the shop drawing. Photograph and record before anything is covered.
2. Mark the **datum line** — level, at the top of the base carcasses — around the whole room from the highest floor point.
3. Fit hanging rails / wall brackets for wall units on the marked line, into masonry with the right fixings (nylon plug + 5 × 50 mm screw minimum into solid brick; toggle or resin into hollow block).
4. Set base carcasses from the corner outward, level and in line, clamp adjacent carcasses face-frame-to-face-frame and connect with cabinet connector screws.
5. Fit tall units and appliance housings; check the aperture against the appliance now, not later.
6. Fit wall units to the rails; align fronts.
7. Template and fit worktops. Dry-fit, scribe, then cut; seal every cut edge; joint with worktop bolts and a colour-matched joint sealant.
8. Fit doors and drawers; adjust hinges and runners to consistent reveals (3 mm typical, 2 mm on tight work).
9. Fit plinths last, scribed to the floor, with removable sections at trap and appliance positions.
10. Fit handles using a drilling jig, then cornice/pelmet, then silicone and final clean.

> ⚠️ Never fit the plinth before the appliances are in. Every integrated dishwasher and washing machine needs the plinth off for installation and for later service.

## Open questions

- Kitchen dimensional standards above are UK/EU-derived; **[ZA]/[NA]** local manufacturers commonly use the same 720 mm carcass and 150 mm plinth, but this was not confirmed against a South African or Namibian manufacturer's published standard.
- Hanging-rail clear heights are drawn from several design sources that do not fully agree; they are practical ranges, not a standard.
- Appliance aperture dimensions must be taken from each appliance's own installation drawing — no generic figure is safe.
