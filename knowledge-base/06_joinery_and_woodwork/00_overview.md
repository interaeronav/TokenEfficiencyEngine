# Joinery and woodwork — domain map and competence ladder

**Summary.** This domain covers everything between a standing tree and a fitted kitchen: wood as a material, the boards and hardware the trade actually buys, the joints and machines that convert them, the dimensional and ergonomic rules that govern fitted furniture, and the commercial apparatus of specifying, quoting and installing joinery. It is written for practice in Namibia and South Africa, where the timber supply, board brands and climate differ materially from the northern-hemisphere literature that dominates the craft. The immediate application is a residential joinery package — kitchen, scullery, dressing room, bedroom wardrobes, garage stores — but the underlying craft knowledge is treated at full depth.

## The domain map

**Material layer.** `01_wood-science.md` establishes why wood moves, how much, and what you must allow. `02_timber-species-southern-africa.md` is the species register actually usable from Windhoek or Cape Town, with CITES and Namibian permit status. `03_sheet-goods-and-boards.md` covers the engineered panels that carry 90 % of fitted-furniture work in the region — PG Bison, Sonae Arauco and imported Egger product.

**Process layer.** `04_joints-and-techniques.md` is the joint catalogue with strength, tolerance and selection logic. `05_machinery-and-tooling.md` sets out three shop scales — site kit, small shop, production shop — with tooling, feeds, speeds, sharpening and safety.

**Product layer.** `06_cabinetmaking-and-fitted-furniture.md` is the practical core: carcass systems, kitchen and wardrobe dimensions, scribing, installation sequence. `07_hardware-systems.md` gives real model designations and drilling patterns from Blum, Hettich, Grass, Salice, Häfele and Lamello. `08_finishes-and-adhesives.md` covers coatings and glues with hot-climate notes.

**Knowledge and commerce layer.** `09_masters-schools-and-learning.md` maps who to learn from. `10_specifying-and-quoting-joinery.md` turns craft into a priceable, contractible deliverable.

## Two different trades under one name

A great deal of confusion in briefing and pricing comes from treating "joinery" as one activity. It is two:

1. **Panel-based fitted furniture** — melamine-faced board, 32 mm system, edgebanded, machine-cut, assembled with dowels/confirmats/Lamello, hung on European hardware. Accuracy comes from the saw and the boring pattern, not from the hand. This is the kitchen, scullery, wardrobe and store work.
2. **Solid-timber joinery and furniture** — frame-and-panel, mortise and tenon, dovetails, movement allowance, seasoning, finishing schedules. Accuracy comes from stock preparation and from understanding the material's seasonal behaviour.

Most residential packages are 80 % the first and 20 % the second (solid doors, worktops, feature shelving, mouldings, scribes). The two demand different machines, different tolerances and different pricing bases. Where a file's guidance applies to only one, it says so.

> ⚠️ Mixing the two carelessly is the commonest failure mode: a solid-timber door in a melamine carcass built to a 3 mm reveal will bind in February and gap in September if the door was not detailed for movement.

## Competence ladder — apprentice to master joiner

The rungs below are a synthesis of the German `Lehrling → Geselle → Meister` progression, the Namibian and South African trade-test routes, and the studio-training tradition. **[NA]** Namibian entry is normally Grade 10 plus an apprenticeship, cooperative or vocational-trainee route through a VTC (Windhoek, Valombola, Eenhana, Okakarara, Zambezi) or a COSDEC, with joiner and cabinetmaker recognised as designated trades. **[ZA]** South African routes run through FP&M SETA registered providers such as Furntech, whose National Certificate: Furniture Making (Wood) sits at NQF 2 (SAQA 49091) and NQF 3 (SAQA 49105).

**Rung 1 — Apprentice / learner (0–18 months).** Machine safety and guarding. Reads a tape and a rule to 1 mm. Squares, marks out, cuts to a line. Sands to schedule. Loads and unloads sheets without damaging faces. Understands why grain direction matters. Can edge-band straight edges cleanly and trim flush.

**Rung 2 — Improver (18 months – 3 years).** Sets and operates table saw, panel saw, planer/thicknesser, bandsaw and edgebander competently. Cuts accurate housings, rebates and dados. Bores 32 mm system lines from a jig or line-borer. Assembles carcasses square to ±1 mm on the diagonal. Fits concealed hinges and drawer runners to a drilling pattern without adjustment gymnastics.

**Rung 3 — Journeyman joiner (3–6 years).** Works from a cutting list they produced themselves. Sets a spindle moulder safely with limited-projection tooling and a power feed. Cuts and fits mortise-and-tenon and dovetail joinery by machine and by hand. Scribes to out-of-plumb walls and out-of-level floors. Installs a kitchen single-handed and gets the worktop joints tight. Understands moisture content and will refuse timber that is wet.

**Rung 4 — Setter-out / shop foreman (6–10 years).** Converts an architect's drawing into shop drawings, a cutting list, a hardware schedule and a nesting plan. Calculates board yield and waste. Sets and calibrates every machine in the shop and diagnoses a cut from its surface. Manages sequencing so that a site programme is met. Prices work and knows the labour hours per item type.

**Rung 5 — Master joiner (10 years +).** Designs the construction, not just the object: chooses joint systems for the loads, the material and the shop's capability. Understands finishing chemistry, adhesive selection and hot-climate application. Can build in solid timber that survives a 25 %-RH swing. Trains others and can defend the work in a technical dispute. In the German system this is formalised as the Meisterprüfung, whose Meisterstück (master piece) is the crowning practical component and whose certificate is a legal precondition for opening a joinery business.

**Rung 6 — Craft author.** Contributes to the craft: original construction methods, teaching, or a body of work that others study. The lineage in `09_masters-schools-and-learning.md` — Krenov, Maloof, Nakashima, Frid, the Barnsley tradition, the miyadaiku — sits here.

## How to use this domain with an active work package

For a kitchen/scullery/dressing-room/wardrobe/garage-store package, the reading order is:

1. `06_cabinetmaking-and-fitted-furniture.md` — fix the dimensional grid first.
2. `07_hardware-systems.md` — choose hardware **before** finalising carcass sizes; drawer systems dictate internal widths and cabinet depths.
3. `03_sheet-goods-and-boards.md` — pick substrate and edging; check thicknesses against hardware drilling.
4. `10_specifying-and-quoting-joinery.md` — write the schedule and price it.
5. `08_finishes-and-adhesives.md` — for painted or timber-finished elements, and for hot-climate glue-up planning.
6. `01`, `02`, `04`, `05` as the solid-timber and shop-capability content demands.
