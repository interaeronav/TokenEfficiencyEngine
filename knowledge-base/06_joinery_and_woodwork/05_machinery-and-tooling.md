# Machinery and tooling for a joinery shop

**Summary.** Three shop scales cover almost all joinery work: a **site/hand kit** built around a tracksaw and cordless tools; a **small shop** of five or six core machines that can build a whole kitchen; and a **production shop** with a beam saw, automatic edgebander and CNC nesting router. The step changes are not in the machines but in the *reference systems* — a tracksaw references a rail, a table saw references a fence, a CNC references a datum corner — and in extraction, which is the single most under-specified item in small shops.

## Key facts

- **Rim (cutting) speed:** `V (m/s) = π × D (mm) × n (rpm) / 60 000`. A 300 mm blade at 4 000 rpm runs at **62.8 m/s** — inside the usual 50–80 m/s working band for TCT blades on wood and board.
- **Duct transport velocity:** target **~20 m/s (4 000 fpm)** in branch runs carrying chips; below ~15 m/s chips drop out and block the duct.
- **Extraction volume per machine (minimum → recommended):** table saw 350 → 400 cfm (≈595 → 680 m³/h); bandsaw 350 → 400; jointer/planer 150–200 mm 350 → 450; thicknesser 300 mm 400 → 450; thicknesser 380 mm+ 450 → 550; drum sander 550 → 600+; router table 300 → 350. Minimum flow by duct diameter at 20 m/s: 100 mm ≈ 350 cfm, 125 mm ≈ 545 cfm, 150 mm ≈ 785 cfm.
- **Size the extractor at 1.5–2× the largest single machine demand** to cover duct losses.
- **EVA edgebanding glue** runs at 170–200 °C, 120–200 g/m²; **PUR** at 120–140 °C, 80–150 g/m². Open time ~30–45 s for both.

> ⚠️ The spindle moulder is the machine that maims. UK HSE identifies the two hazards as contact with the tool and ejection of the tool or workpiece. Only use tooling marked **MAN** (hand-feed rated) with limited cutter projection to **EN 847-1**, always use a power feed where the work allows, and use false fences, tunnel guards and jigs for grooving and stopped work.

## 1. Scale one — site / hand kit

The minimum viable kit for installing and adapting fitted joinery on site, and for building simple work.

| Tool | Spec that matters |
|---|---|
| Plunge tracksaw + rails | 160–165 mm blade, 1.4 m + 3 m rails, splinterguard, riving knife |
| Cordless drill/driver + impact driver | 18 V, plus a Ø35 mm Forstner for hinge cups |
| Cordless router / trim router | 8 mm collet minimum for anything but trimming |
| Jigsaw with clean-cut blades | for sink and hob cut-outs |
| Random orbital sander, 125/150 mm | with extraction |
| Biscuit joiner or Domino DF 500 | site carcass and mitre work |
| Compound mitre saw | 250–305 mm, negative-hook blade |
| Hand tools | block, smoothing and shoulder planes; chisels 6/12/19/25 mm; marking gauge; 600 mm and 1 200 mm straightedge; 300 mm try square; digital angle finder; profile gauge; scribing compass |
| Levels | 600 mm and 1 800 mm spirit; self-levelling cross-line laser |
| Clamps | 8 × 900 mm F-clamps minimum, plus edge/face clamps for scribes |
| M-class vacuum | 20+ litre, auto start, HEPA |

The 32 mm system can be worked entirely from this kit using a **shelf-pin/system-hole jig** (Festool LR 32 or equivalent) referenced off the panel edge.

## 2. Scale two — small shop

Adds the ability to produce accurate parts from raw stock and full sheets.

- **Panel saw (sliding table saw)** — 2.6–3.2 m carriage, 300–315 mm blade, **scoring unit** (essential for melamine), rip fence to 1 250 mm, digital fence readout. This is the single highest-value machine in a fitted-furniture shop.
- **Surface planer/jointer + thicknesser** (or a combination machine) — 300–410 mm width, spiral/helical cutterblock preferred for interlocked-grain species (kiaat, saligna, meranti) and for much lower noise.
- **Spindle moulder** — 30 mm spindle minimum, 1 500–10 000 rpm variable, sliding table, tilting spindle useful; **power feed (4-roller, variable speed) is not optional**.
- **Bandsaw** — 400–500 mm wheels, 300 mm+ resaw capacity, 20 mm blade for resawing, 6–10 mm for curves.
- **Hollow-chisel mortiser** or slot mortiser.
- **Edgebander** — a manual/portable bander for small shops (hot-air or cartridge type); a compact automatic bander with end-trim, flush-trim, scraper and buffing gives a genuinely different quality of edge.
- **Sanders** — wide-belt or drum sander (600 mm+) transforms panel work; plus random-orbit and an oscillating edge sander.
- **Drill press + line boring jig**, or a 21-spindle line borer for 32 mm work.
- **Extraction** — bag/cartridge filter unit of at least 2 000–3 000 m³/h with 150 mm mains and 100 mm branches, plus a separate M-class vac for portables.
- **Spray booth or extracted spray area** — see `08_finishes-and-adhesives.md`.

## 3. Scale three — production shop

- **Beam saw (horizontal panel saw)** — programmable, scoring, automatic pusher; cuts a full book of sheets to an optimised pattern.
- **CNC nesting router** — 3-axis minimum, 1 300 × 2 500 mm or larger table (sized for **2750 × 1830 mm** local sheets — a 1 250 × 2 500 mm European table will not take a local sheet), vacuum bed, drill bank with 32 mm-spaced spindles, tool changer. Replaces the line borer, the dado head and most jigs.
- **Automatic edgebander** — pre-milling, PUR/EVA, corner rounding, flush and profile trim, scraper, buff. Pre-milling is the feature that makes the difference on saw-cut edges.
- **Case clamp / press** for square assembly.
- **Wide-belt sander**, 1 100–1 300 mm, two-head.
- **Moulder (four/five-sided planer)** if solid mouldings are a business line.
- **Centralised extraction** with a briquetting or silo system and make-up air.

## 4. Saw blade selection

**Tooth grind:**
- **FTG (flat top)** — ripping solid wood with the grain; chisel action, fast, poor across grain.
- **ATB (alternate top bevel)** — the general-purpose and crosscut grind; slices fibres before removing waste.
- **TCG (triple chip)** — chamfered tooth alternating with a flat raker; laminates, HPL, plastics, aluminium, and heavy melamine work. Longest life on abrasive material.
- **Hi-ATB (25–40° bevel)** — best possible finish on melamine and veneer, shortest life, intolerant of anything hard.

**Tooth count (300 mm blade, scale from a 250 mm reference):**

| Application | 250 mm blade | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ripping solid timber | 24–30 T FTG/ATB | large gullets clear stringy chips |
| General site/framing | 40 T ATB | compromise |
| Crosscutting hardwood | 60–80 T ATB | supports fibres |
| Melamine, veneered ply, HPL | 80–100 T Hi-ATB or TCG | scoring blade also required |
| Non-ferrous / aluminium trim | 80–100 T TCG, negative hook | |

**Hook angle:** +15° to +20° for table-saw ripping and hand-held circular saws; +5° to +10° general purpose; 0° to −5° for mitre saws and sliding compound saws; −5° to −6° for aluminium and radial-arm saws.

> ⚠️ Fitting a high positive-hook blade to a sliding mitre saw makes the head climb the workpiece and pull toward the operator. Match the hook to the machine, not to the material alone.

**Diagnosing cuts:** burning while ripping usually means *too many teeth* (clogged gullets), not a dull blade. Chipping on the underside of melamine means the scoring blade is set too narrow or too shallow. Chipping on top means the blade is dull or the tooth count is too low. A tapering cut means the fence is not parallel to the blade.

**Scoring blade setup:** set the scorer **0.1–0.2 mm wider** than the main blade kerf, centred on it, and 1.5–2 mm deep. Re-check after every scorer sharpening.

## 5. Cutter and tooling selection

- **Spindle moulder tooling** must be marked with the maker, the permitted speed range, the dimensions and, for hand-fed use, **MAN**. Limited-projection (chip-limiter) blocks restrict the bite each cutter can take and are the legal and practical default for hand-fed work.
- **Cutterblock types:** solid profiled blocks (safest, one profile), limiter blocks with replaceable knives (flexible), and Euro/serrated-back blocks. Never use loose knives in an unlimited-projection block by hand.
- **Router cutters:** 8 mm or 12 mm shank for anything structural; up-cut spirals for slots that must clear chips, down-cut spirals for a clean top face on melamine, compression spirals for two-sided board (the one to use on a CNC nesting job).
- **Planer knives vs helical heads:** helical/spiral heads with indexable carbide inserts cut noise by roughly 10 dB, virtually eliminate tear-out on interlocked grain, and turn resharpening into rotating an insert — a strong choice given kiaat, saligna and meranti.

## 6. Feeds and speeds

- **Rim speed:** `V = π·D·n / 60 000` m/s. Keep TCT blades in the ~50–80 m/s band; below that the finish suffers, above it the blade heats and the carbide chips.
- **Feed per tooth (`fz`)** governs chip thickness: `feed rate (m/min) = fz (mm) × z (teeth) × n (rpm) / 1000`. For panel sizing, `fz` of roughly 0.05–0.15 mm/tooth gives a clean edge; heavier feeds tear, lighter feeds burnish and burn.
- **Spindle moulder:** 6 000–8 000 rpm for typical 120–140 mm blocks; large blocks (180 mm+) must be run slower — always inside the speed range marked on the tool.
- **Power feed:** 4–8 m/min for profiling in hardwood, faster in softwood. Two rollers before the cutter and one after, angled 2–3° into the fence.
- **Golden rule:** if the machine is bogging, reduce depth of cut before reducing feed rate. Reducing feed alone increases rubbing and burning.

## 7. Sharpening

- **Carbide (TCT) saw blades and cutters:** professional CNC grinding only. Budget for a rotating set — a blade in the shop, a blade at the grinder.
- **HSS planer knives:** jig-ground at 30–35°, honed on the back. Set with a magnetic knife-setting jig to the exact same projection on each knife or the cutterblock will hammer.
- **Chisels and plane irons:** grind at 25°, hone a 30° micro-bevel. Water stones (1 000 / 6 000), diamond plates or a slow-speed dry grinder with a fine CBN wheel. Flatten the back once, properly, and never again.
- **Hollow-chisel mortiser:** ream the chisel with a taper cone reamer; set the bit **0.4–0.8 mm** below the chisel points so the bit does not friction-weld into the chisel.
- **Scrapers:** the single most useful sharpening skill for interlocked-grain hardwood; burnish a hook at ~10–15°.

## 8. Setup and calibration

Do these in order, and re-do them after any machine is moved:

1. **Machine level and flat.** A twisted saw table or planer bed makes every downstream setting a lie.
2. **Table saw:** blade parallel to the slot/carriage within **0.05 mm** over the blade diameter; fence parallel or 0.05–0.1 mm open at the back; riving knife aligned to the blade plane; 90° and 45° stops set with a square, not the scale.
3. **Panel saw carriage:** parallel to the blade, and the crosscut fence square to the carriage travel — check with a 5-cut test, not a square.
4. **Surface planer:** outfeed table exactly level with the knife arc apex (a knife lifts a straightedge 2–3 mm); infeed sets depth of cut; fence square.
5. **Thicknesser:** bed rollers, pressure bar and chipbreaker set relative to the knife circle; table parallel to the cutterblock across the width.
6. **Spindle moulder:** fences co-planar (or offset deliberately for a full-width cut), guard and pressures set, power feed aligned, speed correct for the block.
7. **Bandsaw:** wheels coplanar, blade tracking on the crown, guides 0.1 mm clear, thrust bearing 0.5 mm behind the blade, table square, drift compensated on the fence.
8. **Edgebander:** glue temperature, glue volume, pressure roller alignment, trim knife height, and *board temperature* — cold board in a cold shop is the commonest cause of edge failure.

## 9. Machine safety

- **Guards on, always.** The crown guard and riving knife on a table saw prevent the two commonest injuries.
- **Push sticks and push blocks** at every machine, stored at the machine.
- **No hand-feeding shorter than 300 mm** on a planer or spindle moulder without a jig or carrier.
- **Spindle moulder:** MAN-marked, limited-projection tooling only for hand feed; power feed wherever the work permits; false fence, tunnel guard, jigs and stop blocks for grooving and stopped/dropped-on work; never start a cut against the direction of rotation (climb cut) by hand.
- **Isolate before adjusting.** Lock-off, not just "switched off".
- **No loose clothing, gloves or jewellery** at rotating machinery. Gloves at a spindle or bandsaw are more dangerous than bare hands.
- **Dust is a carcinogen.** Hardwood dust and MDF dust are respiratory sensitisers and carcinogens; extraction at source plus P2/FFP2 minimum, and an M-class vacuum, not a broom.
- **Noise:** planers, spindles and routers exceed 85 dB(A); provide and enforce hearing protection.
- **Training and supervision:** the HSE position — and good practice anywhere — is that spindle moulder and panel saw operation requires specific training and demonstrated competence, not general shop experience.

## Open questions

- The 50–80 m/s rim-speed band and the 0.05–0.15 mm feed-per-tooth range are widely used industry practice but were not confirmed against a manufacturer's published tooling handbook (Leitz, Leuco or CMT) in this research pass.
- Extraction figures are converted from a US cfm reference; verify against the machine manufacturer's stated extraction port volume and static pressure requirement before sizing a system.
- No specific SCM, Felder or Altendorf model specifications were verified; machine recommendations here are generic by capability, not by model.
