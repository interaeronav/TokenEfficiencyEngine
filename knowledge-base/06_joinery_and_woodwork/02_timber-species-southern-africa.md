# Timber species available and used in southern Africa

**Summary.** Practical species choice in Namibia and South Africa is narrower than the catalogue suggests. Structural and painted work runs on plantation SA pine; commodity furniture on saligna and meranti; premium local work on kiaat (mukwa) and, less often, Zambezi teak; imported American white oak and ash have taken most of the high-end furniture market. Every indigenous Namibian hardwood of commercial interest is a protected species under the Forest Regulations 2015 and needs harvest, transport, marketing and export licences, and *Pterocarpus* is additionally CITES Appendix II. Prices below are indicative bands only and must be re-quoted.

## Key facts

- **[NA]** Harvesting, transporting, selling, marketing, transiting, exporting or importing forest produce without a valid licence is prohibited (Forest Regulations 2015, reg. 12(1)). 106 protected plant species are listed in Annexure 2, including *Pterocarpus angolensis*, *Baikiaea plurijuga*, *Colophospermum mopane*, *Combretum imberbe* and *Adansonia digitata*.
- **[NA]** Indicative licence fees (Forest Regulations 2015): commercial harvesting permit 3 months **N$60**; communal harvesting 7 days **N$20**; own-use harvesting 3 days **N$10**; commercial transport permit 7 days **N$20**; export of value-added product **N$20/tonne** up to 10 t; export of raw product **N$50/tonne** up to 10 t; protected living tree, commercial **N$200**. Export of *unprocessed* forest produce is not permitted without the Director's authorisation.
- **CITES:** *Pterocarpus* spp. (all species, including *P. angolensis*) were listed on **Appendix II with annotation #17** effective **23 February 2023**, alongside *Afzelia* spp. and *Khaya* spp. *Dalbergia* spp. have been on Appendix II under annotation #15 since 2 January 2017. Annotation #17 covers logs, sawn wood, veneer sheets, plywood and transformed wood.
- Main Namibian harvest regions: Kavango East, Kavango West (together ~34 % of national standing wood stock) and Zambezi.

> ⚠️ A Namibian joinery contract that specifies kiaat must budget time and money for the permit chain, and any cross-border movement of kiaat components — including a finished wardrobe shipped to South Africa — is a CITES Appendix II export. Get the CITES export permit before the item leaves the shop, not after.

## Species register

### Plantation softwood

**SA pine — *Pinus patula*, *P. elliottii*, *P. taeda*.** Density 575 kg/m³ @12 % MC (*patula*); Janka ~2 450 N; shrinkage R 4.1 / T 7.9 / V 12.6 %, T/R 1.9. Non-durable to perishable — treat anything that will be damp. Works easily with hand and machine tools, glues and finishes well, but resin pockets bleed through lacquer and earlywood tears if cutters are dull. Available everywhere in South Africa and Namibia as sawn stock and as finger-jointed, laminated panel. **Use:** carcass framing, plinths, painted joinery, drawer sides, shop jigs, finger-jointed panel for painted doors and shelving. **Availability:** universal. **Indicative price band:** lowest of all — the base against which everything else is compared.
Grading in SA runs on SANS grade rules for structural timber; for joinery ask for **clear/select** or "furniture grade" and expect a large price step over builders' grade.

### Eucalypts

**Saligna / rose gum — *Eucalyptus grandis* (marketed locally as "saligna", historically *E. saligna*).** Density 640 kg/m³; Janka 5 600 N; shrinkage R 5.9 / T 10.1 / V 15.5 %, T/R 1.7. Rare Woods SA describe it as "difficult to dry, and the local wood not very durable, but it is easy to machine and finish" and "a well priced timber species". Durable to very durable above ground indoors, poor termite resistance. **Use:** benches, laboratory and school furniture, flooring, utility worktops, painted or stained furniture, solid worktops where a cheap dense hardwood is wanted. **Watch:** the highest movement of any species in common use here — 6–7 mm on a 500 mm flat-sawn panel across a normal Windhoek seasonal swing. Quarter-saw or laminate narrow strips. **Availability:** good in South Africa, imported into Namibia. **Price band:** low-to-mid; the cheapest true hardwood.

### Indigenous / regional hardwoods

**Kiaat, mukwa, bloodwood — *Pterocarpus angolensis*.** The premium Namibian furniture timber. Density 605 kg/m³; Janka 6 050 N; shrinkage R 1.7 / T 2.8 / V 5.4 %, T/R 1.6 — exceptionally stable, which is why it dominates local solid-wood furniture. Durable to moderately durable, good insect resistance in heartwood (sapwood is not). Works well by hand and machine; interlocked grain tears on planing — take light cuts or scrape. Glues, stains and finishes well. Golden-brown darkening with age. IUCN near threatened; **CITES Appendix II (#17) since Feb 2023**; **[NA]** protected species requiring the full permit chain. **Use:** doors, solid tops, feature shelving, stair parts, drawer fronts, turned and carved work. **Availability:** legally harvested Namibian stock is limited and increasingly scrutinised; a lot of "kiaat" on the market originates in Angola or Zambia. Demand documentation. **Price band:** high — several multiples of saligna.

**Zambezi teak, Rhodesian teak — *Baikiaea plurijuga*.** Density 890 kg/m³; Janka 13 300 N; shrinkage R 2.6 / T 4.5 / V 6.9 %. Very durable, good insect resistance in heartwood. High silica content severely blunts cutting edges — budget for carbide and for resharpening. Glues, turns and finishes well and is dimensionally very stable in service. IUCN near threatened; not CITES-listed; **[NA]** protected species. **Use:** flooring (its classic use), thresholds, worktops, millwork, heavy-wear surfaces. **Availability:** limited, mostly as flooring stock. **Price band:** mid-to-high.

**Mopane — *Colophospermum mopane*.** Very dense, very hard, extremely durable, dark chocolate heartwood with narrow pale sapwood. **[NA]** protected species. Mostly used locally for turnery, small furniture, carving, poles and charcoal rather than cabinet work; sizes are small and drying is difficult. **Price band:** low as raw material, high as usable furniture stock because of conversion losses.

**African rosewood / false mopane — *Guibourtia coleosperma* (ushiyi).** One of the three principal Namibian commercial indigenous timbers alongside kiaat and Zambezi teak, per TRAFFIC. **[NA]** protected. Note that the CITES Appendix II *Guibourtia* listing covers *G. demeusei*, *G. pellegriniana* and *G. tessmannii* (bubinga) — **not** *G. coleosperma* — but this should be re-verified against the current appendices before any export.

**Wild syringa (*Burkea africana*), silver cluster-leaf (*Terminalia sericea*), sickle bush (*Dichrostachys cinerea*)** — secondary Namibian species used locally, principally for poles, fencing, charcoal and small craft items rather than joinery.

**Camelthorn (*Vachellia/Acacia erioloba*) and leadwood (*Combretum imberbe*)** — both protected in Namibia. Occasionally used for slabs, turnery and feature furniture. Extremely hard, very slow-drying, very prone to checking. Treat as specialist material, not a joinery timber.

### Imported hardwoods in common trade

**Meranti (dark red / light red) — *Shorea* spp.** Density 675 kg/m³ (dark red); Janka ~3 560 N; shrinkage R 3.9 / T 7.8 / V 12.5 %. Moderately durable to non-durable, insect-susceptible. Interlocked grain tears when planed; otherwise glues, stains and finishes well; poor steam-bending. Not itself CITES-listed, but many *Shorea* species are critically endangered — ask for FSC/PEFC. **Use:** the default South African window, door and frame timber; also mouldings, painted joinery and carcass work. Sold in a well-defined range of stock sections, which is much of its appeal. **Price band:** mid.

**American white oak — *Quercus alba*.** Density 755 kg/m³; Janka 5 990 N; shrinkage R 5.6 / T 10.5 / V 16.3 %, T/R 1.9. Very durable, works well, steam-bends, glues and finishes well — but high movement, so quarter-sawn (rift/QS) is worth the premium for wide components. Tannin reacts with ferrous metals and with lime/cement: never leave steel clamps or screws in contact with damp oak, and keep it away from wet plaster. **Use:** the current default premium kitchen and furniture hardwood in South Africa. **Availability:** good via importers (kiln-dried, NHLA-graded FAS/1 Common). **Price band:** high.

**American white ash — *Fraxinus americana*.** Similar working properties to oak, lighter in colour, excellent for steam-bending and for painted or stained work; less durable and not for damp locations. **Price band:** high, usually a little under white oak.

**Imbuia — *Ocotea porosa*.** Density 660 kg/m³; Janka 4 300 N; shrinkage R 3.0 / T 6.4 / V 9.5 %, T/R 2.1. Durable, moderately insect-resistant, machines and finishes well, characteristic spicy scent, can irritate. IUCN vulnerable; not CITES-listed. Historically important in South African furniture; now scarce and mostly reclaimed. **Use:** restoration, veneer, feature panels. **Price band:** high and erratic.

**African blackwood — *Dalbergia melanoxylon*.** Density 1 270 kg/m³; Janka 16 320 N — among the densest commercial timbers. Very durable. Worked more like metal than wood; superb turning material. **CITES Appendix II** (genus-wide *Dalbergia* listing). **Use:** instrument parts, inlay, handles, small turned work — not cabinet stock. **Price band:** sold by the kilogram, not the cubic metre.

## Quick selection table

| Requirement | First choice | Alternative |
|---|---|---|
| Painted joinery, carcass framing | SA pine (finger-jointed) | Meranti |
| Cheap dense worktop or bench | Saligna | SA pine laminated |
| Stable solid doors, Namibian project | Kiaat (permits!) | Quarter-sawn white oak |
| Premium visible furniture, ZA project | White oak (QS) | Kiaat, ash |
| Windows and external doors | Meranti | Treated SA pine, saligna (treated) |
| High-wear floor or threshold | Zambezi teak | White oak |
| Turnery, small feature items | Mopane, blackwood | Kiaat offcuts |

## Buying practice

1. **Specify moisture content in the order**, not just the species: "kiln-dried to 7 % ± 2 % MC, equalised and conditioned, case-hardening test result to be supplied."
2. **Specify "heartwood only, no sapwood"** for kiaat, Zambezi teak and mopane — sapwood carries powder-post beetle.
3. **Specify the cut** where stability matters: "quarter-sawn/rift, 45–90° growth-ring angle" for wide panels and tops.
4. **Buy in the round volume you need plus 25–35 % for solid hardwood** — conversion losses on rough-sawn, waney-edged, variable-length stock are much higher than on board.
5. **Ask for the permit paper up front** on any Namibian indigenous timber, and keep copies with the job file; a CITES export permit cannot be issued retrospectively.
