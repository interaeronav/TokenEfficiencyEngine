# Alibaba business structure and financials, FY2026 and the June 2026 quarter

**Summary.** As of the quarter ended 30 June 2026 Alibaba reports in four segments — Alibaba E-commerce Group, AI Cloud and Compute Services, AI Labs and Applications, and All Others — having twice restructured its reporting in eighteen months. The familiar six-unit "1+6+N" framing from 2023 no longer describes the accounts. Group revenue for FY2026 (year ended 31 March 2026) was RMB 1,023,670 million (US$148,401 million), the first year above RMB 1 trillion, but operating income fell to RMB 50,150 million from RMB 140,905 million as AI investment landed. In the June 2026 quarter, cloud grew 45% and e-commerce grew 4%.

> ⚠️ **Alibaba's fiscal year ends 31 March.** FY2026 = 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026. Alibaba labels its quarters by calendar month ("June quarter 2026" = the three months ended 30 June 2026), which is the *first* quarter of its fiscal 2027. Every figure below states which period it belongs to.

## The segment structure — three versions in three years

Reading Alibaba's accounts requires knowing which structure a figure belongs to.

**Version A — "1+6+N", announced March 2023.** Six business groups: Taobao and Tmall Group; Alibaba International Digital Commerce Group (AIDC); Cloud Intelligence Group; Cainiao Smart Logistics Network; Local Services Group; Digital Media and Entertainment Group. This is the structure most secondary sources still describe. It is obsolete.

**Version B — FY2026 reporting (the 20-F filed May 2026).** During fiscal 2026 Taobao and Tmall Group, Ele.me and Fliggy were combined into **Alibaba China E-commerce Group**, and Cainiao, Amap and the media group (renamed Hujing Digital Media and Entertainment Group) were reclassified into **All others**. ## The businesses, one by one

### Alibaba E-commerce Group

Formed in the June 2026 quarter by combining Alibaba China E-Commerce Group, Alibaba International Digital Commerce Group, Freshippo, and certain commerce businesses from Cainiao. Four revenue lines.

**China E-commerce — Taobao and Tmall.** The historic core. **Taobao** is the C2C/long-tail marketplace; **Tmall** is the B2C platform for brands and authorised retailers. Monetisation is overwhelmingly advertising and search placement ("customer management revenue", CMR), not commission — which is why CMR is the number to watch. In the June 2026 quarter CMR fell 7% year over year to RMB 82,547 million; excluding a contra-revenue effect from a new business development programme, Alibaba says it would have grown 1% like-for-like. Alibaba attributes the slowdown to "weaker transaction activities" while noting that monthly active consumers on the Taobao app rose because of quick commerce.

**88VIP**, the top-tier paid loyalty programme, reached approximately **64 million members** at 30 June 2026, growing at double-digit percentages year over year. It links Taobao, Taobao Instant Commerce, Fliggy and Amap.

**China Quick Commerce.** The fastest-growing line and the most expensive. Comprises **Taobao Instant Commerce** (on-demand delivery, launched 2025 out of the Ele.me capability), **Freshippo** (Hema, the fresh-grocery chain), and Tmall Supermarket's on-demand business. Revenue grew 45% to RMB 53,295 million in the June 2026 quarter, reported net of subsidies treated as contra revenue. Alibaba reports improving unit economics quarter over quarter — higher average order value, better fulfilment efficiency, a mix shift toward high-value food and non-food — while maintaining share. This is a direct assault on **Meituan**'s home market and the reason group margins compressed through FY2026.

**International E-commerce.** **AliExpress** (global cross-border retail), **Trendyol** (Türkiye — the largest single contributor by some measures), **Lazada** (South-East Asia), **Daraz** (South Asia, principally Pakistan and Bangladesh). Revenue was RMB 27,761 million in the June 2026 quarter, down 1% year over year. The notable item: **AliExpress achieved operating profit in that quarter** for the first time, driven by logistics optimisation and cost efficiency, with a rising proportion of sales from local (in-market) supply rather than China-shipped parcels. The competitive set here is PDD's **Temu**, **Shein**, and Amazon; the structural pressure is the erosion of de minimis import exemptions in the US and EU, which raised the cost of the direct-from-China model and forced the "local-to-local" shift the 20-F describes.

**Global Wholesale.** **Alibaba.com**, the original B2B marketplace, plus 1688 domestically. RMB 13,906 million in the June 2026 quarter, +7%. Small, profitable, unglamorous, and the oldest business in the group.

**Competitive position.** Alibaba's Chinese retail share has been eroding for a decade against **PDD Holdings** (Pinduoduo domestically, Temu internationally), **JD.com** (self-operated logistics and electronics), and **Douyin/ByteDance** e-commerce (livestream and short-video commerce, which took a large share of discovery-driven purchasing). Precise current market-share percentages are `needs-verification` — Alibaba's 20-F does not name competitors or quantify share, and third-party estimates vary widely by methodology.

### AI Cloud and Compute Services

Formed in the June 2026 quarter by combining **Cloud Intelligence Group** with **T-Head**, the chip design subsidiary — an explicit statement that Alibaba now regards silicon and cloud as one product.

- Revenue in the quarter ended 30 June 2026: **RMB 48,437 million (US$7,139 million)**, with total and external revenue growth both accelerating to **45%**.
- **AI-related product revenue: RMB 12,376 million (US$1,824 million)** — the twelfth consecutive quarter of triple-digit year-over-year growth. That is roughly 26% of segment revenue.
- Adjusted EBITA RMB 5,628 million, up 133%, a 12% EBITA margin.
- FY2026 (full year, on the old Cloud Intelligence Group basis): RMB 158,132 million, +34%.
- Market position: ranked **first in China's AI cloud market with 38.1% share** (Omdia, "AI Cloud Market: China – 2025"). The FY2026 20-F cites a 35.8% figure, so the share is rising on the measure Alibaba chooses to quote.
- Customer base: served approximately **67% of A-share listed companies in China** in FY2026.

### AI Labs and Applications

Also new in the June 2026 quarter: the Qwen model labs, the **Qwen Consumer Business Group** (the Qwen app), and **QwenWork** were pulled out of "All others" and consolidated. Revenue RMB 3,338 million (+16%) against adjusted EBITA of **negative RMB 13,861 million**. This is the R&D and land-grab line.

- **Qwen app** — consumer AI assistant, launched November 2025, integrated with Taobao and Tmall, Taobao Instant Commerce, Fliggy, Damai, Amap and Alipay. Alibaba reports 250 million users have had a first AI-driven shopping experience through its agentic features.
- **QwenWork** — enterprise "AI-native workforce agent" platform, distributed through Alibaba Cloud and DingTalk.
- **Wukong** — the enterprise agentic platform named in the FY2026 20-F.
- **Qoder** — the agentic coding product.

### All Others

Now mainly **Alibaba Health**, **Hujing Digital Media and Entertainment Group** (Youku, Alibaba Pictures, Damai), **Amap** (AutoNavi — mapping and navigation, and increasingly a local-services front end), **Lingxi Games**, **DingTalk**, and residual technology businesses. RMB 28,803 million in the June 2026 quarter, +1%, adjusted EBITA negative RMB 3,343 million.

**Cainiao**, the logistics network, was reclassified during FY2026 — its commerce-facing operations moved into the E-commerce Group and the remainder sits here. Cainiao operates cross-border fulfilment, the domestic parcel network partnership model, and international express; its planned IPO was withdrawn in March 2024.

**Ele.me**, the food-delivery business, was folded into the China E-commerce structure during FY2026 and its capability became the basis of Taobao Instant Commerce.

### Ant Group — affiliated, not consolidated

Alibaba holds a **33% equity interest in Ant Group on a fully diluted basis**, received in September 2019 under the amended Share and Asset Purchase Agreement (SAPA). Ant is accounted for under the equity method, so its results appear in Alibaba's share of results of equity method investees, not in revenue — and its performance is an explicit source of quarterly volatility named in Alibaba's own risk factors.

Ant operates **Alipay** (the payment and escrow platform, which provides payment services to Alibaba's marketplaces under a long-term commercial agreement), consumer credit, wealth management (Yu'e Bao), insurance distribution, and technology services. Since the November 2020 IPO suspension it has been restructured as a financial holding company under PBOC supervision. The commercial relationships between Alibaba and Ant are extensive and disclosed as related-party transactions: the Alipay commercial agreement, an intellectual property licence agreement (IPLA) with a September 2019 cross-licence, a shared services agreement, an SME loan cooperation framework, a trademark agreement, and cloud computing and marketplace software services supplied by Alibaba to Ant.

Ant's current valuation and financials are `needs-verification` — it is private and Alibaba does not disclose them separately.

## Corporate structure notes

**VIE structure.** Alibaba is a Cayman Islands holding company. Certain PRC businesses — notably those requiring licences restricted to domestic ownership — are operated through variable interest entities controlled by contract, not equity. The 20-F names Taobao (China) Software Co., Ltd., Zhejiang Tmall Technology Co., Ltd. and Hangzhou AliCloud Apsara Information Technology Co., Ltd. as the relevant subsidiary counterparties in the enhanced VIE arrangements. This is the structure at the heart of the 2010–2011 Alipay dispute and remains a standing risk factor.

**Listings.** NYSE (BABA, since 19 September 2014) and Hong Kong Stock Exchange (9988 HKD counter, 89988 RMB counter). One ADS = eight ordinary shares.

**Capital returns and raises.** In the quarter ended 30 June 2026 Alibaba repurchased 13.4 million ordinary shares (≈1.7 million ADSs) for US$162 million — a very small buyback by its historic standards, consistent with capital being redirected to capex. On 23–24 August 2026 it announced and priced an **HK$80 billion placing of new shares** in Hong Kong. Other bank borrowings stood at RMB 71,774 million (US$10,405 million) at 31 March 2026, "primarily used for our capital expenditures and other working capital purposes."

**Seasonality.** The fourth calendar quarter (Alibaba's fiscal Q3) is the largest, driven by the 11.11 Global Shopping Festival and merchants' marketing budget allocation. The first calendar quarter is the smallest because of Chinese New Year.

## How to read Alibaba's numbers

1. **Adjusted EBITA is the segment metric management steers by** — it excludes share-based compensation, amortisation of intangibles, impairments and certain other items. Segment adjusted EBITA reconciles to consolidated adjusted EBITA, not to operating income.
2. **Customer management revenue is the quality-of-earnings line.** Direct sales and quick-commerce revenue are recorded gross with cost of inventory, so they inflate revenue and depress margin. A quarter where CMR falls 7% and total revenue rises 9% is a worse quarter than the headline suggests.
3. **Free cash flow, not net income, shows the capex.** Operating cash flow rose 11% year over year in the June 2026 quarter; free cash flow went RMB 26 billion further negative. The difference is the data centres.
4. **The convenience translation rate changes.** US$ figures in different filings use different RMB/US$ rates; compare RMB series when tracking growth.
