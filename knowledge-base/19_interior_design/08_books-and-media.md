# Interior design books, magazines and media — annotated

**Summary.** An annotated bibliography organised by what each source is genuinely *for*. The field's literature splits sharply: a **decorating canon** written by practitioners, largely about taste and judgement, which does not teach technique; and a **technical reference shelf**, which teaches technique and says nothing about taste. You need both, and almost nobody reads both.

> ⚠️ **Edition warning.** Titles and authors below are confirmed where a source is cited. **Publication years and edition numbers are given only where confirmed; where they are not confirmed they are omitted or marked.** Do not cite an edition number from this file without checking the publisher — technical references in particular are revised frequently and citing the wrong edition of Ching or Neufert will produce wrong numbers.

## 1. The founding texts

**Edith Wharton and Ogden Codman Jr., *The Decoration of Houses*.** The book that started the argument. Its thesis is that decoration is a branch of **architecture**, governed by proportion, symmetry and the logic of the plan, and that the Victorian upholsterer's room — draped, padded, cluttered — was a failure of reasoning. Read it for the argument, not the schemes. It is the intellectual ancestor of every "get the plan right first" position in file `05`.

**Elsie de Wolfe, *The House in Good Taste* (1913).** Confirmed as an "influential design manual that helped establish interior decoration as a legitimate profession." Written (with substantial ghostwriting by Ruby Ross Wood, who later employed Billy Baldwin) as both manual and manifesto: light, air, pale colour, chintz, mirrors and comfort against Victorian gloom. Read it as the founding document of the *profession*, and note that its author had no design training whatsoever (file `02`).

## 2. The practitioner canon — what taste sounds like from inside

**Billy Baldwin.** ***Billy Baldwin Decorates*** **(1972)**, ***Billy Baldwin Remembers*** **(1974)** and ***Billy Baldwin: An Autobiography*** **(1985, posthumous)** — all three confirmed. *Decorates* is the working book: room by room, with the reasoning made explicit. Baldwin is the best writer on **comfort as a design objective** and on why a room must accommodate a real evening rather than a photograph.

**David Hicks.** Wrote extensively — *David Hicks on Living — with Taste*, *David Hicks on Decoration* and successors — through the 1960s and 70s. **Titles and dates `needs-verification`.** He is the essential source on **geometric pattern, colour blocking, and the tablescape** — his term for the composed arrangement of objects on a surface, which is still the standard technique.

**Albert Hadley.** *Albert Hadley: The Story of America's Preeminent Interior Designer* and the Parish-Hadley volumes. Hadley taught at Parsons and it shows: his writing is the most *pedagogical* in the canon, focused on line, structure and the discipline of the plan. **Titles and dates `needs-verification`.**

**Mark Hampton.** ***Mark Hampton on Decorating*** **(1989)** and ***Legendary Decorators of the Twentieth Century*** **(1992)** — both confirmed. *On Decorating* is arguably the best single book on decorating judgement in English, written by the most academically trained decorator of his generation (art history MFA, NYU Institute of Fine Arts). *Legendary Decorators* is the field's own history of itself, illustrated with Hampton's watercolours, and is the best short course in the 20th-century canon available.

**Sister Parish.** *Sister Parish: The Life of the Legendary American Interior Designer* (by Apple Parish Bartlett and Susan Bartlett Crater) and *Parish-Hadley: Sixty Years of American Design*. Read for the **American country** vocabulary and for the anti-matching method described in file `05`. **Dates `needs-verification`.**

**Bunny Williams.** ***An Affair with a House*** is the standard, plus *On Garden Style* and later volumes. The best available book on **how a house accumulates** rather than how it is installed. **Dates `needs-verification`.**

**Miles Redd, Martyn Lawrence Bullard, Michael S. Smith, Kelly Wearstler.** The contemporary American monographs. Wearstler's are confirmed: ***Modern Glamour*** **(2004)**, ***Domicilium Decoratus*** **(2006)**, ***Hue*** **(2010)**, ***Rhapsody*** **(2012)**, ***Evocative Style*** **(2019)** — *Hue* is genuinely useful as a colour workbook rather than a portfolio.

**Nate Berkus.** ***Home Rules*** **(2005)** and ***The Things That Matter*** **(2012)**, a *New York Times* bestseller — both confirmed. *The Things That Matter* is the accessible statement of the "objects with histories" position (file `05`) and is the right book to give a client who does not know what they want.

## 3. Contemporary philosophy

**Ilse Crawford.** ***The Sensual Home*** **(1997)**, ***Home Is Where the Heart Is?*** **(2009)**, ***A Frame for Life: The Designs of StudioIlse*** **(2014)** — all three confirmed. *The Sensual Home* is the pivot: it reframed domestic design around the senses and well-being rather than style, and everything human-centred in the field since descends from it. If you read one contemporary book on interiors, read this one.

**Axel Vervoordt.** Five volumes confirmed: ***Portraits of Interiors***, ***Timeless Interiors***, ***Wabi Inspirations***, ***Living with Light***, ***Stories and Reflections***. **Publication years `needs-verification`.** *Wabi Inspirations* carries the philosophy; *Living with Light* is the most useful practically; *Timeless Interiors* is the best browsing volume. All are large-format image books — they teach by looking, not by instruction.

**Vincent Van Duysen.** A sequence of monographs from **2001** onward (confirmed from his site), plus special issues to 2025. Read for the resolution of **junctions and materials** — his details are the current reference standard for restrained luxury.

**Rose Uniacke.** A monograph on her London house and work exists; **title and date `needs-verification`.** Her value is the demonstration of extreme restraint combined with serious antiques.

**Kelly Hoppen.** Thirteen books, beginning with ***East Meets West: Global Design for Contemporary Interiors*** **(1997)** — confirmed — and including ***Kelly Hoppen Design Masterclass*** **(2013)**. The taupe-and-neutral East-West vocabulary she codified dominated 1990s–2000s commercial residential design. *Masterclass* is a genuine how-to and is the best of them for a learner.

**Faye Toogood.** Studio monographs and exhibition catalogues; the intellectual position is best read in interviews. Her stated scepticism about adding objects to the world (file `05`) is the most useful thing in her writing.

## 4. The technical reference shelf

These are the books that make you competent. Buy the current edition; check before citing.

**Francis D. K. Ching, *Interior Design Illustrated*.** The indispensable one. Ching's hand-drawn diagrammatic method teaches spatial thinking, construction, systems, lighting and human factors simultaneously. Also: ***Architectural Graphics*** (drawing conventions), ***Building Construction Illustrated*** (how buildings go together), ***A Visual Dictionary of Architecture*** (vocabulary). **Editions `needs-verification`.** If you buy one book from this file, buy *Interior Design Illustrated*.

**Julius Panero and Martin Zelnik, *Human Dimension and Interior Space*.** The anthropometric source book: percentile-based human dimensions applied to every interior situation. Old but not superseded. **Edition/date `needs-verification`.**

**Ernst Neufert, *Architects' Data*.** The German dimensional bible, now in many editions and languages. Broader than interiors and more authoritative on building types; the standard first check for "how big should this be".

**Joseph De Chiara, Julius Panero and Martin Zelnik, *Time-Saver Standards for Interior Design and Space Planning*.** The heavyweight American reference — space planning by building type, with detailed layouts. Expensive, worth it for anyone doing contract work. **Edition `needs-verification`.**

**Corky Binggeli, *Building Systems for Interior Designers*.** The single best explanation of HVAC, plumbing, electrical, fire protection, acoustics and lighting **written for interior designers rather than engineers**. This is the book that closes the most common competence gap in the profession.

**Maureen Mitton, *Interior Design Visual Presentation*.** How to draw, render, board, present and build a portfolio — the practical companion to Ching. Also *Residential Interior Design: A Guide to Planning Spaces* (Mitton and Nystuen), which is the best planning book for houses.

**Gary Gordon, *Interior Lighting for Designers*.** The standard lighting text for interiors: photometrics, calculation, sources, controls, and design method. Complements it: the **IES Lighting Handbook** (the professional reference, expensive) and **Sally Storey**'s more practical *Lighting Design* books for residential work.

**W. Otie Kilmer and Rosemary Kilmer, *Construction Drawings and Details for Interiors*.** The documentation standard — what a drawing set actually contains and how each sheet is drawn.

**Christine M. Piotrowski, *Professional Practice for Interior Designers*.** Contracts, fees, ethics, project administration, business structure. The main academic text behind file `10`.

## 5. Colour, textile and material references

**Colour.** The **Munsell Book of Color** (physical atlas, expensive, definitive); the **NCS Index** (the practical European specification tool); the **RAL** decks (K7 Classic for metalwork and spray finishes). Books: **Josef Albers, *Interaction of Color*** — the essential training in *seeing* colour relationships, and unmatched for the exercises; **Johannes Itten, *The Art of Color***; **Michel Pastoureau's** single-colour histories (*Blue*, *Black*, *Green*, *Red*, *Yellow*) for cultural meaning; **Kassia St Clair, *The Secret Lives of Colour*** for readable pigment history.

**Textiles.** **Susan Meller and Joost Elffers, *Textile Designs*** — the great pattern-history reference. **Judith Gura / Fiona Leslie** on historic textiles. For performance and specification, the mill's own technical data sheets and the standards named in file `06` are the actual references; there is no good single book.

**Materials.** **Chris Lefteri's** material series (*Wood*, *Metals*, *Plastics*, *Glass*, *Ceramics*) for properties and processes; **Material ConneXion**'s library for innovation; **Rudolf Ballmann / Manuel Kubitza** style material atlases. For timber specifically, see domain `06_joinery_and_woodwork`.

## 6. Magazines

| Title | Publisher / country | What it is genuinely for |
|---|---|---|
| ***The World of Interiors*** | Condé Nast, UK | The most serious interiors magazine in the world. Photographs real, often eccentric, unstyled-looking houses; strong on antiques, craft and unfashionable places. **The best single subscription for developing an eye.** Faye Toogood was an editor/stylist here. |
| ***Architectural Digest*** (US + international editions) | Condé Nast | The industry's status register (AD100, file `03`). AD France, AD Italia, AD Middle East and AD India are frequently better than the US edition. |
| ***Elle Decor*** / ***Elle Decoration*** | Hearst / Hearst UK | Accessible, trend-forward, commercially useful. **Ilse Crawford was the launch editor of *Elle Decoration*.** |
| ***Cabana*** | Italy | Biannual, maximalist, fabric-covered, obsessive about pattern, textile and the historic European interior. The antidote to minimalism. |
| ***Milk Decoration*** | France | Family and children's interiors done seriously; strong on colour and small spaces. |
| ***Casa Vogue*** | Condé Nast Italia/Brasil | Italian and Brazilian design; excellent on contemporary furniture and art-adjacent interiors. |
| ***Interni*** | Italy | The Milan industry journal — furniture, product, contract, Salone del Mobile coverage. The trade's technical magazine. |
| ***Frame*** | Netherlands | Spatial and retail design; runs the FRAME Awards (file `03`). |
| ***Dezeen*** (online) | UK | The volume publication of record for contemporary interiors; runs Dezeen Awards. |
| ***Visi*** **[ZA]** | South Africa | The best record of contemporary South African interiors, architecture and design. Essential for regional practice. |
| ***House and Leisure*** **[ZA]** | South Africa | South African homes and lifestyle; now primarily digital. **Current publication status `needs-verification`.** |
| ***Wallpaper**** | UK | Design, architecture and lifestyle; strong on product and travel. |
| ***AD PRO*** / trade newsletters | US | Business-of-design coverage — fees, contracts, the trade. Useful for file `10`. |

**How to use magazines properly:** do not collect images. Keep a physical or digital file organised by *problem* — "how to end a curtain at a bay window", "banquette details", "small bathroom plans", "brass patinas". A library organised by problem is usable; one organised by prettiness is not.

## 7. Documentaries, series and film

- ***Abstract: The Art of Design*** (Netflix) — **Ilse Crawford's episode is confirmed** and is the single best short introduction to the human-centred position in this domain. Other episodes (Bjarke Ingels, Paula Scher, Es Devlin) are relevant to spatial and graphic thinking.
- ***The World of Interiors*** and AD's **Open Door** video series (YouTube) — house tours; useful for reading plans and circulation from video.
- ***Queer Eye*** (Netflix) — Bobby Berk 2018–24, one Primetime Emmy from four nominations. Relevant as a study in **fast, low-budget, emotionally-led** domestic transformation, not as technical instruction.
- ***Million Dollar Decorators*** (Bravo, 2011–13) — Martyn Lawrence Bullard. A genuinely informative portrait of the **client relationship** in high-end decorating, whatever its production values.
- ***Dragons' Den*** (BBC Two, 2013–15) — Kelly Hoppen as a Dragon; relevant to file `10` on the business of design.
- **Films for interiors:** *The Draughtsman's Contract*, *Barry Lyndon* (candlelight and Georgian interiors), *A Single Man* (Tom Ford; mid-century), *Playtime* (Tati; modernist office space as comedy), *In the Mood for Love* (colour and texture), *Parasite* (plan and section as narrative), *Call Me by Your Name* (Italian domestic patina). Watch for **how the camera moves through a plan.**
- **Exhibitions as media:** Vervoordt's ***Artempo — Where Time Becomes Art*** (Venice, 2007) has a substantial catalogue and is worth pursuing second-hand.

## 8. Podcasts

The best of these are business and process rather than styling.
- ***The Business of Home Podcast*** (Dennis Scully) — the most useful in the field for money, fees, trade relationships and studio management. Read alongside file `10`.
- ***The Great Indoors*** (Sophie Robinson and Kate Watson-Smyth) — UK, accessible, practical decorating.
- ***Chaise Lounge*** (Nick May) — long-form interviews with working American designers on how they actually run projects.
- ***Design Matters*** (Debbie Millman) — design broadly, not interiors specifically, but the best interviewing in design media.
- ***Monocle on Design***, ***Dezeen's*** audio output — contemporary coverage.
- ***Clever*** (Amy Devers) — designers' formation stories; a good companion to file `02`.

## 9. Instagram, YouTube and online sources

**Use these for material and detail, not for schemes.** The algorithmic feed rewards the photogenic and punishes the technical; treat it as a sample library.
- **Studio accounts worth following** for detail rather than styling: Vincent Van Duysen, Studio KO, Rose Uniacke, Studioilse, Pierre Yovanovitch, India Mahdavi, Studio Ashby, Axel Vervoordt.
- **Southern African** **[ZA] [NA]**: *Visi*; Mabeo Furniture (Botswana); Houtlander, Dokter and Misses, Egg Designs, Ashanti Design, Mungo (South Africa). Follow makers, not stylists.
- **YouTube:** AD's *Open Door*; *The Local Project* (Australian, excellent architectural interiors with plans); *NEVER TOO SMALL* (small-space planning, genuinely instructive on circulation); *Nick Lewis* and *Aaron Christian* interviews.
- **Reference and archive:** museum collections online — the V&A (textiles, furniture, wallpaper), the Cooper Hewitt, the Met, the RIBA collections. These are free, high-resolution and correctly catalogued, which no social platform is.
- **Trade portals:** the Design Centre Chelsea Harbour (UK), the D&D Building and Decoration & Design Building trade showrooms (US), Salone del Mobile (Milan) — their exhibitor lists are the fastest way to learn who makes what.

## 10. A reading order for someone starting from nothing

1. **Ching, *Interior Design Illustrated*** — read cover to cover, redrawing the diagrams by hand.
2. **Crawford, *The Sensual Home*** — to establish why you are doing any of it.
3. **Hampton, *Mark Hampton on Decorating*** — for judgement.
4. **Panero & Zelnik** — as a reference, kept open on the desk.
5. **Binggeli, *Building Systems for Interior Designers*** — the competence gap closed.
6. **Albers, *Interaction of Color*** — and do the exercises, physically.
7. **Gordon, *Interior Lighting for Designers*** — once you have a real project.
8. **Piotrowski, *Professional Practice for Interior Designers*** — before you sign a contract.
9. **Mark Hampton, *Legendary Decorators of the Twentieth Century*** — the history, once the technique is in place.
10. **A year of *The World of Interiors*** — the eye.
