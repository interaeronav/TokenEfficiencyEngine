# The major firms — profiles with dated financials

**Summary.** Twenty-four company profiles covering the whole stack. Every financial figure carries the period it refers to. Where a current figure could not be verified from a primary or reliable secondary source in this research pass, the entry says so explicitly rather than guessing — several entries below are therefore incomplete on purpose.

> ⚠️ **Reading rule.** Semiconductor financials swing violently with the memory cycle and, since 2023, with the AI capex cycle. A figure more than two quarters old may be badly stale. Every number here is dated; check the date before using it.

## Foundry and manufacturing

### TSMC (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company)

**What it does.** The pure-play foundry model's inventor (1987) and the world's most important manufacturing company. Manufactures logic for essentially every leading fabless firm, plus advanced packaging (CoWoS, InFO, SoIC) that has become as strategically scarce as the wafers.

**Financials.** 2Q 2026: net revenue **US$40.20 bn**, gross margin **67.7%**, operating margin **60.3%**, at an assumed exchange rate of NT$31.60 per US$ (TSMC investor relations). FY2024: revenue **NT$2.9 trillion**, net income **NT$1,173 bn**, **83,800 employees**. Expected 2025 output: **15.02 million 12-inch-equivalent wafers**.

**Position.** Approximately **70%** of global foundry revenue, and a far higher share of leading-edge logic. **N2 entered volume production in 4Q 2025**; **A16 is production-ready in 2H 2026**. A gross margin near 68% at a company with this capital intensity is the clearest evidence of pricing power anywhere in hardware.

**Dependencies.** ASML (EUV, sole source), Zeiss optics, Japanese photoresist and mask blanks, electricity and water in Taiwan, and — existentially — the geopolitical status quo in the Taiwan Strait. Also on a handful of customers: NVIDIA and Apple together represent a very large share of leading-edge revenue.

**Diversification.** Fab 23 in Kumamoto, Japan (operational December 2024); Arizona (Phoenix, with a third fab funded by a US$6.6 bn CHIPS award in April 2024 targeted at 2 nm); Dresden, Germany (planned operational 2029). Reported investment of "over US$45 bn" in a 2 nm fab (2025).

### Samsung Electronics

**What it does.** Simultaneously the world's largest memory maker (DRAM and NAND), the number-two foundry, a display maker, and a consumer-electronics giant — a structure no competitor replicates.

**Financials.** Samsung's latest consolidated annual figures could not be verified in this pass. Verified data points: **Q1 2026 operating profit of approximately US$36.15 bn**, with reporting of a full-year 2026 profit expectation around US$200 bn (treat the latter as a projection, `needs-verification`). Samsung overtook Intel as the largest semiconductor company by revenue in 2017.

**Position.** Memory leadership is genuine and long-standing. Foundry is the persistent weak point: **SF3E was the industry's first GAA node in production (mid-2022)** and **SF2 has been in mass production since 2025** with reported yields of **50–60% into early 2026** — first to the technology, behind on the execution that customers buy.

**Dependencies.** ASML; the memory cycle; the Exynos/Qualcomm balance in its own phones; Korean domestic politics and its own governance history.

### Intel

**What it does.** x86 CPUs for client and server, discrete GPUs, and Intel Foundry — an attempt to become a merchant foundry while remaining an IDM.

**Financials.** FY2025: revenue **US$52.9 bn**, net **loss of US$0.3 bn**, total assets US$211.4 bn, **85,100 employees**.

**Position.** Technologically, 2025–2026 is the best news Intel has had in a decade: **18A is in high-volume manufacturing** with **RibbonFET GAA and PowerVia backside power** — genuinely ahead of TSMC on backside power — claiming +18% performance at iso-power, −38% power at iso-performance and +30% density versus Intel 3.1. Commercially, the picture is unresolved: Intel Foundry needs large external customers to justify 14A, and Intel has said as much publicly. The **US government took a 9.9% equity stake in August 2025**, converting US$11.1 bn of CHIPS money into shares.

**Dependencies.** ASML (Intel took the first High-NA EXE shipment in December 2023); external foundry customers; and its own ability to hold a process schedule, which is the specific thing it failed at from 2015 to 2021.

### SMIC

**What it does.** China's leading foundry, 350 nm down to a 7 nm-class process.

**Financials.** The most recent figures verified in this pass are stale: **2018 revenue US$3.6 bn, net profit US$149 m**. Current figures `needs-verification`.

**Position.** Established a **7 nm-class process on 21 July 2022** using DUV multi-patterning only, and was reported at "**N+3**" (a scaled evolution of its 7 nm class) as of December 2025. Manufactures Huawei's Kirin 9000s (7 nm, 2023), Kirin 9020 (7 nm, Q4 2024) and Kirin 9030 (Q4 2025), and the Ascend 910B/910C accelerators.

**Dependencies and constraints.** On the **US Entity List since December 2020**; designated a military end-user in September 2020; added to Taiwan's export-control list in June 2025. No EUV access. Every density step costs additional DUV exposures, which compounds cost, cycle time and yield loss (`06`).

### Rapidus

Covered in detail in `06`. Founded 10 August 2022; ¥802.5 bn (US$5.4 bn) of Japanese state support in FY2025 within a US$65 bn programme to 2030; IBM 2 nm nanosheet technology and imec partnership; pilot line from 1 April 2025; **237.31 MTr/mm² prototype on 18 July 2025**; mass production target 2H 2027 at Chitose, Hokkaido.

## Fabless and design

### NVIDIA

**What it does.** GPUs and, increasingly, whole AI systems — accelerators, NVLink fabric, networking (Mellanox), Grace CPUs, and the CUDA software stack.

**Financials.** FY2026: revenue **US$215 bn**, net income **US$120 bn**, **42,000 employees** (per Wikipedia's summary of NVIDIA's reporting). FY2025: revenue **US$130.5 bn**, net income **US$72.8 bn**. Became the **first company to reach a US$5 trillion market capitalisation on 29 October 2025** (it was US$3.66 tn on 7 January 2025).

**Architecture generations.** Ampere (announced 14 May 2020) → Hopper (H100) → **Blackwell** (announced 18 March 2024): GB100 die of **104 bn transistors** on TSMC custom **4NP**; B100/B200 pairs two dies (**208 bn transistors**) over a **10 TB/s NV-HBI** link with full cache coherence; GB200 delivers roughly **20 PFLOPS FP4** dense. Consumer GB202: **>92.2 bn transistors, 750 mm², 24,576 CUDA cores**. **Vera Rubin** announced at GTC in March 2026 as Blackwell's successor.

**The CUDA moat.** Not the hardware. It is the compiler, cuBLAS/cuDNN/CUTLASS/NCCL/TensorRT, the fact that every framework's default path is CUDA, and eighteen years of accumulated third-party code. Competitors must match not a chip but an ecosystem. NVIDIA's December 2025 licensing agreement with Groq (reported at ~US$20 bn) is a signal that it is also willing to buy adjacent inference technology rather than compete with it.

**Dependencies.** TSMC (N4/N3 class wafers *and* CoWoS packaging), SK Hynix/Samsung/Micron for HBM, and US export-control policy, which has repeatedly reshaped its China business.

### AMD

**Financials.** 2025: revenue **US$34.6 bn**, net income **US$4.335 bn**, total assets US$76.9 bn, **31,000 employees**. 2024: revenue US$25.7 bn, net income US$1.641 bn, 28,000 employees.

**Position.** The chiplet pioneer in volume — CPU core dies on the leading node paired with a mature-node IO die since Zen 2 (2019), and 3D V-Cache using TSMC SoIC hybrid bonding. Server CPU share has risen steadily against Intel. In AI accelerators the **MI400 series was unveiled in June 2025 for 2026**, and in **October 2025 AMD agreed to supply OpenAI with six gigawatts of AI processors over five years** — the single largest validation the Instinct line has received.

**Dependencies.** TSMC entirely; HBM supply; ROCm's ability to close the software gap with CUDA.

### Apple

**What it does.** Designs silicon exclusively for its own products — the deepest vertical integration in consumer electronics.

**The programme.** Apple bought P.A. Semi in 2008 and shipped the A4 in 2010. It holds an Arm **architecture licence**, so it designs its own cores rather than using Arm's. Current lineup:

| A17 Pro | TSMC N3B | 19 bn | 2P + 4E | — | 22 Sep 2023 |
| A18 | TSMC N3E | — | 2P + 4E, 5-core GPU | 60.0 GB/s | 9 Sep 2024 |
| A19 | TSMC N3P | — | 2P + 4E, 5-core GPU | 68.3 GB/s | 9 Sep 2025 |
| M1 | TSMC 5 nm | 16 bn | 4P + 4E | 68.25 GB/s | Nov 2020 |
| M2 | TSMC N5P | 20 bn | 4P + 4E | 102.4 GB/s | Jun 2022 |
| M3 | TSMC N3B | 25 bn | 4P + 4E | 102.4 GB/s | Nov 2023 |
| M4 | TSMC N3E | 28 bn | 3–4P + 4–6E | 120 GB/s | May 2024 |
| M5 | TSMC N3P | — | 3–4P + 4–6E | 153.6 GB/s | Oct 2025 |

**Design methodology.** Very wide, moderate-frequency out-of-order cores (the opposite of the x86 frequency race), unified memory shared by CPU/GPU/Neural Engine, aggressive fixed-function accelerators (media codecs, image signal processor, Neural Engine), and a single SoC family scaled from phone to workstation by adding blocks and, in the Ultra parts, by fusing two dies with a silicon interposer.

**Foundry relationship.** Apple is consistently TSMC's **lead customer on each new node**, taking first-year capacity at a premium. This is a genuine strategic asset: it means Apple ships on a node roughly a year before its competitors, and it gives TSMC a guaranteed anchor tenant for a new fab's ramp.

**Strategic logic.** Vertical integration lets Apple co-design silicon, OS and application frameworks, extract efficiency no merchant vendor can match for its specific workloads, and remove a supplier's margin. The cost is that Apple bears the full NRE of a leading-edge SoC family — which only its volumes and ASPs make affordable.

### Qualcomm

**What it does.** Snapdragon mobile SoCs (QCT) plus a patent-licensing business on cellular standards (QTL) that has historically produced most of the profit.

**Financials.** Fiscal year ended **28 September 2025**; specific revenue and net income figures could not be verified in this pass (`needs-verification`). Segment mix: **QCT ~80% of revenue, QTL ~19%, QSI <1%**. The licensing model earns roughly **US$20 per smartphone**, with royalties described as about 5% of device value or ~US$30 per device — characterised as 5–10× typical patent-holder rates, and the subject of two decades of litigation and regulatory action.

**Position.** Dominant in premium Android SoCs; expanding into automotive (Snapdragon Digital Chassis), PCs (Snapdragon X with the Oryon core from the Nuvia acquisition), and XR. The structural risk is customer in-housing — Apple has been building its own modem, and Samsung alternates between Exynos and Snapdragon.

### Broadcom

**What it does.** Two businesses under one roof: semiconductors (networking switches — Tomahawk/Jericho, custom AI ASICs, wireless, storage, broadband) and infrastructure software (CA, Symantec, VMware).

**Financials.** FY2023: revenue **US$35.8 bn**, net income **US$14.08 bn**. Segment mix as of 2025: **semiconductors 58%, infrastructure software 42%**. FY2024 and FY2025 figures — materially higher following the VMware acquisition — could not be verified in this pass (`needs-verification`).

**Position.** The quiet giant of AI silicon. Broadcom co-develops the **custom "XPU" accelerators** for Google (TPU), Meta (MTIA) and ByteDance, and in 2024 began work with OpenAI on a custom inference chip. Its networking silicon is in most AI clusters that are not using NVIDIA's fabric. This makes Broadcom the principal beneficiary of any move by hyperscalers away from merchant GPUs.

### Arm Holdings

**Financials.** FY2025: revenue **US$4.01 bn**, operating income **US$831 m**, net income **US$792 m**, **8,330 employees**. SoftBank Group holds **87.1%**. IPO on Nasdaq **14 September 2023** at a **US$54.5 bn** valuation, raising **US$4.87 bn**.

**Position.** The ISA under essentially every phone, and increasingly under servers (AWS Graviton, NVIDIA Grace, Ampere), cars and microcontrollers. Business model: architecture licences (design your own core — Apple, Qualcomm) and core licences (use Cortex/Neoverse), each with an up-front fee plus per-unit royalty. Arm has been moving up the value chain toward complete compute subsystems (CSS) and, reportedly, its own chips — a move that puts it into competition with its licensees.

**Dependency and risk.** RISC-V is the structural threat, particularly in embedded and in China. Arm China's governance dispute and the failed NVIDIA acquisition (abandoned February 2022) are the other recurring risks. Royalty rates on newer architectures are higher, which is the mechanism by which Arm grows faster than unit shipments.

### HiSilicon (Huawei)

**What it does.** Huawei's captive design house: **Kirin** smartphone SoCs, **Ascend** AI accelerators, **Kunpeng** server CPUs.

**Position.** US export controls in 2020 required licences for both American suppliers and non-American firms using American technology, and Huawei announced it would stop producing Kirin chipsets from **15 September 2020**. The company subsequently re-emerged using SMIC: **Kirin 9000s (SMIC 7 nm, Q3 2023)**, **Kirin 9010 (Q2 2024)**, **Kirin 9020 (Q4 2024)**, **Kirin 9030 (Q4 2025)**; **Ascend 910B**, **Ascend 910C (SMIC 7 nm N+2, May 2025)**, **Ascend 920 (6 nm, late 2025)**. Revenue is not separately disclosed.

## Memory

### Micron Technology

**Financials.** FY2025: revenue **US$37.378 bn**, operating income **US$9.77 bn**, net income **US$8.539 bn**, total assets US$82.8 bn, **53,000 employees**. Reached a **US$1 trillion market capitalisation on 26 May 2026** on HBM demand.

**Position.** One of the "Big Three" DRAM makers with Samsung and SK Hynix, and the only US-headquartered one — which makes it a policy favourite (a US$6.1 bn CHIPS award in April 2024). Investments announced: ~US$24 bn in Singapore (January 2026) for 700,000 ft² of cleanroom; US$200 bn total US commitment including US$15 bn in Boise and US$100 bn in Clay, New York. Discontinued the Crucial consumer brand in February 2026 to concentrate on datacentre and AI.

### SK Hynix

**Financials.** 2024: revenue **₩66.19 tn (US$47.96 bn)**, operating income **₩23.47 tn (US$18.16 bn)**, net income **₩19.80 tn**, **46,863 employees**. Q1 2026 operating profit reported at approximately **US$25 bn** — a figure that conveys how extreme the 2025–2026 memory upcycle became.

**Position.** **38% of the global DRAM market as of Q2 2025**, and the HBM leader — it began mass production of **12-layer HBM3E on 26 September 2024**, the first in the world, and has been NVIDIA's principal HBM supplier. HBM leadership converted a commodity-memory business into a strategically scarce one.

### YMTC (Yangtze Memory Technologies)

Founded **26 July 2016** with about **US$24 bn** of investment from Tsinghua Unigroup, the Hubei provincial government and the national IC fund. Its **Xtacking** architecture (2018) bonds a separately-fabricated logic wafer to the memory array wafer — a genuine architectural innovation, not a copy. NAND progression: 32-layer (2018), 64-layer TLC (September 2019), 128-layer 1.33 Tb (April 2020), **232-layer (announced August 2022, shipping November 2022)**. Roughly 100,000 wafers/month and ~8,000 employees (2023). **Added to the US Entity List on 15 December 2022.**

### CXMT (ChangXin Memory Technologies)

Founded 2016. DRAM on a 19 nm process (LPDDR4/DDR4) as of 2020; **DDR5 unveiled and commercially sold from 2025**, with LPDDR5/5X in the line-up. Capacity grew from 20,000 wafers/month (December 2019) to **720,000 wafers per quarter in Q4 2025**. Global DRAM share **4% in Q2 2025**, targeting 10%. HBM production targeted for end-2026 at a Shanghai back-end facility. IPO: a ¥29.5 bn raise announced December 2025, approved by the CSRC in **June 2026**. US federal procurement ban from 2022; added to the US DoD's Chinese military-linked companies list in **June 2026**.

## Analog, power and automotive

### Texas Instruments

2023: revenue **US$17.519 bn**, net income **US$6.510 bn**, **34,000 employees** (2022: US$20.028 bn / US$8.749 bn). Analog and embedded processing are **more than 80% of revenue**; TI held the largest analog market share as of 2020 (over US$10 bn of a US$57 bn market). Strategy: own 300 mm analog capacity — analog on 300 mm gives roughly a 40% die-cost advantage over 200 mm — including the Lehi, Utah fab bought from Micron for **US$900 m** in 2021, plus Sherman, Texas. 2024/2025 figures `needs-verification`.

### Analog Devices

FY2025: revenue **US$11.02 bn**, net income **US$2.267 bn**, **24,500 employees**. Data converters are **more than 50% of revenue**; also amplifiers, RF, power and interface. Acquired **Maxim Integrated** (agreed July 2020, valuing the combined company at **US$68 bn**; China clearance August 2021). Positioned at the high-performance end of analog where TI competes on breadth and cost.

### NXP Semiconductors

2024: revenue **US$12.61 bn**, approximately **34,000 employees** across 30+ countries. Third-largest European semiconductor company by market capitalisation as of 2024. Automotive-centric: ADAS radar, automotive Ethernet, secure elements and NFC (NXP invented much of the NFC ecosystem), plus microcontrollers and edge processing. Net income not verified in this pass.

### Infineon Technologies

FY2025: revenue **€14.662 bn**, operating income **€1.515 bn**, net income **€1.015 bn**, total assets €30.47 bn, **57,077 employees**. Four divisions: Automotive, Green Industrial Power, Power & Sensor Systems, Connected Secure Systems. Germany's largest chipmaker. Acquired Cypress (2020) and **GaN Systems (2023)**. The leader in power semiconductors — IGBTs, silicon and SiC MOSFETs — and therefore levered to electric vehicles, industrial drives and renewables rather than to AI.

### STMicroelectronics

2024: revenue **US$13.27 bn**, operating income **US$1.676 bn**, net income **US$1.565 bn**, **49,602 employees**. Franco-Italian; products span MCUs (STM32, the dominant hobbyist and industrial Arm MCU family), MEMS sensors, power, and automotive. **Silicon carbide** is the strategic bet: 200 mm SiC wafers from Norrköping, Sweden (2021), a SiC line in Bouskoura, Morocco (2022), and an EU-supported SiC substrate plant in Catania, Italy with **€293 m** of funding targeted for 2026. Runs 300 mm fabs at Crolles (France) and Agrate Brianza (Italy).

## Equipment

### ASML

2024: revenue **€28.26 bn**, net income **€7.572 bn**, **43,395 employees**. **83% of worldwide lithography sales (2025)** and effectively 100% of EUV. Products: DUV (i-line, KrF, ArF dry and immersion), EUV NXE (0.33 NA, 13 nm resolution), EUV EXE High-NA (0.55 NA, 8 nm resolution), plus metrology (YieldStar) and computational lithography. An NXE tool weighs ~180 tonnes and costs up to ~**US$200 m**; an EXE costs ~**US$370 m**. First High-NA shipments: **Intel December 2023, TSMC late 2024**.

ASML is itself an integrator: Zeiss SMT makes the optics, Trumpf the CO₂ lasers, Cymer (ASML-owned) the sources. It is the single most concentrated chokepoint in the industry and consequently the principal instrument of Dutch and US export policy toward China.

### Applied Materials

FY2024: revenue **US$27.176 bn**, net income **US$7.177 bn**. The broadest equipment portfolio — ALD, CVD, PVD, epitaxy, etch, ion implantation, CMP, plus metrology and a large service business (Applied Global Services) — and a display/solar segment. Employee count not verified in this pass.

### Lam Research

Fiscal year ends 30 June; FY2025 revenue and net income could not be verified in this pass (`needs-verification`). Product lines: plasma etch (its core strength), thin-film deposition, wafer cleaning and photoresist strip. Lam is disproportionately levered to **3D NAND**, where extreme-aspect-ratio etch and deposition are the enabling processes, and is a leader in dry-resist development with ASML and imec.

### KLA Corporation

FY2025: revenue **US$12.2 bn**, net income **US$4.06 bn**, approximately **15,000 employees**. Process control: wafer and reticle inspection, defect review, CD and overlay metrology, plus the software that turns inspection data into yield decisions. KLA's near-monopoly in high-end inspection is less discussed than ASML's in lithography but is comparably durable — you cannot ramp a node you cannot measure.

### Tokyo Electron

Approximately **12,742 employees** (June 2023); market capitalisation US$114.6 bn in February 2024, then the third-most valuable company in Japan. Three segments: semiconductor production equipment (coater/developer tracks — where it is dominant and paired with every ASML scanner — plus etch, thermal, deposition, cleaning and wafer probers), flat panel display equipment, and other. Revenue and net income for the latest fiscal year `needs-verification`.

## EDA

### Synopsys

FY2024: revenue approximately **US$6.12 bn**, approximately **28,000 employees**; **31% of the EDA market (2024)**. Acquired **Ansys for US$35 bn** (announced 16 January 2024, closed **17 July 2025**), adding multiphysics, thermal and electromagnetic simulation. Flagship tools: Design Compiler, Fusion Compiler, IC Compiler II, **PrimeTime** (the STA signoff reference), VCS, Verdi, VC Formal, TestMAX, StarRC, IC Validator, ZeBu, HAPS. Also one of the two largest silicon-IP vendors.

### Cadence Design Systems

**30% of the EDA market (2024)**. The most recent financials verified in this pass are stale — **US$3 bn revenue and 9,300 employees in 2021** — and current figures are `needs-verification`; note that by 2022 roughly 40% of revenue came from "systems" customers rather than semiconductor firms. Flagship tools: Genus, Innovus, Tempus, Voltus, Quantus, Pegasus, Xcelium, **JasperGold** (formal), Palladium (emulation), **Virtuoso** (the analog/custom design standard), Spectre, Allegro.

### Siemens EDA

Formerly Mentor Graphics, acquired by Siemens in 2017 for US$4.5 bn. **13% of the EDA market (2024)**. Owns **Calibre**, the DRC/LVS physical-verification signoff standard that most foundries qualify first, plus Questa, Tessent (DFT), Catapult (HLS) and Veloce (emulation). Financials are not separately reported within Siemens.
