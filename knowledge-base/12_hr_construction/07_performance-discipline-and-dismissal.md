# Performance, discipline and dismissal on site

**Summary.** A dismissal survives challenge only if it is **substantively fair** (a valid reason, and dismissal an appropriate sanction) and **procedurally fair** (a proper process before the decision). **[NA]** s.33 of the Labour Act 11 of 2007 puts both requirements on the employer and places the onus on the employer to prove them. **[ZA]** LRA s.188 does the same, and **Schedule 8, the Code of Good Practice: Dismissal**, sets the standard every CCMA commissioner applies. The three lawful reasons are **misconduct**, **incapacity** (poor performance or ill health) and **operational requirements**. On construction sites the recurring problems are absenteeism, alcohol, theft of materials and tools, insubordination, and — most seriously — deliberate breach of safety rules.

> ⚠️ Legally consequential. Do not dismiss without a hearing, do not chair a hearing into conduct you personally witnessed and investigated, and do not backdate warnings. Each of these singly is enough to make a dismissal unfair and to convert a justified sacking into a compensation award of up to 12 months' remuneration **[ZA]** s.194 LRA.

## Key facts

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Substantive fairness | A rule existed; it was valid and reasonable; the employee knew it or should have; it was consistently applied; dismissal is an appropriate sanction |
| Procedural fairness | Investigation → written notice of allegations and hearing → time to prepare → representation → opportunity to be heard → impartial chairperson → decision with reasons → right of appeal/referral |
| Onus | On the **employer** to prove both — **[NA]** s.33; **[ZA]** s.192 LRA |
| Standard of proof | Balance of probabilities, not beyond reasonable doubt |
| Notice periods | **[NA]** 1 day / 1 week / 1 month by service (s.30); **[ZA]** 1 week / 2 weeks / 4 weeks (s.37 BCEA) |
| Severance (operational requirements) | **[NA]** ≥ 1 week per completed year (s.35); **[ZA]** 1 week per completed year (s.41 BCEA) |
| Referral time limit | **[ZA]** **30 days** from dismissal to the CCMA/council; **[NA]** to the Labour Commissioner (*period needs verification*) |
| Compensation cap | **[ZA]** 12 months' remuneration (24 months if automatically unfair), s.194 LRA |
| Record retention | **[ZA]** 3 years (s.31 BCEA); keep disciplinary records for the same period |

## 1. Performance management on site

Construction performance is measurable in ways office work is not — output per day, rework percentage, snag list length, adherence to tolerance, attendance, safety observations. Use it.

**A workable rhythm for a small contractor:**
- **Daily**: the foreman allocates work and inspects it before it is closed up. A defect found at first-fix is a coaching moment; the same defect found at handover is a disciplinary matter.
- **Weekly**: a fifteen-minute gang review — output against target, rework, safety observations, attendance. Note it in the site diary.
- **Quarterly or per phase**: a written **performance review** against the job description (form in file `10`), covering quality, productivity, safety behaviour, reliability, teamwork and development. Signed by both.
- **Annually**: pay and progression decision, with the review record as its basis.

**Standards must be communicated before they can be enforced.** Schedule 8 is explicit: for poor performance, the employee must have been made aware of the required standard, evaluated against it, given **training, guidance, counselling or a reasonable opportunity to improve**, and dismissal considered only if improvement does not follow and alternatives (including transfer to work they can do) have been considered.

**Probation.** Schedule 8's probation guidelines allow a lower threshold for dismissing a probationer — but still require: a reasonable probation period; evaluation and feedback during it; reasons given; an opportunity to make representations; and the right to be assisted. Do not treat probation as an at-will period.

## 2. Progressive discipline

Discipline exists to **correct behaviour**, not to punish. Schedule 8 requires efforts to correct through graduated measures such as counselling and warnings, reserving dismissal for repeated offences or serious misconduct.

**A defensible construction disciplinary code — sanction guide**

| Offence | 1st | 2nd | 3rd |
|---|---|---|---|
| Late arrival | Verbal | Written | Final written |
| Absence without leave (1 day) | Written | Final written | Dismissal |
| Absence without leave (3+ consecutive days, no contact) | Final written or dismissal (desertion enquiry) | Dismissal | — |
| Poor workmanship (not wilful) | Counselling + retraining | Written | Final written |
| Failure to wear issued PPE | Written | Final written | Dismissal |
| Removing or defeating a safety control (edge protection, guard, lockout) | **Final written or dismissal** | Dismissal | — |
| Working at height without required fall protection | **Final written or dismissal** | Dismissal | — |
| Reporting for duty under the influence of alcohol or drugs | Final written (with referral to support) | Dismissal | — |
| Insubordination (refusing a lawful instruction) | Final written | Dismissal | — |
| Gross insubordination, threats, assault | **Dismissal** | — | — |
| Theft of materials, tools, fuel, or dishonesty | **Dismissal** | — | — |
| Wilful damage to property or plant | **Dismissal** | — | — |
| Sleeping on duty in a safety-critical role | Final written or dismissal | Dismissal | — |
| Falsifying a time sheet or clocking for another | **Dismissal** | — | — |
| Sexual harassment | Final written to **dismissal** depending on severity | Dismissal | — |

This is a **guide**, not a tariff. The chairperson must consider the circumstances: the seriousness of the offence, the employee's length of service and disciplinary record, mitigating factors, whether the employee shows remorse, whether the trust relationship can survive, and — critically — **consistency** with how the employer treated comparable cases.

**Validity of warnings.** State an expiry on the face of the warning: typically **3 months** for a verbal, **6 months** for a written, **12 months** for a final written. An expired warning cannot be relied on as a step in progression, though it may be relevant to the pattern.

**Right to refuse unsafe work.** No employee may be disciplined for refusing to perform work that they reasonably believe is unsafe, or for reporting a hazard or an incident. Discipline in those circumstances is likely to be an automatically unfair dismissal **[ZA]** s.187 LRA and a contravention of the fundamental rights provisions **[NA]** ss.5–7 Labour Act.

## 3. The disciplinary hearing

### 3.1 Before the hearing

1. **Investigate.** Gather statements, photographs, time sheets, the toolbox talk register, the PPE issue register, plant logs, CCTV or gate records. Do it within days, not weeks.
2. **Decide on precautionary suspension** only where the employee's presence would prejudice the investigation or endanger others. Suspension must be **on full pay** and should be brief, confirmed in writing with reasons.
3. **Issue a written notice of hearing** at least **48 hours** in advance (longer for complex matters), stating: the date, time and place; each allegation in plain, specific terms — what, when, where, which rule; the right to be represented by a fellow employee or trade union representative; the right to call witnesses and cross-examine; the right to an interpreter; and that dismissal is a possible outcome. Have the employee sign for receipt; if they refuse, record the refusal in front of a witness.
4. **Provide the evidence** the employer will rely on, or make it available for inspection.

### 3.2 Who may chair

The chairperson must be **impartial** and must not have been the complainant, the investigator, or a witness to the incident. On a small site this is the hardest requirement to satisfy. Options: use a director or manager from another site or from head office; use the owner where the foreman investigated and will give evidence; use an external chairperson (an HR consultant or attorney) for serious matters; **[ZA]** use an accredited CCMA commissioner to conduct a **pre-dismissal arbitration** under s.188A of the LRA with the employee's consent — the award is final and binding and cannot be re-litigated.

### 3.3 Representation

The employee may be assisted by a **fellow employee or a trade union representative**. Legal representation at an internal hearing is generally not a right, unless the disciplinary code or a collective agreement grants it, or the matter is exceptionally complex. Do not permit the employer legal representation while refusing it to the employee.

### 3.4 Running the hearing

Order of proceedings:
1. Chairperson introduces those present, confirms the employee understands the allegations, confirms representation and language, and explains the procedure.
2. Employee **pleads** to each allegation.
3. Employer presents evidence and witnesses; the employee or representative cross-examines.
4. Employee presents their case and witnesses; the employer cross-examines.
5. Both sides make closing submissions on the **merits** only.
6. Chairperson finds guilty or not guilty on each allegation, **with reasons**.
7. If guilty, both sides address **mitigation and aggravation** — length of service, record, remorse, personal circumstances, seriousness, consistency.
8. Chairperson decides the sanction, with reasons.

**Record it.** Handwritten minutes signed by the chairperson are sufficient; an audio recording with the employee's knowledge is better. Note who was present, what was said, what documents were produced. A hearing with no record is, in evidential terms, a hearing that did not happen.

### 3.5 After the hearing

Issue a **written outcome letter** stating the allegations, the finding on each, the sanction, the reasons, the effective date, and the right of appeal (internal, where the code provides) or referral. **[ZA]** state the right to refer an unfair dismissal dispute to the CCMA or the applicable bargaining council **within 30 days**. **[NA]** state the right to refer to the **Labour Commissioner**.

On dismissal, pay out: notice or pay in lieu, accrued annual leave, any outstanding wages and pro-rata benefits. **[ZA]** issue a **certificate of service** (s.42 BCEA). **[NA]** the final payment must include accrued leave on a pro-rata basis (s.37).

## 4. Substantive versus procedural fairness

These are separate tests and a dismissal can fail either.

**Substantive** — was there a good enough reason? Ask, in order: Did a rule exist? Was it valid and reasonable? Did the employee know of it, or could they reasonably be expected to? Has the employer applied the rule consistently to others? Is dismissal an appropriate sanction for **this** contravention by **this** employee?

**Procedural** — was the process fair? Investigation, notice, time, representation, hearing, impartial chair, reasons, appeal.

Remedies differ. **[ZA]** a substantively unfair dismissal usually attracts **reinstatement with back pay** (s.193 LRA); a dismissal that was substantively fair but procedurally defective usually attracts **compensation only**, often modest. That asymmetry is why the reason matters more than the paperwork — but the paperwork is what proves the reason.

**Consistency** is where employers most often lose. If two men were both found asleep in the shade and one was dismissed and one warned, the dismissal is unfair unless a real distinguishing factor is on record.

## 5. Dismissal for incapacity

**Poor performance.** The process is coaching, not discipline: set and communicate the standard; evaluate; identify the cause (skill, tools, materials, instruction, health); provide training, guidance and support; set a reasonable improvement period with review dates; consider alternatives including transfer or demotion with consent; and only then, after a hearing at which the employee may be assisted, consider dismissal.

**Ill health or injury.** Investigate the extent of the incapacity, whether it is temporary or permanent, and whether the work or the workplace can be adapted or alternative work found. Schedule 8 expects **greater accommodation where the incapacity arose from an injury on duty**. Consult the employee and their medical practitioner; obtain the employee's consent for medical information. Consider light duties, reduced hours, or redeployment before dismissal. Dismissal for incapacity is a **no-fault** dismissal: notice is payable and the employee should be assisted with any disability or compensation claim (file `08`).

**Absenteeism** sits between misconduct and incapacity. Unauthorised absence is misconduct; absence caused by a genuine chronic condition is incapacity. Investigate before you choose the track.

**Desertion.** Where an employee simply stops coming, do not treat the contract as automatically terminated. Send a written instruction to return by a stated date to their last known address and, in practice, contact them by phone; if they do not return or explain, hold a hearing **in absentia** and record that the notice was served, then issue the outcome.

## 6. Dismissal for operational requirements (retrenchment)

**[NA] s.34 Labour Act** — give **at least four weeks' written notice** to the Labour Commissioner, the affected employees and their trade union; negotiate in good faith on the need for the retrenchment, selection criteria, timing and severance.

**[ZA] s.189 LRA** — issue a written invitation to consult disclosing: the reasons; the alternatives considered and why they were rejected; the number and categories of employees affected; the proposed selection method; the timing; severance; assistance offered; and the possibility of future re-employment. Consult on ways to **avoid, minimise, change the timing of and mitigate** the dismissals, and on selection criteria. **s.189A** adds facilitation and a 60-day period for employers with 50+ employees above defined thresholds.

**Selection criteria** must be fair and objective. Defensible criteria: LIFO within a category; skills and qualifications required for the remaining work; disciplinary record; attendance. Not defensible: union membership, pregnancy, age, race, sex, or "the foreman's preference".

**The construction-specific trap.** Demobilising a site at completion is a retrenchment if the employees were on indefinite contracts. If they were on genuine project contracts with an identifiable completing event, the contract simply ends — no dismissal, no severance (subject to **[ZA]** s.198B for fixed terms over 24 months). Which of those you are in was decided when the contract was drafted (file `04`).

**Severance** is one week's remuneration per completed year of continuous service in both countries. An employee who unreasonably refuses an offer of suitable alternative employment forfeits severance **[ZA]** s.41(4) BCEA.

## 7. Templates a small builder needs

Full drafts are in file `10`. Each must be adapted and legally reviewed.

**1. Written warning** — employee name and number; date and place of the incident; the rule contravened; a factual description of what happened; the employee's explanation; the sanction; the **validity period**; the improvement required and by when; the consequence of repetition; signature of issuer, employee and witness; and a note if the employee refused to sign (refusal does not invalidate the warning).

**2. Notice of disciplinary hearing** — date, time, venue; each allegation stated specifically with the rule contravened; rights (representation by a fellow employee or shop steward, to call and question witnesses, to an interpreter); a list of the employer's evidence and witnesses; a statement that dismissal is a possible outcome; the deadline to confirm attendance; and an acknowledgement of receipt.

**3. Outcome letter** — the allegations; the finding on each with brief reasons; mitigating and aggravating factors considered; the sanction and effective date; payments due; the right of appeal or referral and the time limit; and the certificate of service **[ZA]**.

**4. Performance improvement plan** — the standard, the current gap with evidence, the support to be given, the review dates, and the consequence of non-improvement.

**5. Incapacity/ill-health consultation record** — medical evidence considered, accommodations explored, alternative work considered, and the outcome.

**6. Retrenchment consultation notice** — the s.189 / s.34 disclosure list above.

**7. Counselling record** — for the informal step before a warning; brief, dated, signed. This is the document small builders never keep and always need.
