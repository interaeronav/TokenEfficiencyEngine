# Subcontractors and labour brokers

**Summary.** Construction runs on third-party labour, and third-party labour is where the largest HR liabilities sit. Three questions decide everything: **is this person an employee or a genuine contractor**; **if they are someone else's employee, am I nonetheless liable**; and **have I verified compliance before they walked on site**. **[ZA]** LRA s.198 makes the client and the temporary employment service **jointly and severally liable** for breaches of the BCEA, a collective agreement, an arbitration award or a sectoral determination, and s.198A **deems** a below-threshold TES worker to be the client's employee after three months. **[NA]** the Employment Services Act 8 of 2011 licenses private employment agencies and requires placed workers to be on terms no less favourable than comparable employees. On top of that, **[ZA]** reg 7 of the Construction Regulations 2014 makes the principal contractor responsible for verifying every contractor's competence and compensation-fund good standing **before work starts**.

> ⚠️ Legally consequential. Letting an unverified subcontractor on site is the single most common way a compliant contractor acquires someone else's liabilities — for wages, for bargaining council contributions, for tax, and for an injured worker who turns out to have no compensation cover.

## 1. The legal test — employee or contractor

### 1.1 The statutory presumption **[ZA]**

Under LRA s.200A and BCEA s.83A, a person earning below the earnings threshold is **presumed to be an employee**, regardless of the form of the contract, if **any one** of the following is present:

- the manner in which they work is subject to the control or direction of another;
- their hours of work are subject to the control or direction of another;
- in the case of a person working for an organisation, they form **part of that organisation**;
- they have worked for that other person for an average of at least **40 hours per month over the last three months**;
- they are **economically dependent** on the person for whom they work;
- they are **provided with tools of trade or work equipment** by the other person;
- they **only work for or render services to one person**.

One factor triggers the presumption; the employer must then rebut it.

### 1.2 The dominant impression test **[NA]** and generally

Namibian and South African courts weigh: who **controls** how, when and where the work is done; whether the person is **integrated** into the organisation; **economic dependence**; who supplies **tools, plant and materials**; whether the person may **delegate or substitute**; whether they bear the **risk of profit and loss** and must remedy defects at their own cost; **exclusivity**; whether they work fixed hours; whether they are subject to the employer's **disciplinary code**; and how they are paid — for time attended or for a priced deliverable.

No single factor decides it. The label on the document decides nothing.

### 1.3 The construction fact patterns

| Arrangement | Likely status | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Plumbing firm with its own staff, vehicles and tools, priced per house, works for six builders | **Genuine subcontractor** | Own organisation, own risk, non-exclusive, priced deliverable |
| "Self-employed" bricklayer, your mortar, your scaffold, your foreman's instructions, your hours, only you | **Employee** | Control, tools, exclusivity, integration |
| Labour-only gang paid per m² but sequenced, supervised and equipped by you | **Employees of you**, probably all of them | The gang boss is a supervisor, not an entrepreneur |
| Registered TES supplying five general workers for eight months **[ZA]** | Deemed **your** employees after 3 months (s.198A) if below the threshold | Statutory deeming |
| Specialist scaffolder, own design, own erectors, own certification, priced per erection | **Genuine subcontractor** | Specialist skill, own method, own liability |
| Ex-employee now "consulting" on the same site doing the same job | **Employee** | Nothing changed but the paperwork |

### 1.4 What it costs to get wrong

Retrospective leave, overtime, notice and severance; retrospective PAYE, UIF/social security and levies with interest and penalties; **[ZA]** bargaining council contributions for the whole period; unfair dismissal jurisdiction; and — the worst case — an injured "contractor" with no compensation cover, which strips the employer of the COIDA s.35 substitution defence and exposes it to a full common-law damages claim.

## 2. Labour brokers / temporary employment services

### 2.1 **[ZA]**

**s.198** — the TES is the employer of the worker it places, and the worker is not an employee of the client. But **s.198(4)** makes the TES and the client **jointly and severally liable** where the TES contravenes a collective agreement of a bargaining council regulating conditions of employment, a binding arbitration award, the BCEA, or a sectoral determination. In practice, the worker sues the client, which is the party with money.

**s.198A** — for an employee earning **below the earnings threshold**, a "temporary service" means work for the client for **three months or less**, or as a substitute for an absent employee, or a category determined by a bargaining council or sectoral determination. Anything else means the employee is **deemed to be the employee of the client**, employed indefinitely, and must be treated **on the whole not less favourably** than comparable employees of the client doing the same or similar work.

**s.198D** routes disputes about ss.198A–C to the CCMA or the council.

**Practical consequence for a builder:** using a labour broker to staff a nine-month project with general labour does not avoid employment. It costs the broker's margin **and** delivers deemed employees at month four, with the client's own terms and conditions applying.

### 2.2 **[NA]**

The Namibian attempt to ban labour hire outright was struck down; the current framework is the **Employment Services Act 8 of 2011**:

- **s.19** — no person may operate as a private employment agency without a licence.
- **ss.20–21** — application to the Director; licence issued for a specified period.
- **s.22** — cancellation for non-compliance after 30 days' notice.
- **s.24** — the agency may not charge the job seeker, and the employer may not deduct a placement fee from wages.
- **s.26** — an agency may not discriminate on protected grounds, may not refer workers to an employer with compliance violations or **without an affirmative action compliance certificate**, and must ensure placed workers receive **terms and conditions not less favourable** than comparable incumbents (s.26(2)(a)).

**Verify the licence** before engaging an agency. Engaging an unlicensed agency puts your workforce's legal status in doubt and, given s.26, your own affirmative action compliance certificate becomes a prerequisite for being supplied at all.

## 3. Joint and several liability — the wider map

Beyond LRA s.198(4), a principal contractor can pick up a subcontractor's liabilities through:

- **[ZA] Construction Regulations reg 7** — the principal contractor must satisfy itself that every contractor it appoints has the necessary competencies and resources, must ensure **before work starts** that each contractor is registered and in **good standing** with the compensation fund or a licensed compensation insurer, must appoint each contractor **in writing**, must conduct **periodic audits at least every 30 days**, and must **stop** any contractor whose work does not accord with the health and safety plan.
- **[ZA] COIDA s.89** (mandator liability) — where a contractor's employee is injured and the contractor has not paid its assessment, the Commissioner may look to the mandator. *Section number and mechanism need verification.*
- **[ZA] Bargaining council agreements** — extended agreements bind non-party employers in scope; councils audit sites and pursue arrears from whoever is there.
- **Tax** — **[ZA]** SARS may hold a principal liable where it should have withheld; **[NA]** similar exposure on PAYE where the "contractor" is in truth an employee.
- **Client contract flow-down** — most JBCC, NEC and FIDIC-based contracts, and all public procurement, oblige the main contractor to ensure subcontractor compliance with labour and safety law, backed by indemnities and step-in rights.

## 4. What a subcontract must contain

A construction subcontract that also does its HR job should cover:

**Commercial**
1. **Parties** with registration numbers, tax numbers, and registered addresses.
2. **Scope of works** — precisely, with drawings, specification and quantities annexed; and an express statement of what is **excluded**.
3. **Price and basis** — lump sum, measured rates, or dayworks with the rate schedule.
4. **Programme** — start, completion, sectional dates, and the obligation to resource to the main programme.
5. **Payment terms** — application date, valuation date, certification period, payment period, and the method. Say plainly whether payment is **pay-when-paid**; note that pay-if-paid clauses are commercially aggressive and may be unenforceable in some contexts.
6. **Retention** — the percentage (commonly 5–10 % of the value of work done), the limit, the release of half at practical completion and the balance at the end of the defects liability period (commonly 12 months), and any retention bond alternative.
7. **Variations** — how instructed, how valued, and that no variation is valid unless in writing.
8. **Defects liability** — duration, obligation to return, and the right to engage others at the subcontractor's cost.
9. **Penalties/damages** for delay, and the extension of time mechanism.
10. **Termination** — for breach, for insolvency, and for convenience with the consequences of each.
11. **Dispute resolution** — negotiation, then adjudication/mediation, then arbitration or court.

**HR, safety and compliance**
12. **Status clause** — express statement that the subcontractor is an independent contractor, is the **sole employer** of its personnel, and will comply with all labour legislation; with an **indemnity** in favour of the main contractor for any claim by the subcontractor's personnel.
13. **Compliance warranties** — registration and good standing with the compensation fund; tax compliance; bargaining council registration and contributions **[ZA]**; social security registration **[NA]**; minimum wage compliance; and the right to require proof at any time.
14. **Health and safety** — obligation to comply with the site health and safety plan, to produce its own health and safety plan and file, to make the required written appointments, to provide medical certificates of fitness **[ZA]**, to attend induction, to report incidents immediately, and to accept the main contractor's right to **stop the work** and to remove any individual from site.
15. **Personnel** — the right to require removal of any person; a prohibition on employing anyone under 18 on site; verification of trade and plant competence; and a requirement that all personnel be inducted before starting.
16. **Insurance** — the policies required, minimum limits, and the obligation to produce certificates before commencement and on renewal.
17. **No further subcontracting** without written consent.
18. **Records and audit** — the right to inspect wage records, time sheets, PPE registers and training records; and cooperation with council or inspectorate audits.

## 5. Payment terms and retention

- **Match your outflow to your inflow** but do not shift all cash risk downward: a subcontractor starved of cash abandons the site, and replacing them mid-works costs more than the retention saved.
- **Retention** protects against defects, not against poor pricing. Release it on time; unreleased retention is the most common cause of subcontractor litigation.
- **Never pay a labour-only gang in cash without a record.** Cash payments to a gang boss for "the boys' wages" is the exact fact pattern that turns you into their employer and leaves you with no evidence of what they were paid.
- Where a subcontractor's workers are unpaid, expect them to approach **you**. Have a contractual right to pay them directly and set off against the subcontract sum — a clause worth having even if rarely used.

## 6. Insurance and compensation verification

Require, before site access and at each renewal:

| Cover | Purpose | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Compensation fund** — COIDA **[ZA]** / ECF via SSC **[NA]** | Statutory injury cover for the subcontractor's employees | **Letter of good standing**, current and dated |
| **Public liability** | Third-party injury and property damage | Certificate with the limit stated |
| **Contract works / CAR** | Damage to the works and materials | Certificate; check whether the main policy covers subcontractors |
| **Motor and goods-in-transit** | Vehicles and plant on and to site | Certificate |
| **Professional indemnity** | Only where the subcontractor designs (e.g. temporary works, specialist steel) | Certificate |
| **Plant / machinery breakdown** | Owned plant | Certificate |

> ⚠️ A letter of good standing has an expiry date. Diarise it. A subcontractor whose good standing lapses mid-project is uninsured from that date, and an injury in that window lands on the principal contractor.

## 7. Compliance checklist before anyone goes on site

Run this once per subcontractor per project, and keep the completed sheet in the health and safety file and the commercial file.

**Company**
- [ ] Company registration certificate (BIPA **[NA]** / CIPC **[ZA]**)
- [ ] Tax clearance / tax compliance status pin
- [ ] VAT registration where applicable
- [ ] **[ZA]** CIDB registration and grade appropriate to the value and class of works
- [ ] **[ZA]** NHBRC registration where the work is home building
- [ ] **[NA]** Affirmative action compliance certificate where a relevant employer
- [ ] **[ZA]** B-BBEE certificate / affidavit where required by the main contract
- [ ] **[ZA]** Bargaining council registration and contributions up to date, where in scope
- [ ] **[NA]** Social Security Commission registration
- [ ] Bank details verified by a call to a known number — not from the invoice

**Insurance and compensation**
- [ ] Compensation fund **letter of good standing**, current, expiry diarised
- [ ] Public liability certificate, limit adequate
- [ ] Motor and any plant cover
- [ ] Professional indemnity where they design

**Contract**
- [ ] Signed subcontract with scope, price, programme, payment, retention
- [ ] Written appointment as a contractor **[ZA]** reg 7
- [ ] Indemnity and status clause signed
- [ ] No unauthorised further subcontracting

**Health and safety**
- [ ] Subcontractor health and safety plan received and accepted
- [ ] Health and safety file opened
- [ ] Written appointments made and competence evidence supplied
- [ ] Risk assessments for their scope, by a competent person
- [ ] Fall protection plan where working at height
- [ ] Method statements / safe work procedures for high-risk activities
- [ ] Plant and equipment certificates (scaffold, lifting gear, ladders, electrical)
- [ ] Baseline audit scheduled; **30-day audit cycle** diarised

**People**
- [ ] List of personnel with ID numbers and next of kin
- [ ] **[ZA]** medical certificates of fitness (Annexure 3) for each
- [ ] Trade, plant and licence certificates verified with the issuing body
- [ ] Every person **inducted** and signed on the register before starting
- [ ] PPE issued and recorded
- [ ] Nobody under 18 on the list
- [ ] Work permits for foreign nationals sighted and copied

**During the works**
- [ ] Monthly audit completed and recorded
- [ ] Good standing and insurance re-checked at expiry
- [ ] Incident reports received and closed out
- [ ] Toolbox talks attended and registered
- [ ] Wage/time records spot-checked where there is any doubt about payment of the workforce
