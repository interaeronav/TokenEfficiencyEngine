# Recruitment and selection for construction

**Summary.** Construction recruitment is a competence problem before it is a sourcing problem. CVs are unreliable, qualifications are frequently claimed rather than held, and the cost of a wrong hire is measured in defective work, rework, delay and injury. The reliable method is: define the role against the programme, source through channels that reach tradespeople rather than office workers, verify credentials with the issuing authority, and **test the hands** with a structured practical assessment before offering employment. Selection must also be lawful — non-discriminatory under **[NA]** s.5 Labour Act and the Affirmative Action (Employment) Act, and under **[ZA]** Chapter II of the Employment Equity Act.

> ⚠️ Selection criteria must be job-related. Excluding candidates on grounds of sex, pregnancy, marital status, family responsibility, race, ethnicity, religion, disability or **HIV status** is unlawful in both countries. HIV testing as a condition of employment is prohibited in South Africa without Labour Court authorisation, and discrimination on HIV status is prohibited in Namibia under s.5 of the Labour Act.

## 1. Define the role against the programme, not the org chart

Labour demand in construction is a function of the works programme. Before advertising, answer: which activity, from when to when, how many, what output rate, and what supervision ratio. A house build typically needs a bricklayer-to-labourer ratio of about 1:1 to 1:2 depending on scaffold and material handling arrangements; a slab pour needs a short, sharp peak of concrete hands. Over-hiring at the wrong time is the single largest avoidable labour cost.

## 3. Sourcing channels in southern Africa

**Formal channels**
- **[NA]** the **Employment Services Bureau** under the Employment Services Act 8 of 2011 registers job seekers and vacancies (ss.13–14). Designated employers must notify vacancies to the Bureau and consider referred candidates in good faith (s.16) — non-compliance carries a fine up to N$20 000 or two years.
- **[ZA]** the Department of Employment and Labour's **Employment Services of South Africa (ESSA)** database and labour centres.
- **Licensed private employment agencies** — **[NA]** must hold a licence under ss.19–22 of the Employment Services Act, may not charge the job seeker (s.24) and must place workers on terms not less favourable than comparable employees (s.26). **[ZA]** TES/labour brokers must be registered; see file `09`.
- **Training institutions** — NTA-accredited VTCs (Valombola, Zambezi, Nakayale, Okakarara, Rundu, Windhoek), and **[ZA]** TVET colleges and CETA-accredited providers. Best channel for apprentices and entry-level trades.
- **Trade unions and bargaining councils** — **[ZA]** councils often run or know of registered labour pools.

**Informal channels — realistically, most site hiring**
- **Foreman referral.** The most common and, when the foreman is good, the most effective. Control it: referrals still go through the same test and verification, and a referral bonus paid on completion of probation is cheaper than a bad hire.
- **Site gate hiring.** Unavoidable for peak labour. Impose a minimum: identity document copied, next of kin recorded, induction done, contract signed before the first hour of work.
- **Traditional authority and community structures** in rural areas — in Ohangwena and comparable regions, going through the headman or the local councillor for general labour both finds people and buys social licence for the project.
- **WhatsApp groups and Facebook trade groups** — now the dominant informal channel for artisans in both countries.
- **Supplier and merchant noticeboards** — builders' merchants are where tradesmen physically are.
- **Poaching from other sites.** Effective and legal, but check for restraint clauses at supervisory level and expect reciprocity.

**Local content and community expectations.** Many public and donor-funded contracts carry local labour targets. Record where each hire comes from; you will be asked to prove local employment percentages.

## 4. Trade testing and practical assessment

A structured practical test is the highest-validity selection tool in construction, and it is cheap. Design it as follows:

- **Time-boxed**: 45–120 minutes.
- **Representative**: a real task from the works — build a 1 m corner in stretcher bond to a gauge rod; set out a right angle by 3-4-5 and prove it with a diagonal; install and terminate a socket circuit on a test board; cut and fit a hip rafter; float a 1 m² patch of plaster.
- **Marked against a written rubric** with objective criteria: dimensional accuracy against a stated tolerance, plumb and level, joint consistency, correct tool selection, safe working, housekeeping, and time.
- **Observed**, with the assessor noting *how* the candidate works — do they check with a level, do they wear the PPE they were given, do they ask when unsure.
- **Recorded** — photograph the finished sample with a tape in frame and file the marked rubric.

Reference the tolerances in `02_building_construction/13_quality-defects-and-tolerances.md` so the rubric matches the standard you will hold them to on site.

For **plant operators**, the practical must be on the actual machine class, on a marked-out course, with a pre-use inspection included in the assessment — an operator who cannot conduct a pre-use inspection is not competent regardless of licence.

## 5. Verifying trade qualifications and credentials

Claimed qualifications must be verified with the **issuing body**, not accepted as a photocopy. A certificate photocopy is worth nothing.

**[NA] Namibia**
- **Namibia Training Authority (NTA)** — accredits providers and programmes and issues awards and certificates under s.5(3) of the Vocational Education and Training Act 1 of 2008. Trade testing and certification historically ran through the **National Trade Testing and Certification Centre**, preserved by transitional provision s.49(13).
- **Namibia Qualifications Authority (NQA)** — evaluates and verifies qualifications, including foreign ones. Essential for the large number of Zimbabwean, Zambian and Angolan artisans working in Namibia.
- **Affirmative action compliance certificate** — under s.41 of the Affirmative Action (Employment) Act; relevant when you are the one being vetted, and required before a licensed employment agency may refer workers to you (s.26).

**[ZA] South Africa**
- **QCTO** and the relevant SETA (**CETA** for construction) for occupational qualifications and trade certificates; **SAQA** for verification and for foreign qualification evaluation.
- **Section 28 trade test certificate** (Manpower Training Act legacy) or the current occupational certificate — verify the number.
- **Wireman's licence / Certificate of Compliance issuer registration** for electricians, via the Department of Employment and Labour and the ECA(SA).
- **PIRB registration** for plumbers issuing Certificates of Compliance.
- **SACPCMP** registration for construction health and safety officers and construction managers.
- **CIDB** contractor grading and **NHBRC** home builder registration — these are **company** registrations, not individual credentials, and belong in subcontractor vetting (file `09`) and in `05_companies_and_industry`. Do not accept a CIDB grade as evidence that an individual is a qualified artisan.
- **Medical certificate of fitness** to Annexure 3 of the Construction Regulations 2014 — required before the employee starts construction work.

> ⚠️ Verify before the first shift, not after. An unqualified electrician or unlicensed crane operator on site is a criminal exposure for the employer and voids insurance.

## 6. References and background checks

Take **two references**, at least one from a direct supervisor on a named project. Ask closed, verifiable questions: dates of employment, site, job title, reason for leaving, whether they would re-employ, any disciplinary record relating to safety or dishonesty. Record the answers in writing with the date and the name and number of the referee.

**Criminal checks** must be job-related and consented to. They are defensible for storemen, drivers, cash handlers, security and anyone with keys or access to plant; harder to justify for a general labourer. **[ZA]** clearance is via SAPS/AFISWITCH; **[NA]** via the Namibian Police police clearance certificate. Obtain **written consent** on the application form.

**Driving licences and PrDP** must be sighted in original and the expiry recorded in the personnel file.

**Credit checks** are rarely justifiable in construction and are restricted under **[ZA]** the National Credit Act and the EEA's psychometric and assessment provisions.

## 7. Interviewing for site roles

Structure beats intuition. Use the same five to seven questions for every candidate for a role, score them 1–5 against defined anchors, and keep the scored sheet — it is your defence in a discrimination claim.

Question types that work on site:
- **Behavioural**: "Tell me about a time the drawing and the site did not match. What did you do?"
- **Situational safety**: "You arrive and the scaffold has no guard rail on the third lift but the foreman says finish the gable today. What do you do?" — you are testing whether they will stop work.
- **Technical**: mix ratios, gauge rod setting, DPC laps, fall of a drain, bond patterns, torque of a bolted connection. Ask what they would do, then ask why.
- **Reliability**: how they get to site, whether they have accommodation, family obligations, and what happened at the end of their last three jobs.

Interview in the candidate's working language where possible — Oshiwambo, Otjiherero, Afrikaans, isiZulu, isiXhosa, Sesotho, Chichewa/Shona for migrant workers — and record if an interpreter was used. Literacy should be assessed, not assumed: many excellent artisans read drawings but not paragraphs, which changes how you issue instructions, contracts and warnings.

## 8. Reusable job descriptions (site roles)

Each of the following is a skeleton. Add the project name, reporting line, contract type and pay basis. Full template layout is in file `10`.

**General labourer.** *Purpose:* provide manual support to trades and keep the site clean and safe. *Duties:* mix mortar and concrete by hand or mixer to the specified ratio; load, barrow and stack materials; assist with setting out pegs and profiles; excavate by hand; clean and back-fill; keep access routes clear; hand tools cleaned and returned to store daily; comply with all safety instructions and wear issued PPE. *Requirements:* physically fit; able to follow verbal instruction in a site language; no formal qualification required. *Reports to:* chargehand.

**Bricklayer.** *Purpose:* build masonry to line, level, plumb and gauge. *Duties:* set out and build walls in the specified bond; mix or receive mortar to the specified ratio; place DPC and cavity trays with correct laps; build in frames, lintels, wall ties and reinforcement; form openings and returns; point and clean down; erect and use scaffolds safely under a competent supervisor; check own work with line, level, gauge rod and square. *Requirements:* trade certificate or verified trade test; able to read a floor plan and section; **[ZA]** medical certificate of fitness. *Standard:* work within the tolerances in the project specification.

**Carpenter (formwork and roof).** *Duties:* fabricate, erect, plumb, brace and strike formwork to line and level; cut and assemble roof structure to the truss/rafter layout; fix battens, fascias and barge boards; hang doors and fit ironmongery; use and maintain power tools safely. *Requirements:* trade certificate or verified test; able to interpret a truss layout and setting-out dimensions.

**Steel fixer.** *Duties:* cut, bend, place and tie reinforcement to the bar bending schedule; place and maintain cover spacers; check laps, starter bars and cover before pour; sign off own work for inspection. *Requirements:* able to read a bar bending schedule and reinforcement drawing.

**Plumber.** *Duties:* install, test and commission water supply, sanitary and drainage installations; set out and lay drainage to the specified fall; pressure-test and record; issue or support the issue of a Certificate of Compliance. *Requirements:* trade certificate; **[ZA]** PIRB registration where a CoC is required.

**Electrician.** *Duties:* install, test and certify electrical installations to the wiring code; install temporary site supply and distribution; inspect and maintain portable equipment. *Requirements:* trade certificate; **[ZA]** registration as a qualified/installation electrician and wireman's licence for CoC issue.

**Plant operator (TLB / excavator).** *Duties:* conduct and record a documented pre-use inspection; operate the machine within its rated capacity; work safely around services, excavations and pedestrians; refuel, grease and report defects; keep the operator's log. *Requirements:* valid operator certificate for the machine class; medical fitness; practical assessment passed on the actual machine.

**Chargehand.** *Duties:* lead a gang on one activity; allocate labour and materials daily; check work against drawing and tolerance before it is closed up; conduct toolbox talks; keep the daily labour allocation sheet. *Requirements:* trade competence plus demonstrated ability to instruct.

**Site foreman.** *Duties:* run day-to-day site operations; set out; sequence trades; control materials and plant; enforce the health and safety plan; keep the site diary; record time; conduct first-line discipline; interface with subcontractors and inspectors. *Requirements:* trade background plus supervisory experience; literacy and numeracy; **[ZA]** may be a written appointee under the Construction Regulations.

**Health and safety officer.** *Duties:* implement the health and safety plan; conduct inspections and audits; maintain the health and safety file; investigate incidents and complete statutory reports; deliver induction and toolbox talks; maintain the register of appointments, medicals and training. *Requirements:* **[ZA]** SACPCMP registration and written appointment under reg 8(5); **[NA]** appointment as a safety officer under reg 6 of GN 156 of 1997.

**Contracts manager.** *Duties:* commercial and delivery accountability for multiple projects; let and manage subcontracts; approve valuations and variations; manage cash flow and claims; recruit and develop site management; own the client relationship. *Requirements:* construction management or QS qualification, or long-form equivalent experience; commercial literacy; driver's licence.
