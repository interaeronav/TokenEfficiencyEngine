# Employment contracts and onboarding in construction

**Summary.** Every construction worker must have a written statement of their terms before or on the day they start, and must be inducted onto the site before they do any work. **[ZA]** BCEA s.29 requires written particulars; **[NA]** the Labour Act 11 of 2007 requires records and particulars and makes the employer bear the onus in any dispute about terms. The dominant legal risk in construction contracting is **misclassification** — treating an employee as an independent contractor, a "casual", or a subcontractor's worker in order to avoid statutory cost. Both jurisdictions apply substance over form, with statutory presumptions that make the employer's label almost irrelevant.

> ⚠️ Legally consequential. A contract labelled "independent contractor", "casual" or "piece worker" does not determine status. If the reality is employment, every statutory entitlement — leave, overtime, notice, severance, social security, compensation cover — applies retrospectively, with penalties.

## Key facts

| Contract type | **[NA]** position | **[ZA]** position |
|---|---|---|
| **Permanent / indefinite** | Default; terminable on s.30 notice for a fair reason | Default; terminable on s.37 BCEA notice for a fair reason under LRA s.188 |
| **Fixed-term (date-ended)** | Permitted; repeated renewal risks a reasonable expectation of renewal and a s.33 dismissal claim | For **below-threshold** employees, > 3 months only on justifiable grounds (LRA s.198B); otherwise deemed indefinite |
| **Project-based (event-ended)** | Permitted; the completing event must be objectively identifiable | A "specific project of limited duration" is an express justifiable ground under s.198B |
| **Casual / short engagement** | No separate "casual" category in the Act; the Act applies from day one | No "casual" category; employees working < 24 h/month are excluded from some BCEA provisions only |
| **Piece / task work** | Lawful basis of pay, not a status; s.10 conversion tables apply; minimum wage still applies per hour worked | Lawful basis of pay; the national minimum wage applies per **ordinary hour worked** |
| **Independent contractor** | Genuine only where the substance is contracting; s.128A presumption (*section number needs verification*) | Presumed employee where any listed factor is present — LRA s.200A / BCEA s.83A |
| **Learner / apprentice** | VET Act contract of apprenticeship | Learnership agreement under the Skills Development Act; a learner is an employee for most purposes |
| **Temporary employment service (labour broker)** | Regulated by the Employment Services Act 8 of 2011 | LRA s.198; deemed client employment after 3 months for below-threshold employees (s.198A) |

## 1. What a written contract must contain

Both statutes require substantially the same minimum content. Draft to the higher standard and use it in both countries.

1. **Full names and addresses** of employer and employee, and the employee's identity/passport number and work permit where applicable.
2. **Place of work** — and, for construction, an express clause permitting deployment to any site of the employer within a defined area, with the transport and subsistence consequences stated.
3. **Job title and a description of the work** — attach the job description.
4. **Date of commencement**, and for a fixed term or project contract, the **date or event of termination** stated precisely.
5. **Ordinary hours and days of work**, including the shift pattern and the site start and finish times.
6. **Wage or salary**, the rate, and the **basis** — per hour, per day, per task, per month — plus the method of calculating overtime, Sunday and public holiday pay.
7. **Pay interval and pay day**, and the method of payment (bank transfer, mobile money, cash).
8. **Allowances and payments in kind** — travel, subsistence, accommodation, danger or height allowance, tool allowance — and their value.
9. **Deductions** the employer will make, itemised.
10. **Leave** — annual, sick, compassionate/family responsibility, maternity/parental — expressed as the statutory entitlement or better.
11. **Notice** required from each side.
12. **Probation** period, if any, and how it will be assessed.
13. **Disciplinary code and grievance procedure** — incorporate by reference and hand over a copy at induction.
14. **Confidentiality**, and where justified at supervisory level, restraint of trade — narrowly drawn.
15. **Company property and tools** — issue list, condition, and the (lawful) basis on which deductions may be made for loss or damage.
16. **Health and safety obligations** — compliance with the site health and safety plan, PPE, drug and alcohol policy, reporting of injuries and near misses.
17. **A statement of the collective agreement or bargaining council** that applies, if any **[ZA]**.
18. **Signature of both parties, dated**, with a note recording the language in which the contract was explained if the employee's literacy required it.

**[ZA]** s.30 BCEA additionally requires the employer to display a statement of employees' rights; s.31 requires records to be kept for **three years**.

A contract skeleton is in file `10`.

## 2. Contract types in practice

### 2.1 Permanent (indefinite)

Right for the core team you intend to keep between projects: contracts manager, site agent, foremen, key artisans, storeman, office staff. Cheaper than it looks — it removes the administrative churn described in file `00` and it is the only way to retain the people who make the firm's margin. The cost is that ending it for lack of work is an **operational-requirements dismissal** requiring **[NA]** s.34 or **[ZA]** s.189 process and severance.

### 2.2 Fixed-term (date-ended)

Right where the need genuinely has a date: a seasonal peak, cover for maternity leave, a defined 90-day finishing programme. **[ZA]** for employees earning below the earnings threshold, LRA s.198B permits a fixed term longer than three months only on a justifiable ground, and a "justifiable ground" must be real and provable. If it is not, the employee is **deemed employed indefinitely**. Where a justified fixed term for a project runs longer than **24 months**, severance of one week per completed year is payable on expiry.

**[NA]** there is no equivalent statutory cap, but non-renewal where the employee reasonably expected renewal will be tested against s.33.

### 2.3 Project-based (event-ended)

The construction-native form and usually the correct one. Requirements for it to hold:
- The **project must be identified** — name, erf number, client, scope.
- The **completing event must be objectively ascertainable**, not left to the employer's opinion. "Until completion of the brickwork to wall plate level at Erf 1234" works. "Until the employer no longer requires your services" does not — that is an indefinite contract with a disguised dismissal clause.
- The employee must be told, and it must be recorded, that employment ends when the event occurs.
- If the employee is moved to a different project, issue a **new contract** or a written variation — do not let the original run on silently.

### 2.4 Casual and piece/task work

Neither statute recognises "casual" as a status that removes rights. A person engaged for three days is an employee for those three days: minimum wage applies, hours must be recorded, overtime is payable, and social security/compensation registration applies.

**Piece and task rates** are a lawful *basis of pay*: N$/m² of plaster, R/m³ of trench, a rate per door hung. They must still deliver at least the minimum wage for **every ordinary hour actually worked**, so the employer must record hours as well as output. Task work also creates a quality incentive problem — pay on **measured and accepted** work, with an inspection step before payment, or it will produce speed at the cost of tolerance.

### 2.5 Learners and apprentices

**[NA]** apprenticeship contracts under the transitional provisions of the VET Act 1 of 2008 (s.49(12)). **[ZA]** learnership agreements registered with the SETA are tripartite (employer, learner, training provider) and the learner is treated as an employee for most BCEA/LRA purposes, with a learnership determination governing minimum allowances. See file `06`.

## 3. Induction and site orientation

Induction is a **legal obligation, not a courtesy**. **[ZA]** reg 9 of the Construction Regulations 2014 requires that all employees be informed, instructed and trained by a competent person about the hazards and control measures **before work commences**, and thereafter as the monitoring and review plan requires. **[NA]** GN 156 of 1997 imposes equivalent duties of instruction and supervision.

**The induction pack — deliver on day one, before the first hour of work:**

1. **Site rules and access control** — gate procedure, signing in and out, prohibited areas, working hours, no unauthorised visitors.
2. **The health and safety plan in summary** — the site's top five risks and what is done about them.
3. **Emergency arrangements** — assembly point, first aider names, first aid station location (**[NA]** marked with a green cross on a white background, reg 231 GN 156), fire extinguisher locations, emergency numbers, nearest clinic/hospital and how the injured are transported.
4. **PPE issue** — hard hat, boots, high-visibility vest, gloves, eye protection, hearing protection and any task-specific equipment, against a **signed issue register**. The employer pays; **[ZA]** s.33A BCEA prohibits charging the employee.
5. **Incident and near-miss reporting** — who to tell, immediately, and the fact that reporting is protected.
6. **Task-specific hazard briefing** for the work the person will actually do.
7. **Disciplinary code and grievance procedure** — handed over and explained.
8. **Introduction to the chargehand and foreman**, and confirmation of the reporting line.
9. **Site tour** — welfare facilities, drinking water, eating area, toilets, store, muster point.
10. **Confirmation of understanding** — a signed induction record naming the inductor, the date, the topics covered, and the language used. Where literacy is limited, use pictorial material and record that it was explained verbally.

Induct **every person who enters the site to work** — including subcontractors' employees, delivery drivers who go beyond the gate, and the client's own people. Keep the register in the health and safety file.

## 5. Misclassification — the biggest single exposure

### 5.1 The statutory presumptions

**[ZA]** LRA s.200A and BCEA s.83A: regardless of the form of the contract, a person who earns below the earnings threshold is **presumed to be an employee** if any one of the following is present —
- the manner in which the person works is subject to the control or direction of another;
- their hours of work are subject to the control or direction of another;
- in the case of a person who works for an organisation, the person forms **part of that organisation**;
- the person has worked for that other person for an average of at least **40 hours per month over the last three months**;
- the person is **economically dependent** on the person for whom they work;
- the person is **provided with tools of trade or work equipment** by the other person;
- the person **only works for or renders services to one person**.

The presumption is rebuttable, but the burden sits on the employer.

**[NA]** the Labour Act contains an equivalent presumption of employment inserted by amendment — commonly cited as **s.128A** — using substantially the same factors. *The section number needs verification against a current consolidation.* Namibian courts apply the **dominant impression test**: control, integration into the organisation, economic dependence, provision of tools, exclusivity, the right to delegate, and who bears the risk of profit and loss.

### 5.2 How construction gets it wrong

- **The "own-account" bricklayer.** Turns up daily, works the employer's hours, uses the employer's mixer, scaffold and trowel, takes instruction from the foreman, works for nobody else, is paid weekly. He is an employee whatever his invoice says.
- **The labour-only gang boss.** Recruits five men, is paid a lump sum per square metre, but the employer directs the sequence and hours, provides all materials and plant, and the gang works on no other site. Risk: the employer is the true employer of all six.
- **The "commission-only" sales or estimating role.** Usually an employee.
- **"Consultants" who are ex-employees doing the same job.** The classic revenue and labour inspectorate target.

### 5.3 The consequences

- Retrospective liability for **leave, overtime, notice, severance** and public holiday pay.
- Retrospective **PAYE, UIF and SDL** **[ZA]** or **PAYE and social security** **[NA]**, with interest and penalties, recovered from the employer.
- **Compensation fund** exposure: an unregistered "contractor" who is injured is your employee for COIDA/ECF purposes, and if you are not in good standing you lose the s.35 COIDA shield against a civil claim.
- **[ZA]** bargaining council levies and fund contributions for the whole period, recoverable with interest.
- Unfair dismissal jurisdiction: the CCMA or Labour Commissioner will hear the claim once employment status is established.

### 5.4 How to stay on the right side

A genuine independent contractor: has their own registered business and tax number; carries their **own** compensation cover and public liability insurance; supplies their **own** tools, plant and often materials; controls **how** and largely **when** the work is done; may send a substitute; bears the risk of defective work at their own cost; works for other clients; invoices against a scope and priced deliverable, not against hours attended; is not subject to the employer's disciplinary code.

Where you want that relationship, **write a subcontract, not an employment contract** — see file `09` — and then behave consistently with it. The paperwork is the smaller half; the behaviour is what a tribunal looks at.
