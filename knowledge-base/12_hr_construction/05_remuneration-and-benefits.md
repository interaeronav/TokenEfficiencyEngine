# Remuneration and benefits in construction

**Summary.** Construction pay is built from a base rate, a set of site allowances, and premium payments for time worked outside ordinary hours. The base can be hourly, daily, monthly or task-based; the statutory floor is the national minimum wage per **ordinary hour worked** in both countries, raised where a bargaining council agreement applies **[ZA]**. Overtime is **1.5 ×** in both jurisdictions, Sunday **2 ×**, and public holidays attract a premium. Deductions are tightly controlled: capped at **one third** of remuneration **[NA]** s.12 Labour Act, and permitted only on defined grounds **[ZA]** s.34 BCEA. Paying a rural workforce is a logistics problem in its own right.

> ⚠️ Legally consequential. All rand and Namibian dollar amounts, levy percentages, tax tables and contribution ceilings in this file are **illustrative or unverified**. Confirm current figures with SARS, the Department of Employment and Labour, the Namibian Ministry of Finance, the Social Security Commission and the applicable bargaining council before running a payroll.

## Key facts

| Item | **[NA]** Namibia | **[ZA]** South Africa |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary hours | 45 h/week (s.16) | 45 h/week (s.9 BCEA) |
| Overtime rate | ≥ 1.5 × basic hourly wage (s.17) | 1.5 × wage (s.10 BCEA) |
| Overtime cap | 10 h/week | 10 h/week (15 by collective agreement, limited period) |
| Sunday | 2 × hourly basic wage (s.21) | 2 ×, or 1.5 × if ordinarily works Sundays (s.16 BCEA) |
| Public holiday | Normal daily remuneration + hourly basic wage for hours worked (s.22) | Paid day off; 2 × if worked (s.18 BCEA) |
| Night work | +6 % for 20h00–07h00 (s.19) | Allowance or reduced hours, 18h00–06h00 (s.17 BCEA) |
| Pay timing | ≤ 1 hour after the working day ends (s.11) | On the agreed day, in cash/cheque/bank transfer (s.32 BCEA) |
| Deduction cap | **One third** of remuneration (s.12) | Damage/loss deduction ≤ ¼ of remuneration and ≤ actual loss (s.34 BCEA) |
| Payslip | Required as part of particulars and records | Required at each payment (s.33 BCEA) |
| Record retention | Employer must keep records | **3 years** (s.31 BCEA) |
| Statutory deductions from the employee | PAYE; social security employee share | PAYE; UIF employee share |
| Employer-only charges | Social security employer share; Employees' Compensation Fund assessment; **VET levy 1 %** where annual payroll ≥ **N$1 000 000** | UIF employer share; COIDA assessment; **SDL** on payroll above the exemption threshold; bargaining council levies and fund contributions |

## 1. Choosing a pay basis

| Basis | Use for | Advantages | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hourly** | Artisans, plant operators, most site labour | Directly matches attendance; simple overtime maths | Pays for presence, not output; requires reliable time recording |
| **Daily** | Casual peaks, general labour | Simple, well understood, matches how workers think | A "day" must still be converted to hours for minimum wage and overtime; short days create disputes |
| **Weekly wage** | Chargehands, foremen on smaller firms | Predictable | Must still record hours |
| **Monthly salary** | Site agents, managers, QS, office | Administratively cheap | Senior staff may be excluded from BCEA hours provisions above the earnings threshold **[ZA]** — check before assuming no overtime is payable |
| **Task / piece rate** | Plastering, tiling, painting, block laying, trench excavation, roof sheeting | Strong productivity signal; transfers some programme risk | Quality collapse if not inspected; still must satisfy minimum wage per hour; measurement disputes |
| **Composite "all-in" rate** | Subcontract gangs | Simple pricing | Almost always a misclassification trap if the people are in reality your employees (file `04`) |

**Task rates in practice.** Set the rate from a measured output rate: if a competent plasterer places 12–18 m² of 15 mm plaster a day, and the target daily earnings are X, the rate per m² follows. Then:
- **Measure and accept before paying.** Build an inspection into the payment cycle (`02_building_construction/13_quality-defects-and-tolerances.md` for the acceptance criteria).
- **Record hours anyway.** You need them for the minimum-wage floor, for overtime, and for injury-on-duty earnings calculations.
- **Do not let task rates drive unsafe speed.** Where a task rate is paid for work at height or in excavations, expect corner-cutting and supervise accordingly.

## 2. Allowances

Allowances are contractual, not statutory (unless a bargaining council agreement imposes them **[ZA]**). Common construction allowances:

- **Travel / transport.** Either a per-kilometre or per-day allowance, or employer-provided transport. On remote sites, employer transport is usually cheaper and safer; but the vehicle must be licensed and suitable — **transporting workers in the open load bed of a bakkie is unlawful or heavily restricted** and should be treated as prohibited.
- **Subsistence / living-out allowance.** Paid where the employee is away from home overnight. Both tax systems allow a deemed daily subsistence amount free of tax within prescribed limits — *verify the current daily amounts with SARS / Namibian Inland Revenue*.
- **Accommodation.** **[ZA]** reg 30 of the Construction Regulations 2014 obliges the contractor to provide reasonable and suitable living accommodation for workers far removed from their homes where transport or other accommodation is not available. If accommodation is provided in kind, state its value in the contract; note **[ZA]** s.39 BCEA on employees in employer-provided accommodation and notice.
- **Danger / height / confined space allowance.** Common for steel erectors, roof sheeters, riggers and shaft work. A premium does not substitute for control measures.
- **Tool allowance.** For artisans supplying their own hand tools. Never charge an employee for PPE — **[ZA]** s.33A BCEA prohibits it and it is contrary to OHS duties in both countries.
- **Standby / call-out.** For plant fitters and maintenance staff.
- **Acting / responsibility allowance.** For a leading hand temporarily running a gang.

## 3. Overtime calculation — worked examples

Take an employee on a basic hourly rate of **R** per hour, working a five-day, 45-hour week (9 hours/day).

**Ordinary week, no overtime**: 45 × R.

**Two hours of overtime on Wednesday**: 45 × R + (2 × 1.5 × R) = 45R + 3R = 48R equivalent hours.

**Saturday morning, 5 hours, not an ordinary working day**: 5 × 1.5 × R = 7.5R (assuming the weekly ordinary hours are already exhausted).

**Sunday, 6 hours, employee does not ordinarily work Sundays**: 6 × 2 × R = 12R. **[ZA]** if the employee works less than their ordinary shift on a Sunday they must be paid the greater of double the hours worked or their ordinary daily wage.

**Public holiday, 8 hours worked, employee ordinarily works that day**: **[ZA]** the employee is entitled to their ordinary day's pay for the holiday **plus** payment for hours worked, giving effectively double time. **[NA]** s.22 gives normal daily remuneration plus the hourly basic wage for hours worked.

**Night shift 20h00–05h00 [NA]**: the hours falling between 20h00 and 07h00 attract the additional 6 % under s.19, in addition to any overtime premium.

**Daily-paid worker.** Convert the daily rate to an hourly rate using the contractual ordinary hours per day before applying premiums. **[NA]** s.10 provides the statutory conversion tables — use them rather than an ad hoc divisor.

**Task-rate worker.** Overtime is calculated on the notional hourly rate derived from the task earnings for the period, not on nothing. This is where most task-rate payrolls are legally wrong.

## 4. Bonuses and productivity incentives

- **Completion bonus.** A fixed amount payable on the employee remaining to the end of a defined phase and the work passing inspection. Effective against the end-of-project attrition that leaves a site with no one to do the snags.
- **Programme / milestone bonus.** Paid to a gang for hitting a milestone **with zero rework and zero lost-time injuries**. Always couple productivity bonuses to quality and safety conditions, or you buy accidents.
- **Attendance bonus.** Blunt but powerful where absenteeism is the constraint. Must be drafted so that statutory leave — annual, sick with a certificate, maternity, compassionate — does not forfeit it, otherwise it is an unlawful penalty on the exercise of a statutory right.
- **13th cheque / annual bonus.** Common for monthly staff. If it is contractual it is enforceable; if it is discretionary, say so in writing every year, or custom will make it contractual.
- **Retention / loyalty payment.** For scarce trades on long projects.

> ⚠️ Never structure an incentive that pays more when incidents go unreported. Safety bonuses tied to "zero recorded injuries" reliably suppress reporting. Tie them to leading indicators — inspections completed, toolbox talks held, near misses **reported** and closed out.

## 5. Payroll administration

A minimum viable construction payroll cycle:

1. **Daily**: foreman completes the site time sheet — name, employee number, trade, hours in/out, activity/cost code, absence reason. Signed by the employee or marked with a witnessed thumbprint where literacy is limited.
2. **Weekly**: time sheets collected, checked against the site diary and gate register, queries resolved **before** they become disputes, hours captured.
3. **Per cycle**: gross calculated (base + overtime + premiums + allowances + task earnings), statutory deductions computed, voluntary deductions applied within the cap, net paid, payslip issued.
4. **Monthly**: statutory returns and payments filed.
5. **Annually**: reconciliation and tax certificates; return of earnings **[ZA]** COIDA s.82; VET levy claim window **[NA]**.

**Cost coding matters.** Every hour should carry an activity or cost code so that labour cost lands against the correct programme activity for the valuation and for the next estimate (`02_building_construction/11_estimating-and-measurement.md`).

## 6. Statutory deductions and employer charges

### 6.1 **[NA]** Namibia

| Charge | Who pays | Basis | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PAYE** | Employee (withheld) | Progressive tax tables | Namibia Revenue Agency |
| **Social security (MSD Fund)** | **Employer and employee**, split equally | A percentage of basic wage between a prescribed floor and ceiling | Social Security Commission, s.21 Social Security Act 34 of 1994 |
| **Employees' Compensation Fund** | **Employer only** | Annual assessment on payroll × industry risk class | Social Security Commission |
| **VET levy** | **Employer only** | **1 % of actual annual payroll**, for employers with an annual payroll of **N$1 000 000 or more**; payable **monthly in arrears by the 20th** of the following month | National Training Fund / Namibia Training Authority |
| Union subscription, medical aid, pension | Employee, by written consent | As agreed | Third party |

The commonly quoted social security figures — **0.9 % employer + 0.9 % employee** of basic wage, subject to a minimum and maximum monthly basic wage — could **not be verified** in this pass and are `needs-verification`.

**VET levy claim-back.** Levy-paying employers may claim training costs back through the NTA's **Employer Training Grant**: eligible costs include facilitator fees, materials, assessment and certification, and subsistence and travel allowances for trainees. Claims must be submitted **within 31 days after the financial year end (31 March)**, and the employer must be up to date on levy payments with no outstanding penalties. Late levy payment attracts interest (s.38 VET Act) and a **10 % penalty** (s.39).

### 6.2 **[ZA]** South Africa

| Charge | Who pays | Basis | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PAYE** | Employee (withheld) | Tax tables | SARS, monthly EMP201 |
| **UIF** | **Employer and employee**, 1 % each | Remuneration up to a monthly ceiling | SARS / UIF; monthly UI-19 declarations |
| **SDL** | **Employer only** | ~**1 %** of leviable payroll; employers below the payroll exemption threshold are exempt (ss.3, 4 SDLA); paid to SARS (s.6); distributed to SETAs and the National Skills Fund (ss.8–9) | CETA and the NSF |
| **COIDA assessment** | **Employer only** | Annual assessment on the return of earnings × risk-class tariff (ss.82, 83, 85, 86) | Compensation Fund |
| **Bargaining council levies and funds** | Employer and employee, per the agreement | Council levy, holiday/leave pay fund, sick pay fund, provident fund | The council (e.g. BCCEI) |
| Union subscription, medical aid, retirement | Employee, by written consent or agreement | As agreed | Third party; **s.34A BCEA** requires the employer to pay over benefit-fund contributions within the prescribed period |

The **1 % UIF each**, the **UIF monthly earnings ceiling**, the **1 % SDL** and the **R500 000 payroll exemption threshold** are quoted from general practice and are `needs-verification`.

> ⚠️ **[ZA]** s.34A BCEA — failing to pay over deducted benefit-fund contributions is a criminal offence and directors can be held personally liable. Deducted money is not working capital.

## 7. Payslip requirements

**[ZA]** s.33 BCEA requires written information about remuneration at each payment, containing at minimum: employer's name and address; employee's name and occupation; the period for which payment is made; the employee's remuneration in money; the amount and purpose of any deduction; the actual amount paid; and, if relevant to the calculation, the employee's rate of pay and overtime rate, the number of ordinary and overtime hours worked in the period, the number of hours worked on a Sunday or public holiday, and — where an averaging agreement applies — the total ordinary and overtime hours worked in the averaging period.

**[NA]** the Labour Act requires equivalent particulars and record-keeping; build the payslip to the South African specification and it will satisfy both.

Payslips must be given **in a sealed envelope or otherwise privately**, at the workplace, during working hours or within 15 minutes of the start or end of them. A payslip template is in file `10`.

## 8. Paying a rural workforce

On a site like Okongo in Ohangwena, or a rural Eastern Cape or Limpopo contract, the constraints are real: the nearest bank branch may be 60–200 km away, network coverage is patchy, and a proportion of the workforce has no bank account or no documentation to open one.

**Options, in order of preference:**

1. **Bank transfer to individual accounts.** Cheapest and safest at scale. Help new employees open **basic/entry-level accounts** — in Namibia through Bank Windhoek, FNB, Standard Bank and Nedbank basic accounts; in South Africa through Capitec, Postbank/SASSA-linked accounts, TymeBank and similar. Budget one paid half-day for account opening during onboarding; it pays for itself in the first cycle.
2. **Mobile money.** Namibia: **MTC MoneyPlus/Blue Wallet** and bank-linked e-wallets; South Africa: FNB **eWallet**, Capitec send-cash, Shoprite/Checkers money transfer and Pep Money — the retailer-based cash-out networks reach villages that banks do not. Confirm the recipient's registered number carefully; a digit error sends money to a stranger and is usually irrecoverable.
3. **Cash, as a last resort.** If you must pay cash:
   - Use a **registered cash-in-transit service** if the amount justifies it.
   - **Vary the day, route and time**; never announce the pay run.
   - Two people minimum, no lone runs, no advertising the vehicle.
   - Count and pack into individual **sealed, named envelopes off site**.
   - Pay against **signature or witnessed thumbprint** on a pay register, one employee at a time, in a private space.
   - Keep the pay register — it is your proof of payment.
   - Insure cash in transit and on premises; check the policy limit against the actual payroll.

**Practical mitigations:** pay fortnightly rather than weekly to halve the number of cash exposures; offer a small **savings deduction** by written consent so workers are not carrying a month's wages home; arrange a mobile bank or ATM visit on pay day where the volume justifies it; and coordinate pay day with the local market day so people can convert wages into goods without a second journey.

> ⚠️ Cash pay runs are the single largest violent-crime exposure on a rural construction project. Treat the pay run as a risk-assessed activity with a written procedure, not an errand.
