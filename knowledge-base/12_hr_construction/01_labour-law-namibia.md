# Namibian labour law for construction employers

**Summary.** The **Labour Act 11 of 2007** is the operative statute for every employment relationship in Namibia, including construction. It sets a 45-hour ordinary week, overtime at one and a half times basic wage, four consecutive weeks' annual leave, 30 days' sick leave per 36-month cycle, five days' compassionate leave, twelve weeks' maternity leave, notice periods graded by service, severance pay of one week per year of continuous service, and a fairness test for every dismissal. Around it sit the **Social Security Act 34 of 1994**, the **Employees' Compensation Act 30 of 1941** (as amended), the **Affirmative Action (Employment) Act 29 of 1998**, the **Employment Services Act 8 of 2011**, and the **Vocational Education and Training Act 1 of 2008**. Section numbers below are taken from the Legal Assistance Centre's annotated consolidation.

> ⚠️ Legally consequential. Section numbers and entitlements are cited from the LAC annotated text of the Labour Act 11 of 2007. Amendments (notably the Labour Amendment Act 2 of 2012) and gazetted wage orders may have changed the position. Confirm against the current Government Gazette before acting on any figure.

## 1. Fundamental protections (Chapter 2)

- **s.3** prohibits child labour. No child under 14 may be employed; 14–16 year olds may not do work in construction; and restrictions apply up to 18, including night work and hazardous work. In construction this is effectively an absolute bar on anyone under 18 on an active site.
- **s.4** prohibits forced labour.
- **s.5** prohibits discrimination on grounds including race, sex, religion, marital status, family responsibilities, disability, and **HIV status** — see file `08`.
- **s.6** protects freedom of association and trade union membership.
- **s.7** routes disputes over these fundamental rights to the Labour Commissioner.

## 2. Contracts of employment

The Act does not require a contract to be in writing to be valid, but it does require the employer to give the employee written particulars and to keep records. A construction contract of employment should state: the parties; the place(s) of work (which for construction should be drafted to allow deployment between sites); the job title and duties; the date of commencement; the type and duration of the contract; ordinary hours and days; the rate of pay and the pay interval; overtime, Sunday and public-holiday arrangements; deductions; leave; notice; and the disciplinary and grievance procedure. See file `04`.

**Fixed-term and project contracts** are permissible. The Act does not impose the South African three-month justification test. The risk is instead that an indefinitely renewed fixed-term contract creates a reasonable expectation of renewal, and that non-renewal is then attacked as a dismissal under s.33. Draft the completing event precisely ("on practical completion of the superstructure works at Erf 1234, Okongo") and avoid serial renewals.

**Probation.** A probationary period is lawful and common (typically one to three months), but probation does **not** suspend the fairness requirement. A probationer dismissed for poor performance must still have been told the standard, given a fair opportunity to meet it, and heard.

## 3. Working time (Chapter 3, ss.16–22)

Ordinary hours are capped at **45 per week** (s.16). Overtime is capped at **10 hours per week** and must be paid at **not less than one and a half times** the basic wage (s.17). This matters on construction sites where the programme pushes long days: a 07h00–17h00 six-day week already breaches the ordinary-hours ceiling and generates overtime liability.

A **meal interval of at least one hour** is required after five hours of continuous work (s.18). Night work between 20h00 and 07h00 attracts a **6 % additional payment** (s.19). Sunday work is paid at **double** the hourly basic wage (s.21). Work on a public holiday is paid at the employee's normal daily remuneration **plus** the hourly basic wage for hours worked (s.22).

**Public holidays** are set by the Public Holidays Act 26 of 1990: New Year's Day, Independence Day (21 March), Good Friday, Easter Monday, Workers' Day (1 May), Cassinga Day (4 May), Africa Day (25 May), Ascension Day, Heroes' Day (26 August), Human Rights Day (10 December — *formerly Women's Day / Human Rights Day; verify current designation*), Christmas Day and Family Day (26 December). Where a public holiday falls on a Sunday, the following Monday is a public holiday.

> ⚠️ Time records are the employer's evidence. Keep a daily site time sheet signed by the employee or their foreman. In a dispute over unpaid overtime, an employer who cannot produce records will usually lose.

## 4. Leave (ss.23–26)

- **Annual leave (s.23):** not less than **four consecutive weeks** of paid leave per annual leave cycle for an employee working five days a week; pro rata for shorter weeks. Leave may not be substituted by payment except on termination.
- **Sick leave (s.24):** not less than **30 working days** in a 36-month cycle for a five-day-week employee (36 days for a six-day week). During the first 12 months, entitlement typically accrues at one day per 26 days worked. A medical certificate may be required for absences exceeding the prescribed threshold.
- **Compassionate leave (s.25):** **five working days** in a 12-month period on the death or serious illness of a defined close family member. This provision is heavily used in construction workforces with extended-family obligations; apply it consistently and record it.
- **Maternity leave (s.26):** **12 weeks** — four weeks before the expected date of confinement and eight weeks after. Employment may not be terminated because of pregnancy. Payment during maternity leave comes from the **Social Security Commission's** Maternity Leave, Sick Leave and Death Benefit Fund, not from the employer, provided the employee is registered and has the required contribution history.

## 5. Remuneration and deductions (ss.10–12)

Section 10 provides the conversion tables between hourly, daily, weekly and monthly pay — essential when a construction workforce is on mixed bases. Section 11 requires payment **not later than one hour after completion of the working day** on the agreed pay day, in Namibian currency, at or near the workplace.

Section 12 controls **deductions**. Deductions are permitted only where required or permitted by law (PAYE, social security, a garnishee or court order), or with the employee's written consent for a specific amount, and the aggregate may not exceed **one third** of remuneration. Deductions for damaged tools, lost materials or breakages require written agreement and, in practice, a fair process establishing fault — a blanket "damage deduction" clause is not enforceable on its own.

**Minimum wage.** A national minimum wage has been introduced under the Labour Act's wage-order machinery. Reporting places it at **N$18.00 per hour with effect from 1 January 2025**, but this figure could **not be verified** against the Government Gazette in this pass — treat as `needs-verification` and confirm the current rate and any construction-specific wage order with the Ministry of Labour before pricing labour.

## 6. Termination, unfair dismissal and retrenchment

**Notice (s.30)** is graded: one day for service of four weeks or less; one week for more than four weeks up to one year; one month for more than one year. Notice must be in writing (except to an illiterate employee, where it must be explained). Payment in lieu of notice is permitted.

**Severance pay (s.35):** an employee with more than 12 months' continuous service is entitled to severance of **at least one week's remuneration for each completed year of continuous service** where they are dismissed (other than for misconduct or poor performance following a fair process), die in service, or resign on reaching the age of 65.

**Unfair dismissal (s.33):** a dismissal is unfair unless the employer proves both a **valid and fair reason** and that a **fair procedure** was followed. Valid reasons fall into the familiar three categories: misconduct, incapacity (poor performance or ill health), and operational requirements. Certain dismissals are automatically unfair — for trade union membership or activity, for pregnancy, for exercising a right under the Act, for HIV status, or for participating in a lawful strike. The **onus is on the employer**.

**Collective termination / retrenchment (s.34):** where an employer intends to dismiss employees for operational reasons, it must give **at least four weeks' written notice** to the Labour Commissioner, the affected employees and their union, and must negotiate in good faith over the need for the retrenchment, selection criteria, timing, and severance. Selection criteria must be fair and objective — LIFO, skills retention, and disciplinary record are the usual defensible ones. On a construction project the honest reason is often "the works are complete" — but if the workforce was on indefinite contracts, that is an operational-requirements dismissal and s.34 applies.

## 7. Disciplinary and grievance procedure

The Act does not prescribe a code, but s.33's procedural fairness requirement imports the standard elements: an investigation; a written notice of the allegation and of the hearing with adequate time to prepare; the right to be heard; the right to representation by a fellow employee or union representative; an impartial chairperson; a decision communicated with reasons; and a right of appeal or referral. See file `07` for the operating procedure and templates.

## 8. The Labour Commissioner, conciliation and arbitration

Disputes are referred to the **Labour Commissioner** (ss.82–90 and Chapter 8). The process is: refer the dispute on the prescribed form within the applicable time limit (six months for most disputes; **30 days** for a dispute about dismissal — *verify the current period*); the Commissioner appoints a **conciliator/arbitrator**; conciliation is attempted; if unresolved, the same or another arbitrator conducts a **compulsory arbitration** and issues a binding award. Arbitration awards may be enforced as orders of the **Labour Court** and are reviewable by it (s.115 and Chapter 9). Legal representation at arbitration is restricted in some categories of dispute.

The process is free, relatively quick, and heavily used by construction workers. The employer's defence is nearly always documentary: contract, time records, warnings, hearing notice, minutes, outcome letter.

## 9. Social Security Act 34 of 1994

The Act establishes four funds: the **Maternity Leave, Sick Leave and Death Benefit Fund** (s.28), the **National Medical Benefit Fund** (s.32, not in force), the **National Pension Fund** (s.34, not in force) and the **Development Fund** (s.37). Section 20 obliges every employer to **register itself and every employee** with the Commission. Section 21 obliges employer and employee to pay the **prescribed contributions**.

The rates are set by regulation, not in the Act. The widely used figures are **1.8 % of basic wage, split 0.9 % employer and 0.9 % employee**, applied to a basic wage between a prescribed floor and ceiling, with contributions payable by the 30th of the following month. **These percentages and thresholds could not be verified in this pass** — confirm the current rate, minimum and maximum monthly basic wage, and maximum contribution directly with the Social Security Commission.

The Development Fund finances training and employment-creation schemes, including the Employment and Skills Development grant schemes relevant to construction apprentices (file `06`).

## 10. Employees' Compensation Act 30 of 1941 (as amended)

Administered by the Social Security Commission as the **Employees' Compensation Fund (ECF)**. Every employer must register and pay an annual assessment based on payroll and industry risk class — construction is a high-risk class. Employees make **no contribution**. Benefits cover medical treatment, temporary total disablement (a percentage of earnings), permanent disablement (a pension or lump sum according to degree), and death benefits to dependants.

**Reporting:** the employee must report the accident to the employer as soon as practicable; the employer must report to the Commission within the prescribed period. Claims are subject to prescription periods. See file `08` for the operating procedure.

> ⚠️ An unregistered employer remains liable for the full cost of an injury, plus penalties, and loses the statutory protection against a civil claim. Registration and good standing must be verified for every subcontractor before they come on site (file `09`).

## 11. Affirmative Action (Employment) Act 29 of 1998

- **Designated groups (s.18):** racially disadvantaged persons; women; persons with disabilities.
- **Relevant employer (s.20):** identified by the Minister by Gazette notice, either by name or by criteria based on employee numbers. The commonly applied threshold is **50 or more employees** — *verify against the current notice*.
- **Affirmative action plan (s.23):** a three-year plan setting out measures to remove barriers, numerical goals for representation, timetables, and a **designated senior employee** accountable for implementation.
- **Consultation (s.24):** with employee representatives and trade unions.
- **Reporting (s.27):** first report within **18 months** of being identified as a relevant employer, thereafter **annually**.
- **Employment Equity Commission (ss.3, 6).**
- **Compliance certificate (s.41):** valid until the next report is due or a report is disapproved or not submitted. **This certificate is required for government tenders** — a construction firm without one is excluded from public work.
- **Penalties (s.47):** fines up to N$16 000 or four years' imprisonment for obstruction or false statements; graduated penalties for other contraventions.

## 12. Employment Services Act 8 of 2011

- Establishes the **Employment Services Bureau** (s.13) and an integrated employment information system (s.14) registering job seekers and vacancies.
- **s.16:** designated employers must notify the Bureau of vacancies within the prescribed period and consider referred candidates in good faith. Penalties up to **N$20 000** or two years.
- **s.17:** employers must submit an employer profile including workforce demographics and salary information.
- **ss.19–22:** no person may operate as a **private employment agency** without a licence; licences are issued by the Director and may be cancelled after 30 days' notice.
- **s.24:** an agency may not charge the job seeker, and an employer may not deduct a placement fee from wages.
- **s.26:** placed workers must receive terms **not less favourable** than comparable employees of the user, and agencies may not place workers with employers who lack an affirmative action compliance certificate or who have compliance violations.

This is the statute that governs "labour hire" in Namibia after the Supreme Court struck down the outright ban in the *Africa Personnel Services* litigation. See file `09`.
