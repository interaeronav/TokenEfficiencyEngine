# The textbooks and manuals — the canonical medical bibliography

**Summary.** Medicine has an unusually stable canon: a few dozen books, most of them older than the doctors reading them, that define what a field's shared knowledge is. This file lists that canon by subject with an honest note on what each book is actually for — reference, course text, exam preparation, or ward pocket — because buying the wrong category is the commonest and most expensive student mistake. It then covers the third-party examination-preparation ecosystem that has partly displaced textbooks in undergraduate medicine, and the point-of-care databases that have entirely displaced them in practice.

> ⚠️ **Verify editions before purchase.** Medical textbooks revise on a 3–6 year cycle. Edition numbers in this file are given **only where they were confirmed against a publisher or reference source in this pass**, with the confirmation date; everywhere else the book is named without an edition number deliberately. Check the publisher's listing. Also: none of these books is a substitute for a clinician, and none of the therapeutic content in any of them is medical advice to any reader of this knowledge base.

## How to read this list

Every book below falls into one of five categories, and the category matters more than the title:

- **REFERENCE** — encyclopaedic, thousands of pages, meant to be consulted not read. Buying one as a student is almost always a mistake.
- **COURSE TEXT** — designed to be worked through over a term or a year, at the level a curriculum expects.
- **REVIEW / EXAM** — condensed, high-yield, organised for retrieval under time pressure. Excellent for passing, insufficient for understanding.
- **ATLAS** — images are the content.
- **POCKET / WARD** — designed to be carried and consulted in seconds during clinical work.

## Anatomy

- ***Gray's Anatomy*** (Elsevier; **42nd edition**, senior editor Susan Standring; 43rd announced for December 2025) — **REFERENCE**. First published in **1858**. This is the anatomists' and surgeons' reference, not a student book; students became "a secondary market" and were eventually spun off into their own title.
- ***Gray's Anatomy for Students*** (Drake, Vogl, Mitchell; Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The student derivative: clean regional organisation, good clinical boxes, excellent schematic art. The main modern competitor to Moore.
- ***Moore's Clinically Oriented Anatomy*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **COURSE TEXT**. The dominant course text in the Commonwealth, South Africa and much of the US. Strong on clinical correlation ("Blue Boxes"); dense prose. *Moore's Essential Clinical Anatomy* is the abridgement.
- ***Netter's Atlas of Human Anatomy*** (Elsevier) — **ATLAS**. Frank Netter's painted plates are the visual vocabulary of anatomy teaching worldwide. Idealised: real cadavers look nothing like this, which is the standing criticism.
- ***Rohen, Yokochi & Lütjen-Drecoll, Color Atlas of Anatomy*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **ATLAS**. Photographs of real dissections. The correct companion to Netter and much better preparation for a spotter examination.
- ***Snell's Clinical Anatomy by Regions*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **COURSE TEXT**. Concise, clinically framed, widely used in South Asian and some African curricula.
- ***Last's Anatomy: Regional and Applied*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE/COURSE TEXT**. The classic British surgical anatomy text; still favoured for MRCS and FCS preparation.
- ***Grant's Dissector*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **LAB MANUAL**. The step-by-step guide used at the table.
- ***Blumenfeld, Neuroanatomy through Clinical Cases*** (Sinauer/OUP) — **COURSE TEXT**. The best book for learning localisation rather than nomenclature.
- ***Junqueira's Basic Histology*** and ***Ross & Pawlina, Histology: A Text and Atlas*** — **COURSE TEXT**; ***Wheater's Functional Histology*** — **ATLAS**.
- ***Langman's Medical Embryology*** (Sadler) — **COURSE TEXT**, compact; ***Moore, The Developing Human*** — fuller.

## Physiology

- ***Guyton and Hall Textbook of Medical Physiology*** (Elsevier; **15th edition, 2025**) — **COURSE TEXT**. First published **1956**; the world's best-selling medical physiology text, translated into 13–15 languages. Its distinguishing quality is patient, mechanistic explanation at length. Written single-handedly by Arthur Guyton through the 8th edition (1991); John E. Hall co-authored from the 9th.
- ***Boron & Boulpaep, Medical Physiology*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT / REFERENCE**. More molecular, more rigorous, better cell physiology. The choice for students who want the biophysics to be real, and for MD-PhD candidates.
- ***Ganong's Review of Medical Physiology*** (McGraw-Hill) — **REVIEW / REFERENCE**. Dense, authoritative, compact; better as a look-up than a first read.
- ***Costanzo, Physiology*** and ***BRS Physiology*** — **REVIEW**. The efficient exam route.
- ***West's Respiratory Physiology*** and ***West's Pulmonary Pathophysiology*** — the short classics; **[note]** John B. West's respiratory books remain the standard single-system introduction.
- ***Lilly, Pathophysiology of Heart Disease*** — the best single bridge from cardiac physiology into cardiology.

## Biochemistry and metabolism

- ***Lehninger Principles of Biochemistry*** (Nelson & Cox; Macmillan) — **COURSE TEXT / REFERENCE**. The serious biochemistry text; more than a medical curriculum needs, and the right book if you want the chemistry.
- ***Harper's Illustrated Biochemistry*** (McGraw-Hill) — **COURSE TEXT**. The traditional medical biochemistry book; strong on clinical correlation, thin on mechanism.
- ***Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Biochemistry*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **REVIEW**. The book most medical students actually use; diagram-driven, ruthless about what to keep.
- ***Marks' Basic Medical Biochemistry: A Clinical Approach*** — **COURSE TEXT**. The best at integrating metabolism into whole-patient physiology.

## Genetics, cell and molecular biology

- ***Alberts et al., Molecular Biology of the Cell*** (Norton) — **REFERENCE**. The field's standard; *Essential Cell Biology* is the short version.
- ***Thompson & Thompson Genetics and Genomics in Medicine*** (Nussbaum; Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The medical genetics standard.
- ***Emery and Rimoin's Principles and Practice of Medical Genetics and Genomics*** — **REFERENCE**.

## Immunology

- ***Janeway's Immunobiology*** (Murphy & Weaver; Norton) — **COURSE TEXT / REFERENCE**. The intellectual reference; explains why the immune system is built as it is. Harder than most curricula require and worth it.
- ***Abbas, Lichtman & Pillai, Cellular and Molecular Immunology*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The medical-school standard; ***Basic Immunology*** is its short form.
- ***Roitt's Essential Immunology*** (Wiley) — **COURSE TEXT**, compact, good diagrams.

## Microbiology and infectious disease

- ***Jawetz, Melnick & Adelberg's Medical Microbiology*** (McGraw-Hill) — **COURSE TEXT**. The long-standing standard.
- ***Levinson, Review of Medical Microbiology and Immunology*** (McGraw-Hill) — **REVIEW**. Compact, exam-shaped, widely used for USMLE.
- ***Murray, Rosenthal & Pfaller, Medical Microbiology*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**, fuller and better organised by mechanism.
- ***Mandell, Douglas, and Bennett's Principles and Practice of Infectious Diseases*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. The ID specialist's encyclopaedia.
- ***The Sanford Guide to Antimicrobial Therapy*** — **POCKET**. The pocket antimicrobial reference used in North American practice; updated annually and available as an app.

## Pharmacology

- ***Katzung's Basic and Clinical Pharmacology*** (McGraw-Hill) — **COURSE TEXT**. The dominant US course text; clinically oriented, case-based.
- ***Rang & Dale's Pharmacology*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The dominant UK/Commonwealth text; stronger on receptor mechanism and drug-discovery context.
- ***Goodman & Gilman's The Pharmacological Basis of Therapeutics*** (McGraw-Hill) — **REFERENCE**. Encyclopaedic; the book you consult, not the book you study. First published 1941 and still the field's court of last resort.
- ***Lippincott Illustrated Reviews: Pharmacology*** — **REVIEW**. The efficient exam text.

## Pathology

- ***Robbins & Cotran Pathologic Basis of Disease*** (Kumar, Abbas & Aster; Elsevier; **10th edition, 18 May 2020**, ISBN 9780323531139) — **COURSE TEXT / REFERENCE**. "Big Robbins". The single most important book in the preclinical canon: it is where basic science becomes disease, and its general-pathology chapters are read by clinicians long after graduation.
- ***Robbins Basic Pathology*** ("baby Robbins") — **COURSE TEXT**. The abridgement most students actually read.
- ***Robbins Essential Pathology*** — **REVIEW**. Shorter again.
- ***Rosai and Ackerman's Surgical Pathology*** and ***Sternberg's Diagnostic Surgical Pathology*** — **REFERENCE**, for pathology trainees.

## Internal medicine

- ***Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine*** (McGraw-Hill; **22nd edition, 2025**; two volumes) — **REFERENCE**. First published **1950** under Tinsley R. Harrison, whose founding idea — clinical medicine interwoven with pathophysiology — still structures the book. Widely described as the most authoritative internal medicine text in English. As a student book it is unusable cover-to-cover and superb chapter-by-chapter.
- ***Goldman-Cecil Medicine*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. Harrison's principal competitor; many clinicians find it more concise and better organised for looking something up quickly.
- ***Kumar & Clark's Clinical Medicine*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The standard UK/Commonwealth undergraduate medicine text; the one most British, Irish, South African and Namibian students own.
- ***Davidson's Principles and Practice of Medicine*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The other great British undergraduate text, first published in 1952; shorter and better illustrated than Kumar & Clark, and dominant in South Asia and much of Africa.
- ***Oxford Handbook of Clinical Medicine*** (OUP) — **POCKET**. The single most-carried book in Commonwealth medicine. Terse, opinionated, occasionally eccentric, genuinely useful at 03:00. The Oxford Handbook series extends to clinical specialties, general practice, emergency medicine, clinical examination and practical skills, and acute medicine.
- ***The Washington Manual of Medical Therapeutics*** — **POCKET**, US equivalent; ***Pocket Medicine*** (the "Massachusetts General Hospital Pocket Medicine", the "green book") — **POCKET**, extremely dense, ubiquitous among US residents.
- ***Talley & O'Connor's Clinical Examination*** — see clinical examination below; widely used in Australasia and South Africa as the medicine companion.

## Surgery

- ***Sabiston Textbook of Surgery*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. The US general-surgery reference, subtitled *The Biological Basis of Modern Surgical Practice*.
- ***Schwartz's Principles of Surgery*** (McGraw-Hill) — **REFERENCE / COURSE TEXT**. The other US standard; more accessible than Sabiston for trainees.
- ***Bailey & Love's Short Practice of Surgery*** (CRC Press) — **COURSE TEXT**. The British and Commonwealth undergraduate and MRCS standard, first published 1932; despite "Short" in the title it is a very large book.
- ***Browse's Introduction to the Symptoms and Signs of Surgical Disease*** — **COURSE TEXT**. The best book on surgical clinical method.
- ***Zollinger's Atlas of Surgical Operations*** and ***Operative Techniques in Surgery*** — **ATLAS**, for operating.
- ***Rutherford's Vascular Surgery***, ***Campbell's Operative Orthopaedics***, ***Youmans and Winn Neurological Surgery***, ***Campbell-Walsh-Wein Urology***, ***Cummings Otolaryngology*** — **REFERENCE**, the subspecialty encyclopaedias.

## Paediatrics

- ***Nelson Textbook of Pediatrics*** (Kliegman et al.; Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. The global paediatric reference; two volumes. *Nelson Essentials of Pediatrics* is the student abridgement.
- ***Illustrated Textbook of Paediatrics*** (Lissauer & Carroll; Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The UK/Commonwealth undergraduate standard.
- ***Coovadia's Paediatrics and Child Health*** (OUP Southern Africa) — **[ZA]/[NA]** **COURSE TEXT**. The southern African paediatrics text, written for the local disease profile — malnutrition, HIV, TB, diarrhoeal disease — and used across the region.
- ***The Harriet Lane Handbook*** (Johns Hopkins; Elsevier) — **POCKET**. The paediatric ward manual.

## Obstetrics and gynaecology

- ***Williams Obstetrics*** (McGraw-Hill) — **REFERENCE**. First published 1903; the obstetric reference of record in the English-speaking world.
- ***Williams Gynecology*** — **REFERENCE**, its counterpart.
- ***Obstetrics: Normal and Problem Pregnancies*** (Gabbe; Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**, the main US alternative.
- ***Ten Teachers*** (*Obstetrics by Ten Teachers* / *Gynaecology by Ten Teachers*; CRC Press) — **COURSE TEXT**. The British undergraduate standard, in print since 1917.
- ***Cronjé's Obstetrics and Gynaecology in Southern Africa*** — **[ZA]** **COURSE TEXT**.

## Psychiatry

- ***Kaplan & Sadock's Synopsis of Psychiatry*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **COURSE TEXT**. The standard psychiatry text; the full ***Comprehensive Textbook of Psychiatry*** is the **REFERENCE**.
- ***Shorter Oxford Textbook of Psychiatry*** (OUP) — **COURSE TEXT**. The British standard; more sceptical, more epidemiologically grounded.
- ***DSM-5-TR*** — the **Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, Fifth Edition, Text Revision**, published by the American Psychiatric Association in **2022** (revising DSM-5, 2013). A **CLASSIFICATION**, not a textbook: it defines operational criteria for research and billing comparability. It is not a diagnostic instrument for lay use and does not describe treatment.
- ***ICD-11*** — the WHO's *International Classification of Diseases, Eleventh Revision*, adopted by the World Health Assembly in 2019 and in force for reporting from **1 January 2022**. Chapter 06 covers mental, behavioural and neurodevelopmental disorders. ICD-11 is the international statistical standard; DSM is the North American clinical/research convention. They differ in several places — most discussed being ICD-11's dimensional personality-disorder model and its inclusion of complex PTSD and of gaming disorder.

> ⚠️ DSM-5-TR and ICD-11 criteria are classification instruments for trained clinicians. They are listed here bibliographically. They are not a self-assessment tool and should not be used as one.

## Emergency medicine and critical care

- ***Tintinalli's Emergency Medicine: A Comprehensive Study Guide*** (McGraw-Hill) — **REFERENCE / COURSE TEXT**. The emergency medicine standard for examinations.
- ***Rosen's Emergency Medicine: Concepts and Clinical Practice*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. The deeper, more discursive alternative; better on reasoning and controversy.
- ***Marino's The ICU Book*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **COURSE TEXT**. Paul Marino's book is the most-recommended introduction to intensive care in English: physiologically argued, opinionated, readable. ***The Little ICU Book*** is the pocket form.
- ***Oh's Intensive Care Manual*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**, the Australasian standard.
- ***Irwin and Rippe's Intensive Care Medicine*** — **REFERENCE**, the US alternative.
- ***The Ventilator Book*** (Owens) — short, and the usual first recommendation for mechanical ventilation.

## Anaesthesia

- ***Miller's Anesthesia*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. The field's encyclopaedia; ***Basics of Anesthesia*** ("Baby Miller") is the trainee entry point.
- ***Morgan & Mikhail's Clinical Anesthesiology*** (McGraw-Hill) — **COURSE TEXT**. The book most anaesthesia residents actually learn from.
- ***Oxford Handbook of Anaesthesia*** — **POCKET**.

## Radiology and imaging

- ***Learning Radiology: Recognizing the Basics*** (Herring; Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**, the standard medical-student introduction.
- ***Felson's Principles of Chest Roentgenology*** — **COURSE TEXT**, the classic programmed text for chest films.
- ***Grainger & Allison's Diagnostic Radiology*** — **REFERENCE**, the British standard.
- ***Radiopaedia.org*** — free, community-edited, case-based; effectively the working reference for a generation of trainees and the best single free imaging resource.
- ***Radiology Assistant*** (radiologyassistant.nl) — free, structured approach articles, widely used for systematic reading.

## Clinical examination and clinical method

- ***Macleod's Clinical Examination*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The Commonwealth standard; paired with ***Macleod's Clinical Diagnosis***.
- ***Bates' Guide to Physical Examination and History Taking*** (Wolters Kluwer) — **COURSE TEXT**. The US standard; excellent photography and a strong communication-skills thread.
- ***Talley & O'Connor's Clinical Examination*** (Elsevier) — **COURSE TEXT**. The Australasian standard, heavily used in South Africa; ***Examination Medicine*** is its long-case companion for postgraduate clinical examinations.
- ***Hutchison's Clinical Methods*** (Elsevier) — the venerable British text, first published 1897.
- ***McGee, Evidence-Based Physical Diagnosis*** (Elsevier) — **REFERENCE**. The book that attaches likelihood ratios to physical signs and tells you honestly which ones are worthless.
- ***The Rational Clinical Examination*** (JAMA/McGraw-Hill) — the collected JAMA series; the same project in journal form.

## Other essentials

- ***The Oxford Handbook of Clinical Specialties***, ***Oxford Handbook of General Practice***, ***Oxford Handbook of Clinical Diagnosis*** — **POCKET**.
- ***Clinical Pharmacology*** (Bennett & Brown) — the bridge between pharmacology and prescribing.
- ***Greenhalgh, How to Read a Paper*** (Wiley/BMJ Books) — the standard introduction to critical appraisal; see `05`.
- ***Straus, Glasziou, Richardson & Haynes, Evidence-Based Medicine: How to Practice and Teach It*** — the Sackett lineage text.
- ***Hennekens & Buring, Epidemiology in Medicine*** and ***Rothman, Modern Epidemiology*** — the epidemiology texts a clinician-researcher graduates to.
- ***Gordis Epidemiology*** — the accessible undergraduate epidemiology text.

## The examination-preparation ecosystem

For **[US]** students in particular, third-party resources have partly displaced institutional teaching. The commonly used stack:

- ***First Aid for the USMLE Step 1*** (McGraw-Hill) — an annually revised, densely abbreviated compendium of the Step 1 corpus. Not a textbook: it is an index of facts, meant to be annotated across a two-year period and then reviewed. Its dominance is such that many US curricula are informally reverse-engineered against it. Companion volumes exist for Step 2 CK and Step 3.
- ***UWorld*** — the question bank of record. Subscription-based, several thousand vignette questions with long explanations; widely regarded as the single highest-yield resource for both Step 1 and Step 2 CK, and increasingly used as a primary learning tool rather than a testing one. Also produces banks for COMLEX, NCLEX, ABIM and others.
- ***AMBOSS*** — a German-origin integrated platform combining a question bank with a hyperlinked clinical knowledge library; strong on the "learning card" model and popular in Europe and increasingly in the US.
- ***Anki*** — free, open-source spaced-repetition flashcard software. The delivery mechanism for the community-built decks below. Spaced repetition has good general cognitive-science support for durable retention of large factual corpora, which is precisely the medical-student problem.
- ***AnKing*** — the dominant crowd-maintained Anki deck for USMLE Step 1 and Step 2, assembled from and continually re-tagged against First Aid, Boards and Beyond, Pathoma, Sketchy and UWorld. Tens of thousands of cards; the maintained "V12"-style releases are versioned and community-curated.
- ***Boards and Beyond*** (Jason Ryan) — video lectures covering the Step 1 basic-science corpus; the usual "first pass" video resource.
- ***Pathoma*** (Husain Sattar) — a short pathology text plus video series, near-universally used and widely regarded as the best-taught pathology in the ecosystem.
- ***Sketchy*** (SketchyMedical) — visual mnemonic videos in which each microorganism or drug class is encoded as a scene with symbolic objects. Extraordinarily effective for microbiology and pharmacology, which are the two most arbitrary-recall-heavy subjects.
- ***Osmosis*** — video and question platform, more institutional (many schools license it) and more generalist.
- ***Physeo***, ***Dirty Medicine***, ***Divine Intervention*** (podcast) — smaller supplements with strong followings for specific subjects.
- **[UK]** ***Passmedicine***, ***Quesmed***, ***PasTest***, ***BMJ OnExamination*** — the UK equivalents for finals, the MLA, and MRCP/MRCS.
- **[UK]** ***Zero to Finals*** (Tom Watchman) — free written notes and videos, very widely used for UK finals.
- **[ZA]/[NA]** Southern African students typically use the UK-facing resources plus local texts (Coovadia, Cronjé) and past papers, since no large local question-bank market exists.

**An honest note on this ecosystem.** It is extremely effective at producing examination performance and it has genuine pedagogical merit — spaced repetition and question-first learning are better supported by evidence than lectures are. But it also produces a characteristic failure mode: students who can answer a vignette and cannot reason through an unfamiliar patient, because the resources train **recognition of test items**, not the construction of a differential from an undifferentiated story. Every clinical educator interviewed in the literature on this makes the same complaint. The corrective is deliberate exposure to real, messy patients and to long-form texts — which is what the clinical years are supposed to be for.

## Point-of-care references: what clinicians actually use

Once qualified, almost no one consults a textbook during a clinical shift. They use one of these.

| Resource | Owner | What it is | Access model |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UpToDate** | Wolters Kluwer Health | Synthesised, continuously updated topic reviews with explicit recommendations, written by named expert authors and peer reviewed; drug monographs, interaction checker, calculators; **7,600+ physician contributors**. Founded **1992** by Burton Rose and Joseph Rush, originally nephrology-only. | Paid. Individual US physician subscription ≈ **US$579/year (2026)**, cheaper for multi-year. Institutional licences are the usual route; free or discounted access via humanitarian programmes and in some low-income settings. |
| **DynaMed** | EBSCO | Competitor to UpToDate; more explicitly evidence-graded and terser, with visible levels of evidence attached to each statement. | Paid, mostly institutional. |
| **BMJ Best Practice** | BMJ Group | Structured topic pages organised around the clinical workflow (assessment → diagnosis → management → follow-up), with GRADE-informed evidence tables. Free at point of use for NHS staff in England via NICE/Health Education England licensing. | Paid; free to many NHS and Commonwealth users through national licences. |
| **Lexicomp** | Wolters Kluwer | Drug information database: monographs, interactions, IV compatibility, paediatric and neonatal dosing datasets. Bundled with UpToDate in many licences. | Paid. |
| **Micromedex** | Merative (formerly IBM Watson Health / Truven) | The other major drug and toxicology database; **POISINDEX** is its toxicology component and is a reference standard in poison centres. | Paid, institutional. |
| **British National Formulary (BNF)** and **BNF for Children** | BMJ Group and Royal Pharmaceutical Society, published for NICE | **[UK]** The UK's authoritative prescribing reference; twice-yearly print editions plus continuously updated digital. Free online and via app to UK users through NICE. The single most important prescribing document in UK and much Commonwealth practice. | Free in the UK; paid elsewhere. |
| **Medscape** | WebMD (Internet Brands) | Free, advertising-supported: drug reference, interaction checker, condition summaries, news, CME. Widely used precisely because it costs nothing. | Free with registration; advertising-funded. |
| **Epocrates** | athenahealth | Free/freemium drug reference app, historically the first widely adopted clinical smartphone tool. | Freemium. |
| **MDCalc** | MDCalc | Free clinical decision-rule and score calculator library (Wells, CHA₂DS₂-VASc, CURB-65, qSOFA, MELD…) with explicit notes on validation and appropriate use. | Free. |
| **Sanford Guide** | Antimicrobial Therapy, Inc. | **[US]** Antimicrobial reference, print pocket and app. | Paid subscription. |
| **EMC / DailyMed** | MHRA-linked (UK) / NLM (US) | Free access to the official product characteristics/labelling (SmPC in the UK/EU, package insert in the US). The regulatory ground truth beneath every commercial database. | Free. |
| **Cochrane Library** | Cochrane / Wiley | Systematic reviews (see `06`). | Paid; free national licences in many countries, including through HINARI in low-income settings. |
| **HINARI / Research4Life** | WHO-led partnership | Free or very low-cost access to major biomedical journals and some databases for institutions in eligible low- and middle-income countries. | Free/low-cost, institution must be registered. **[NA]/[ZA]** Relevant to access in the region. |

**Access reality in southern Africa.** **[ZA]** Large South African academic hospitals and universities generally hold UpToDate and major journal packages; district and rural facilities frequently do not. **[NA]** Namibian access is thinner still, and clinicians there rely disproportionately on free resources — Medscape, MDCalc, Radiopaedia, WHO guidelines, the South African **Standard Treatment Guidelines and Essential Medicines List (STGs/EML)** and the Namibian equivalent, and HINARI where the institution is registered. Any recommendation to "check UpToDate" carries an implicit assumption about institutional wealth that does not hold across the region.
