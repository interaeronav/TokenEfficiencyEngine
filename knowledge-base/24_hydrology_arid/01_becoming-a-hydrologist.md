# Becoming a hydrologist — education, registration and self-study

**Summary.** There is no single "hydrology degree" pathway. Practitioners arrive from civil/environmental engineering (the design and water-resources route), geology (the hydrogeology route), or physical geography / earth or environmental science (the catchment-process route). The dividing line matters professionally: in South Africa an engineering-route hydrologist registers with ECSA as a Pr Eng, a science-route hydrogeologist registers with SACNASP as a Pr Sci Nat, and the two carry different signing rights. This file sets out the undergraduate foundations, the leading graduate programmes worldwide and in southern Africa, the registration regimes, and a self-study curriculum with the mathematics stated honestly rather than hidden.

## Key facts

| Route | Undergraduate | Typical postgraduate | Registration (ZA) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water-resources / drainage engineering | BSc/BEng Civil or Environmental Engineering (4 yr) | MSc/MEng Water Resources | **[ZA]** ECSA — Pr Eng / Pr Tech Eng |
| Hydrogeology | BSc Geology + Hydrogeology honours | MSc Hydrogeology (UFS IGS, UWC) | **[ZA]** SACNASP — Pr Sci Nat (Earth Science / Water Resources fields) |
| Catchment / surface-water hydrology | BSc Earth Science, Environmental Science, Physical Geography | MSc/PhD Hydrology (Rhodes IWR) | **[ZA]** SACNASP Pr Sci Nat |
| International practice | any of the above | MSc IHE Delft, Wageningen, ETH, Imperial, Newcastle | **[UK]** CIWEM C.WEM; **[US]** AIH PH / state PG or PE |

> ⚠️ **[ZA]** Signing off a stormwater or dam design is reserved engineering work under the Engineering Profession Act; signing a geohydrological assessment is natural-science work under SACNASP. Doing the other profession's reserved work is an offence in both directions. Check the current identification-of-work determinations before committing to a route. Marked `needs-verification` — the reserved-work framework has been contested and amended repeatedly.

## Undergraduate foundations

Pick the degree by the work you want to do, not by the word "hydrology" in a module list.

- **Civil / environmental engineering** gives you fluid mechanics, hydraulics, structures, geotechnics, and the professional-registration path to design culverts, channels, reservoirs and reticulation. Weakness: little geology, little geochemistry.
- **Geology** gives you stratigraphy, structure, sedimentology and mineralogy — indispensable for understanding why an aquifer exists where it does. The Ohangwena aquifer story is a *geological* story (a Cretaceous-to-Recent subsiding basin filled with 400–600 m of Kalahari sediments, with a swelling-clay aquitard sealing the deep freshwater body). Weakness: usually thin on differential equations and on hydraulics.
- **Earth / environmental science / physical geography** gives you geomorphology, soils, climate and GIS, and is the most natural entry to catchment hydrology and ecohydrology. Weakness: mathematical depth varies enormously between institutions — check the calculus and statistics content before enrolling.
- **Physics or applied mathematics** is an unconventional but strong base; the conversion to hydrology at MSc level is straightforward and the modelling and inverse-problem work will come easily.

Whichever you pick, take every available course in: calculus through partial differential equations, probability and statistics, fluid mechanics, numerical methods, GIS, and at least one programming language (Python).

## Leading graduate programmes

**North America**
- **Colorado School of Mines** — hydrologic science and engineering, strongly quantitative, with the Integrated GroundWater Modeling Center historically attached. <https://www.mines.edu/>
- **University of Arizona, Department of Hydrology and Atmospheric Sciences** — the historic centre of *arid-zone* hydrology in the USA; Tucson's setting makes it the obvious international peer group for Namibian conditions. (Site was unreachable through the research proxy on 2026-08-25; department name flagged `needs-verification`.)
- **UC Davis** — hydrologic sciences graduate group, strong on agricultural water, groundwater–surface water interaction and California's managed aquifer recharge programme.
- **Utah State University** — Watershed Sciences; home of the Utah Water Research Laboratory and a long tradition in rangeland/semi-arid hydrology and in open hydrologic software (HydroShare, TauDEM).

**Europe**
- **IHE Delft Institute for Water Education** (formerly UNESCO-IHE) — the largest international postgraduate water education institute, MSc programmes in Water Science and Engineering with hydrology/water-resources and groundwater specialisations, and a heavy cohort of African professionals. <https://www.un-ihe.org/>
- **Wageningen University & Research** — Hydrology and Environmental Hydraulics; also the publisher of Kruseman & de Ridder's pumping-test manual. <https://www.wur.nl/>
- **ETH Zürich** — Environmental Engineering / Hydrology, exceptional in physically-based modelling and in machine-learning hydrology. <https://ethz.ch/>
- **Imperial College London** — Environmental Engineering / Hydrology; the group of Howard Wheater (co-editor of *Hydrological Modelling in Arid and Semi-Arid Areas*) was based there, which makes it historically important for this domain. <https://www.imperial.ac.uk/>
- **Newcastle University** — water resources and hydroinformatics; strong on rainfall extremes, urban drainage and climate-change downscaling. <https://www.ncl.ac.uk/>

**Southern Africa — the practical route from Namibia**
- **University of the Free State, Institute for Groundwater Studies (IGS)**, Bloemfontein — the leading dedicated groundwater teaching and consulting institute in the region; MSc/PhD in geohydrology, plus short courses in pumping-test analysis, borehole siting and groundwater modelling that practitioners across SADC attend. <https://www.ufs.ac.za/igs>
- **University of the Western Cape, Department of Earth Sciences**, Cape Town — hydrogeology group with a long record in Namibian and SADC groundwater; Eberhard Braune, author of the Groundwater Project's *Managed Aquifer Recharge in Southern Africa*, is based there. <https://www.uwc.ac.za/>
- **Rhodes University, Institute for Water Research (IWR)**, Makhanda — the southern African home of catchment-scale surface-water hydrology, the Pitman model and its descendants, and now the ARUA Water Centre of Excellence. <https://www.ru.ac.za/iwr/>
- **Stellenbosch University**, Department of Earth Sciences and the Civil Engineering water division — strong hydrogeology and water-resources engineering. <https://www.sun.ac.za/english/faculty/science/earthsciences>
- **University of Namibia (UNAM)** — geology and the water-focused research that produced the SASSCAL Cuvelai groundwater work (Wanke, Hamutoko, Hipondoka). If you want to work on the Ohangwena system specifically, this is the local research base. <https://www.unam.edu.na/>
- **Namibia University of Science and Technology (NUST)**, formerly the Polytechnic of Namibia — civil engineering, land administration, natural resources management and geo-information; the practical engineering-technician-to-engineer route inside Namibia. <https://www.nust.na/>

Namibia's water sector also trains through employment: NamWater, the Ministry responsible for agriculture, water and land reform, the regional councils, and consulting firms take graduates and put them through borehole siting, pump testing and rural-water-supply work that no degree teaches.

## Professional registration

**[ZA] South Africa**
- **ECSA** (Engineering Council of South Africa) — registration as Professional Engineer (Pr Eng), Professional Engineering Technologist, or Professional Engineering Technician. Route: accredited degree → candidate registration → typically 3+ years of structured training and experience → professional review. <https://www.ecsa.co.za/>
- **SACNASP** (South African Council for Natural Scientific Professions) — registration as Professional Natural Scientist (Pr Sci Nat) in a field of practice such as Earth Science or Water Resources Science. Route: recognised BSc(Hons)-equivalent or higher → candidate natural scientist → 3 years supervised experience → professional registration. This is the standard credential for consulting hydrogeologists in South Africa and is widely recognised in Namibia. <https://www.sacnasp.org.za/>
- **Ground Water Division of the Geological Society of South Africa (GWD-GSSA)** — not a licensing body but *the* professional community for groundwater in the region: the biennial groundwater conference, a "find a specialist" roll, technical guidance and continuing-education courses. Membership is the cheapest single thing you can do to plug into regional practice. <https://gwd.org.za/>

**[US] United States**
- **American Institute of Hydrology (AIH)** — certification as **Professional Hydrologist (PH)**, with subsidiary designations in groundwater and surface water, plus Hydrologist-in-Training. AIH certification is a national, voluntary credential. <https://aihydrology.org/>
- **State licensure** is the legally binding layer: a Professional Geologist (PG) licence in states that license geologists, or a Professional Engineer (PE) licence for engineering design. Requirements are state-by-state; the ASBOG Fundamentals of Geology / Practice of Geology examinations underpin most PG licences.

**[UK] United Kingdom**
- **CIWEM** (Chartered Institution of Water and Environmental Management) — Chartered Water and Environmental Manager (C.WEM), and CIWEM is a licensed body for Chartered Environmentalist (CEnv) and Chartered Scientist (CSci). Route: accredited degree or equivalent → membership → professional review interview. <https://www.ciwem.org/>
- The Geological Society (CGeol) is the parallel credential for hydrogeologists on the geology side.

**[NA] Namibia.** Namibia has the Engineering Council of Namibia for engineers; there is no separate Namibian hydrogeologist register, and SACNASP Pr Sci Nat is the credential most consultants carry. `needs-verification` — confirm current Engineering Council of Namibia requirements and whether reciprocity with ECSA applies before relying on this.

## A concrete self-study curriculum

Stated honestly: you cannot do hydrology properly without the mathematics. The floor is single-variable and multivariable calculus, ordinary differential equations, an introduction to partial differential equations (the diffusion equation, because that *is* the groundwater flow equation and the heat equation and the Richards equation), and applied probability and statistics (distributions, fitting, return periods, regression). Add fluid mechanics — continuity, Bernoulli, Navier-Stokes in outline, laminar vs turbulent flow, boundary layers — because Darcy's law and Manning's equation are both special cases you should understand rather than memorise.

**Phase 1 — foundations (3–6 months, ~6 h/week)**
1. Calculus refresh through multivariable and vector calculus (gradient, divergence).
2. ODEs and an introduction to the diffusion equation.
3. Probability and statistics with an emphasis on extreme-value distributions.
4. Python: numpy, pandas, matplotlib, and enough xarray to open a NetCDF.

**Phase 2 — core hydrology (6 months)**
5. Read Dingman *Physical Hydrology* or Brutsaert *Hydrology: An Introduction* cover to cover, working the problems.
6. Work through the free Groundwater Project books in order: *Basic Hydrogeology* → *Hydrogeologic Properties of Earth Materials and Principles of Groundwater Flow* → *Groundwater* (the full Freeze & Cherry text, free) → *Analysis and Evaluation of Pumping Test Data* (Kruseman & de Ridder, 2nd ed., free). <https://gw-project.org/books/>
7. Chow, Maidment & Mays *Applied Hydrology* for the unit hydrograph, routing and frequency analysis.

**Phase 3 — arid-zone specialisation (3–6 months)**
8. Wheater, Sorooshian & Sharma, *Hydrological Modelling in Arid and Semi-Arid Areas*.
9. Simmers (ed.) on recharge estimation, plus the Groundwater Project's *Quantification of Groundwater Recharge*.
10. Read the SASSCAL Cuvelai papers (see `04`) as a worked regional case.

**Phase 4 — tools (continuous)**
11. MODFLOW 6 via the free *Getting Started with MODFLOW* book and Flopy in Python.
12. HEC-HMS and HEC-RAS (both free) on a real local catchment.
13. QGIS with a DEM: delineate your own catchment, compute time of concentration, size a culvert (see `06`).

**Phase 5 — do real work.** Instrument something. A single tipping-bucket rain gauge and a water-level logger in a borehole, read for two seasons, will teach you more about dryland hydrology than another textbook. The single most valuable habit is keeping a continuous, dated, quality-flagged record.
