# The world's largest contractors — who they are and what they are actually good at

**Summary.** The global contracting league table is dominated by four Chinese state-owned groups whose revenues are an order of magnitude larger than anyone else's, followed by a European tier (VINCI, ACS/Hochtief, Bouygues, Strabag, Skanska, Ferrovial), a US tier that is mostly engineering-led rather than construction-led (Bechtel, Fluor, Turner as Hochtief's subsidiary), and Asian majors (Samsung C&T, the Japanese "Big Five", Larsen & Toubro). Size in this industry is not the same as capability: several of the largest firms are essentially state infrastructure delivery arms, while several of the most technically formidable are comparatively small. The benchmark ranking is **ENR's Top 250 Global Contractors** (total revenue) and **Top 250 International Contractors** (revenue earned outside the home country), published annually by *Engineering News-Record*, the US trade weekly founded in 1874.

> ⚠️ Every revenue figure below is stated **with its currency and reporting year**. Construction revenue moves 10–30% year on year and Chinese figures convert differently depending on the FX date used. Do not quote a figure without the year. Figures marked *(Wikipedia infobox)* are secondary and should be re-verified against the company's own annual report before being used in a bid, valuation or legal document.

## The Chinese state groups — scale without precedent

Four Chinese state-owned enterprises have topped the ENR Global list for over a decade. Their scale is not a construction-market phenomenon so much as a state-investment phenomenon: they are the delivery arms of China's domestic infrastructure and property programme, and increasingly of the Belt and Road Initiative abroad.

**CSCEC (中国建筑集团有限公司)** — Beijing, founded 1957, state-owned, listed subsidiary CSCEC Ltd on the Shanghai Stock Exchange (601668), a component of both the SSE 50 and CSI 300. Revenue **CN¥2.187 trillion / US$302.5 bn (2024)**, operating income CN¥96.84 bn. Business spans general contracting, EPC turnkey, architectural and engineering design, urban planning, real estate investment and development, property management, modular and prefabricated building units. Operationally CSCEC is known for **volume and speed on repeatable building typologies** — high-rise residential, commercial towers, stadia — executed with very large directly employed workforces and a vertically integrated supply chain that includes its own design institutes and precast plants. Its most visible export product is the turnkey public building: stadiums, convention centres, ministry headquarters and airports across Africa, frequently financed by Chinese policy banks.

**CRCC (China Railway Construction Corporation)** — Beijing, traces to 1948 as the PLA Railway Engineering Corps, restructured 1990, listed 2008. Revenue **CN¥1.067 trillion / US$148.2 bn (2024)**; total assets CN¥1.863 trillion. Specialities: heavy rail, high-speed rail, metros, long tunnels, bridges, plus survey/design/planning, infrastructure investment and leasing, and logistics. Operationally CRCC is the world's most experienced **tunnelling and railway earthworks** organisation by sheer repetition.

**CREC (China Railway Group Limited, 中国中铁)** — Beijing, incorporated 2007 as the listed vehicle of China Railway Engineering Corporation, roughly 282,000 employees (2020). Railways, highways, installation, design, survey, manufacturing, real estate, resources, mining. CREC and CRCC are frequently confused; they are separate groups with near-identical capability sets, both descended from the railway ministry system.

**CCCC (China Communications Construction Company)** — Beijing, formed 2005, revenue **≈US$136.7 bn (2023)**, net income ≈US$1.6 bn. Specialities: **ports, harbours, dredging, reclamation, bridges and roads**. Its subsidiary **China Harbour Engineering Company (CHEC)** is the dominant Chinese marine contractor in Africa and built the Walvis Bay New Container Terminal in Namibia. CCCC's dredging fleet is the largest in the world; land reclamation is its signature capability.

> ⚠️ Chinese state contractors compete on a fundamentally different basis from Western firms: they arrive bundled with concessional finance from Chinese policy banks, they bring their own management and often significant expatriate labour, and their bid prices are frequently unmatchable by locally financed competitors. This is the single most important structural fact in African public works procurement today, and the direct cause of the local-content disputes described in `03_namibian-contractors-and-industry.md`.

## The European majors

**VINCI SA** — Nanterre, France, founded 1899 by Alexandre Giros and Louis Loucheur. Revenue **€71.6 bn (2024)**, operating income €8.8 bn, net income €5.3 bn, total assets €129.5 bn. VINCI is the archetype of the modern **concessions-plus-contracting** model: it builds motorways, tunnels, bridges and airports and then owns and operates them for decades. VINCI Airports is one of the world's largest private airport operators. Flagship works include the Millau Viaduct, the Stade de France, and numerous French motorway concessions. Operationally VINCI is known for **disciplined decentralisation** — thousands of small autonomous business units with local P&L responsibility — combined with a group balance sheet that lets it hold long-dated concessions. The concessions earnings smooth the contracting cycle; this is why VINCI's margins are structurally better than a pure contractor's.

**Bouygues SA** — Paris, founded 1952 by Francis Bouygues. Revenue **€56.9 bn (2025)**, operating income €2.33 bn, 196,184 employees. Bouygues is a conglomerate: Bouygues Construction, Bouygues Immobilier (property), Colas (roads and materials, itself a global top-10 road builder), Equans (multi-technical services, acquired from Engie), Bouygues Telecom and TF1 (broadcasting). Known operationally for **complex urban buildings and civil structures**, and for Colas's global asphalt and road-materials footprint, which reaches into Africa.

**ACS Group** — Madrid, formed 1997, chaired by Florentino Pérez. Revenue **€41.6 bn (2024)**, net income €828 m. ACS is a holding structure: it controls **Hochtief** (Germany), which in turn owns **CIMIC** (Australia) and **Turner Construction** (USA). Its speciality is public works, transport infrastructure concessions, facility management, environmental and industrial services. ACS is heavily exposed to North American and Australian infrastructure through those subsidiaries, and increasingly to data centres.

**Hochtief AG** — Essen, Germany, founded 1873. Revenue **€27.756 bn (2023)**, net income €314 m. Controlled by ACS. Hochtief is the parent of Turner and CIMIC and has become, in effect, ACS's international delivery platform. Strong in transport infrastructure, tunnelling, airports and — most significantly in the current cycle — **hyperscale data centre construction**.

**Turner Construction** — New York (66 Hudson Boulevard East), founded 1902, revenue **US$29.2 bn (2025)**, roughly 15,000 employees, subsidiary of Hochtief. Turner is the largest **construction manager** in the United States. It self-performs very little; its product is preconstruction services, cost planning, trade-package procurement and site management. Known operationally for depth in healthcare, higher education, aviation, sports venues and data centres, and for having industrialised the CM-at-risk model.

**Skanska AB** — Stockholm, founded 1887. Revenue **SEK 176.7 bn (2025)**, operating income SEK 6.6 bn. Four streams: construction, residential development, commercial property development and infrastructure development (PPP). Skanska has a deliberate reputation for **safety and ethics leadership** — it was one of the earliest large contractors to publish an explicit zero-accident vision and to withdraw from markets it considered corrupt. Strong in the Nordics, UK, central Europe and the USA.

**Strabag SE** — Vienna, founded 1835, a *Societas Europaea* listed in Vienna. Revenue **€17,422 m (2024)**, operating income €1,062 m, net income €828 m, 78,175 employees. Central and eastern Europe's dominant contractor: roads, tunnels, rail, buildings and a very large materials business (asphalt, aggregates, cement). Operationally Strabag is a **self-performing** contractor to an unusual degree, owning its own plant and materials production — the opposite of Turner's model.

**Ferrovial SE** — headquartered in Amsterdam (redomiciled from Spain), founded 1952 by Rafael del Pino. Revenue **€9.63 bn (2025)**, net income €888 m, 22,609 employees. Divisions: Airports, Construction, Highways. Ferrovial is best understood as an **infrastructure investor with a construction arm**: it holds stakes in Heathrow (historically), the 407 ETR toll road in Toronto and managed-lane toll roads in Texas. Its construction business, Ferrovial Construction (including Budimex in Poland and Webber in the USA), executes the assets it invests in.

## The US engineering-led contractors

**Bechtel Corporation** — Reston, Virginia; founded 1898 by Warren A. Bechtel; still privately owned by the Bechtel family, currently chaired by Brendan Bechtel. Revenue **US$58.2 bn (2024)**. The company describes a history spanning 126 years, 160 countries and 25,000 projects. Divisions: Energy; Infrastructure; Manufacturing & Technology; Mining & Metals; Nuclear, Security & Environmental. Bechtel's flagship works include the Hoover Dam (as part of Six Companies), the Channel Tunnel rail link, Jubail Industrial City in Saudi Arabia, and the Boston Central Artery/Tunnel project. Operationally Bechtel is the archetypal **EPC and programme-management** firm: it is hired when a client needs a megaproject organisation built from scratch, and it is unusual in retaining deep in-house engineering, procurement and construction capability under one roof. Its reputation is for execution capacity on projects that nobody else will take — and, correspondingly, for being expensive and for the Big Dig controversy.

**Fluor Corporation** — Irving, Texas; founded 1912; NYSE-listed. Revenue **US$16.3 bn (2024)**. Fluor is an EPC and project-management firm concentrated in energy, chemicals, mining and metals, infrastructure and government services. Operationally it is a **reimbursable-contract specialist** that suffered badly when it took fixed-price risk on large infrastructure in the late 2010s — a cautionary tale that reshaped US megaproject contracting toward more collaborative models.

## The Asian majors

**Samsung C&T Corporation (삼성물산)** — Seoul, founded 1938 as the first Samsung company, over 17,000 employees. Four working groups: Engineering & Construction, Trading & Investment, Fashion, Resort. Samsung C&T is technically formidable at **supertall buildings** — it was the lead of the joint venture (with BESIX of Belgium and Arabtec of the UAE) that built the Burj Khalifa, and it built Taipei 101 and the Petronas Towers' Tower 2. Its speciality is high-rise structural engineering, self-climbing formwork and high-pressure concrete pumping.

**Obayashi Corporation** — Tokyo (Shinagawa Intercity Tower B), founded by Yoshigoro Obayashi. Architectural design, civil engineering design, construction, engineering, real estate. Obayashi built the Tokyo Skytree.

**Kajima Corporation** — Tokyo (Motoakasaka, Minato-ku), founded 1840. One of Japan's "Big Five" general contractors alongside Obayashi, Shimizu, Taisei and Takenaka.

**Shimizu Corporation** — Tokyo. Another of the Big Five, notable for research-led construction and for a large in-house institute of technology.

The Japanese Big Five share a distinctive operational signature: **design-build under one roof (*sekkei-sekō*), extreme quality control, seismic engineering depth, and heavy investment in construction robotics and automation**. They are less internationally aggressive than Chinese or European firms, deriving most revenue domestically, but their quality systems are widely regarded as the industry's best.

**Larsen & Toubro (L&T)** — Mumbai (L&T House, Ballard Estate), founded 7 February 1946 by Danish engineers Henning Holck-Larsen and Søren Kristian Toubro. Revenue **₹290,860 crore (FY2025)**. L&T is a conglomerate: EPC and construction, heavy engineering, defence systems and naval vessels, power generation equipment, mining and construction equipment, valves, mass rapid transit, real estate, IT services and financial services. Operationally L&T is the closest thing outside China to a fully vertically integrated infrastructure contractor — it fabricates its own heavy equipment, designs its own plants and builds them. It is the dominant contractor in India and increasingly in the Gulf.

## How to read a contractor's numbers

- **Revenue is not capability.** Revenue on a cost-reimbursable EPC contract includes flow-through procurement; a US$20 bn EPC revenue may embed US$14 bn of equipment purchases. Compare **operating margin** and **self-performed work**.
- **Order book / backlog** is the leading indicator. A contractor with 18 months of secured backlog is safe; one with 6 months is about to cut prices.
- **Where the profit comes from.** VINCI and Ferrovial earn a large share of profit from concessions, not construction. ACS earns it from services. Pure contracting margins are 1–5% almost everywhere.
- **International vs total.** ENR publishes both. A firm high on the Global list but low on the International list (all four Chinese majors, historically, and the Japanese Big Five) is a domestic-market business.

## Relevance to southern Africa

- **CCCC/CHEC, CSCEC, China Jiangxi International, China Gezhouba, Sinohydro** are the Chinese contractors actually encountered in Namibian and South African public works.
- **Bouygues** (through the Bombela consortium on Gautrain) and **Colas** are the French presence.
- **Stefanutti Stocks, WBHO, Raubex, Concor** are the regional contractors that would appear well down an ENR list; WBHO's FY2025 revenue of R28.5 bn is roughly US$1.5–1.6 bn, which places it far below the global leaders but among the largest in Africa.
- **Bechtel** has appeared in South Africa in advisory and programme-management roles rather than as a main contractor.
