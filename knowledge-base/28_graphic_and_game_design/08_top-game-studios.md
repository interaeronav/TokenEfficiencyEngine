# The game studio register — first-party groups, independents, publishers and the 2023–2026 contraction

**Summary.** This is the register: who owns whom, who made what, what engine they use, and what they are known for creatively. It closes with an honest account of the 2023–2026 contraction, which is the single most important context for anyone entering the industry. Ownership in games changes fast — Tencent, Sony, Microsoft, Krafton, Savvy/PIF and Embracer have all restructured large parts of the map since 2020 — so treat every ownership claim as accurate to **August 2026** and re-verify before acting on it.

## Key facts

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Layoffs 2023 | >**10,500** roles |
| Layoffs 2024 | ~**14,600** roles — more than 2022 and 2023 combined |
| Layoffs 2026 to 1 July | ~**4,600** roles, **22** studios closed |
| Embracer Group | cut **7,761** employees, closed/divested **44** studios, cancelled **80** projects |
| Studios closed outright, 2023–25 | over **30** |
| EA go-private deal | announced **29 September 2025**, **US$55 billion**; PIF 93.4%, Silver Lake 5.5%, Affinity Partners 1.1%; expected to close by June 2026 |
| Ubisoft headcount | 20,279 (2022) → 19,410 (Sept 2023) → 18,666 (Sept 2024) → **17,097 (Sept 2025)** |
| Tencent–Ubisoft Vantage Studios | **€1.16 bn for 25%** of a subsidiary valued at **€4 bn**, holding Assassin's Creed, Far Cry and Rainbow Six |
| GTA V sales | **>220 million** copies since September 2013 |
| Stardew Valley sales | **50 million** by February 2026 |
| Hollow Knight: Silksong sales | **>7 million** by mid-December 2025 |

> ⚠️ Headcount figures published in company filings and encyclopaedic sources lag reality by 6–18 months in a contracting market. Any studio headcount below is a *last verified* figure with its date, not a current one.

## 1. First-party groups

### Nintendo — Entertainment Planning & Development (EPD)

Formed **16 September 2015** by merging Entertainment Analysis & Development (EAD) and Software Planning & Development (SPD), under president Tatsumi Kimishima. Based in Kyoto. Headed by Executive General Manager **Shinya Takahashi**, with Senior General Manager Yoshiaki Koizumi; deputies Katsuya Eguchi, Hisashi Nogami, Takayuki Shimamura; senior officers Takashi Tezuka, Yoshio Sakamoto, Kensuke Tanabe, Eiji Aonuma and Koji Kondo.

**Structure** — nine known production groups plus support groups (Programming, Design and Sound Management):

| Group | Remit |
|---|---|
| Co-Production | External-studio games (Mario Tennis, Kirby, Fire Emblem, Xenoblade) |
| No. 3 | The Legend of Zelda |
| No. 4 | Casual/experimental (Nintendo Labo, Ring Fit Adventure, Nintendo Switch Sports) |
| No. 5 | Animal Crossing, Splatoon |
| No. 6 | Oversees external studios (Paper Mario, Metroid Prime, Luigi's Mansion) |
| No. 8 | 3D Super Mario, Donkey Kong |
| No. 9 | Mario Kart, ARMS |
| No. 10 | 2D Super Mario, Pikmin |
| Smart Device | Mobile |

**Known for:** designing outward from a mechanic rather than from a fiction; unusually long pre-production; the highest polish-per-feature ratio in the industry; and a willingness to cancel. Engine work is proprietary and not publicly documented.

## 2. Major independents and publishers

### Rockstar Games / Take-Two Interactive
Founded **December 1998** by Terry Donovan, Gary Foreman, Dan Houser, Sam Houser and Jamie King. Subsidiary of **Take-Two Interactive**. **Over 2,000 employees as of 2018.** Studios: Rockstar North (Edinburgh), San Diego, Toronto, London, Leeds, Lincoln, India, Dundee, LA, New England, Australia, plus Cfx.re. Engine: **RAGE** (Rockstar Advanced Game Engine), in development since 2006. **GTA V has sold over 220 million copies since September 2013** — the second best-selling game of all time. *GTA VI* remains in development following the September 2022 leak of 90 development videos. In **October 2025** Rockstar terminated 30–40 employees; labour organisations alleged union-busting, which the company disputed. **Known for:** open-world density and simulation depth at a scale nobody else attempts, and for a development culture that has been repeatedly criticised over crunch.

### Electronic Arts
Founded **27 May 1982** by Trip Hawkins; Redwood City, California; CEO Andrew Wilson. **~14,500 employees as of 2025.** Studios: **DICE** (Stockholm — Battlefield, **Frostbite** engine), **BioWare** (Edmonton/Austin — Mass Effect, Dragon Age), **Respawn** (Sherman Oaks — Titanfall, Apex Legends, Star Wars Jedi), **Motive** (Montréal), **Criterion** (Guildford), **Maxis** (The Sims). Franchises: Battlefield, The Sims, Mass Effect, Dragon Age, Madden, EA Sports FC, Star Wars. **On 29 September 2025 EA announced a US$55 billion deal to go private** — Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund 93.4%, Silver Lake 5.5%, Affinity Partners 1.1% — expected to close by June 2026. Between 2023 and 2025 EA closed Cliffhanger Games, Industrial Toys and (earlier) Visceral, and ran repeated layoff rounds totalling thousands of roles.

### Ubisoft
Founded **28 March 1986** by the five Guillemot brothers; headquartered Saint-Mandé, France; **40+ studios worldwide**. Headcount: 20,279 (2022) → 19,410 (Sept 2023) → 18,666 (Sept 2024) → **17,097 (Sept 2025)**. Franchises: Assassin's Creed, Tom Clancy's, Far Cry, Watch Dogs, Rayman, Prince of Persia, Just Dance, Anno, Rabbids. Engines: **Anvil** (Assassin's Creed, Ghost Recon), **Snowdrop** (The Division, Star Wars Outlaws), **Dunia 2** (Far Cry). **Tencent holds ~9.99%** of Ubisoft and in 2025 agreed to invest **€1.16 billion for 25%** of **Vantage Studios**, a new subsidiary valued at **€4 billion** housing Assassin's Creed, Far Cry and Rainbow Six. Cuts: 124 + 45 (2023); 45 (2024); **277 in December 2024** with San Francisco and Osaka closing; **185 in January 2025** with Leamington closing; **~100 at Red Storm in March 2026**.

### Capcom
Founded **1979** (as I.R.M. Corporation, 30 May; Capcom Co., Ltd. from 11 June 1983) by **Kenzo Tsujimoto**; Chūō-ku, Osaka. Franchises with lifetime sales: **Resident Evil >170M**, **Monster Hunter >120M**, **Street Fighter >50M**, **Mega Man >40M**, **Devil May Cry >30M**. Engine: **RE Engine**, which replaced MT Framework — *Resident Evil 7* (2017), the RE remakes, *Monster Hunter: World* (2018) and successors. Acquired Swordcanes Studio (Tokyo, July 2023) and Minimum Studios (Taiwan, July 2024). In **March 2026** MiSK Foundation's investment arm acquired a 5% stake, raised to **6% in April 2026**. **Known for:** the most consistent technical execution in Japanese development and a genuine engine advantage.

### Square Enix
Formed **1 April 2003** from the merger of Square and Enix; Shinjuku, Tokyo. **4,604 employees as of March 2025.** Franchises: **Final Fantasy (173M units)**, **Dragon Quest (85M)**, **Kingdom Hearts (36M)**. **Sold its western studios to Embracer on 1 May 2022 for US$300 million** — Crystal Dynamics, Eidos-Montréal, Square Enix Montréal, plus Tomb Raider, Deus Ex, Thief, Legacy of Kain and over 50 games — a transaction widely regarded as extraordinarily cheap and a marker of the pre-contraction acquisition frenzy. In **March 2024** it announced it would be "more selective", cancelling numerous unannounced titles and taking a **¥22.1 billion content-abandonment loss**, prioritising the third *Final Fantasy VII Remake* instalment. Engines: **Luminous** (current proprietary), legacy Crystal Tools, plus Unreal Engine and Unity.

### FromSoftware
Founded **1 November 1986**, Tokyo. Ownership: **Kadokawa Corporation 70%**, **Sixjoy Hong Kong (Tencent) 16%**, **Sony Interactive Entertainment 14%** (as of 2022). **456 employees as of May 2025.** Games: King's Field (1994), Armored Core (1997–), *Demon's Souls* (2009), Dark Souls trilogy (2011–16), *Bloodborne* (2015), *Sekiro* (2019), ***Elden Ring* (2022)**, *The Duskbloods* (2026, Switch 2). President and representative director **Hidetaka Miyazaki** since 2014. Proprietary engine. **Known for:** difficulty as a design thesis, environmental storytelling, world architecture that folds back on itself, and an unusually small headcount relative to output.

### Sega / Sega Sammy
**Sega** founded **3 June 1960** as Nihon Goraku Bussan by Martin Bromley and Richard Stewart; Shinagawa, Tokyo. Subsidiary of **Sega Sammy Holdings**, formed **2004** when Sammy acquired Sega. Studios: **Sonic Team**, **Ryu Ga Gotoku Studio** (Yakuza / Like a Dragon), **Atlus** (Persona, Shin Megami Tensei), **Creative Assembly** (Total War), **Sports Interactive** (Football Manager), **Two Point Studios**, Sega Sapporo. Acquired **Rovio Entertainment (Angry Birds) in 2023 for US$776 million**. **Known for:** Atlus's stylistic confidence (Persona's UI design is the most-imitated in the industry) and RGG Studio's extraordinary release cadence.

### Bandai Namco, Konami — *summary only*
**Bandai Namco Entertainment** (Tokyo) — Tekken, Ace Combat, Elden Ring publishing, Dark Souls publishing, Souls-adjacent partnerships, plus an enormous anime licensing business. **Konami** (Tokyo) — Metal Gear, Silent Hill, Castlevania, Yu-Gi-Oh!, eFootball; withdrew substantially from AAA development in the late 2010s in favour of pachinko, card games and mobile, and has partially returned with remakes (*Silent Hill 2*, *Metal Gear Solid Delta*) developed largely by external studios. **Both entries `needs-verification`** — neither page was fetched in this pass.

### Larian Studios
Founded **1996** by **Swen Vincke**; headquartered **Ghent, Belgium**, with studios in Quebec City, Dublin, Kuala Lumpur, Guildford, Barcelona and Warsaw. Games: Divine Divinity (2002), Divinity II (2010), Divinity: Original Sin (2014), Original Sin II (2017), ***Baldur's Gate 3*** (Early Access October 2020; full release August 2023). BG3 received universal acclaim and **dominated The Game Awards 2023 with 6 wins including Game of the Year**, plus D.I.C.E. Game of the Year. Engine: the **Divinity Engine**. **Known for:** systemic RPG design with genuinely divergent state tracking, extremely long early-access development with public iteration, and a public position against the industry's prevailing monetisation practices.

### CD Projekt / CD Projekt Red
Founded **May 1994** by **Marcin Iwiński and Michał Kiciński** in Warsaw with US$2,000, initially as game retailers. **1,134 employees group-wide as of September 2024**; CD Projekt Red specifically **615 as of December 2023**. The Witcher series from 2007 (**over 33 million franchise copies by 2017**); *Cyberpunk 2077* (December 2020). The Cyberpunk launch "eroded players' trust" through console technical failures and marketing that downplayed them; the company settled a class action for **US$1.85 million**, though the game recouped its full development cost on pre-orders alone. Engine: **REDengine**, abandoned — in **March 2022** CD Projekt announced a partnership with Epic to move to **Unreal Engine 5**. In development: *Orion* (Cyberpunk sequel), *Polaris* (new Witcher trilogy) and a Witcher 1 remake, all on UE5.

### Valve
Founded **24 August 1996** in Kirkland, Washington, by former Microsoft employees **Gabe Newell and Mike Harrington**; now in Bellevue. Newell owns over 50%. **~336 employees as of 2021** (~250 in 2012). Games: Half-Life (1998), Counter-Strike, Portal (2007), Team Fortress 2 (2007), Left 4 Dead, Dota 2 (2013), Half-Life: Alyx (2020). Engines: GoldSrc, Source, **Source 2** (2015). **Steam** launched 2003 and is the dominant PC distribution platform (an estimated US$3.4 billion generated by 2017). Hardware: **Steam Deck** (2022) on Linux-based SteamOS with Proton. Operates a **flat organisation** with open allocation — no traditional managers, employees choose their projects — an approach that has produced both extraordinary products and documented problems with project momentum and diversity.

### Epic Games
Founded **1991** as Potomac Computer Systems by **Tim Sweeney**; Cary, North Carolina since 1999. Ownership: **Tencent 40%** (acquired 2012 for US$330 million), **Sony 4.9%** (2020–22), **Disney 9% pending** (announced 2024); Sweeney remains controlling shareholder. **Over 4,000 employees** following the 2023 layoffs. Products: **Unreal Engine** (Guinness-recognised as the most successful game engine, 2014; free to develop with since 2015 under a 5% royalty), **Fortnite** (Battle Royale 2017; over US$1 billion earned by July 2018; 250 million players by March 2019), the Epic Games Store, Fab, MetaHuman and RealityCapture. *Epic v. Apple*: Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers ruled largely for Apple in September 2021 but issued an anti-steering injunction; Apple's compliance was found insufficient in April 2025, permitting the Epic Games Store's return to iOS. **On 24 March 2026 Epic cut over 1,000 positions** following reduced engagement with Fortnite — the single largest layoff event of 2026 to that point. See `13_software_unreal_engine` for the engine.

### Riot Games
Founded **September 2006** by **Brandon Beck and Marc Merrill**; Los Angeles. **Tencent acquired 93% in February 2011 and the remaining 7% in December 2015** — Riot is wholly Tencent-owned. **~2,500 employees as of May 2018**; 23 offices as of 2023. Games: **League of Legends** (27 October 2009; over 100 million monthly players by 2016), **Valorant** (2 June 2020), Wild Rift, Legends of Runeterra, Teamfight Tactics. Operates the LoL Championship Series across 12 regional leagues with 100+ teams, plus the Valorant Champions Tour. ***Arcane*** with French studio Fortiche released on Netflix in November 2021 to critical acclaim. **In January 2024 Riot laid off 530 employees (~11%)**, sunsetting the Riot Forge publishing label and the Riot Esports Network.

### HoYoverse / miHoYo
Founded **13 February 2012** (studio established January 2011) by **Cai Haoyu, Liu Wei and Luo Yuhao**, three Shanghai Jiao Tong University computer science students; Shanghai. **~5,000 employees as of 2022.** Games: Honkai Impact 3rd (2016), **Genshin Impact (2020)**, Honkai: Star Rail (2023), Zenless Zone Zero (2024). Genshin's development budget exceeded **US$100 million** and was **recouped two weeks after release**. **HoYoverse** launched 14 February 2022 as a Singapore-based publishing subsidiary (Cognosphere Pte. Ltd.) with offices in Montréal, Los Angeles, Tokyo and Seoul. Engine: **Unity**, with proprietary physics-based animation and 3D systems layered on. **Known for:** cross-platform gacha production values that redefined the mobile ceiling, and an art direction and animation standard that most Western mobile studios cannot match.

### Tencent Games
Established **2003**. Five internal studio groups: **TiMi Studio Group** (2008), **LightSpeed Studios**, **Morefun Studio Group**, **NExT Studios**, **Aurora Studios**. Owns **Riot Games** outright (2015). Holds a **40% stake in Epic Games**, **~9.99% of Ubisoft** plus 25% of Vantage Studios, **16% of FromSoftware** via Sixjoy Hong Kong, and a convertible position in **Remedy** that could reach ~20%. Titles: Honor of Kings (US$112M in March 2020), PUBG Mobile (US$232M combined with the China version, March 2020), Call of Duty: Mobile. **Note:** Tencent's full holdings list — Supercell, Grinding Gear Games, Sumo Digital, Techland, Funcom, Klei, Don't Nod and many others — is widely reported but was **not verified** on the page fetched; treat percentages for those as `needs-verification`.

### NetEase
Chinese publisher and developer (Hangzhou), the second-largest Chinese games company after Tencent; operator of Blizzard's titles in China (a partnership that lapsed and was later restored), developer of *Marvel Rivals* and *Naraka: Bladepoint*, and owner of a set of Western studios acquired 2020–23. **`needs-verification`** — not fetched in this pass. NetEase materially reduced its Western studio investments during the 2024–25 contraction.

### Krafton
Founded **5 November 2018** as parent to Bluehole (established March 2007); Seoul. CEO Kim Chang-han; chairman Chang Byung-gyu (14.89%). **~13,000+ employees across all subsidiaries.** Subsidiaries include **PUBG Studios**, **Bluehole Studio**, **Striking Distance Studios**, **Unknown Worlds** (Subnautica), **Tango Gameworks**, **Neon Giant** and 15+ others. Games: **PUBG: Battlegrounds (2017)**, TERA, inZOI (2024 early access), Subnautica (2018, 2021), Hi-Fi Rush (2023). Acquisitions: **Tango Gameworks and the Hi-Fi Rush IP, August 2024, US$96M+**; Nautilus Mobile (March 2025, US$14M); Eleventh Hour Games / *Last Epoch* (July 2025). IPO 10 August 2021 at ~US$19.32 billion valuation.

### Remedy Entertainment
Founded **18 August 1995**; Espoo, Finland. **385 employees as of 2025.** Games: Death Rally (1996), *Max Payne* (2001), *Max Payne 2* (2003), *Alan Wake* (2010), *Quantum Break* (2016), *Control* (2019), ***Alan Wake 2* (October 2023)**, *FBC: Firebreak* (17 June 2025). Engine: **Northlight**, proprietary, first used in Quantum Break, with ray tracing and high-fidelity facial capture. *FBC: Firebreak* "failed to meet sales expectations", leading the company to anticipate a negative annual profit. *Control 2* is in development with **Annapurna Pictures financing 50%**. **Tencent** agreed a **€15 million convertible loan in September 2024** that could raise its stake from ~14% to ~20%. **Known for:** a distinctive live-action/game hybrid aesthetic, a connected "Remedy Connected Universe", and technical rendering leadership.

### Housemarque, Supergiant, Team Cherry, ConcernedApe, Motion Twin

- **Housemarque** — Helsinki; acquired by Sony 2021; *Resogun*, *Nex Machina*, *Returnal* (2021). The clearest case of arcade design lineage surviving into AAA.
- **Supergiant Games** — founded **2009** by **Amir Rao and Gavin Simon** (both ex-EA Los Angeles); San Francisco. **Seven permanent staff at founding, all still with the studio as of 2025.** Bastion (2011), Transistor (2014), Pyre (2017), **Hades (2020)** — over **1 million copies**, Game of the Year at BAFTA and D.I.C.E. — and **Hades II (early access May 2024, release 25 September 2025)**. Proprietary engine. **Known for:** art direction, reactive narrative at enormous line counts, and an unmatched staff-retention record.
- **Team Cherry** — Australian; co-founded and co-directed by **Ari Gibson and William Pellen**; a very small team. *Hollow Knight* (2017) and ***Hollow Knight: Silksong* (4 September 2025)** — **over 7 million copies by mid-December 2025**, more than 5 million players in the first three days, and a peak of over **500,000 concurrent players on Steam** on launch day, third on Steam's all-time concurrent list at peak.
- **ConcernedApe (Eric Barone)** — solo developer. Built ***Stardew Valley*** over approximately four and a half years after a 2011 computer science degree, doing all writing, art, audio and programming himself, in C# with **Microsoft XNA**, migrated to **MonoGame in 2021**. Released 26 February 2016; **50 million copies by February 2026** (26 million PC, 7.9 million Switch); a Switch 2 edition on 25 December 2025. Continues to ship free major content updates a decade after launch. The single strongest counter-example to the "you need a studio" argument.
- **Motion Twin** — Bordeaux, France; a worker cooperative with no hierarchy and equal pay, developer of *Dead Cells* (2018). Its structure is as notable as its game. **`needs-verification`** — not fetched in this pass.

### Devolver Digital and its studios
Founded **25 June 2009** by **Nigel Lowrie, Harry Miller, Graeme Struthers, Rick Stults and Mike Wilson**; Austin, Texas. A digital-first indie publisher created explicitly to avoid the retail-cost problem that killed the founders' earlier ventures (Gathering of Developers, Gamecock Media). Published: *Serious Sam HD* (2009, Croteam), ***Hotline Miami*** (2012, Dennaton — the breakout hit), *Fall Guys* (2020, Mediatonic), *Hollow Knight* (as publisher outside certain regions), *Katana ZERO*, *Cult of the Lamb*, *Inscryption*, *Return of the Obra Dinn*. Owned studios: **Croteam, Dodge Roll, Doinksoft, Firefly Studios, Good Shepherd Entertainment, Nerial, System Era Softworks.** Listed on the London Stock Exchange's AIM (ticker **DEVO**) on **4 November 2021**, valued at **£694.6 million (US$950 million)**. **Known for:** an anti-corporate marketing persona and a genuinely developer-favourable deal structure.

### African studios worth naming

- **Free Lives** — **Cape Town, South Africa**; founded **April 2012** by **Evan Greenwood** (creative director and programmer). Games: *The Expendabros* (2014), ***Broforce*** (2015 — approximately **US$3 million** in sales by 2016), *Genital Jousting* (EA 2016, full 2018), *GORN* (2017), ***Terra Nil*** (2023), *Cricket Through the Ages* (2024), *Side Effects* (2025). Primary publisher **Devolver Digital**, signed after a team member met them at a Berlin indie festival. Described as one of the most prolific Devolver developers. Some staff have historically lived on-site with a communal kitchen. **The most internationally successful African game studio.**
- **Nyamakop** — Johannesburg/Cape Town; *Semblance* (2018), the first sub-Saharan African game published on Nintendo Switch, and *Relooted* (announced). **`needs-verification`.**
- **Sea Monster Entertainment** — Cape Town; serious games, gamified learning and client work. **`needs-verification`.**
- **Kiro'o Games** — Yaoundé, Cameroon; *Aurion: Legacy of the Kori-Odan* (2016), the first commercially released Cameroonian RPG. **`needs-verification`.**
- **Maliyo Games** — Lagos, Nigeria; mobile games for African markets, and an in-house training academy. **`needs-verification`.**
- **Leti Arts** — Accra, Ghana and Nairobi, Kenya; *Africa's Legends*, built on African mythology. **`needs-verification`.**
- **Celestial Games** — South Africa; historically notable for *Toxic Bunny* (1996), one of the earliest commercially released South African games. **`needs-verification`.**

**[ZA] [NA] Regional context:** the southern African industry is small, concentrated in Cape Town and Johannesburg, and oriented toward mobile, serious games and client work, with a handful of internationally published indies. There is a recognised regional events circuit (Africa Games Week, Playtopia, rAge). It is not large enough to absorb a graduating cohort; plan on remote work for overseas studios, self-publishing, or emigration. Namibia has no commercial studio of note.

## 3. The 2023–2026 contraction, honestly

### The numbers

| Period | Roles cut | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2023 | **>10,500** | |
| 2024 | **~14,600** | More than 2022 and 2023 combined; January 2024 the peak month |
| 2025 | ongoing | Unity's sixth layoff round in February 2025; no single verified annual total obtained |
| 2026 (to 1 July) | **~4,600** | **22 studios closed** |

**The largest single actors:** Embracer Group cut **7,761 employees**, closed or divested **44 studios** and cancelled **80 projects** after a US$2 billion deal with Saudi Arabia's Savvy Games Group collapsed. Unity ran six layoff rounds between June 2022 and February 2025, roughly **3,165–3,365 redundancies** through the first five, alongside the runtime-fee debacle. Microsoft Gaming cut 1,900 in January 2024 and restructured again in July 2025. Sony cut 900 in February 2024. EA, Take-Two, Ubisoft, Sega, Riot and Epic all ran multiple rounds.

**2026 to date:** Epic Games cut **over 1,000** on 24 March; Bungie cut ~**300** on 25 June; Bluepoint Games closed 19 February; Meta's Reality Labs closed **Sanzaru (130)**, **Twisted Pixel (65)** and **Armature Studio (40)** on 13 January; Playtika cut 500 on 14 January; Ubisoft cut 258+ across Winnipeg, Belgrade and San Francisco; Keywords San Francisco 128; Eidos Montréal 124.

### The causes, as stated in the sources

1. **Post-pandemic correction.** Studios over-hired during the COVID engagement surge and made aggressive acquisitions on the assumption that the elevated demand was the new baseline. It reverted to trend.
2. **Rising development costs.** AAA budgets moved from the US$50–150 million band to **US$200 million+**, with Call of Duty and GTA-scale projects cited at **US$250–300 million+**. At those numbers a single miss can end a studio.
3. **Economic pressure.** Higher interest rates ended the cheap capital that funded the acquisition wave and the speculative studio formation.
4. **Failed strategic bets.** Metaverse investment underperformed badly (Meta's Reality Labs lost US$13.72 billion in 2021 alone) and the live-service gold rush produced expensive failures — *Concord* closed Firewalk within weeks of launch.
5. **Market stagnation.** The industry returned to pre-pandemic growth trends and entered what the sources call a "reset phase".

### Studios closed outright, 2023–2026 (partial)

Monolith Productions, Arkane Austin, Tango Gameworks (later revived under Krafton), Alpha Dog Games, Ready at Dawn, Volition, Sony's London Studio, Pixelopus, Firewalk Studios, Neon Koi, Riot Forge, Bluepoint Games, The Initiative, Sanzaru, Twisted Pixel, Armature Studio — **over 30 in total across 2023–25**, and 22 more in the first half of 2026. Newly founded AAA studios also failed before shipping anything: Ridgeline Games, Deviation Games, Crop Circle Games, Smilegate Barcelona.

### The structural damage

**Junior roles were eliminated first and have not returned.** UK junior positions fell from **9.4% of postings in 2022 to 2.9% in 2023**. The industry commentary in the cited sources is explicit: this is an unsustainable skills structure with insufficient training pipelines for emerging talent. Geographically the heaviest cuts fell on North America and Europe — the US worst affected, then Canada, the UK and Poland.

### What is actually working

The contraction is not uniform. Three things thrived through it:

1. **Small teams with distinctive games.** *Stardew Valley* at 50 million; *Silksong* at 7 million in three months; *Balatro*, *Vampire Survivors*, *Lethal Company*, *Dave the Diver*, *Animal Well* — all made by teams of one to a dozen.
2. **Long-tail live games that already have their audience.** *Fortnite*, *League of Legends*, *Genshin Impact*, *Roblox*, *Minecraft* — though Epic's March 2026 cuts show even these are not immune.
3. **Studios with a proprietary engine advantage and disciplined scope.** Capcom (RE Engine), FromSoftware (456 people producing *Elden Ring*), Remedy (Northlight) — though Remedy's *FBC: Firebreak* demonstrates that engine advantage does not protect a misjudged product.

**The practical implication for anyone entering:** the mid-budget AAA path is the one that broke. The small-team path and the technically-specialised path both remain open. See `10_portfolio-and-breaking-in.md`.
