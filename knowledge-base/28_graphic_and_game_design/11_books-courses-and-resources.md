# Books, courses, talks and communities — an annotated register

**Summary.** A working register of what to read, watch, take and join, annotated with what each thing is actually for and who it is not for. Books are grouped by discipline and ordered by usefulness rather than prestige. Free resources are marked. Every URL in the tables was included as a reference; the ones actually fetched and verified in this pass are listed under `## Sources` and marked ✅ in the tables.

## Key facts

| Item | Value |
|---|---|

## 1. Graphic design books

### Start here

| Book | Author | Why | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***Thinking with Type*** | Ellen Lupton | The best entry point to typography in any language. Short, well-designed, exercise-driven, covers letter/word/text/grid in that order. Read first, always. | Anyone who already sets long-form text professionally |
| ***The Vignelli Canon*** | Massimo Vignelli | ~100 pages, **free PDF**, the entire modernist argument stated by someone who lived it. Intangibles then tangibles. | Anyone looking for technique rather than position |
| ***Grid Systems in Graphic Design*** | Josef Müller-Brockmann | The definitive treatment of the grid, from 1981. Bilingual German/English. Half the book is worked construction. | Screen-only designers who want CSS answers |
| ***The Elements of Typographic Style*** | Robert Bringhurst | The reference work. Poetic, opinionated, exhaustive. Read slowly, twice, over years. Bringhurst's "the typographer's one essential task" framing is the field's best statement of purpose. | A beginner — it will not teach you to start |
| ***Practical Typography*** ✅ | Matthew Butterick | Web-native, free to read, reader-supported and ad-free since 2013. Fundamentals, composition, formatting, font recommendations, page layout, sample documents, and a technical appendix on PDFs, embedding and web. The best *practical* typography resource online. | Anyone wanting design theory rather than rules |

### History and position

| Book | Author | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ***Meggs' History of Graphic Design*** | Philip B. Meggs (now Meggs & Purvis) | The standard survey, cave paintings to the present. Expensive, heavy, and best used as a reference you dip into rather than read straight through |
| ***Graphic Design: A Concise History*** | Richard Hollis | The short, sharp, better-written alternative to Meggs |
| ***Swiss Graphic Design: The Origins and Growth of an International Style 1920–1965*** | Richard Hollis | The definitive account of the movement in `03 §2` |
| ***Thoughts on Design*** (1947) | Paul Rand | The argument for design as a business function, made when nobody believed it |
| ***Seventy-Nine Short Essays on Design*** (2007) | Michael Bierut | The most honest account of what the job is actually like, by the best writer in the field |
| ***The Art of Looking Sideways*** (2001) | Alan Fletcher | A 1,000-page commonplace book that models how a designer's mind actually works |
| ***Designing Design*** (2011) and ***White*** (2010) | Kenya Hara | The non-Western position: emptiness as a container, design as re-presentation of the ordinary. The two most quietly influential design books of the century |
| ***Interaction of Color*** (1963) | Josef Albers | Colour as perception rather than pigment. Do the studies with paper |

### Craft and specialism

| Book | Author | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ***Typographie: A Manual of Design*** (1967) | Emil Ruder | The most rigorous single text on typographic space |
| ***Graphic Design Manual*** (1965) | Armin Hofmann | Form taught through extreme reduction: point, line, plane |
| ***Detail in Typography*** | Jost Hochuli | Forty pages on micro-typography — letter, word, line, column. Nothing else covers this ground |
| ***Designing Books: Practice and Theory*** | Jost Hochuli & Robin Kinross | The book-design reference |
| ***Die neue Typographie*** (1928) | Jan Tschichold | The modernist manifesto; read alongside his later recantation in *The Form of the Book* |
| ***How to Be a Graphic Designer Without Losing Your Soul*** | Adrian Shaughnessy | The practice-and-money book: clients, pricing, contracts, studios |
| ***Logo Modernism*** | Jens Müller / Taschen | 6,000 marks from 1940–80. A reference for the visual argument that a mark can be reduced |
| ***Making and Breaking the Grid*** | Timothy Samara | The applied companion to Müller-Brockmann |

## 2. Game design books

| Book | Author | Why | Not for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ***The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses*** | Jesse Schell | The single most useful game design book. 100+ "lenses" — questions to interrogate a design with. Source of the **elemental tetrad**. Read cover to cover once, then use as a reference forever | Anyone wanting a step-by-step method |
| ***A Theory of Fun for Game Design*** | Raph Koster | Short, illustrated, argues that fun is the sensation of a brain learning a pattern — which explains why games stop being fun exactly when mastered. Read in an afternoon; think about it for years | Anyone wanting practical technique |
| ***Rules of Play: Game Design Fundamentals*** | Katie Salen & Eric Zimmerman | The academic foundation. Three schemas — Rules, Play, Culture. Dense, long, and the reference every games-studies course is built on | Casual reading |
| ***The Art of Failure*** / ***Half-Real*** | Jesper Juul | The best games-studies writing on why we play games we lose at, and on the rule/fiction duality |
| ***Rise of the Videogame Zinesters*** | Anna Anthropy | The argument that games should be made by everyone, plus a genuinely practical on-ramp. The most politically important book on this list |
| ***Fundamentals of Game Design*** | Ernest Adams | The comprehensive textbook — genre by genre, system by system. Dry but complete |
| ***An Architectural Approach to Level Design*** | Christopher W. Totten | The level design book. Treats space as architecture: circulation, sightlines, thresholds, affordance. The only serious book-length treatment of the subject |
| ***Game Feel*** | Steve Swink | Real-time control, simulated space and polish, decomposed and measurable. The book behind `06 §6` |
| ***Blood, Sweat, and Pixels*** | Jason Schreier | Ten production war stories. Read it *before* deciding you want this career; it is the most accurate available account of what shipping actually costs |
| ***Press Reset*** | Jason Schreier | The sequel, on studio closures and the industry's disposal of its own people. Essential context for `08 §3` |
| ***The Ultimate History of Video Games*** (vols 1–2) | Steven L. Kent | The industry's narrative history from Pong onward |
| ***Characteristics of Games*** | Elias, Garfield & Gutschera | Formal analysis of game structures by, among others, the designer of *Magic: The Gathering*. Excellent on length, luck, player count and comeback mechanics |
| ***Advanced Game Design: A Systems Approach*** | Michael Sellers | The best single book on systems design specifically |
| ***Level Up! The Guide to Great Video Game Design*** | Scott Rogers | Practical, cheerful, production-oriented. Good first book for a teenager |

## 3. UI/UX and product books

| Book | Author | Why |
|---|---|---|
| ***Don't Make Me Think*** | Steve Krug | Two hours, and it fixes most beginner usability instincts |
| ***The Design of Everyday Things*** | Don Norman | Affordances, signifiers, mapping, feedback. The founding text; co-founder of NN/g |
| ***About Face*** | Alan Cooper et al. | The interaction design reference; goal-directed design and personas |
| ***Refactoring UI*** | Adam Wathan & Steve Schoger | Blunt, practical visual-design rules for developers and for designers who want checkable heuristics |
| ***Design Systems*** | Alla Kholmatova | The best book on the organisational reality of running a design system |
| ***Articulating Design Decisions*** | Tom Greever | How to defend work to stakeholders — the skill that determines whether good design ships |
| ***Information Architecture*** ("the polar bear book") | Rosenfeld, Morville & Arango | The IA reference |
| ***Inclusive Design Patterns*** / ***Accessibility for Everyone*** | Heydon Pickering / Laura Kalbag | The practical accessibility books |

**GDC talks worth watching by name** (all findable on the GDC YouTube channel or Vault):
- *Juice It or Lose It* — Martin Jonasson & Petri Purho. Twenty layers of feedback added to a bare Breakout clone. The fastest lesson in game feel that exists.
- *Level Design Workshop* series — the annual GDC level design track; the *Half-Life 2*, *Dishonored* and *Doom 2016* entries are the ones to start with.
- *The Door Problem* material and Liz England's essay of the same name — the clearest explanation of what every discipline on a game team actually does.
- *Eight Frames in Half a Second* (Overwatch netcode) and the Overwatch *Gameplay Architecture and Netcode* talk — the reference explanations of how a networked shooter works.
- *Building a Better Centaur* (Halo AI) and the *Killzone 2* AI talks — behaviour trees and utility AI in production.
- *Math for Game Programmers* series — annual, and the most concentrated maths teaching in the industry.
- *AI and Level Design in Rain World*, *The Simulation of Dwarf Fortress*, and *Spelunky* postmortems — for emergent systems.
- Jason Schreier's and the IGDA's industry/career sessions — for the business reality.

| **Interaction Design Foundation** | IxDF | Online, subscription | Comprehensive UX curriculum; cheap; credential value is modest |
| **Type@Cooper / Type West** | Cooper Union / Letterform Archive | In-person and online, paid | The two best type-design programmes outside a full master's |
| **Futur / Skillshare / Domestika** | various | Online, paid | Highly variable. Useful for a specific technique, useless as a curriculum |

**A note on courses generally:** in both fields, no course is a credential. Courses are a way to acquire a specific skill efficiently. The output — the thing you make during the course — is the only part an employer will look at. Choose courses that force you to produce a portfolio piece.

## 7. A suggested reading order

**Graphic design, from zero:**
Lupton *Thinking with Type* → Vignelli *Canon* (free) → Butterick *Practical Typography* (free) → Müller-Brockmann *Grid Systems* → Hollis *Concise History* → Bringhurst *Elements* → Bierut *Seventy-Nine Short Essays* → Hara *Designing Design* → Hochuli *Detail in Typography*.

**Game design, from zero:**
Koster *A Theory of Fun* → Schell *The Art of Game Design* → Schreier *Blood, Sweat, and Pixels* → Totten *An Architectural Approach to Level Design* → Swink *Game Feel* → Sellers *Advanced Game Design* → Salen & Zimmerman *Rules of Play*. Watch *Juice It or Lose It* and the GMTK level design series in parallel.

**Product design, from zero:**
Krug *Don't Make Me Think* → Norman *The Design of Everyday Things* → Nielsen's 10 heuristics (free) → WCAG 2.2 Understanding 1.4.3 and 1.4.11 (free) → Wathan & Schoger *Refactoring UI* → Kholmatova *Design Systems* → Rosenfeld et al. *Information Architecture* → Greever *Articulating Design Decisions*.

**And in all three:** read one book, then make one thing. The ratio matters more than the list.

## Open questions

- **Most URLs in the tables above were not individually fetched.** Only those marked ✅ were verified in this pass. Treat unmarked URLs as `needs-verification` — several are long-standing and stable, but link rot is real.
- **ldjam.com could not be fetched** — its TLS certificate had expired at the time of access. The Compo/Jam format description is from established convention.
- **No book ISBNs, editions or prices are given** because none were verified. Check the current edition before purchase — *Meggs*, *Bringhurst*, *The Art of Game Design* and *About Face* all have materially different editions.
- **The Vignelli Canon's current free-PDF host was not verified.** Wikipedia confirms it was released as a free e-book in 2009 and expanded in 2010; vignelli.com has been intermittently unavailable. Search for the current mirror rather than relying on a URL here.
- **Course details** (duration, price, current availability) for Coursera, edX, CGMA, School of Motion, IxDF, Type@Cooper and Type West were not verified.
- **Southern African community links** (Make Games South Africa, Africa Games Week, Playtopia, rAge) were not verified and have no URLs given for that reason.
