# Remote-site risk register and contingency planning

**Summary.** Remote construction does not face different risks from urban construction; it faces the same risks with **longer recovery times and fewer options**. That asymmetry is the whole of remote risk management: mitigations that reduce *recovery time* are worth far more than mitigations that reduce *probability*. This file gives a worked risk register for a remote northern Namibian building project — nine risk families with likelihood, impact, mitigation and contingency — plus quantified contingency sizing and a reusable register template.

## Key facts

| Concept | Value |
|---|---|
| Risk score | **Likelihood (1–5) × Impact (1–5)** = 1–25 |
| Escalation threshold | Score **≥ 12** goes to the client/steering group |
| Programme contingency, typical remote project | **15–25 %** of duration |
| Cost contingency, typical remote project | **10–20 %** of works value |
| Main rainy season | **February–April**; secondary September–November |
| Namibian dollar | Pegged **1:1** to the rand — no NAD/ZAR currency risk |
| Overload enforcement | <2 t excess: fine + detention; >2 t: court + impound |
| Nearest hospital to Okongo | **On site in the village** — 62-bed district hospital |

### Scoring scales used

| Score | Likelihood | Impact (programme) | Impact (cost) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Rare (<5 % in project life) | <2 days | <0.5 % of works value |
| 2 | Unlikely (5–20 %) | 2–5 days | 0.5–2 % |
| 3 | Possible (20–50 %) | 1–3 weeks | 2–5 % |
| 4 | Likely (50–80 %) | 3–8 weeks | 5–10 % |
| 5 | Almost certain (>80 %) | >8 weeks | >10 % |

## 1. Supply failure

**Description.** A key material fails to arrive: supplier default, production delay, wrong goods, quantity short, vehicle unavailable, or an order that was never acknowledged.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 4 — Likely. On a 9-month remote project with 8 waves and ~60 orders, at least one significant failure is close to certain. |
| **Impact** | 4 — 3–8 weeks if it is a long-lead item; 1 week if it is a commodity. |
| **Score** | **16 — escalate** |

**Mitigation**
- Buffer stock on the three or four materials that stop work (see `01`).
- Dual-source every commodity: two cement suppliers, two block sources, two transporters on account.
- Written order acknowledgement with a stated ex-works date; unacknowledged orders reported weekly.
- Structured expediting at 25 %, 75 % and ex-works minus 5 days (see `05`).
- Pre-dispatch inspection on fabricated items.
- Specify substitutable products; avoid single-source specifications where the performance can be met by several products.

**Contingency**
- Pre-agreed premium-freight route (dedicated vehicle from Windhoek or Oshakati) with a known cost.
- Resequencing plan: a list, prepared in advance, of work that can be brought forward if a trade is blocked.
- Emergency purchase authority delegated to the site manager up to a stated limit, so a N$8 000 fix is not waiting on a N$0 approval process.
- Local substitution options identified for each commodity at the start.

## 2. Weather

**Description.** The February–April rains make the last mile impassable, flood *oshanas*, stop earthworks and concreting, and damage stored material. Rainfall is also "highly variable, and droughts are common" — so the risk includes *water shortage* as well as excess.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 5 — Almost certain that the rains will affect the programme in some way. |
| **Impact** | 3 — 1–3 weeks of disruption if planned for; 8+ weeks if not. |
| **Score** | **15 — escalate** |

**Mitigation**
- Season-weighted delivery calendar: all heavy deliveries complete **before February**.
- Programme to be weather-tight (roof on, openings closed) before the rains.
- Stock 6–10 weeks of finishes and internal work material before February.
- Access preparation and drainage in the dry season (see `03`).
- Covered storage and raised stacks in place before the first rain, not after it.
- Rainwater harvesting from the new roof as a water-shortage hedge.

**Contingency**
- A declared wet-season work plan: internal work, joinery fitting, services first fix, snagging, training.
- Light-vehicle-only delivery protocol for the wet months, with a reduced pick list.
- Extension-of-time provisions in the contract with agreed weather-day definitions, so the argument happens before the rain and not after.
- Standing water pumps and a drainage kit on site.

## 3. Vehicle and plant breakdown

**Description.** A delivery vehicle breaks down or bogs; the mixer, compactor, generator, block machine or water pump fails; the site vehicle is off the road.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 4 — Likely over 9 months in dust, heat and corrugation. |
| **Impact** | 3 — days to weeks depending on the item and the spares position. |
| **Score** | **12 — escalate** |

**Mitigation**
- Halve service intervals for air and fuel filters in dust.
- Critical spares held on site: filters, belts, hoses, a spare water pump, a spare generator (or a second small one), tyres, a battery, plug-in consumables for the mixer and vibrator.
- Prefer duplicated small plant over a single large unit — two 2.5 kVA generators beat one 5 kVA.
- Prefer locally repairable equipment; check that parts exist in Ondangwa or Oshakati before buying.
- Recovery capability on site: rated recovery points, a snatch strap, a jack and boards, and a relationship with a local tractor owner.
- Daily plant checks logged by the operator.

**Contingency**
- Named recovery contractor and tractor owner with agreed call-out rates.
- Hire arrangement pre-negotiated with an Ondangwa/Oshakati plant hire firm.
- Manual fallback methods documented: hand mixing, hand compaction, hand pumping — slow but not stopped.

## 4. Theft and pilferage

**Description.** Continuous small-scale loss of fuel, tools, cable, brass and cement; occasionally a break-in for plant or solar equipment.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 4 — Likely; some loss is near-universal. |
| **Impact** | 2–3 — usually cost rather than programme, unless a critical tool or the fuel supply goes. |
| **Score** | **10–12** |

**Mitigation**
- Fenced compound, one gate, gate register.
- Container stores with lock boxes and closed-shackle padlocks.
- Issue against requisition; daily fuel reconciliation by dip and meter.
- Resident local security, properly paid and paid on time.
- Serial-number and photograph register for all plant and power tools.
- High-value items delivered late and fitted promptly.
- Community ownership of the project (see `07`) — the highest-yield control there is.

**Contingency**
- Insurance with a known excess; check whether the policy covers an unattended remote site and what security conditions it imposes.
- Replacement stock of the most-stolen consumables held in the secure store.
- A documented loss-reporting route to the client, so a pattern is visible early.

## 5. Labour

**Description.** Insufficient skilled labour; absenteeism (funerals, harvest, pension days); disputes over wages or hiring fairness; illness; loss of a key supervisor.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 3 — Possible to likely. |
| **Impact** | 3 — 1–3 weeks. |
| **Score** | **9–12** |

**Mitigation**
- Transparent recruitment through the village council and headman.
- Correct, timely, verifiable wage payment — the most common trigger of labour trouble.
- Programme around the agricultural calendar and known local events.
- Train two people for every critical skill so no single absence stops work.
- Written terms in a language people read; explained verbally too (14.8 % of adults never attended school).
- Grievance route with a named owner and a response time.
- Accommodation, water, food and sanitation of a standard people will stay for.

**Contingency**
- A named regional labour broker or artisan contact in Oshakati/Ondangwa.
- Supervisor succession: a deputy who has actually run the site for a week.
- Contingency for bringing in a specialist trade at short notice, including travel and accommodation.

## 6. Cash flow

**Description.** Payment certificates delayed; a large wave of material must be paid for before the corresponding work is certified; deposits tie up capital months ahead of use; retention held.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 4 — Likely, and structurally worse on wave-based procurement than on urban JIT. |
| **Impact** | 4 — a stopped project is the usual outcome. |
| **Score** | **16 — escalate** |

**Mitigation**
- Model cash flow **against the delivery calendar**, not against a smooth S-curve. The waves create step changes in outflow.
- Negotiate payment terms that recognise the pattern: material-on-site payment provisions, or advance payments against a bond.
- Open supplier accounts early and get the credit limits raised before they are needed, not after an order is stopped.
- Stage deposits so several long-lead deposits do not fall in the same month.
- Invoice promptly and completely; on remote projects the commonest cause of certificate delay is missing paperwork from site.

**Contingency**
- Agreed overdraft or facility headroom, arranged before mobilisation.
- A prioritised deferral list: which orders can slip a wave without stopping work.
- Client-direct purchase of one or two very large items as a fallback.

## 7. Currency and price

**Description.** Cost escalation between tender and purchase; exchange-rate movement on imported components.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 3 — Possible over a 9–12 month project. |
| **Impact** | 2–3 |
| **Score** | **6–9** |

**Notes and mitigation**
- **There is no NAD/ZAR currency risk**: the Namibian dollar is pegged 1:1 to the rand and both circulate in Namibia. South African purchases therefore carry price risk but not FX risk.
- **Real FX exposure sits on non-SACU imports** — European ironmongery, Chinese sanitaryware, imported plant — priced in USD or EUR.
- Fix prices with suppliers for a stated validity period and place orders within it.
- Order long-lead imported items early, which converts FX risk into inventory risk (usually the smaller of the two).
- Include a fuel-escalation clause in freight contracts knowingly, rather than discovering it on the invoice.
- Where imported content is significant, consider a forward cover arrangement or ask the supplier to quote in NAD/ZAR and carry the risk themselves.

## 8. Quality rejection

**Description.** Material fails on delivery or on test; work is rejected by the engineer; site-made blocks fail crushing tests; the wrong specification is delivered and installed.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 3 — Possible, and higher where informal supply is used. |
| **Impact** | 4 — a rejection on site costs a whole freight cycle; rejected *work* costs demolition plus rebuild. |
| **Score** | **12 — escalate** |

**Mitigation**
- Test before ordering, not after delivering: qualify every informal source with samples (see `07`).
- Pre-dispatch inspection on fabricated goods.
- Goods-inward inspection against the order **before the vehicle leaves**, with photographs.
- Hold points in the programme: no covering up until inspected.
- Keep a reference sample on site for site-made products.
- Cube and block testing on a defined frequency, with results reviewed weekly.
- Design tolerantly for materials of variable quality.

**Contingency**
- Rejection rights and retention written into local purchase orders.
- A defined disposal route for rejected material — including offering it to the community for non-structural use, which converts a loss into goodwill.
- Allow for a re-order freight cycle in the programme for the highest-risk items.

## 9. Access and permitting

**Description.** Route becomes impassable or restricted; a bridge or culvert fails; an abnormal load permit is refused or delayed; a weighbridge stops a load; a border delay.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Likelihood** | 3 |
| **Impact** | 3 |
| **Score** | **9** |

**Mitigation**
- Route survey before the first heavy delivery; culvert and crossing capacities assessed.
- Design out abnormal loads at design stage (splice trusses, bolted frames, smaller tanks).
- Apply for abnormal-load permits early and confirm route approval **before fabrication starts**.
- Contract the transporter to comply with mass limits, and check the load ticket — the overload penalty regime (fine and detention under 2 t excess, court and impound above) is not recoverable within a delivery window.
- Documents sent ahead electronically for any cross-border movement; use a clearing agent.

**Contingency**
- Alternative route identified and driven.
- Transhipment point on the tar identified, with a hard standing, so a load can be split.
- Fallback to smaller vehicles priced in advance.

## 10. Sizing the contingency

Contingency should be **derived**, not guessed. A simple expected-value approach:

For each risk: `Expected cost = probability × cost impact`, and `Expected delay = probability × programme impact`.

*Illustrative, for a N$6 000 000 works value:*

| Risk | Prob. | Cost impact | Expected cost | Delay impact | Expected delay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Supply failure | 0.65 | N$180 000 | N$117 000 | 25 d | 16 d |
| Weather | 0.85 | N$120 000 | N$102 000 | 15 d | 13 d |
| Breakdown | 0.60 | N$90 000 | N$54 000 | 8 d | 5 d |
| Theft | 0.70 | N$70 000 | N$49 000 | 2 d | 1 d |
| Labour | 0.40 | N$110 000 | N$44 000 | 12 d | 5 d |
| Cash flow | 0.50 | N$150 000 | N$75 000 | 20 d | 10 d |
| Currency/price | 0.35 | N$130 000 | N$45 500 | 0 d | 0 d |
| Quality rejection | 0.35 | N$200 000 | N$70 000 | 20 d | 7 d |
| Access/permitting | 0.30 | N$95 000 | N$28 500 | 12 d | 4 d |
| **Total expected** | | | **N$585 000** | | **61 days** |

That is **9.75 % of works value** and roughly **12 weeks** — before any allowance for correlation. Note that these risks are **positively correlated** (rain causes access failure, breakdown and supply failure simultaneously), so a simple sum understates the tail. A common adjustment is to add 25–50 % to the expected value for correlation, giving roughly **12–15 % cost contingency and 15–18 weeks** — consistent with the 10–20 % / 15–25 % rules of thumb.

**Use it as two separate pots:**
- **Cost contingency** — drawn against identified risk events, with a register entry for each drawdown.
- **Programme contingency (float)** — held at project level, not distributed into activities, and released by the project manager against evidence. Distributed float is always consumed; held float is available when it is needed.

## 11. Reusable risk register template

```
PROJECT RISK REGISTER — [Project] — Rev [n] — [date] — Owner: [name]

Columns:
  ID              Unique reference (R-01, R-02 …)
  Date raised     ISO date
  Category        Supply / Weather / Plant / Security / Labour / Financial /
                  Quality / Access / HSE / External
  Description     "If <cause>, then <event>, resulting in <effect>"
  Likelihood      1–5 (see scale)
  Impact-cost     1–5
  Impact-time     1–5
  Inherent score  L × max(Ic, It)
  Mitigation      Actions to reduce likelihood or impact — with owner and due date
  Residual L      1–5 after mitigation
  Residual I      1–5 after mitigation
  Residual score  L × I
  Contingency     What we do if it happens anyway
  Trigger         The observable event that says "start the contingency now"
  Cost allowance  N$ held against this risk
  Time allowance  Days held against this risk
  Owner           Named individual, not a department
  Review date     When it is next examined
  Status          Open / Monitoring / Realised / Closed
  Closure note    What actually happened

Rules of use:
  1. Every risk has one named owner. "The team" is not an owner.
  2. Every risk has a TRIGGER — the observable fact that starts the contingency.
     A contingency without a trigger is never invoked in time.
  3. Reviewed weekly on site; monthly with the client. Scores are changed at
     review, and the change is recorded, not overwritten.
  4. Realised risks are not deleted. They are marked Realised with a closure
     note, because the next project needs them.
  5. Any residual score >= 12 is escalated to the client/steering group with a
     named decision required.
  6. New risks may be raised by anyone on site; the log is open.
  7. Drawdowns from the contingency pot are recorded against a risk ID.
```
