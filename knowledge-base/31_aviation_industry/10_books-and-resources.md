# Books, references and data sources — an annotated register

**Summary.** What to read, in what order, for each thread in this domain, with an honest note on what each source is good for and what it is not. **FREE** marks material available at no cost from a primary source; **PAID** marks commercial books and subscriptions; **PAYWALLED** marks standards and documents that are technically public but priced. Bibliographic details below are from working knowledge unless a URL is given; edition numbers and years should be confirmed against a bookseller or library catalogue before citing, and are flagged where I am least confident.

## 1. The airline business

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Rigas Doganis, *Flying Off Course: Airline Economics and Marketing*** (Routledge; 5th edition c. 2019) — **PAID** | The standard textbook of airline economics for four decades. Cost structure, unit costs, pricing, yield management, network economics, and the industry's chronic profitability problem, written by someone who ran an airline (Olympic) as well as teaching. Start here. |
| **Rigas Doganis, *The Airline Business*** (Routledge; 3rd edition c. 2019) — **PAID** | The companion volume: regulation, liberalisation, bilaterals and open skies, alliances, ownership and control, low-cost carriers, and the political economy. The chapter on ownership and control restrictions is the clearest explanation available of why airline nationality still exists. |
| **Peter Belobaba, Amedeo Odoni and Cynthia Barnhart (eds.), *The Global Airline Industry*** (Wiley; 2nd edition 2015; a 3rd edition may exist — verify) — **PAID** | The MIT text. Multi-author, more quantitative than Doganis, and the definitive treatment of revenue management (Belobaba wrote EMSR), airline schedule planning and fleet assignment (Barnhart), and airports and ATC (Odoni). If you want the mathematics of RM and network planning, this is the book. |
| **Bijan Vasigh, Ken Fleming and Thomas Tacker, *Introduction to Air Transport Economics: From Theory to Applications*** (Routledge) — **PAID** | More accessible than Belobaba, with worked problems. Good for the finance and economics fundamentals, cost of capital, demand estimation and forecasting. |
| **Bijan Vasigh, Reza Taleghani and Darryl Jenkins, *Aircraft Finance: Strategies for Managing Capital Costs in a Turbulent Industry*** — **PAID** | Structures, valuation, lease accounting. |
| **Peter Morrell, *Airline Finance*** (Routledge; 5th edition c. 2021) — **PAID** | The best single book on how an airline is financed and how to read its accounts. Balance sheet analysis, lease vs. buy, IFRS 16, hedging, and airline valuation. |
| **Nawal Taneja** (numerous volumes on airline strategy and transformation) — **PAID** | Strategy-consultant register; useful for framing, light on numbers. |
| **Michael Porter's five forces applied to airlines** — the analysis is scattered across the literature rather than in one book; Doganis and Belobaba both do it. |

## 2. Operations, scheduling and network planning

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **Belobaba/Odoni/Barnhart, chapters on schedule planning, fleet assignment and crew scheduling** — **PAID** | The single best treatment in a textbook. |
| **Cynthia Barnhart and Barry Smith (eds.), *Quantitative Problem Solving Methods in the Airline Industry*** (Springer, INFORMS series) — **PAID** | Research-level: fleet assignment, aircraft routing, crew pairing and rostering, revenue management, disruption recovery, with the actual formulations. If you are building or evaluating an optimiser, this is the reference. |
| **Ahmed Abdelghany and Khaled Abdelghany, *Airline Network Planning and Scheduling*** and *Modeling Applications in the Airline Industry* — **PAID** | Practitioner-oriented, with implementable models. |
| **Amedeo Odoni and Richard de Neufville, *Airport Systems: Planning, Design and Management*** (McGraw-Hill; 2nd edition 2013) — **PAID** | The airport-side equivalent of Doganis. Capacity, delay, terminal design, ground access, airfield geometry, demand forecasting. |
| **Michael Ball et al. (eds.), *Handbook of Transportation Science / Air Transportation*** — **PAID** | Reference-level. |
| **IATA Worldwide Airport Slot Guidelines (WASG)** — https://www.iata.org/ — **FREE** (the guidelines themselves are published) | The operative document for slot allocation. Read it alongside EU Regulation 95/93. |
| **IATA Airport Handling Manual (AHM) and Ground Operations Manual (IGOM)** — **PAID** | The de facto standards for ground handling. Expensive; essential if you work in the area. |

## 3. Human factors and safety — the canon

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **James Reason, *Human Error*** (Cambridge, 1990) — **PAID** | The foundational text. The GEMS framework, the slip/lapse/mistake/violation taxonomy, latent conditions, and the origin of the Swiss cheese model. Dense and worth the effort. |
| **James Reason, *Managing the Risks of Organizational Accidents*** (Ashgate, 1997) — **PAID** | The applied version, written for managers. Safety culture, the "vulnerable system syndrome", the unrocked boat, and the navigational aids for a safety programme. If you only read one Reason, read this one. |
| **James Reason, *The Human Contribution*** (Ashgate, 2008) — **PAID** | The late and more optimistic Reason: heroic recovery, error wisdom, and the limits of the systems approach. |
| **Sidney Dekker, *The Field Guide to Understanding 'Human Error'*** (Routledge; 3rd edition 2014) — **PAID** | The best short book in the field. Old view vs. new view, hindsight bias, counterfactuals, "human error as a symptom of trouble deeper in the system", and how to write an investigation that explains rather than judges. Read it before you ever investigate anything. |
| **Sidney Dekker, *Drift into Failure*** (Ashgate, 2011) — **PAID** | How systems migrate to the boundary of safe operation through locally rational decisions. |
| **Sidney Dekker, *Just Culture: Restoring Trust and Accountability in Your Organization*** (3rd edition, Routledge 2016) — **PAID** | The most honest treatment of the tension between accountability and learning, including the criminalisation problem. |
| **Erik Hollnagel, *Safety-I and Safety-II: The Past and Future of Safety Management*** (Ashgate, 2014) — **PAID** | The core statement of the resilience position. |
| **Erik Hollnagel, *The ETTO Principle*** (2009) and ***FRAM: The Functional Resonance Analysis Method*** (2012) — **PAID** | The efficiency-thoroughness trade-off, and the modelling method. FRAM is intellectually interesting and operationally hard. |
| **Hollnagel, Woods and Leveson (eds.), *Resilience Engineering: Concepts and Precepts*** (2006) — **PAID** | The founding collection. |
| **Nancy Leveson, *Engineering a Safer World: Systems Thinking Applied to Safety*** (MIT Press, 2011) — **FREE** (MIT Press open access PDF) | STAMP and STPA: safety as a control problem rather than a failure-chain problem. Increasingly used in aerospace certification. The free PDF is a genuine gift. |
| **Earl Wiener and David Nagel (eds.), *Human Factors in Aviation*** (Academic Press, 1988) — **PAID** | The original comprehensive handbook. Wiener's own chapter on cockpit automation, and the "clumsy automation" and "ironies" material, remains the best statement of the automation problem. |
| **Eduardo Salas and Dan Maurino (eds.), *Human Factors in Aviation*, 2nd edition** (Academic Press, 2010) and ***Human Factors in Aviation and Aerospace*, 3rd edition** (2023) — **PAID** | The successors. Salas is the authority on team training and CRM effectiveness; Maurino brought the ICAO operational perspective. |
| **Robert Helmreich, Ashleigh Merritt and John Wilhelm, "The Evolution of Crew Resource Management Training in Commercial Aviation"** (*International Journal of Aviation Psychology*, 1999) — **FREE** (widely available) | The generations-of-CRM paper. Short and definitive. |
| **Helmreich and Merritt, *Culture at Work in Aviation and Medicine*** (1998) — **PAID** | National, organisational and professional culture and their interaction with CRM. |
| **Scott Shappell and Douglas Wiegmann, *A Human Error Approach to Aviation Accident Analysis: The Human Factors Analysis and Classification System*** (Ashgate, 2003) — **PAID** | HFACS in full, with the coding guidance. |
| **Christopher Wickens et al., *Engineering Psychology and Human Performance*** — **PAID** | The cognitive underpinnings: attention, workload, decision making, displays. |
| **Charles Perrow, *Normal Accidents*** (1984) — **PAID** | Interactive complexity and tight coupling. Wrong about aviation in its conclusions and indispensable in its analysis. |
| **Diane Vaughan, *The Challenger Launch Decision*** (1996) — **PAID** | "Normalisation of deviance" — the concept every safety manager quotes and few have read the source of. Not aviation, entirely applicable. |
| **Jan Hagen, *Confronting Mistakes*** — **PAID** | Error management from the leadership perspective; readable. |
| **Matthew Syed, *Black Box Thinking*** (2015) — **PAID** | Popular, aviation-flattering, useful for explaining the field to people outside it. Not a source. |

### Accident reports as primary literature

Reading the actual reports is worth more than any secondary account. All **FREE**:

- **BEA (France)** — https://bea.aero — AF447 (the final report, 2012, is a model of its kind), Air Inter 148, Concorde.
- **NTSB (US)** — https://www.ntsb.gov — Asiana 214, United 232, US Airways 1549, Colgan 3407, and the accident dockets which contain the underlying factual material.
- **AAIB (UK)** — https://www.gov.uk/government/organisations/air-accidents-investigation-branch — BA038 (Heathrow 777 fuel icing), Kegworth, the monthly bulletins which are the best routine reading in the field.
- **ATSB (Australia)** — https://www.atsb.gov.au — QF32 (the A380 uncontained engine failure) is a case study in resilience.
- **TSB Canada**, **BFU Germany**, **JTSB Japan**, **DSB Netherlands** (the MH17 report), **KNKT Indonesia** (Lion Air 610), **Ethiopian AIB** (ET302).
- **Joint Authorities Technical Review (JATR) report on the 737 MAX certification** (2019) and the **US House Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure final report** (2020) — both **FREE** and both essential for understanding how certification can fail.

## 4. Technical and regulatory references

### Free primary sources — the best value in aviation

**FAA Handbooks and Manuals** — https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation — all **FREE** PDF:

| Number | Title |
|---|---|
| FAA-H-8083-25C | Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge |
| FAA-H-8083-3C | Airplane Flying Handbook |
| FAA-H-8083-15B | Instrument Flying Handbook |
| FAA-H-8083-1B | Weight and Balance Handbook |
| FAA-H-8083-2A | Risk Management Handbook |
| FAA-H-8083-28 | Aviation Weather Handbook (consolidates the old AC 00-6, 00-45 and 00-24) |
| FAA-H-8083-30B | Aviation Maintenance Technician Handbook — General |
| FAA-H-8083-31B | Aviation Maintenance Technician Handbook — Airframe |
| FAA-H-8083-32B | Aviation Maintenance Technician Handbook — Powerplant |
| FAA-H-8083-21 | Rotorcraft/Helicopter Flying Handbook |
| FAA-H-8083-5 | Weight-Shift Control Aircraft Flying Handbook |
| FAA-H-8083-29 | Powered Parachute Flying Handbook |
| FAA-G-8082-22 | Remote Pilot — Small UAS Study Guide |
| (unnumbered in the list) | Helicopter Flying Handbook, Glider Flying Handbook, Balloon Flying Handbook, Seaplane/Skiplane Handbook |

Also **FREE** from the FAA: the **Aeronautical Information Manual (AIM)**, the **Instrument Procedures Handbook**, the **Advisory Circular** series (AC 120-xx for operations, AC 25-xx for transport category design, AC 61-136 for training devices, AC 120-76D for EFB, AC 00-xx for weather), the **Aviation Instructor's Handbook**, and the **Order 8900.1 Flight Standards Information Management System** — which is what your FAA inspector actually works from.

**EASA Easy Access Rules** — https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/document-library/easy-access-rules — **FREE**, in PDF, online and XML. These consolidate the Implementing Rule, the Acceptable Means of Compliance and the Guidance Material into a single readable document, which is otherwise a three-document cross-reference exercise. The set covers, among others:

- Air Operations (Regulation (EU) No 965/2012 — Part-ARO, Part-ORO, Part-CAT, Part-SPA, Part-NCC, Part-NCO, Part-SPO), currently at Revision 24 (March 2026)
- Aircrew (Regulation (EU) No 1178/2011 — Part-FCL, Part-MED, Part-CC, Part-ARA, Part-ORA)
- Continuing Airworthiness (Regulation (EU) No 1321/2014 — Part-M, Part-ML, Part-145, Part-66, Part-147, Part-CAMO, Part-CAO)
- Initial Airworthiness (Part-21)
- CS-25 (Large Aeroplanes), CS-23, CS-E, CS-P, CS-APU, CS-ETSO
- Aerodromes, ATM/ANS, Standardised European Rules of the Air (SERA), U-space, Information Security (Part-IS)
- **CS-FSTD(A)** and **CS-FSTD(H)** for simulators

**eCFR** — https://www.ecfr.gov — **FREE** and always current. Title 14 for aviation: Parts 1, 21, 23, 25, 33, 39, 43, 45, 47, 60, 61, 65, 91, 117, 119, 121, 125, 135, 141, 142, 145, 147.

**UK CAA** — https://www.caa.co.uk — **FREE**: CAP publications (CAP 393 Air Navigation Order, CAP 413 Radiotelephony, CAP 371 flight time limitations, CAP 382 MOR scheme), and the UK AIP via NATS AIS.

**ICAO** — https://www.icao.int — mixed. The **Annexes and Doc-series manuals are PAYWALLED** and expensive (the ICAO Store prices individual Annexes in the low hundreds of dollars). Available **FREE**: the **Safety Report**, the **Global Aviation Safety Plan (GASP)** and **Global Air Navigation Plan (GANP)**, the **State Safety Programme** guidance, the **iSTARS** dashboards, the **Safety Management implementation website** (https://www.icao.int/safety/SafetyManagement/), the **CORSIA** documents, and many working papers from Assembly and Council sessions. Doc numbers worth knowing: **4444** PANS-ATM, **8168** PANS-OPS, **8643** aircraft type designators, **9859** Safety Management Manual, **9868** PANS-TRG, **9995** EBT Manual, **9625** FSTD Criteria, **10011** Manual on Aeroplane Upset Prevention and Recovery Training, **9683** Human Factors Training Manual, **9756** Manual of Aircraft Accident and Incident Investigation.

**EUROCONTROL** — https://www.eurocontrol.int — **FREE** and excellent: the Performance Review Reports, the Aviation Outlook forecasts, SKYbrary (see below), the ATM Master Plan, the Network Operations Reports, and the Standard Inputs for Economic Analyses (the source for cost-of-delay figures).

**SKYbrary** — https://skybrary.aero — **FREE**. EUROCONTROL and Flight Safety Foundation's operational safety wiki. The single best free starting point for any operational safety topic; well-referenced, with accident summaries and the underlying source documents linked.

**Flight Safety Foundation** — https://flightsafety.org — **FREE** archives of *Flight Safety Digest*, *AeroSafety World* and the ALAR (Approach and Landing Accident Reduction) Toolkit, which remains one of the most useful practical products the industry has produced.

### Commercial technical references

- **Jeppesen** — charts, the NavData database, JeppView, and the Airway Manual. **PAID**, and the dominant chart provider outside the states that publish their own. **Lido** (Lufthansa Systems) and **NAVBLUE** (Airbus) are the alternatives.
- **OEM manuals** — AFM, FCOM, FCTM, QRH, AMM, IPC, TSM, SRM, WDM. Controlled documents, supplied under licence to the operator. Not publicly available, and the FCTM in particular is the most useful piece of aviation writing most pilots will ever read.
- **RTCA / EUROCAE documents** (DO-178C, DO-254, DO-326A, ARP4754A, ARP4761) — **PAYWALLED**, sold by RTCA and SAE, typically US$200–800 each. There is no free legitimate copy.
- **ARINC specifications** (424, 429, 610, 653, 664, 615A) — **PAYWALLED**, sold by SAE ITC.
- **IATA manuals** — DGR (Dangerous Goods Regulations), AHM, IGOM, ULD Regulations, Perishable Cargo Regulations, Live Animals Regulations, Airport Development Reference Manual, Safety Report, World Air Transport Statistics. All **PAID** and expensive.

## 5. Flight simulation literature

| Work | Note |
|---|---|
| **David Allerton, *Principles of Flight Simulation*** (Wiley/AIAA, 2009) — **PAID** | The single best book on the subject and the one to buy if you buy one. Covers the equations of motion, the aerodynamic and engine models, flight control systems, motion platforms and washout filters, visual systems, instrument displays, real-time software design and simulator qualification, with actual code and actual mathematics. Written by an engineer who built simulators at Cranfield and Sheffield. |
| **David Allerton, *Flight Simulation Software: Design, Development and Testing*** (Wiley, 2022) — **PAID** | The successor volume, focused on the software architecture, real-time issues, and testing. |
| **J. M. Rolfe and K. J. Staples (eds.), *Flight Simulation*** (Cambridge, 1986) — **PAID** (and out of print; library or second-hand) | The classic. Historical, and the treatment of the human factors of simulation, motion cueing and training transfer has not been superseded. The chapter on the vestibular system and motion perception is still cited. |
| **Max Baarspul's papers**, particularly "A review of flight simulation techniques" (*Progress in Aerospace Sciences*, vol. 27, 1990) — **PAID/PAYWALLED** but findable through institutional access | Delft's contribution: the definitive review of flight simulation technique, mathematical modelling of aircraft dynamics for simulation, and the SIMONA research simulator work. Baarspul's papers on motion cueing and on model identification from flight test are the standard references. |
| **ICAO Doc 9625, *Manual of Criteria for the Qualification of Flight Simulation Training Devices*** — Vol I (Aeroplanes, Ed. 4) and Vol II (Helicopters) — **PAYWALLED** | The international framework: the seven fidelity Types and the feature-based approach. |
| **14 CFR Part 60** — https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-14/chapter-I/subchapter-D/part-60 — **FREE** | The FAA's Qualification Performance Standards, with the full objective test tables in Appendix A. The single most useful free document in simulation engineering: it tells you exactly what tolerance every parameter must meet. |
| **EASA CS-FSTD(A) and CS-FSTD(H)** — https://www.easa.europa.eu — **FREE** | The European equivalent. |
| **AIAA Modeling and Simulation Technologies Conference proceedings** — **PAID/PAYWALLED** | The annual venue for simulation engineering research. |
| **Royal Aeronautical Society Flight Simulation Group** — https://www.aerosociety.com — conference papers, some **FREE** | The industry's professional forum; the annual conference papers are the best source on current practice. |
| **JSBSim documentation** — https://jsbsim.sourceforge.net and the GitHub repository — **FREE** | The reference manual is also a decent tutorial on how a flight dynamics model is structured. |
| **Brian Stevens, Frank Lewis and Eric Johnson, *Aircraft Control and Simulation*** (Wiley; 3rd edition 2015) — **PAID** | Equations of motion, aerodynamic modelling, trim and linearisation, control system design, with MATLAB. Adjacent to simulation engineering and the right book for the dynamics. |
| **Jan Roskam, *Airplane Flight Dynamics and Automatic Flight Controls*** — **PAID** | The classical stability-and-control derivation, and where the derivatives in a simulator's data package come from. |
| **Robert Nelson, *Flight Stability and Automatic Control*** — **PAID** | The undergraduate version; clearer for a first pass. |

| **Company annual reports and 20-F/10-K filings** | **FREE** | The only reliable source for airline financials. IAG, Lufthansa, AF-KLM, Ryanair, Wizz, Delta, United, American, Southwest, AerCap, Air Lease all publish. ## 8. A suggested reading order

If you are starting from a professional pilot's knowledge and want the industry:

1. **Doganis, *Flying Off Course*** — the economics.
2. **Doganis, *The Airline Business*** — the regulation and strategy.
3. **Morrell, *Airline Finance*** — how to read an airline's accounts.
4. **Belobaba/Odoni/Barnhart** — the quantitative core; read the RM and schedule planning chapters at least.
5. **Dekker, *The Field Guide*** — then **Reason, *Managing the Risks***.
6. **Leveson, *Engineering a Safer World*** (free) if you want the systems-theoretic view.
7. **Allerton, *Principles of Flight Simulation*** — if the simulator side interests you.
8. **14 CFR Part 60** and **CS-FSTD(A)** (both free) alongside it.
9. **The JATR report and the House T&I MAX report** (free) — the best available case study in how certification, commercial pressure and organisational structure interact.
10. **EUROCONTROL Performance Review Reports** and **IATA's monthly market analyses** (both free) for keeping current.
